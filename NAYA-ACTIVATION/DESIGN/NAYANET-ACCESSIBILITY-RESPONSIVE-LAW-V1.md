# 🔱 NayaNET — Accessibility + Responsive Law V1

**Status:** PROPOSED CANONICAL — HUMAN DIRECTOR RATIFICATION REQUIRED

## 1. Accessibility is Visual Bliss

Accessibility is not a compliance appendix.

> **If a person cannot comfortably perceive or operate NayaNET, the interface is not experiencing Visual Bliss.**

## 2. Vision

Design for users with:
- reduced visual acuity;
- color-vision differences;
- low contrast conditions;
- zoomed interfaces;
- bright ambient light;
- small screens;
- large screens viewed from distance.

## 3. Typography

Generous text is the default.

Do not:
- use tiny body copy;
- require the user to squint;
- hide critical information in small metadata;
- make important actions dependent on very small labels.

## 4. Color independence

Color cannot be the only signal for:
- error;
- success;
- active;
- disabled;
- verified;
- blocked;
- selected.

Pair color with:
- text;
- icon;
- shape;
- position;
- pattern;
- state label;
- other perceivable cues.

## 5. Focus

Every interactive element must have a visible keyboard-focus state.

Focus must be:
- obvious;
- stable;
- aesthetically integrated;
- not dependent on color alone.

## 6. Keyboard

All primary workflows must be operable by keyboard.

Keyboard order should follow logical reading order.

Modal/overlay behavior must be predictable.

Focus must not disappear into a visually inaccessible area.

## 7. Motion

Honor:
**prefers-reduced-motion**

When motion is reduced:
- remove or minimize nonessential movement;
- preserve state clarity;
- preserve depth where possible through static material;
- never remove essential status information.

The design must still feel premium when motion is off.

## 8. Touch

Interactive targets must be comfortably touchable.

Do not place high-consequence controls so close together that accidental activation becomes likely.

## 9. Responsive law

NayaNET is the same environment at every size.

Responsive behavior must preserve:
- identity;
- hierarchy;
- purpose;
- readability;
- major actions;
- truth states.

Do not “mobile optimize” by destroying the desktop information model.

## 10. Breakpoint behavior

At smaller widths:
- navigation may collapse/reconfigure;
- boards may stack;
- secondary information may progressively disclose;
- search may reflow;
- actions may become contextual.

But the experience must remain recognizably NayaNET.

## 11. Zoom and text scaling

The interface should remain useful when users increase text size or browser zoom.

Do not treat zoom as an edge case.

## 12. Accessibility 10/10 gate

Pass only when:
- keyboard workflows work;
- focus is visible;
- motion reduction works;
- semantic structure is correct;
- important states are not color-only;
- text remains readable;
- controls are operable;
- responsive layouts preserve meaning;
- no effect reduces comprehension.

> **Beauty that excludes a human is not elite design.**
