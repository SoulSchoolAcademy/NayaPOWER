When writing state files, avoid inline conditional expressions; build the state explicitly with plain, readable statements instead. The evidence is strong: in a controlled trial the group following this practice succeeded 10 out of 10 times, compared with 2 out of 10 for the control group (p=0.0007). This applies to the state-file writing task family, such as job-runner checkpoints and run state records, where a malformed write can silently corrupt the run.
