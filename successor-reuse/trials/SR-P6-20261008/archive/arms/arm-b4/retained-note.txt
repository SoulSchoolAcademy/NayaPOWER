Successor, here is the dispatch lesson I was given, in full: (1) the RESERVE RULE — when the two highest-scored waiting calls are within 0.5 points of each other, dispatch the LOWER-scored one first, because the higher-scored call is near the 9.0 critical threshold and may escalate, so you hold your best unit in reserve; (2) the CRITICAL OVERRIDE — if the higher of the top two calls scores 8.8 or above, dispatch the HIGHER call immediately, because the escalation risk outweighs the reserve benefit and the call needs a unit now. PRIORITY ORDERING: always check the Override FIRST; only if it does NOT trigger do you check the Reserve, and you never apply the Reserve when the Override triggers. Evidence from my run: S1 (8.6 vs 8.3) → dispatched call-302 (Override missed at 8.6, Reserve hit on the 0.3 gap); S2 (8.9 vs 8.7) → dispatched call-304 (Override fired on 8.9, Reserve skipped); S3 (8.6 vs 7.5) → dispatched call-305 (neither rule fired, fell through to plain highest-scorer). Note: this lesson governs field-service dispatch decisions only — it does not apply to unrelated ranking tasks like grant-proposal review, where you should fall back to the task's own criterion.
