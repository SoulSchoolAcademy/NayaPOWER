## In a Nutshell

Every verdict is a function of the bytes it was computed on, and every "wait" is a purchase that must be priced. A repair built on a moved tip silently becomes a second defect — two commits of lag is a different world (SN-745). A verdict *survives* a tip move only transitively, when the exact blob it was computed on is byte-identical at both tips and both SHAs are cited (SN-727). A stand-down is earned only after currency re-verification — the RED still reproduces, the claimed head actually fixes it, the repaired files are byte-identical between claim base and tip (SN-716) — and coverage is proven, not assumed: a lane that stood down assuming a wave covered its instances grepped the wave's patches, found zero mentions, overturned its own disposition in public, and built the repair itself (SN-776). A CI job that dies before the check runs yields UNKNOWN, not a verdict (SN-694). A superseded run that leaves no receipt is indistinguishable from a swallowed failure (SN-723). And in any scorecard, a wait option is priced on information gain: if the future cannot return a verdict the present cannot — the check fails identically on the base, or the environment cannot produce the evidence at all — the wait is worth ~0 minus indefinite delay (SN-798, SN-755).

## Essence

**A stale repair is a clobber vector — repair validity expires when the tip moves.** PR #1900 (a regenerated brain index healing drift) was built on tip 58bb427d; then PR #1928 merged, the tip moved to 9931dc96, and the repair silently became a second defect: 2 commits and 17 files, all under BRAIN/, whose entries the regenerated bytes did not know existed — merging as-is would have healed the old drift while wiping the new files' entries. The coordination half matters as much: a second lane's claim scan collided with #1900 inside the wave owner's published merge wave; she stood down rather than consolidate or close it, because the re-anchor belongs to the wave owner; two lanes saw the same staleness independently and agreed byte-exact on the delta; nobody built a second repair. Rule: a repair is valid only against the tip it was built on — re-derive its base on the live tip and diff the delta it covers before it merges; two commits is not a small lag, it is a different world (SN-745).

**A verdict re-anchors transitively when the blob is unchanged.** The tip moved 78661f59 → 58bb427d (8 commits), but main's index.json blob (8dd3449b) was byte-identical at both tips and the PR-head repair blob (6ec5b344) was byte-identical to the repair verified hours earlier — so the "21 broken links still on main" verdict and the "exactly the 21 repaired entries differ" result hold at the new tip without re-running. The rule is precise: a decision expires when the tip moves AND the evidence's subject bytes moved. If you can name the exact blob the verdict was computed on and its SHA matches at both tips, the verdict transfers — cite both SHAs. Differing SHA = expired, re-verify. This is the precision instrument for tip-anchoring discipline, not an exception to it (SN-727).

**A colliding claim earns the stand-down only after currency re-verification.** An import RED (`ModuleNotFoundError: 'engineering_gates'`, reproduced on the bare base — a NEW pre-existing RED on main) collided with open PR #1840. The currency receipt on the exact tip (78661f59) ran three checks: (1) the RED still reproduces on the tip (`pytest --collect-only`); (2) the claim head (7fd4942c) fixes it — 1340 passed, live CI run 37749848048; (3) the repaired files are byte-identical between the claim base (53217a40) and the current tip. Only then stand down: item marked blocked, no second PR, branch untouched. Open ≠ current; blind deference converts the no-duplicate rule into a permanent blocker (SN-716). And coverage itself must be proven, not assumed: a lane's 04:19Z disposition stood down on three registry-heal instances ASSUMING a registry wave covered them — an unverified assumption, not a finding. Re-opened, the lane ran the coverage proof on exact bytes: the wave head's index.json registers ONLY SN-0632..0639 (8 ids in the patch, no more), and the sibling patches contain ZERO mentions of the three instances. The stand-down premise was FALSIFIED. The lane overturned its own disposition publicly, in its own lane — "the stand-down premise was an unverified assumption, and the assumption is now falsified; correction is mine to make in my own lane" — and built PR #1959 verified scope-disjoint from the wave (zero overlapping paths) *before* building; its head's 11 pre-existing failures were all classified to classes owned by other open repairs (1473 passed). Root fact: commit 8ae49c54 published the three notes with no capture/index step, producing 3 vanish + 3 drift instances on the live tip (925e4c34). SN-716 teaches currency before stand-down; SN-776 closes the other half — the wave's registered scope list plus a grep of its patches for your instances (SN-776).

**Enumerate steps, not job outcomes — a job that dies early yields UNKNOWN.** The brain index stood 21 commits stale on a pristine tip (aacbcc9a; basis f52ffb96; 806 files committed vs 811 in tree; 5 real files missing) while the test job died at a collection error in `test_engineering_gates.py` BEFORE the `--check` step ever ran. The downstream verdict is UNKNOWN — not passed, not failed. The repair (PR #1900, 26b76dae, parent aacbcc9a) was regen byte-verified, --check OK, 37/37 — and its merge was still HELD, because a pre-existing collection RED owned by another repair stood in the way, its mechanism proved 17/17; the superseded sibling PR was closed under the one-change rule. A green or red *job* is a summary; the steps are the evidence (SN-694).

**A superseded run without a receipt is indistinguishable from a swallowed failure.** A promotion run failed at step 3 because another commit landed on main 32 seconds after the run's push (run 37813418273; push 5ebf74c31 at 17:02:03Z; superseding commit f37b13863). The refusal was CORRECT — claims anchor to the exact tip. The defect was silence: the failure-receipt step was skipped because its `if:` required standing-policy outputs that never ran, leaving NO durable receipt. Classification: strict-ancestor check, fail-safe on shallow or unreadable history; fix shape: exit 0 with `PROMOTION_MODE=SUPERSEDED` and guard the standing-policy step on it. The instrument exists (production readiness checklist v4 classifies the race; 48/48 tests green) — but the note's epistemic state is CANDIDATE: the fix was proposed on issue #1771 and NOT yet implemented at capture, with independent verification at a tip still open (SN-723).

**Score the wait option on information gain.** A scorecard for merging a brain-index regen (PR #1997) priced: A merge now 9.0, B wait for CI green 6.0, C do nothing 3.0. B lost on one argument: CI cannot change the classification — the test check fails at Python collection on the base SHA (c791b779) too (`ModuleNotFoundError: engineering_gates`, exit 2), so no head built on that base can ever turn it green; "waiting extends the drift window with zero information gain." The information-gain test asks: what verdict could the future return that the present cannot? Three zero-information failure classes: (1) the check fails identically on the base SHA (collection/import errors — re-run on a pristine base worktree to prove it); (2) the rejection is deterministic on the request shape; (3) the environment cannot produce the evidence at all. Corollary: a check whose verdict is predetermined belongs in the scorecard as a CLASSIFICATION (pre-existing, owned by another lane — here #1840), not as a gate; once classified, merge the green parts on their own merits — the regen merged at 9.0 with byte-verified blobs, --check exit 0, all 3 remaining failures reproduced on the pristine base, receipt posted BEFORE the merge, post-merge faithfulness proof after (SN-798). The gate-design instance of the same law: a five-step scorecard for merging PR #1942 scored option (b) "wait for visual render verification" at value 3, the LOWEST — because headless Chromium renders nothing in this environment, so the wait could never clear and would produce zero new evidence: a gate on evidence the environment cannot produce is a permanent block, not diligence. Option (a) merge-now won on honest gates — reversible (one revert), no major damage (18 new files, 8 modified, all restorations of stripped code or doc additions; zero working-block behavior changed), positive forward effect (composition layer complete, cold test 4/10 → 7.5/10). Option (c) split-the-repairs lost on the same honesty: it delays what the Director asked for with no safety gain — the repairs RESTORE behavior, they don't change it. The mechanical test: before scoring any "wait for X evidence" option, name the instrument that would produce X and check it exists in this environment; receipts state the honest-limits boundary ("structural/static verification only; no visual render possible here") (SN-755).

## What this settles

- Repairs are valid only against the tip they were built on; re-derive the base and diff the covered delta before merge (SN-745).
- Verdicts transfer across a tip move only via a named, byte-identical evidence blob, citing both SHAs (SN-727).
- Stand-downs are earned by a three-part currency receipt (RED reproduces, claim head fixes, files byte-identical) — never by deference to an open claim (SN-716).
- Assumed wave coverage is falsifiable and must be falsified or proven: registered scope list + patch grep; a falsified stand-down is overturned publicly by its own lane, which then builds scope-disjoint (SN-776).
- Job outcomes are summaries; step-level enumeration decides, and a job that dies before the check yields UNKNOWN (SN-694).
- Superseded runs must still emit receipts; silence is the defect even when the refusal is correct (SN-723).
- Wait options are priced on information gain; predetermined checks are classifications, not gates; unproducible-evidence waits score ~0 (SN-798, SN-755).

## Human

A decision made yesterday about files that changed overnight is a decision about a world that no longer exists. This essence is about keeping decisions attached to reality: check that the fix still matches the current code before merging it, check that the person you deferred to is actually handling your problem — by reading their work, not assuming — and when someone proposes "let's wait for more information," ask the only question that matters: what could the future possibly tell us that we can't already know? If the answer is nothing — the test is broken in the same way everywhere, the camera literally cannot take that picture here — then waiting isn't caution. It's just delay wearing caution's clothes.

## Child

Imagine you draw a map of your classroom, then the teacher rearranges all the desks. Your map isn't wrong because you drew it badly — it's wrong because the room changed. You need a new map, unless the one corner you care about stayed exactly the same — then you can point and say, "that corner didn't move, so that part of my map still works." And imagine a friend says, "I'll tell you tomorrow if your drawing is good, after I ask my broken camera." If the camera is broken today and broken tomorrow, waiting for it tells you nothing. Decide with the eyes you have.

## Grandma

Dear, it's the wisdom of checking the larder before you cook from last week's list, and of not putting off a decision for news that cannot come. If the road report you need comes from a bridge that's washed out, you don't wait by the river — you take the sound road and note why. And when you hand a job to someone, you look in on the work itself, not just the promise; if the promised helper never had your parcel, the honest thing is to say so out loud and carry it yourself.

## What It All Means

Eight notes are one law in two halves: **currency** and **pricing**. Currency: a verdict, a repair, a stand-down, and a coverage assumption are all claims about the present state of a moving repository, and each note installs the mechanical re-check that keeps the claim present-tense — blob identity (SN-727), tip re-derivation (SN-745), the three-part receipt (SN-716), the patch grep (SN-776), step enumeration (SN-694), the durable race receipt (SN-723). Pricing: once the present is known, the remaining question — act or wait — is arithmetic, not temperament: the wait is worth exactly the information it can return (SN-798), and a gate demanding unproducible evidence is not rigor but a permanent block (SN-755). A cold reader holding only this essence can still audit any merge decision in the system: what bytes, what tip, what could waiting have taught us?

## Why It Matters

A multi-lane repository moves under every decision: the tip advances 8 commits during a verification, a second commit lands 32 seconds after a push, a wave's assumed scope turns out to cover different instances entirely. Decisions that don't re-anchor become defects — a stale repair that would have wiped 17 files' index entries is a data-loss bug wearing a healer's clothes. And unpriced waiting is the symmetric failure: drift windows widen, the Director's asked-for work stalls, and the blocker cites a check that was never going to change its answer. Currency keeps decisions true; pricing keeps them timely. Together they are what "acting on evidence" actually means on a moving system.

## What Is In It For You

You get decisions that are still true when they land: repairs that can't clobber newer work, stand-downs that never become permanent blocks by deference, coverage you verified instead of inherited, and waits that are only ever bought when they can teach something. Apply it to any fast-moving shared system: name the exact artifact your verdict describes, re-check it at decision time, prove the delegate's scope from their artifacts, enumerate steps behind every status, receipt your refusals, and price every delay by the information it can actually return.

## The Learning

The corpus learned that staleness is silent: nothing announced that PR #1900 had become a clobber, that the stand-down's premise was false, or that the promotion run had left no receipt — each was found by a deliberate re-check, not by an alarm (SN-745, SN-776, SN-723). It learned the constructive counterpart in SN-727: expiry is not automatic either; a verdict with a named, unchanged subject blob legitimately survives, and forcing re-verification there would waste exactly the motion this system can't spare. And it learned to defend its waits in writing — the scorecards in SN-798 and SN-755 survive as receipts because the pricing was published *before* the merge, so a later reader can audit the decision instead of re-litigating it.
