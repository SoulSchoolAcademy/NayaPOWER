## In a Nutshell

Many hands build on one canon, and the canon survives only if every repair lands on the *source* — the generator, the reference, the template, the block library — never on the instance that happened to show the symptom. A stale score kept regenerating because it lived in the recurring builders' definitions; patching the report would have healed nothing (SN-679). A grant expiry about to be "reported" was instead removed — the mechanism rebuilt so the cliff cannot recur (SN-706). Where a document and its living showcase disagree, the PAGE wins and the document gets fixed, because you score the experience, never the code (SN-712, SN-717). A candidate canon carries its unresolved conflicts explicitly on HOLD and never overwrites the standing contracts (SN-713). A code is ratified only when it is drift-resistant — every law carrying a pass/fail check, because a rule without a check is a wish (SN-707). New domains get adapters over the canonical engine, never a second engine (SN-732). When the reference implementation already exists and scores above the floor, the brief changes from "build the pieces" to "build to the spec" (SN-739). Apps are assembled from law-tagged canonical blocks — the authoring tool cannot express a non-law component — and automation's authority envelope shrinks as automation grows (SN-758, SN-765). The laws ship inside the library, beside the components they govern (SN-769). And control-plane changes announce themselves on the canonical surface — workflow modifications are flagged in the PR body, never smuggled (SN-678).

## Human

A shared style — for design, for decisions, for anything — doesn't live in the examples people copy; it lives in the sources the examples are generated from. Fix one report and the generator reprints the error tomorrow. Let two documents describe the same buttons and the builders will follow whichever they opened first. These notes keep pulling the truth back to one home: the definition, the living page, the canonical engine, the block library, the template — and when something new is needed, the move is always to extend the canon (an adapter, a new block, a template upgrade), never to start a second canon in a corner. Even the conflicts are kept in the canon, labeled HOLD, where everyone can see them — because a disagreement filed openly is information, and a disagreement silently resolved is a fork nobody voted on.

## Child

Imagine a LEGO club with one big instruction book and one box of official pieces. If a page in the book is wrong, you fix the book — you don't just fix your own model and leave the wrong page for the next kid. If the book's picture and a finished model disagree, you trust the model you can touch and fix the picture. If you need a piece that doesn't exist, you make it, check it follows the club rules, and put it IN the box for everyone — you don't sneak in your own different pieces. And there's only ever one instruction book being rewritten at a time, with the tricky pages paper-clipped and marked "not decided yet" instead of quietly filled in.

## Grandma

Dear, it's the rule of the pattern book in a sewing circle: one master pattern, kept true, and corrections written into the pattern — not whispered to whoever happens to be cutting today. The finished dress on the form outranks the sketch when they disagree; you alter the sketch. New stitches get added to the book with a do-and-don't sample sewn beside them, so the teaching is there at the moment hands are working. And when two experienced sewers disagree on a seam, the disagreement is pinned to the page with both names — never settled by whoever sews fastest while the other is out of the room.

## What It All Means

Twelve notes are one law: **canon is a place, and every truth has exactly one address there.** The generator definition (SN-679), the rebuilt mechanism (SN-706), the living showcase (SN-712, SN-717), the conflict-carrying candidate (SN-713), the drift-hardened code (SN-707), the canonical engine (SN-732), the named reference implementation (SN-739), the block library and its assembly pipeline (SN-758, SN-765), the in-library codification (SN-769), and the announced control plane (SN-678) are all the same commitment viewed from different workbenches: when truth and its copies diverge, repair the source and let the copies re-derive — never bless a copy. A cold reader holding only this essence can still locate authority for any artifact in the system: find its canonical home, or file the gap as a HOLD with an owner.

## Why It Matters

Forks of canon are the slowest and most expensive failures a multi-builder system has, because they don't announce themselves as failures: both versions keep working, each with sincere builders, until the divergence surfaces as contradictory products, unenforceable laws, and scorecards grading against different standards. The drift SN-707 names is the same entropy at the level of a single reader. And the instance-fix temptation (SN-679, SN-706) is the fork's quiet cousin — it leaves the source producing the defect while the symptom ledger looks clean. One canon, repaired at the source, extended by adapters and blocks, with conflicts carried in the open, is what lets many hands move at full speed without the grammar fracturing underneath them.

## What Is In It For You

You get one place to look and one place to fix: a standard you can touch (the living page), generators that stop reprinting yesterday's error, adapters that extend the engine without splitting it, and libraries that teach at the moment of use. When your own work forks — two docs, two tools, two versions of the truth — apply the moves: name the canonical home, fix the source, carry unresolved conflicts in the open with owners, harden every rule with a check, and let novelty enter through the library door, never through a bypass.

## The Learning

The corpus learned that canon failures are usually *discovery* failures before they are discipline failures: the second engine, the duplicated build, and the forked schema were each built by a seat who hadn't found the existing canon — hence SN-739's scan-before-briefing and SN-732's inspect-the-canon-first. It learned that ratification is a property of the artifact's resistance to misuse, not of its correctness on the day it was written (SN-707). And it learned the codification placement law the hard way: law filed beside the work gets used; law filed in a museum gets cited — SN-769's do/don't pair at the point of use, and SN-758's assembler that cannot express a non-law component, are the same insight at two depths: the deepest canon is the one the tools themselves cannot violate.
