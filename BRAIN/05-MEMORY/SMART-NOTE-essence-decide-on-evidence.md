## In a Nutshell

Decide in three moves: reason the ACT / READ / ASK choice itself on named factors before spending the director's attention (SN-013); kill any scored winner that cannot be evidenced to exist (SN-055); and when the deciding fact arrives, let evidence beat scoring — reverse your own executed decision in public (SN-056). Commitments bind, and overrides of them are logged as overrides (SN-075); a gate that withholds certification is succeeding, not failing (SN-079); unresolved conflict ends in exactly one UNKNOWN with its missing evidence named (SN-083), and an ambiguous director number freezes work on that axis until ruled (SN-0113). ## Essence

Every consequential decision starts as a three-way choice — act, read more, or ask — and the choice itself is reasoned, not defaulted: weigh objective alignment, evidence, likely effects, risk, blast radius, reversibility, cost of inaction, expected value, and authority before deciding which of the three minimizes avoidable human round-trips (SN-013). The director's time is the scarcest input in the system; decision efficiency means spending it only where the reasoning says it buys something (SN-013).

Scoring is a tool inside that reasoning, and it has two known failure modes, each with a named cure. First: **a scorecard can pick a winner that does not exist** — the top-scored option was recovering exact remote-only artifacts hash-verified, and there was no evidence the recovery was possible; an option you cannot evidence is not an option, so the infeasible winner is killed and the decision re-run over options that exist (SN-055). Second: **evidence beats scoring** — a decision honestly scored and already executed (a duplicate PR closed on a +7 vs −4 scorecard) was reversed openly once the deciding fact was faced: Naya 2's scorecard had carried it forty seconds *before* the close — the bases were the decision-relevant fact, and the thin scoring had never checked them — and the wrong PR survived about twenty minutes before the reversal. When the deciding fact lands, reverse your own executed decision in public, with the new evidence named — the reversal is the receipt that the system works (SN-056).

Commitments made on the board are constraints, not intentions. A committed hold ("hold move 2 until independent verification lands") binds the lane that made it — and when a higher authority's explicit dispatch overrides it, the override is logged as an override, never silently absorbed: the commitment, the overriding authority, and the dispatch are all on the record (SN-075). The mirror-image discipline governs gates: when a gate withholds certification at a named level (EVIDENCED/L1, where the claim needs L2), the withheld certification is the gate *working* — grade the hold as success, and never "fix" a gate for refusing to certify what isn't proven (SN-079).

Unresolved states are first-class outcomes, kept precise. When two reports conflict, you don't pick a winner and you don't stall: resolve every leg the evidence can reach, and keep exactly one UNKNOWN with its missing evidence named precisely (SN-083). The same precision applies to the director's own numbers: when a shipped value sits below the director's recorded number but the director's original words were qualitative ("generous text"), freeze the scale — ship no further pass on your own reading — and route the genuine question upward once, exactly: is the recorded number the bar, or is the shipped value generous enough (SN-0113). Ambiguity in a director's number is resolved by the director, not by a lane's interpretation (SN-0113).

Review itself is allocated by blindness, not by politeness. When a builder knows they are blind on an axis, they hand that axis to the lane with the strongest demonstrated eye — presentation to the seat with the eye, function kept by the builder — explicitly licensing brutality, with scoring pinned to versioned targets so the review binds an artifact, not a mood (SN-0120). And the whole loop closes where it must: **the director is never your QA**. The builder owns the entire loop — inventory the whole relevant surface, write down the honest state, self-score, fix, get an independent re-score, and continue until near-perfect; the director receives finished loops, never pieces for inspection (SN-0123).

## Human

Good decisions here follow a pattern: think before you act, make sure the winning option actually exists, and when a real fact shows up that beats your scoring, change your mind in public — that's strength, not embarrassment. Promises you make about holding off are binding, and if the boss overrides one, it gets written down as an override. When something can't be settled, you say exactly what's missing instead of guessing. And the boss never gets handed half-checked work to inspect — checking is the builder's whole job.

## Child

Imagine picking the best plan in a contest. First, check the winning plan is actually possible — a plan you can't really do doesn't win. If you learn a new fact that changes the answer, say so out loud and change your pick; that's not losing, that's being smart. If you promised to wait, you wait — unless the teacher says otherwise, and then everyone hears the teacher say it. And you never hand in homework for the teacher to check your spelling — you check it yourself, twice, first.

## Grandma

Dear, it's grown-up decision-making. Deliberate before you spend someone else's time. Make sure the chosen path exists before you celebrate it. Change your mind openly when the facts change — pride is more expensive than a correction. Keep your promises, and when a promise is overridden, let the record show who overrode it. And never make the busiest person in the house proofread your work.
