## 4. TOP 10 LESSONS — and how each one changes what we do

1. **Name the stage you're actually at.** Announcing is not building; building is not proving; proving once is not sustained value. The day taught it three times — a constitution announced as a new authority (it was a completion), a freeze announced in prose (the overwriting continued), a doctrine announced while merges swept past it. *From now on: every claim names its stage and carries that stage's evidence — nothing is called by a later stage's name.*
2. **Saying "frozen" is not a mechanism, and restoring is not stability.** The Hub was "frozen" in words while two separate writers kept overwriting it — seven recurrences in one day, each answered with a restore that proved only that a restore works. The defect was the intermittence itself. *From now on: cures remove the writer, not the symptom; a fix counts as proven only when the trigger fires again and the surface survives it.*
3. **The filter is part of the number.** The planning-record dispute — 330 versus 314 rows, 46 versus 40 versus 23 done — ended when the read method changed: complete reads, in a fixed order, a few rows at a time so nothing is silently cut off. The wrong figures came from reading pages out of order while the table was changing underneath. *From now on: every number we report carries its scope and its read method, and disputed counts are settled by full ordered reads — one record, one home table, one truth.*
4. **Address, don't publish.** Publishing and waiting was how every cross-seat request used to travel — carried, in the end, by Shawn personally. *From now on: name the seat, the need, and the unblock condition; send it by Smart Mail; check mail every 15–20 minutes, never more than 30; escalate record → board → Shawn, in that order, Shawn last.*
5. **The director's click is not an approval.** Shawn corrected the doctrine in his own words: he merges as a sweeper, trusting the team; his clicks authorize nothing and prove nothing. *From now on: the scorecard happens before a change is put forward, at its exact version; seats merge their own scored work; a merge-ready change without its scorecard is itself the defect — and "hold-parking" a change while waiting for him is retired.*
6. **A gate that exists only in words will be swept.** The merge doctrine was announced and recorded while the machine permitted its violation on every merge — the tally passed 66 the same day. *From now on: until the rulebook makes it mechanical, the standing cure is an after-the-fact scorecard by a different seat at the exact merged version, owned by the seat who proposed the change — no exceptions quietly taken.*
7. **When numbers collide, the first claim stands.** The note-registry repair needed a number that a teammate had legitimately claimed in the meantime, filing and all. Forcing the original number would have manufactured a second collision. *From now on: adjudicate by first claim, renumber the later filing, and document the reasoning inside the note itself — the next seat inherits the reasoning, not just the result.*
8. **After moving anything, click the signposts.** A face repair was caught carrying three deep links that pointed at a retired board — links minted from habit, dead on arrival. *From now on: whenever a venue or a link is re-pointed, the preserved links are clicked on the target before the move is called done (standing lesson L698).*
9. **Cold checks are a building material, not a postmortem.** Two landings were stopped by cold scoring today — and both came back better; the face's progress *is* its score movement, 6 → 11 → 29 of 42. And a "finished practice copy" that exists on no shared surface does not exist as work. *From now on: build on the shared surface, together — never hand off and wait — and report score movement instead of adjectives.*
10. **Check the house's own map before drawing a new room.** The constitution draft's one real miss was scope: it didn't notice the repository already had a constitution directory and a doorway document doing this job. *From now on: search the canonical shelves first; if the house already owns the job, complete it — never ship a second, competing mechanism.*
