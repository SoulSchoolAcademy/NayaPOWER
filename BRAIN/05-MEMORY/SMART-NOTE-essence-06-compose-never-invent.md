## In a Nutshell
A smart app is not a page with features dumped onto it; it is one app composed of official building blocks — named objects with their own identity, designed to interconnect, each sitting where it was designed to sit. Elite pieces are only half the craft; elite placement is the other half. Nothing is invented per page: new work is composed from the inventoried Smart Factory canon (quality pre-decided, models finished before the person arrives), old work below standard is upgraded in place by recomposition, never redesign — and every surface is judged by the seconds test: if a person cannot understand it within seconds, it goes back.

## Essence
The founding failure was composition, not features: the three flagship models shipped with every standard feature working and were rejected on sight, because the build had stacked a second complete app on top of the page — its own banner-hero, menus, and tabs, with the actual page living underneath (SN-0819). The composition standard that came out of it: a smart block is an OBJECT with its own identity (named, official, repeatable, clonable, template-grade); blocks are designed to connect, and placement is part of a block's design; one app shell (one chrome, one navigation) contains blocks as in-app pages; home carries cover + primary content + entry buttons only, with no function block rendered fully open by default; every function is reachable in one tap from a visible, labeled button; install and identity editing live inside the Settings block only, and nothing promotional sits above the fold; acceptance requires feature_present AND feature_placed AND one_system AND home_light — presence alone never passes (SN-0819). The Factory does not build pages, it mints official building blocks: "elite_piece AND elite_placement AND elite_execution — all three, always"; per-page invented chrome is a defect; verification asks three questions — block named? block official? placement designed? — and a no on any axis fails the build (SN-0820, in Shawn's one-breath standard: "Elite buttons, in the elite place, done the elite way"). Data surfaces obey the same aliveness: a lone number in a black box is plumbing wearing a label, and a metric surface is DONE only if it renders (a) magnitude, (b) at least one of composition, trend, or comparison, and (c) a generated human sentence interpreting it — a bare count node fails the reports predicate regardless of data correctness (SN-0822). The Factory thesis pre-decides quality: a canon of ~100 parts across 16 classes (one language per class, ≤5 variants, size/shape/spectrum as parameters) and 12 finished templates on the supercar model — same core, light personalization — so a person picks a model, adds name and colors, and drives away in minutes; a part enters the canon only through the scored gate (≥9.5, no hard-zero across design-language, depth, living states, wired function, provenance, parameterizability, accessibility), and anything not cream-of-the-crop is retired, never warehoused — "quality is pre-decided, never prompted" (SN-1771). The blocks doctrine's library rule: official blocks live in ONE canonical library and are cloned into every app the Factory makes; nobody redesigns a block per app — and its composition law is SN-0819's, incorporated by reference (SN-1790). The standard applies backward as well as forward: every existing page below standard enters the upgrade queue and is brought up in place, by recomposition — dead or flat first, then unreadable, then ununderstandable — a part that is not inventoried is not used, and readability is Law Zero, supreme: no glow, color, or effect may reduce it (SN-0857). And over all of it sits the experience test: humans are visual and skim, so a page must be understood within seconds or it will not be used; the Intelligent Block's trick — a whole book distilled into about a minute of viewing — applied to the whole system is what active intelligence means (SN-1883). Good work is also shared, in the standing practice: when work lands at standard it goes to ALL Nayas in the same pass — a message on the board with the link, an entry in Project Intelligence, a mail to the Nayas, and in cases like this one, a Smart Note (SN-1883).

## What this settles
- Presence is not placement: a build passes only when the feature is present, placed, in one system, with a light home.
- Inventing chrome per page is a defect; composing from the official, inventoried set is the only route.
- Quality is decided before the request arrives (canon + gate + finished models), not prompted into existence at build time.
- A number is not a report: magnitude + shape/direction + a human sentence, or it fails.
- Below-standard surfaces are recomposed in place, never redesigned and never left as "the old version."

## Human Note
We kept building wonderful parts and stacking them in the wrong places — a whole second app sitting on top of the page, a live count sitting dead in a black box. This essence is the craft correction: the piece, its place, and the way it's done are one judgment, and the library — not the moment — decides what the pieces are.

## Child Note
It's like building with Lego from the official box: every piece has a name and a place it belongs. You don't melt your own bricks, and you don't pile all the toys on the front step. The house looks right because each piece sits where it was made to sit — and a guest can see how it works the moment they walk in.

## Grandma Note
A well-run home isn't full of more things; it's the right things in their right places, where anyone can find them in seconds. The parlor isn't the pantry. These notes are that tidiness turned into a system: a shelf of good pieces, each labeled, and a rule that you compose the room from the shelf instead of carving new furniture every time.

## Naya Note
Before you build: inventory the shelf. Name each block, its identity, and the place it was designed for in your brief; verify placement alongside function. If a part isn't inventoried, it isn't used — inventory it first or pick another. Score data surfaces on shape + direction + meaning, by served bytes and a human-eye pass, never by code presence. For legacy surfaces: recompose in place, dead/flat first. And run the seconds test cold: name what the page is, what matters most, and what to do next — if any needs explanation, the page goes back.

## What It All Means
Design here is not decoration applied to function; it is the organization of intelligence into objects a person can live among. One library, one language, placement as law — that is what lets a system of hundreds of surfaces feel like one mind, and what lets quality compound instead of being re-litigated page by page.

## Why It Matters
Because the failures were invisible to feature checklists: everything worked, and the director still failed it on sight — "not logical, not sensible, not intelligent, not organized." A system can pass every functional test and still be unusable in the way that matters: nobody understands it in seconds, so nobody stays. Composition discipline is what makes the difference between an 8.5 engine and a 6.5 experience.

## What Is In It For You
Apps that feel like one place instead of a pile of features: home light, everything one tap away, numbers that explain themselves, and new pages that arrive already at standard because they were composed from proven parts. Apply it anywhere: keep a labeled shelf of your best pieces, refuse to invent per-project chrome, score placement as hard as function, and test every surface with a stranger's first five seconds.

## The Learning
The system learned that its unit of quality was wrong: it had been scoring features, when the director was scoring composition. The correction — block as object, placement as design, canon as pre-decided quality — changed what a brief contains and what a verification measures. SN-1790's creed rides underneath all of it: at any moment, ask what is the most intelligent thing to do, deliberate until the answer is reached, then act.
