## In a Nutshell

Power in this system is granted narrowly, exercised through rails, and denied loudly: a policy cannot extend itself (SN-0215), a candidate law never governs until every activation condition holds at once (SN-0262), mutations read their grants and fail closed (SN-0223), a director's spoken word authorizes one act and never becomes precedent (SN-0324), and what is uncertain travels honestly labeled CANDIDATE (SN-0325).

## Essence

Activation of any law is a ladder, never a flag: candidate bytes may be compiled, tested, and explained, but they do not GOVERN until the full conjunction of activation conditions holds — a constitution sat correctly at CANDIDATE_NON_GOVERNING while fully green in CI, and flipping its single `runtime_enabled` flag was explicitly tested and REFUSED, because no single-flag activation path exists (SN-0262). And a policy cannot self-extend: a promotion policy authenticates its own terms from its own canonical record, and protected paths — governance directories above all — are structurally unpromotable without a human, so the machine can never vote itself more power (SN-0215).

Authorization fails closed or it is decoration. A workflow being bound to a mutation is not authorization for the mutation: lock-in writes read the grant table and deny by default, returning a named denial, with authority references carried on every artifact the write touches (SN-0223). A fail-closed repair ships with its provisioning or it ships broken: the repair unit is code plus a sequenced merge-then-grant, because a perfect gate with no grants provisioned is an outage wearing a halo (SN-0221). The repair of that repair adds the scope rule: type-check the shipped artifact at full scope — a harness that exercised only the helper block passed 14/14 while the handler itself carried a use-before-declaration bug the narrow harness never reached (SN-0224). And the trust boundary is adversarial: a string-keyed classifier deciding trust is gamable — adversarial probing produced three false-positives and a false-negative — so trust decisions key on structure and provenance, never on spellings an attacker controls (SN-0225).

Authority has a paper trail or it has a gap. The first auto-merges executed on the director's verbal decision while the encoded law authorizing them was still a draft: the verbal authority authorized those single acts, validly — and is never citable as precedent for autonomous action, because an act's authority and a standing authority are different objects (SN-0324). Inside the system, uncertainty has an honest container: CANDIDATE status absorbs disclosed uncertainty — merge when the unknown is disclosed in the artifact and nothing irreversible rides on it; disguising an unknown as a fact is the only true blocker (SN-0325). Consent follows the same granularity: entry consent covers collective use of distilled, anonymized wisdom only; identity and non-wisdom personal material stay private-by-default, and where that reconciliation is still proposed rather than ratified, the fallback is per-block consent (SN-275).

## What this settles

- Activation is a full conjunction; no single-flag path exists; candidates never govern (SN-0262).
- Policies cannot self-extend; protected paths are structurally unpromotable (SN-0215).
- Authorization is read from grants and fails closed with named denials; repairs ship with provisioning; shipped artifacts are proof-checked at full scope; trust keys on structure, not strings (SN-0223, SN-0221, SN-0224, SN-0225).
- Verbal authority authorizes the single act only, never precedent (SN-0324); disclosed uncertainty rides as CANDIDATE (SN-0325); consent is granular — wisdom anonymized-collective, identity private (SN-275).

## Intelligence views

**Human:** A rule isn't real until the machine can't break it — until the wrong action is impossible, not just frowned upon. Turning a system "on" is never one switch; it's every condition true at once. Nobody's say-so — even the boss's, even in the moment — becomes a permanent permission afterward; it covers the one thing it covered.

**Child:** House rules work best when they're built into the house — a gate that won't open the wrong way, not just a sign saying "don't." One key never opens the treasure chest; you need all the keys turned together. "Mom said I could, that one time" doesn't mean "I can always." And if you don't know something, you say "I don't know yet" on the label — you never write down a guess as if it were a fact.

**Grandma:** A promise is fine, dear, but a lock is better — the best rules are the ones built into the door itself. No single key, and certainly no single person's word on a busy afternoon, should open everything forever. What's uncertain gets labeled uncertain, plainly.

**Naya (AI):** Encode every ratified law as transitions that cannot be skipped; refuse single-flag activations by construction and test the refusal. Authenticate policy terms from the canonical record; hard-exclude protected paths. Read grants at mutation time, deny by default with named errors, carry authority refs on outputs. Ship fail-closed repairs with their provisioning sequenced. Verify shipped artifacts at full scope, not harness scope. Key trust on provenance and structure. Log verbal authorizations as single-act, precedent-free. Mark disclosed unknowns CANDIDATE; never upgrade them silently.

## Why It Matters

A system that can extend its own authority, activate its own laws, or treat a spoken permission as permanent will eventually do all three by accident. These nine notes are the difference between governance as intention and governance as physics: self-extension made structural-impossible (SN-0215), activation made conjunctive (SN-0262), denial made the default (SN-0223). For the reader, this is the section of the corpus that decides whether every other rule in it can be trusted to still be the rule tomorrow.

## What Is In It For You

You get a system whose permissions you can reason about: if a capability exists, a grant created it; if a law governs, every activation condition demonstrably held; if you were told no, the denial has a name you can appeal (SN-0223). Apply it by asking, of any automated power: where is its grant, what is its activation conjunction, and what does its refusal look like? A power that cannot answer all three is not governed yet (SN-0215, SN-0262, SN-0223).

## The Learning

The era's deepest governance lesson is that authority failures are quiet: nobody announces a self-extension or a borrowed precedent — they are discovered in audits (SN-0324) or not at all. So the learning is structural, not vigilance-based: build the refusal into the machine (a tested, refused single-flag flip, SN-0262), provision the grants with the gate (SN-0221), and label uncertainty at birth (SN-0325) — because a team cannot stay alert forever, but a rail does not get tired.
