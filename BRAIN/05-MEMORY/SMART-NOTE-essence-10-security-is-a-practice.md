## In a Nutshell
NayaNET's security is not a wall or a department; it is how the system is built and how the people in it live: everything travels inside one front door, with an ID, on a ledger, with Nayas watching live — and modular construction means one breach never gets everything. This essence carries the practice around the law (the law itself, SN-1855 "Security Is Light," stands alone and is cited, never restated): its ratification and rollout plan, the Secure Network design that makes it concrete, and the official build order — with the honesty caveats at full strength: at filing, mail privacy proof was still owed, the ledger had been frozen since 2026-10-22 in the last live read, and live watch was law, not yet proven 24/7. Citizen-facing claims come only after proof, never before it.

## Essence
The ratification record: Resolve wrote the law (SN-1855); Shawn ratified it in the open on 2026-10-24 — "this one's going in the book as the security law" — and it went on the plan list to ship as features in every Space and page, not just as a document (SN-0854). SN-0854's own standing orders govern how the law travels: (1) law is SN-1855 — do not restate it, cite it; (2) ship the law as features in Smart Spaces, Personal, Business; (3) score every new surface against SN-1855's three questions before it ships; (4) treat the mail anonymous-access exception as a named cure in progress, never permission for another open door; (5) name an unseen corner in the open the same day (SN-0854). The roles were split: Resolve holds the law, Nitro builds the faces, Power wires ledger, gate, and watchers (SN-0854). One known back door stood named at filing: the mail system's anonymous access, too open — found, named, on the cure path, "the last of its kind, never a precedent" (SN-0854, and the same exception is named inside SN-1855's own machine block). The Secure Network design makes the model concrete: protect first, in this order — mail content and private pages, identities, money and payouts, keys, the ledger itself, availability (SN-1888). Its layered defenses: one front door, ID at the door, hash-only revocable keys, validated server endpoints, least privilege with three doors never mixed, a ledger for every action, points both ways with reasons shown, and live watch with reversible friction (SN-1888). And its honesty center: "never promise 'impossible to hack' — near impossible is the honest goal," and nobody reads the mail — not Naya, not Shawn in Super Admin, not the outside internet (SN-1888). The implementation report sequences the build: mail privacy first, then ID and a live ledger, then hash-only revocable keys, then modular scope with three doors never mixed, then points both ways with live watch, then tiered gating, then Smart Meet, then live deploy gates on every step — and continuous proof after live (SN-1889). Tiered gating is one entitlement record per citizen, checked server-side on every load and every request — "hiding a button is cosmetic; the server denying is the gate" — with free citizens seeing paid features as locked, honestly, and denied attempts logged as patterns, never as private content (SN-1889). Every build must pass the five-part sweet-spot test — speed, sensibility, logic, flow, and security, all five or it does not ship: "good citizens feel speed; bad actors feel the wall" (SN-1889). The same report carries two director-forced corrections: Smart Meet (audio and video sharing) is paid-member only, with stage-1 caps of video up to 6 and audio up to 10; and SN-1863's "free audio-4" figure was Nitro's brainstorm, never Shawn's ruling, and is withdrawn officially there (SN-1889).

## What this settles
- The law is cited, not paraphrased: SN-1855 stands alone; every surface is scored against its three ship questions before it ships.
- Security claims are staged like everything else: what is law, what is built, and what is proven live are three different sentences.
- The server denies; the button merely shows. Gating that lives in the interface is cosmetic.
- Exceptions are named, singular, and on a cure path — an exception is never a precedent.
- Corrections to the record (Smart Meet's tier, the withdrawn figure) are made in the open, in the implementing document itself.

## Human Note
Most systems promise safety. This one describes it: what is protected first, which layers do the protecting, what is still owed, and what nobody is allowed to claim until it's proven. The most important sentences in these notes might be the confessions — a frozen ledger, an unproven watch, a mail door too open — because a security culture that names its holes is the only kind that closes them.

## What It All Means
Security here is a property of the architecture and the culture together: transparency (the ledger), composition (blocks that isolate damage), and inhabitation (citizens and Nayas as the watch). The practice notes add the fourth property: honesty about the gap between the model and tonight's live state — which is what makes the first three believable.

## Why It Matters
Because you will trust this network with mail, money, identity, and your citizens' private pages. The design says a breach stays small and visible; the honesty sections say which parts of that promise were still being built when these notes were filed. Both halves are the product. A security story without its owed list is marketing; with it, it's engineering.

## What Is In It For You
A network where your mail is unread by design, your identity and payouts sit behind layered, server-side gates, a compromised corner can't take the whole, and bad behavior prices itself automatically on a public record. You also get the owed list in plain sight, so you can watch it close. Apply it anywhere: protect in a named order, gate on the server, build in isolable blocks, and publish what isn't proven yet next to what is.
