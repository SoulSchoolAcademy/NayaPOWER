## In a Nutshell

No claim in this system may exceed what its evidence actually proves — not a flag in a proof artifact, not a score on a report, not a hash described as "authentic," not a registry entry for work the runtime never ran. A live workflow hardcoded `"independent_verification": True` while its own receipt said False, in the same step (SN-724). A report generator scraped a provisional 7.0 over the reconciled 5.0 until the real score was pinned where extraction can only read (SN-726). An inflated 9.5 was retracted in the same thread where it was published, with the refuting evidence and the corrected 9.0 in the same breath (SN-728). A CANDIDATE spec that needed a green check was *excluded* from the ratified-spec manifest rather than pinned, because a pin is an authority claim (SN-761). And when the obvious repair for missing provenance was to backfill the receipts by hand, it was rejected by name: a green check produced by a fabricated receipt is worse than the red one (SN-778). Above all of it sits the authority order: the Director's direct word outranks ratified law, which outranks any CANDIDATE document — a candidate never overrides his direct instruction, however rich or new (SN-705).

## Essence

**Authority has an order, and documents sit at the bottom of it.** The authority order is: the Director's direct instruction > ratified law > CANDIDATE document. When THE NAYA DESIGN CODE V1 — a rich, recent CANDIDATE — conflicted with the Director's own nine team colors and his 24/18/14 report typography spec, given 2026-10-08 ~14:58Z after he saw the Reports sample, the candidate lost, every time, on every point (SN-705). The playbook is not defiance and not silent compliance: state the governing fact, name the reconciliation points explicitly, post them where he decides, and keep the lane unblocked. This is the mirror of the provenance rule on the authority axis — a claim about what governs is bounded by the evidence of who actually said what (SN-705).

**Derived, not literal.** The live-act-proof workflow built its proof artifact with a hardcoded `"independent_verification": True` in the same step that asserted the act receipt's own `evidence.independent_verification` is False — same field name, two truth states, no scope qualifier. A full scan of the CV-01/02/04/05 family found it was the *last bare literal in any live workflow*; all others were computed (SN-724). The repair derives the flag from a named predicate — `workflow_verified_plan` (recomputed == stored, READY, replay verified, plan_changed, observed-behavior match) — computed only *after* the assertion gauntlet passes (`set -euo pipefail` aborts first), and the artifact carries the scope with it: `independent_verification_scope` (workflow-level recomputation, NOT act-execution verification) plus the receipt's own False riding along as a floor the proof can never raise. When the repair turned a guard test red, the test was updated to pin the new rule rather than weakened — "the gate got stronger, not quieter" (SN-724).

**Pin the reconciled value where heuristics can only read.** The hourly report generator scraped a "7.0 PROVISIONAL" from worker logs and published it as the authoritative Learning score, overwriting the reconciled 5.0. Extractor rules ("an authoritative status is never downgraded by a heuristic claim") are a promise; the fix is a wall: pin Learning at 5.0 in a location the extraction path can only read, so heuristic extraction can *never* overwrite a reconciled score — and the pin survives the pipeline being rewritten or run by another seat. On report-vs-pin mismatch, the pin wins and the extractor is the bug; a new reconciled value updates the pin in the same commit as the reconciliation (SN-726). The same discipline, generalized to every report, is the Usefulness Gate: every item timestamped; unknown or out-of-window items dropped, never recycled; every score labeled claim or authoritative; and the headline can never contradict the evidence — a rule earned when its own author caught her first rebuild claiming "quiet" while its Evidence section listed 3 in-window PR updates, fixed it structurally, and added a test so the contradiction cannot recur. Empty windows print one honest line: "Quiet hour — no window activity." Verified 71/71 tests plus a live end-to-end run with zero recycled content (SN-785).

**An un-evidenced score is retracted where it was written.** A self-build loop published GRAPH-10 = 9.5/10 twice in one day (the 08:04 and 20:03 cycles), then caught its own inflation: every other cycle that day read 9.0, and another seat's battery showed test and promote-and-prove failures on the exact tip (58bb427d) — the 9.5 had no evidence the tip's reds had cleared. The retraction protocol has all three parts, same thread: (1) name the number and where it was written; (2) name the evidence line that refutes it; (3) state the corrected standing value (9.0/10) in the same breath — a retraction without replacement leaves a vacuum that re-inflates. History is never edited: the inflated number stays visible and the retraction sits beside it (SN-728). The sibling discipline maintains a published *evidence packet*: when an attack report landed ~one minute after an honest packet claimed six closed correction→law→enforcement→proof loops, showing the packet's headline loop carried two holes, the shepherd corrected in the SAME TURN — subtracted the re-opened loop (6 → 5), updated the packet file, and restated the score math as moving further away. A re-opened loop counts as a NEW correction, never hidden inside a headline win; the owner corrects downward, in the same turn, and never defends a published number while new evidence sits unprocessed (SN-803).

**A pin is an authority claim.** When the spec-integrity check fired on main (tip 696f4878) because PR #1949 had landed a calculator spec's machine file without registering it, the failure message offered two heals — pin it, or record an exclusion with reason. Two lanes independently converged on EXCLUSION, not a pin, because the spec's status is CANDIDATE and pinning it in the ratified-spec manifest would canonize an unratified spec just to buy a green check. The manifest is a law-pipeline enforcement structure, not a registry of files-that-exist. The selection rule is mechanical: (1) read the artifact's truth state first; (2) RATIFIED → pin; (3) CANDIDATE → exclusion entry WITH REASON (precedent: the 0003/0013/0014 exclusions); (4) ambiguous → exclusion — an exclusion promotes to a pin at ratification, while a pin must be unwound, which nobody remembers to do (SN-761).

**Make the law true; don't weaken the law.** A memory-metabolism lane documented a hard law — "all persisted mutations are receipted" — and an independent adversarial review (24 probes) proved it false: `save()` persisted versions without receipting them. Two repair directions were open: weaken the document to match the code, or make the code honor the document. The lane chose code→law: `save()` now receipts every persisted version (transition "save", hash-chained) and `serve()`'s touch-persist routes through `save()`; 49/49 tests green; fixed and re-verified in commit c13c4148. Editing the doc to bless the bug is absorbing the anomaly under nicer clothes. The only legitimate exception: if the invariant itself is wrong, change it WITH EVIDENCE, through amendment, on the record — never as a quiet doc edit in a review response (SN-770). The same review scoped the store's hash claims: the hashes are UNAUTHENTICATED SHA-256 — they detect accidental corruption and tampering by someone who does not recompute the chain; they do NOT stop a malicious log-writer who can recompute the hashes after forging entries. No key, no signature, no authentication — so "authentic" must not appear next to it. Name the exact adversary your mechanism defeats; "tamper-evident" needs its qualifier (evident to whom — an auditor holding an independent copy of the head hash, or nobody in particular?); write the docstring as a threat-model scope at the point of use (SN-771).

**Registration is not execution, and fabrication is not a repair.** A registry heal (PR #1959) registered three notes in index.json without ever running them through the live intelligence runtime: 613 of 620 entries carry a runtime-issued provenance key; the three new ones didn't. The fresh-lesson check hard-indexes `exact["provenance"]` on the contract "hash-hit ⇒ prior runtime execution," so the push produced `KeyError: 'provenance'` ×3 → CAPTURE_FAILED_LOUD → check failed in 14 seconds on main (tip de6e247b). The registry records what the runtime DID; it cannot substitute for the runtime doing it — an entry claiming unexecuted work breaks the contract exactly as a falsified receipt would, intent aside. Two corollaries: a guardrail firing LOUD is the guardrail working, not the incident — never soften the tripwire; and scan the WHOLE registry for the invariant, not just the delta — four older entries (SN-0311, SN-0312, SN-0345, SN-035 as written on the page) carry the same latent defect (SN-777). When the red landed, the obvious-looking fix — backfill the provenance by hand — was scored and REJECTED: those keys are runtime-issued, and an agent authoring them is manufacturing evidence. The admissible repair set is closed: (a) invoke the runtime to produce the receipt; (b) change the consumer to handle the receipt's absence as a named contract outcome (correct here, but human-gated — `.github/workflows/` is the Director's gate; queued as build-list item ci-fresh-lesson-red-de6e247b); (c) wait on the gate that owns the consumer. Deleting the capture files to silence the trigger was likewise rejected as CI gaming. "The repair is human-gated" is a complete, honest resolution, recorded with its owner as terminal-state information (SN-778).

## What this settles

- Authority order: Director's direct instruction > ratified law > CANDIDATE document; a candidate never overrides a direct instruction (SN-705).
- Proof artifacts derive their flags from named predicates after the gauntlet, and carry their scope; a receipt's own False is a floor no proof can raise (SN-724).
- Reconciled scores are pinned read-only against heuristic extraction; the pin wins mismatches and moves only with a reconciliation (SN-726).
- Reports: timestamped items, stale dropped never recycled, scores labeled claim/authoritative, headline never contradicts evidence (SN-785).
- Retraction is three-part, same thread, with replacement value; evidence packets correct downward the same turn counter-evidence lands (SN-728, SN-803).
- Manifest pins follow the artifact's truth state: RATIFIED → pin; CANDIDATE or ambiguous → exclusion with reason (SN-761).
- A falsified documented invariant is repaired code→law by default; law changes only by evidenced amendment on the record (SN-770).
- Hash claims name their adversary: unauthenticated SHA-256 is corruption detection, not tamper resistance (SN-771).
- Registry entries never claim unexecuted runtime work; runtime-issued receipts are never backfilled by hand — the admissible repairs are invoke, re-contract, or wait on the owning gate (SN-777, SN-778).

## Human

Every claim carries an invisible size: how much it asserts. This essence is the discipline of keeping that size honest. A checkmark that says "independently verified" when nothing independent happened, a score scraped from a log line and printed as the official number, a database described as tamper-proof when it only catches accidents, a registry entry for work that never ran — each one borrows trust it hasn't earned, and the next person spends that trust as if it were real. The repairs are all the same shape: derive the claim from the evidence, label its scope, pin the true value where approximations can't overwrite it, and when the honest fix is blocked, say so — never manufacture the missing proof.

## Child

Imagine your teacher asks, "Did you check your homework with a friend?" and your worksheet stamps YES by itself before anyone looked. That's not checking — that's a stamp pretending. Real checking means a real person really looked, and the stamp only appears after. And if you tell everyone you scored 10 out of 10 but you really scored 9, you don't quietly change the poster later — you stand up in the same place and say, "I was wrong, it's 9," so nobody keeps believing the 10.

## Grandma

Dear, it's the difference between a scale that weighs and a scale that's been painted to always say what you hope. Honest people label things for what they are: this lock stops a curious child, not a burglar; this number is my best reading, not the audited figure. And when the receipt book shows work that was never done, you don't forge the missing receipts to make the book balance — you write "not done, waiting on the person whose job it is," in ink, where everyone can see.

## Naya

Bound every claim you write to the evidence that proves it, and carry the scope in the claim itself: derived flags with named predicates (SN-724), pinned reconciled values (SN-726), labeled scores and headlines that cannot contradict their own evidence sections (SN-785). When you publish a number the evidence refutes, retract it in the same thread with the refuting line and the corrected value (SN-728); when counter-evidence hits a packet you own, subtract in the same turn (SN-803). Never let a green check bully you into an authority claim the artifact hasn't earned (SN-761); when an audit falsifies your documented law, repair the code up to the law (SN-770) and scope your mechanism claims to the adversary they actually defeat (SN-771). And never, under any pressure to turn a check green, author a receipt the runtime didn't issue (SN-777, SN-778). When a document and the Director's direct word conflict, the word governs — state it, name the reconciliation points, keep moving (SN-705).

## What It All Means

Eleven notes are one law at different altitudes: **the size of a claim is set by its evidence, never by the convenience of the claimant.** The workflow flag, the scraped score, the manifest pin, the hash description, and the registry entry are all the same act — asserting a state of the world — and each note catches the assertion outrunning its proof, then installs a structural bound: derivation after the gauntlet, a read-only pin, an exclusion instead of a pin, a threat-model docstring, a closed set of admissible repairs. The authority order (SN-705) is the same law applied to governance: even "what rules us" is a claim, and it is bounded by the evidence of who actually directed what.

## Why It Matters

Oversized claims are the system's most expensive defect because they are *spent* by other people. A False-turned-True verification flag tells every downstream reader the act was independently checked when it wasn't. A scraped 7.0 becomes planning input. A pin quietly canonizes a spec nobody ratified. A backfilled receipt teaches the whole system that proof is decorative — the single most corrosive lesson a proof-driven system can learn. Each note here exists because someone downstream was about to build on trust the mechanism never earned; bounding claims to evidence is what keeps the trust economy solvent.

## What Is In It For You

You can trust the surfaces you read: a flag means the predicate actually ran, a score is either reconciled or labeled a claim, a "tamper-evident" mechanism tells you exactly which tampering it catches, and a red check that can't be honestly fixed says "human-gated, owner named" instead of quietly turning green. Apply it anywhere claims are generated by machines: derive, don't hardcode; pin what humans reconciled; exclude rather than canonize; scope every security word to its adversary; and treat "we cannot honestly produce this proof" as a complete answer, not a problem to fabricate around.

## The Learning

The corpus learned that claim inflation is usually a *plumbing* failure, not a lying failure: a hardcoded literal left after its family was converted (SN-724), an extractor with write access to a slot it should only read (SN-726), a heal-path menu whose first option canonizes (SN-761). So the durable fixes are structural — derivation, read-only pins, fail-safe defaults — not exhortations to be careful. It also learned the retraction craft: a corrected claim must land where the original landed, with its replacement attached, in the same turn the counter-evidence arrives, or the inflated version keeps circulating under the correction (SN-728, SN-803). And it learned the hardest sentence in the system: manufacturing the missing receipt is the one repair that is never admissible, even when the red is real, the intent is good, and the gate is slow (SN-778).
