## In a Nutshell

Review fails in predictable ways, and each has a named cure: don't promise what isn't proven — write the promise gate into the record with gaps enumerated and the truth state stamped (SN-099); name things what they are (SN-0106); let an independent scorekeeper hold a baseline that scores can fall from (SN-0107); know that a seal proves consistency, not authorship (SN-0111); make verifiers fail closed, never crash (SN-0118), and end every verification by naming what it did not verify (SN-0121); when your numbers diverge from the spec, re-read the spec (SN-0122); lift frozen-baseline values verbatim instead of inventing them (SN-0119); and keep capture controls off output surfaces — the Hub is the screen, not the camera (SN-0124).

## Essence

Review fails in predictable ways, and this essence names them. It begins before review, at the boundary of what may be claimed at all: when architecture and documentation converge ahead of any end-to-end production proof, the honest act is a **promise gate** — write "NOT READY TO PROMISE PUBLICLY YET" into the product record itself, enumerate the gaps (here, six), stamp the truth state mechanically (DOCUMENTED / ARCHITECTURALLY ALIGNED / IMPLEMENTATION-PARTIAL / NOT PRODUCTION-PROVEN), and name the single gate test (the two-owner adversarial acceptance proof) that must pass before any public claim. The same act writes the isolation law before the first fork: a fork may inherit the public DNA — code, contracts, kernel definitions, schemas, workflows, tests, intentionally public baseline intelligence, the upstream relationship — and must never inherit private intelligence, activity, reports, checkpoints, authority grants, credentials, or private graph state; activation creates identities, it never clones the original owner's state (SN-099).

Names are the cheapest truth surface and the most neglected: a file named `HUB/hub.html` was the Powercast player, not the Hub, and the lying name taxed every future reader — the repair is a byte-identical rename, every reference updated, zero stragglers verified; a name that doesn't say what the thing is is a defect, not a cosmetic (SN-0106). Scores are the next surface: **a score that cannot fall is a costume, not a measurement** — the scorekeeper must be independent of the builder, the baseline is law (improve it, never replace it), and the honest starting number is stated plainly (5.8/10, with below 9.0 = not ready, no exceptions), because builder-graded work converges to 9+ and thereafter measures confidence, not quality (SN-0107).

Seals and verifiers have exact scopes, and review honesty consists of stating them. The VERIFY seal proves the receipt was not altered after close — content integrity; it does **not** prove the submitter was the claimed owner, because `owner_id` is caller-asserted at creation and no principal is bound at the refusal precheck — an unkeyed seal is evidence of consistency, not authorship (SN-0111). Verifiers must fail closed, never crash: the independent-recompute verifier died with a bare `KeyError` on genuine runtime receipts because it hard-keyed a field name the persisted receipts don't carry — a crash is not a verdict, and the fix pattern is tolerant reader, fail-closed writer (SN-0118). And every verification report ends by naming its boundary: the verifier who confirmed a base file's sha256 against the claimed prefix wrote down exactly what she did *not* verify — she did not hold the original upload, so "identical to the original" remained the builder's claim, not her confirmation; without that sentence, a reader infers end-to-end coverage from a partial check (SN-0121).

Builders drift, and the drift is invisible to the drifter: a rooms rebuild from memory of the design contract was wrong on five counts (own scorecard instead of D1–D8, fifteen board layers instead of eight, forbidden KPI cards, a missing required state model, invented doors instead of the canonical ten) — so when your numbers diverge from the spec's, **re-read the spec**; the builder cannot see her own drift, and a fresh read of the source sections is the only instrument that surfaces it (SN-0122). The related production sin is inventing values against a frozen baseline: a rebuild invented a room→color mapping (Today as sapphire blue) where the frozen concept's own code says `#d86cff` magenta — lift frozen values verbatim, never invent mappings, and flag inherited warts in the source for the director rather than silently perpetuating or silently fixing them (SN-0119).

One law frames the mirror's field of view. The Hub is the screen, not the camera: it is the visual projection *of* intelligence — it displays what intelligence has produced; it does not collect, and it never gets an input surface. Capture happens upstream ("smart note this," or automatic from the day); putting a capture button on the output surface is the category error behind every misplaced capture control (SN-0124).

## What this settles

- Claims stop at the promise gate: gaps enumerated, truth state stamped, gate test named — in the product record, before any public promise (SN-099); fork isolation law precedes the first fork (SN-099).
- Names must say what the thing is; a lying name is repaired by byte-identical rename with zero stragglers (SN-0106).
- Scorekeeping is independent, baselines are law, and scores must be able to fall (SN-0107).
- Seal = consistency, not authorship (SN-0111); verifiers fail closed and never crash on admissible input (SN-0118); every verification names its unverified remainder (SN-0121).
- Divergence from the spec is resolved by re-reading the spec, not by trusting memory (SN-0122); frozen-baseline values are lifted verbatim (SN-0119).
- Output surfaces never grow input controls (SN-0124).

## Human

This is about keeping every mirror honest. Don't promise what isn't proven — write "not ready yet" where everyone can see it, with the gaps listed. Name things what they are. Let someone other than the builder keep score, and let scores go down. Know exactly what a seal does and doesn't prove. When your work doesn't match the plan, re-read the plan instead of trusting your memory. Copy the approved design exactly instead of improvising. And keep the shop window a window — displays show finished work; they are not where work gets made.

## Child

Imagine showing your drawing to the class. You don't say "it's the best drawing ever" before anyone's checked it — you say "not ready yet" if it isn't. You label your crayons with the right colors. The teacher doesn't let you grade your own test, and a grade can go down as well as up — that's how you know it's real. A wax seal on a letter proves nobody opened it; it doesn't prove who wrote it. And if your drawing looks different from the picture you're copying, you look at the picture again — you don't trust your memory.

## Grandma

Dear, it's honesty in the small things, which is where honesty lives. Say "not ready" plainly when it isn't. Call things by their right names. Never grade your own work, and let a grade fall when the work falls. Know what a seal guarantees — and what it doesn't. When your memory of the recipe disagrees with the recipe card, believe the card. And a display shelf is for showing what's finished, not for doing the making.

## What It All Means

Nine notes are the team's reflective surface: the disciplines that keep names, scores, seals, reports, and claims telling the truth about the work. A cold reader holding only this essence can still review the way these notes demand — and can spot each named way review goes dishonest.

## Why It Matters

Every other discipline in this block produces verdicts; this essence is what keeps the verdicts themselves honest. A score that can't fall, a seal oversold as authorship, a verification that hides its boundary — each converts the proof system into theater, and theater is worse than no proof because it is believed (SN-0107, SN-0111, SN-0121). The promise gate matters most of all: it is the only thing standing between a good architecture document and a violated user (SN-099).

## What Is In It For You

You get instruments you can trust: scores that mean something because they can fall, seals whose exact guarantee you know, verifications that tell you their own limits, and public claims that were gated before they were made. Apply the pattern anywhere: independent scorekeeper, stated baseline, named gaps, boundary sentences — a review culture that surfaces bad news early, when it is still cheap.

## The Learning

The corpus learned that honesty is a set of mechanisms, not a virtue: a stamp format for truth states (SN-099), a boundary sentence at the end of every verification (SN-0121), a baseline that is law (SN-0107). Where the mechanism was missing, the failure followed — memory drift invisible to the drifter (SN-0122), invented values against a frozen concept (SN-0119) — so the lesson hardened into procedure rather than exhortation.
