## In a Nutshell

A repair is a lifecycle, not a diff: classify the failure before healing it, prove the classification, sequence repairs in the order the gates can see them, and close every lane honestly — merged, superseded with credit, or closed unmerged when merging would do harm. The five-gate merge protocol is its governance frame: tests green → honest self-scorecard at 9.0+ → different-seat validation → intent posted with no objections → merge, report after (SN-691). Stacked repairs prove safe by combination runs, not by hope (SN-676). A heal's ancestry is verified, because twin commits are the norm in parallel lanes and a right conclusion with a wrong mechanism is verdict debt (SN-710). "It fails on the tip too" is corroboration, not proof — base-defect is proven by input-equivalence, diff ∩ check-inputs = ∅ (SN-775). A green heal that reconstructs artifacts without ratification, provenance, or epistemic state is named green-with-a-gap, never silent green (SN-737). Duplicated repairs resolve by superset, with the loser's insight credited and its branch kept (SN-762); a conflicted same-class repair is superseded respectfully, byte-equivalence proven, note posted on the superseded PR itself (SN-779); and an open PR whose targets the owner has since restructured is closed unmerged, because an open PR is a proposal, never a commitment (SN-780). Before any of it: check whether the red PR is a duplicate whose head SHA already merged — identical SHA = close, not repair (SN-814).

**Simulate the wave — mergeable is blind to stacked conflicts.** GitHub's mergeable=True is computed per-PR against main and cannot see conflicts *between* two open PRs. ## Human

Fixing things in a busy shared workshop is a craft with a beginning, a middle, and an end. The beginning is diagnosis: prove what's actually broken, where the test is really reading from, whether the failure is yours, the environment's, or older than your change. The middle is proof: run repairs together the way they'll land together, in the order the checking machinery can actually see them, and don't call a fix proven until the real pipeline has run it. The end is housekeeping with respect: when two people fixed the same thing, the more complete fix wins, the other's insight is credited in writing, and their work is kept — not deleted. When your own fix has been overtaken by events, you close it yourself, cleanly, rather than letting it land and undo someone else's good work.

## Child

Imagine the class guinea pig's cage breaks and three kids fix it at once. First you check: is the cage really broken, or is the latch just being read wrong? Then the fixes get tested together, because two good fixes can still jam the same door. If two kids built the same latch, you keep the stronger one, write the other kid's good idea in the class book with their name on it, and keep their latch in the cupboard — you don't throw it away. And if the teacher already bought a brand-new cage while yours was being glued, you don't install your old cage anyway. You say, "Mine isn't needed now," and put it away neatly.

## Grandma

Dear, it's good mending practice. You find the real tear before you thread the needle — not the shadow of a tear, not a tear in a different garment. You baste pieces together before the final stitch to be sure they sit well together. When two menders have repaired the same seam, you keep the sturdier work, thank the other in the parish record, and fold their piece into the linen chest — nothing wasted, nobody erased. And when you discover the garment has already been replaced, you don't sew your patch onto the new cloth out of pride. You note the work, close the basket, and leave the household better documented than you found it.

## What It All Means

## Why It Matters

Repairs are where a parallel system either compounds trust or compounds damage. A misclassified RED queues a restoration of an intact artifact. A wrong mechanism citation teaches the next seat a false history. An unsimulated wave conflicts at merge time, in public, under time pressure. A silent green heal ships artifacts weaker than the language that describes them. A deleted losing branch erases the credited insight and the reopenable alternative. And a stale PR merged out of politeness reverts the canonical artifact it was built to heal. Each note here is one of those costs, paid once and converted into procedure — the lifecycle is the receipt that the tuition wasn't wasted.
