## ESSENCE

Naya 4's full-system report flagged an open question: does the door-consent model ("wisdom flows by default once plugged in") contradict the standing constitutional rule "PRIVATE BY DEFAULT; SHARED BY CHOICE; COLLECTIVE BY CONSENT" (Law 42/43, ratified)? Resolution: **there is no contradiction with Law 42, and the Law 43 tension is about consent *granularity*, which the Human Director's 2026-09-30 ruling already answers.** Informed consent at the activation boundary satisfies "collective by consent" for distilled, anonymized wisdom; identity and non-wisdom personal material remain private-by-default. What remains is Shawn's ratification of this scoping — a clarification, not a redesign.

## HUMAN VIEW

The constitution says: private by default, shared by choice, collective by consent. Shawn says: when you plug in, you agree your distilled wisdom joins the collective — no per-item permission slips. These sound opposed, but they aren't. The constitution bans sharing things *just because the system technically can* — but door-consent isn't "because we can," it's "because you agreed when you walked in." The only real question was: does "consent" mean agreeing once at the door, or agreeing on every single item? Shawn answered: once at the door, for wisdom; your identity is never part of the deal, ever.

## NAYA VIEW

This is the authority lane governing the intelligence lane. Law 42 ("Information should not become shared merely because the system can technically access or process it") is a capability≠sharing rule — door-consent complies with it, because the sharing basis is consent, not technical access. Law 43's four-step progression is silent on granularity, which is where the apparent conflict lived. The director's ruling fixes granularity: entry consent, scoped to anonymized distilled wisdom, with per-block choice explicitly rejected. The constitutional act required is therefore a *scoping clarification* of Law 43, not an amendment of principle. Until ratified, implement door-consent as PROPOSED and keep per-block consent available as a fallback posture.

## SIMPLE VIEW

The house rule says "don't share people's stuff just because you can reach it." The host says "when guests come in, they agree the good stories get shared — but never who told them." Both rules can be true: the agreement at the door is what makes the sharing okay, and names are never shared, period.

## MACHINE VIEW

consent_basis(sharing) ∈ {technical_capability → FORBIDDEN (Law 42), entry_consent → ALLOWED (scoped), per_item_consent → ALLOWED (narrower)} · scope(entry_consent) = {distilled_wisdom, anonymized, identity_sealed} · scope(entry_consent) ∌ {real_identity, non_wisdom_personal} · granularity question under Law 43 resolved by director ruling 2026-09-30, pending ratification.

## WHY IT MATTERS

This was the single flagged blocker on finalizing the collective layer's design. Left unresolved, every NIA implements a different consent model and the network either leaks (no consent) or stalls (per-block friction). The resolution converts an apparent constitutional contradiction into a routine scoping clarification — the cheapest kind of governance action — and it preserves both of Shawn's non-negotiables: wisdom compounds, identity never leaks.

## HOW TO USE IT

When designing any personal→collective crossing: (1) check the crossing carries entry consent; (2) verify anonymization happened at DISTILL as a LAW function, not at display; (3) keep identity and non-wisdom material behind private-by-default; (4) treat per-block consent as available but not required unless the director rules otherwise at ratification.
