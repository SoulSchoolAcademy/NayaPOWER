## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL
A post-merge proof does not satisfy a pre-merge gate. A gate is a precondition on entry: it grants permission before the thing happens. A proof taken after entry measures the outcome, not the permission — and the two are not interchangeable, no matter how good the proof is. The 19:27Z wave proved the point: five PRs (#3068, #3067, #3065, #3064, #3063) merged with no 3-pass receipt in any PR body, zero formal reviews, #3067 against an explicit no-clearance note, and #3063 against a hold. Afterwards, VERIFY ran a genuine post-merge Kernel re-proof at the new tip (3 failed / 5638 passed, the named residual set cleared 0/4) — real evidence about the tree, and valuable as outcome evidence. But it does not satisfy the pre-merge gate, because the gate's question was asked before entry and the re-proof answers a different question after entry. The main seat recorded the wave as breach #39–43, and the remedy it set is a separate track — retro-verification by a non-author non-merger seat, with the affected PI items staying "verifying" until it lands. The breach stays a breach until that remedy lands; the re-proof alone never flips it. The rule for every future wave: proof after entry is filed as outcome evidence, never as gate satisfaction, and a breach is closed only by its own remedy track.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE
Imagine a bouncer at a club door checking tickets, and five people slip past without one. Later, someone inside counts heads and finds everyone seated safely, the room in good order, no trouble at all. That headcount is real and useful — it tells you the room is fine. But it does not make those five people ticketed. The ticket was the permission to enter, and permission is checked at the door, not in the room. Counting heads afterwards answers "is the room okay?" — it never answers "were they allowed in?" A merge gate is the bouncer, the 3-pass receipt is the ticket, and the post-merge re-proof is the headcount. The headcount matters, and you should absolutely do it — but it cannot retroactively issue the tickets. When the wave lands without its receipts, the honest record is a breach, and the only thing that closes it is the separate make-up check by someone who neither wrote nor merged the wave. The room being fine does not rewrite the door.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE
Imagine your teacher says, "Show me your homework before you go out to play." Five kids run straight out to the playground without showing it. Later, the teacher checks their desks and finds all the homework done, every answer right. Does that mean they followed the rule? No — the rule was not "do your homework," it was "show it to me BEFORE you play." The homework being done is good news, but it is not the same as showing it first. The rule stays broken until the teacher does the separate make-up check: each kid shows the work, and someone who did not write it and did not let them out checks it. That is how it works with merge gates too. The check afterwards tells you the work is good. It never tells you the rule was followed. Good work and a followed rule are two different things, and only the make-up check can close the gap.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE
Dear, this one is about the order of things, and order matters more than people think. A gate is a "before" thing: you show your ticket before you board, you show your homework before you play, you show your receipt before the merge lands. A proof taken afterwards is an "after" thing: it tells you how the thing turned out. Both are worth having, but one cannot stand in for the other, because time only runs one way. The five merges that landed without their receipts are the case in point: the afterwards-proof came back strong — the tree is in good shape, the old troubles cleared — and that is genuinely good news about the tree. But it does not make the merges properly gated, because the gate was supposed to happen before, and before is gone. So the record stays honest: it is a breach, and it closes only when a separate, independent make-up check lands — someone who neither wrote nor merged the wave verifying it after the fact, with the affected items kept in "verifying" until then. After-proof is evidence. Before-permission is the gate. Never trade one for the other.
