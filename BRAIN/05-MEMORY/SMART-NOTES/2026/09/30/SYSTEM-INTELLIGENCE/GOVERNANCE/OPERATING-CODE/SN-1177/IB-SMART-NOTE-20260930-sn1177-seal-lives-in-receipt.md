## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

There's a rule in good science: the person who designed the experiment doesn't get to grade their own paper by peeking at the answers. One of our designers was tempted to "just check" a number on the board before finalizing her design — and refused, because checking would have turned her into a measurer, and measurers can't be impartial designers. Her design had to stand on its own logic, with the proof living in a separate receipt written by someone else. If your design needs you to peek to be right, it isn't a design — it's a wish.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

1. Self-reference rule: no design artifact may certify itself. The seal — the evidence that the design holds — lives in the receipt (an independent measurement, a frozen pre-registration, a different seat's verification), never inside the design file.
2. "Just to check N" is the tell. A designer who measures starts executing: the act of peeking converts the designer into an interested party and voids the design's impartiality (the t0593 scar — name it, don't repeat it).
3. The honest substitute for designer-side certainty is the pre-run BLOCKED gate: declare what would block, freeze the design, and let the receipt speak. Uncertainty before the run is legitimate; certainty bought by peeking is not.
4. Make the design robust to either outcome: reformulate class rules around measured thresholds with disjointness proofs, so the design stands regardless of which ordering the measurement finds. A design that only works if the peek comes back favorable was never sealed.
5. This is the designer-side complement of blinding: blinding protects the *measurement* from the designer; this protects the *design* from self-certification. Both directions matter.
