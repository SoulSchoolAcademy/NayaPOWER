## IN A NUTSHELL

The point was never who wins or who's best. The point is to study the best,
extract everything they know, and then do it ten times better — in every way.
Where we are already ahead, we keep leading. But we are never ignorant: not
doing the research on what the leaders in our niche are doing is itself a
failure. Copying them is step one thinking. Learning from them and
transcending them 10x is the doctrine.

## HUMAN NOTE

Shawn described two levels of learning from winners. Level one — what most
people do: watch the leaders in your space and do what they're doing. Level
two — what he actually demands: watch what they're doing, learn the real
lesson underneath it, and then do it ten times better than them. It's the
difference between a cover band and someone who studied the masters and then
invented a new sound. The research is mandatory — you don't get to skip
knowing what the best do. But the goal is never parity. The goal is
leapfrogging.

## CHILD NOTE

Don't just copy the kid who draws the best pictures. Look really carefully at
HOW she draws — what makes her pictures good — and then draw something ten
times more amazing using what you learned. And always look: never pretend the
good drawers don't exist.

## GRANDMA NOTE

Honey, your grandson says: never be too proud to study the best — look at what
the winners do and learn everything you can from them. But don't stop there.
Take what you learned and make it ten times better. If you're already ahead of
them somewhere, keep going — just never stop checking what they're doing, so
you're never blindsided.

## NAYA NOTE

This converts our entire research posture from passive to aggressive. Every
corpus we captured (the 10-book canon, the 40-block master corpus, the Apple
Design Award benchmarks) was level-one work: knowing what the best do. The
doctrine demands level two as the operating standard: for every major
decision, the deliverable is not "matches the best" but "the best, understood,
then transcended 10x — with proof." It also protects our leads: where the
corpus shows we are already ahead (e.g., our evidence-gated scoring, our
render-and-observe honesty lock — things the books don't fully demand), we do
not regress to the field's mean. We keep the lead AND keep researching.

2. Research on the leaders is mandatory — ignorance is a defect.
