## HUMAN NOTE

Imagine someone prints a new rulebook and puts it on everyone's desk. It is not the rulebook yet — it becomes the rulebook only when the person with the authority says "this is the rulebook." Until then it is a draft, however finished it looks. Worse, if two different desks each get a different "new rulebook," nobody on the team gets to decide which one wins — that's the director's call, and anyone declaring it on their own is seizing authority they don't have. The discipline: placement is an act of writing; ratification is an act of authority. Confusing the two is how a team ends up obeying a law nobody actually made.

## CHILD NOTE

Your teacher writes new classroom rules on the board. They don't count until she says "these are our rules now." And if a helper writes different rules on another board, the helper doesn't get to pick which board is the real one — only the teacher does. Written down is not the same as agreed to.

## GRANDMA NOTE

A law isn't a law because someone wrote it nicely and pinned it up — it's a law when the person who makes laws says so. Two pinned-up versions don't make a dispute the staff can settle by vote; they wait for the one with the gavel. Until the gavel comes down, the old rules still run the house.
