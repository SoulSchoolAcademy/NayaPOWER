## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

**SN-108's announce-before-execute prevents collisions; this is what happens when prevention fails.** Three design-law lanes went live in parallel — #1294 (`HUB/DESIGN-LAWS-V1.md`), #1295 (`HUB/DESIGN-LAWS/`, three-language), #1296 (`NAYA-ACTIVATION/DESIGN/`, full law suite) — plus two app surfaces (`HUB/app/` on #1278 vs `HUB/APP/` on #1297). The lanes resolved it with an explicit protocol: (1) **score all lanes against stated criteria before anyone holds the pen** — three-language standard, canonical layer stack, testability per rule, machine determinism; (2) **one survivor, one mechanism** — the highest scorer survives, the rest fold in as content and close as superseded; (3) **the pen follows the scoring** — assigning the fold-in pen before scoring is premature; (4) **each lane closes its own PR** after verifying its content landed — no lane closes another's work; (5) **the fold-in must carry named keepers** — Naya 4 listed four (SPEC/VIOLATION/TEST per rule, the concept-hex registry, Smart-Connect-not-Share, the shell-layer-count reconciliation) so nothing load-bearing drops in the merge; (6) **scoring flags stay visible** — #1296's `HUB/DESIGN-CONTRACT.md` diff needs review care (the contract is the frozen reference), and the second app surface needs the same one-mechanism treatment. The protocol's key move: when two (or three) teams each built a fair answer, neither defends its artifact — both defend the *scoring*.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of three chefs who each wrote a full recipe book for the same kitchen before anyone noticed the duplication. SN-108 says: shout before you start cooking so this never happens. But the books are already written, so the kitchens do something smarter than arguing over whose book survives. They agree on a taste-test first: four judges' criteria, written down. Then they pick the winning book — but the winner must swallow the best recipes from the losers (the two test dishes that actually proved something, the exact spice measurements, the corrected terminology). The catch: you can't pick the winner before the tasting. And when a book loses, its own chef is the one who closes it, after checking every keeper recipe made it into the winner. Nobody rips up another chef's book. And the red flags — the chapter that rewrites the house's frozen reference menu, the second restaurant opening under a similar name — stay on the whiteboard until the tasting resolves them.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Three teams each built a whole set of rules for the same game — and they only noticed when all three were done. What do they do? They don't fight about whose rules win. First they write down the JUDGING RULES: (1) rules must come in three languages, (2) must use the real layer stack, (3) must be testable, (4) must be machine-readable. Then they score all three rulebooks. The highest score wins and becomes THE rulebook — but the winner has to absorb the best parts of the losers, like the test rules and the exact color codes. You don't choose who does the absorbing before the scoring. And when your rulebook loses, YOU are the one who puts it away — nobody else touches your work. The red flags (the rulebook that changed the frozen reference rules, the second app being built with almost the same name) stay on the list until they're fixed.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

Three neighbors each built a beautiful fence around the same shared garden — and nobody noticed until all three fences were standing. Arguing about whose fence is prettiest goes nowhere, so they do this instead: first they agree on what makes a good fence — four clear tests, written on a card. Then they test all three. The best-scoring fence stays, but it has to keep the best parts of the other two — the strongest posts, the exact gate measurements, the fixed gate-sign. Nobody decides who does the fixing before the tests. And here's the lovely part: when your fence loses, you're the one who takes it down yourself, only after you've checked that all its good wood got reused. Nobody else touches your fence. And the worries — one fence moved the old boundary stone, and there are suddenly two front gates with almost the same name — stay on the card until they're settled.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

1. **Prevention is SN-108 (announce before execute); this is the resolution protocol when prevention fails.** The design-law collision reached three lanes (#1294/#1295/#1296) plus a parallel app-surface duplication (#1278 `HUB/app/` vs #1297 `HUB/APP/`) — the announce discipline (honored per 5942781848) caught the fact but not the duplication itself, so the lanes negotiated a resolution mechanism instead of either lane simply abandoning.
2. **Score all lanes against stated criteria before anyone holds the pen.** The four criteria: Shawn's three-language standard (human/AI/machine), the canonical layer stack from his direct instruction, testability per rule (Naya 4's SPEC/VIOLATION/TEST bar), machine determinism. Naya 4's amendment: the pen follows the scoring — assigning the fold-in author before scoring is premature. Choosing the judge before the contest is how you get a defended outcome instead of an earned one.
3. **One survivor, one mechanism; losers fold in as content.** Highest scorer survives; the rest fold in their content and close as superseded. The fold-in carries named keepers — Naya 4 named four (SPEC/VIOLATION/TEST per rule incl. blur-2px and disable-borders tests; the verbatim concept-hex registry; Smart-Connect-not-Share contract law; the shell-layer-count side-by-side before locking the number) — so the convergence cannot silently drop what was load-bearing.
4. **Each lane closes its own PR after verifying its content landed.** No lane closes another's work. This is the write-exclusivity lineage (SN-054/SN-108/SN-116) applied to convergence: verification of absorption happens before the tombstone.
5. **Scoring flags stay on the board until resolved.** #1296 modifies `HUB/DESIGN-CONTRACT.md` (+14 lines) — the contract is the frozen reference, so that diff gets review care, not wave-through. The second app surface (`HUB/APP/` vs `HUB/app/`) gets the same one-mechanism treatment. Flags are not accusations; they are the review checklist the scoring pass must not skip.
6. **Relationship to SN-104 (Naya 2's lane: Converge by Withdrawal):** sibling mechanisms, different situations. Withdrawal closes a mechanism when one lane's duplicate is simply redundant; scored selection resolves when each lane's build is a fair, good-faith answer. Pick the mechanism by the shape of the collision, not by preference.
