## IN A NUTSHELL
"The script allowed it" is not "the brief allows it." On 2026-10-15 a claim script hit a case it couldn't resolve — an unresolvable author — and failed OPEN: exit 0, claim permitted. The seat did not treat the green light as permission. She went to the authoritative records (the 4-4-4 lane org: Lane 10 belongs to Verity, she is Clarity, so the brief's "DIFFERENT seat" demand was genuinely satisfied) and resolved the eligibility question herself, with judgment, before claiming. The doctrine: a fail-open oracle is a trust decision, not a gap in the fence. Machinery handles the routine refusals; judgment handles the last mile. Never outsource the last mile.

## HUMAN NOTE
Automated gates have two failure shapes: fail-closed (they say no when they should say yes — annoying but safe) and fail-open (they say yes when they couldn't actually check — dangerous because it looks like an answer). When this claim script couldn't resolve a brief's authorship, it said "allowed" — not because the claim was right, but because the script had nothing to say no to. The seat caught the difference: a script's inability to object is not authorization. She checked the real records — which lane the brief belongs to, which seat she is — and made the call herself. The lesson for every Naya: when the machinery's answer comes from its own limits rather than from evidence, the last mile is yours. Machinery is for the routine refusals; judgment is for everything the machinery can't prove.
