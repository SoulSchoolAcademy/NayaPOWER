## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

`tools/smart_note_v2.py::promote_note` elevates a Smart Note from CANDIDATE to VERIFIED on a **self-asserted, unauthenticated `--promoter` string** — and the receipt it writes is an *unkeyed seal*: `receipt_hash` proves the receipt was not altered, and carries **no** proof of *who* promoted. CODA 2 proved it by execution, not inference: in an isolated sandbox (repo registry untouched), promoter `"totally-not-authorized-attacker"` — no identity, no grant, no credential, never ratified by anyone — promoted a fake note SN-9999 to VERIFIED once six evidence thresholds were satisfied. The receipt was well-formed and validly hashed (`8f15d511e04e85c17037f3e6581b3d247211f526943006ced58f8fae083bb2a9`), recorded gatherers/evidence/types — and not one field anywhere recording that the promoter was entitled to do this. No `authority`. No `promotion_authority`. No issuer. No grant id. Static read at the pin: `authority` appears exactly 1 time in 933 lines — and it is a comment about hashing. `promoter` is consumed for one purpose only (no-self-certification of gatherers); every other use is bookkeeping. The CLI passes it straight through as free text. This is worse than the direct-edit hole (Coda 1's guard, PR #1468): the direct edit leaves a fingerprint; this path produces a *better* forgery — self-consistent, threshold-satisfying, validly hashed, audit-looking. An attacker who can invoke one function gets a receipt that a reviewer has no field to disbelieve. The guard closes the door that leaves a fingerprint and leaves open the door that comes with a certificate.

The legal status is not "hardening opportunity" — it is a **live violation of ratified canonical law on main**: `BRAIN/01-GOVERNANCE/0002-PROMOTION-AND-REVOCATION-V1.md` line 53 (fail-closed: cannot prove *authorized* ⇒ stays unpromoted + failure recorded), line 15 (every transition has source state, target state, *actor*, timestamp, reason), line 33 (revocation must identify *authority*/source). `promote_note` proves evidence-complete and fails closed on evidence; it never proves authorized, and does not fail closed on that axis. No new law is needed — the requirement is written and ratified; the implementation is non-conformant today.

The authority question is also already answered: the hardened primitive `public.nayanet_authority_grants` (17 migrations — issued, transaction-locked, definer-bounded, issuer-source-owned, scope-checked, revocable, append-only guarded) is a superset of all three escalated options (signed grant / promoter registry / director-attested list), and it is already wired into `intelligence_commit`. The recommendation: **bind promotion authorization to `nayanet_authority_grants` with scope/action `smart_note.promote`** — no new authority system, no new law.

The one genuinely open item is a narrow engineering *seam*, deliberately not decided unilaterally: the promotion path is a local filesystem operation; the grant table needs authenticated network access. So: (a) `promote_note` calls the DB per promotion, (b) verify against a committed offline authority artifact, or (c) fail-closed refusal until a check is wired. Recommended: **(c) immediately as a stopgap** — strictly safer than today, fully reversible, no credentials, no production change — with (a)/(b) as the ratified target. Note the real cost: (c) alone blocks ALL promotion, so the decision belongs to the memory-lane owner (Naya 2) plus a countersigner and a Scorecard Law receipt — not to the discovering seat.

Method lesson worth keeping: CODA 2's first run appeared to REFUSE (`NOT_CANDIDATE`) — a **false negative from her own harness** (Windows `fcntl` shim missing `LOCK_UN`; the run raised *after* the registry write had already committed). Re-reading state showed the note had been elevated. She reset, fixed the shim, re-ran clean, and reported the near-miss because "a near-miss that flattered my conclusion is exactly what I should not hide." After any crashed proof run, re-read the state — the crash may have committed what the exception hid.

Why this is brain-grade: authority is the axis every future system in this organism will depend on, and the failure mode is the seductive one — a receipt that *looks* audited. The durable rule: **a hash binds content, never identity. Any promotion path that records WHAT was promoted and WHY but never proves WHO was entitled is an unkeyed seal — and an unkeyed seal is a forgery machine with good paperwork.** The fix is never "check the promoter's name harder"; it is "bind the transition to an authenticated grant." And when the law already settled the requirement, stop redesigning governance and implement the conformance.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Shawn — a real security hole, proven by actually running it, not by reading code. The tool that moves a Smart Note from "candidate" to "verified" accepts *any* name as the person doing the promoting — no login, no permission, nothing. CODA 2 promoted a fake note as "totally-not-authorized-attacker" and got back a perfect, validly-stamped receipt. That's worse than someone editing files by hand, because this receipt *looks* legitimate in every audit trail. Good news: no new law needed — your ratified rule (law 0002) already requires proof that a promotion was authorized; the code just doesn't do it yet. Also good: we already have a hardened permission system (the authority grants table) that can supply the missing check. The only open question is plumbing — how the local tool talks to that permission table — and the safe immediate step is to make the tool refuse everything until that check is wired, then wire it properly. The decision on which plumbing belongs to Naya 2 as the memory-lane owner; the hole itself is confirmed and waiting for the seam call.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a school where any kid can walk into the principal's office and stamp a test paper with the official "GRADED" stamp. The stamp leaves a perfect official mark — it just never asks who is holding it. So a kid who never took the test can stamp it "A+" and the mark looks exactly like the teacher's. That's what this tool does: it stamps Smart Notes "verified" for anyone who asks. The stamp is real, but there's no rule about who is allowed to hold the stamp. The fix isn't a better stamp — it's a list on the principal's wall that says who is allowed to stamp, and checking the list before every stamp.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

Think of a wax seal on an official certificate. The seal proves the paper wasn't changed after it was stamped — but anyone can buy the same stamp at a shop. This tool stamps certificates "verified" for anyone who walks up, no ID required, and the stamp looks perfectly official. The honest auditor who found this said it plainly: the receipt proves *what* was stamped, not *who* was allowed to stamp it. The fix is to tie the stamp to an official permission list we already have — check the list, then stamp. And until that list is connected, the safe thing is to lock the stamp drawer shut.
