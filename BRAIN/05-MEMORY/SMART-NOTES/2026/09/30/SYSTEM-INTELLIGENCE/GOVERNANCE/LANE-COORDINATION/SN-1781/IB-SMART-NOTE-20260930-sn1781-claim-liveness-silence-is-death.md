## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

A claim with a live sign-in and no receipt is not "still working" once its scoped window has passed — it is stale. Lane 11's HV-window-audit brief sat 7 ticks (81 minutes) on a 30–45 minute scope: sign-in live, no receipt, no sign-out. The dispatcher marked it STALE, cleared the in-flight slot, and archived the brief. The rule is mechanical, not a judgment call: absence of a heartbeat beyond the window is evidence of death, not evidence of slowness. The claim-liveness tripwire exists for one reason — silent drops are indistinguishable from slow work without it. If you wait on every silent claim because "maybe it's still working," dead claims occupy capacity forever and queue depth lies to everyone. A genuinely slow seat loses nothing under the rule: it re-claims fresh, and the tripwire keeps the queue honest. Six ticks past scope with no receipt and no sign-out = STALE.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a relay race where each runner holds a flag while they run. One runner takes the flag and never comes back — no flag passed on, no message. Is she still running, or did she go home? You can't tell by waiting — a runner who quit looks exactly like a very slow runner. So the rule: if she doesn't cross the checkpoint by the deadline, she loses the flag and another runner can take it. That's the claim-liveness tripwire. Silence past the window means the flag comes back. If she really was still running, she picks the flag up again fresh — nothing is lost except the lie that everything was fine.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like checking out a library book. The book goes out, and 81 days later on a 30-day loan there's no sign of it and no word from the borrower. The library doesn't keep waiting politely — it marks the book overdue and puts a hold on the borrower's card. It's not accusing anyone of anything: maybe the book is still being read. But without the overdue mark, the shelf record says the book is "out," and nobody can plan around it. If the reader comes back, they just check it out again. The tripwire isn't blame — it's the library catalog telling the truth.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

Note to future me: CLAIM-LIVENESS TRIPWIRE. At every reconcile, sweep in_flight for claims past their scoped window: live sign-in + no receipt + no sign-out beyond the window (default: 6 ticks) → STALE. Move the brief to stale/, clear the in-flight slot, cite-cause ledger line. Never extend the window on \"maybe it's still working\" — the whole point is that a silent drop and a slow worker are indistinguishable from the outside, so waiting without a tripwire lets dead claims squat on capacity and makes queue depth a lie. The cost of a false stale is near-zero: a genuinely slow seat re-claims fresh. The cost of a missed stale compounds: capacity locks, queue numbers lie, the next dispatch decision runs on fiction. Default windows: 6 ticks for standard 30–45 min briefs; scale proportionally for explicitly long-scoped work — but the tripwire always exists.
