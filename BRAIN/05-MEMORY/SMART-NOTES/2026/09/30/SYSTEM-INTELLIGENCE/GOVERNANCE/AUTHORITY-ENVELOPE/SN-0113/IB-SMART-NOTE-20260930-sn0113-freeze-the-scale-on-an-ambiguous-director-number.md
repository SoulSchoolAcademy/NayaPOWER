## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

**A lane found its shipped value below the director's recorded number — 15px body / 11px floor in the app (PR #1278) vs the recorded 17–18px body / 16px floor (#554 comment 5942259151) — while the director's original words had been "generous text" with no exact numbers. Instead of shipping another type pass on its own reading, the lane froze the scale and routed the genuine question upward: "Is 17–18px your actual bar — meaning my 15px undershoots and the app needs another type pass — or is 15px generous enough? I won't touch the scale again until you rule."** The discipline: never let an ambiguous number burn another pass. Freeze, name the conflict exactly (both numbers, where each was recorded, why they disagree), ask the authority, and wait for the ruling. An authority number is not clarified by iteration — it is clarified by the authority.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of a builder who was told "make the text generous," shipped 15px, then found a spec sheet saying 17–18px. The temptation is to quietly resize everything and ship again — but if the original instruction was really "generous," then 15px might already be right, and a second pass would just burn a day on the wrong target. The mature move is to stop, show both numbers side by side, and ask the person who decides: "Which one is it? I won't touch it again until you say." One freeze beats two guesses.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Your teacher says "write big." You write pretty big. Then a note on the board says letters must be 2cm tall — but yours are 1.5cm. Do you erase everything and start over? No! You ask the teacher: "Did you mean exactly 2cm, or is my 1.5cm big enough?" You don't rewrite the whole page twice on a guess. Stop, ask, then write — once.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

The boss says "make it readable," you set the type a touch larger, and then a memo arrives with exact numbers bigger than yours. The smart employee doesn't redo the whole job on a hunch — she brings both numbers to the boss and asks which one is real. One honest question saves a wasted afternoon of rework.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

1. **An ambiguous authority number is a question, not a task.** When the recorded number (17–18px / 16px floor) conflicts with your shipped value (15px / 11px floor) and the original directive was qualitative ("generous text"), you do not have a task — you have a question. Treat it as one.
2. **Name the conflict exactly.** Both numbers, where each was recorded, and why they disagree. The question that got asked here was exemplary: exact values, exact stakes ("meaning my 15px undershoots and the app needs another type pass — or is 15px generous enough?"), and an explicit commitment ("I won't touch the scale again until you rule").
3. **Freeze is a decision, not a stall.** Freezing the scale is the highest-value move: it protects the lane from shipping a second pass against the wrong target and protects the director from being presented with a fait accompli built on a misread. A freeze with the question named is work, not waiting.
4. **Separate the take-or-leave note from the ruling.** The same comment also demonstrated the duplicate-close discipline: closing her own superseded PR #1288 while leaving her one dissenting design note (constitution-at-head layering vs separate file) on the close comment for the surviving lane's author to take or leave. Close the duplicate openly; don't bury the dissent.
5. **Iteration does not clarify authority.** No number of type passes converges on the director's actual bar — only the director's answer does. When the standard itself is the unknown, the only valid action is the question.
