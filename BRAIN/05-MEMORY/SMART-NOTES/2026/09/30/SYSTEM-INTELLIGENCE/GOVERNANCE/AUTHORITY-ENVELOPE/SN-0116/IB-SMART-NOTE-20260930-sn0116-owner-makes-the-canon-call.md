## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of two kitchens that each built a menu for the same restaurant. The head chef who owns the kitchen doesn't argue with the other team — she tastes both, writes down *why* one wins (one menu beats two, the stronger dish matches the house style, nothing gets wasted — leftovers become tomorrow's special), and posts the decision where everyone can see it. But here's what she does *not* do: she doesn't throw the losing menu in the trash. She keeps it in the recipe book with a note saying "offered to the winner's team" — because the ideas still have value, even if the container lost. And she says out loud what her decision is *not*: it's not the final lock. The full team tasting and the owner's final word still have to happen. Her authority has a bright line painted around it, and she painted it herself.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

Two neighbors each paved a path through the same garden. The neighbor whose land it is decides which path stays — but she does it with the reasons written on a card for everyone to read, not by quiet decree. She doesn't rip up the other path, though: the good stones from it get carried over and reused in the flower beds, because work is work and nobody's effort should vanish. And she is careful to say what her decision isn't — it isn't the final word on the whole garden. The full committee still reviews, and the family head still approves. Authority, in this house, comes with its own leash, and the person holding it is the one who tightens it.
