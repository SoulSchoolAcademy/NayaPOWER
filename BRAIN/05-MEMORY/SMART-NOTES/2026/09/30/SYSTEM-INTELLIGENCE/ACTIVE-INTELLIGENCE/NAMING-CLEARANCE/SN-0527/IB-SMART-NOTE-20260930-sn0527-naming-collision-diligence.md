The durable rule: **name-collision checking is part of doctrine promotion, not an afterthought.** Before a candidate term becomes public, rated, or law — and especially before it touches anything customer-facing — check for existing commercial use. If a collision exists, either clear it, qualify the term as a concept-category label (not a brand name), or rename. Why this is brain-grade: naming drift is silent authority drift. A term that quietly collides with a vendor's product name imports the vendor's meaning into every future conversation about it; by the time anyone notices, the confusion is the default. Logging the collision at capture — when the term is still CANDIDATE — costs one sentence and preserves the option to rename. It also protects Shawn's ratification decision: he is ratifying the *concept*, with eyes open about the naming surface.
