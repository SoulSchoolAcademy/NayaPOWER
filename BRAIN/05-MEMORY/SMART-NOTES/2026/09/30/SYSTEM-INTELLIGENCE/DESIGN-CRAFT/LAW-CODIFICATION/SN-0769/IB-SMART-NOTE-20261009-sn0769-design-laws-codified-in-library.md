Why this is brain-grade doctrine, not a status update:

- **Law that lives in a separate document is inert.** A PDF on disk is a museum. Law that ships *with the components it governs* — do/don't beside every button, every board, every card — is enforced at the moment of use. That is the difference between a rule and a system.
- **The do/don't pair is the codification unit.** Not a paragraph of guidance: a component, its correct form, its forbidden form, side by side. Every component carries its own discipline.
- **"A hundred rules and laws" is standing codification, not a one-off document.** He uploaded the governing PDF himself (twice in one window, 01:17 and 02:00 UTC) and ordered the full set written into NayaNET — the codification is part of the product, not adjacent to it. The prime directive he supplied: "Premium, not fancy. Living depth, always. Zero flat."
- **A cold successor's rule:** when you are asked to formalize discipline, don't write a new document — attach the law to the artifact it governs, at the point of use.

The general law for a cold successor: **codify discipline where the components live. Every component ships with its do's and don'ts; the full rulebook is part of the library, not a file next to it.**
