Why this is brain-grade: teams routinely "validate" repairs with experiments that can only produce the answer they want. The EVENT_ID_REPLAY finding names a whole class of self-validating experiments: re-running edited evidence through a pipeline whose identity model keys off the thing you edited. The acceptance criterion for any causal experiment is therefore positional: **a green result must be possible ONLY IF the hypothesis is true**. If green is also consistent with the hypothesis being false (replay guard ate it, cache served it, the gate wasn't the binding constraint), the experiment is theater and the repair that follows it is luck. And the historical REDs stay RED — rewriting failures to green after the fact destroys the evidence trail future verification needs (SN-0080's frozen-evidence retirement said never mutate the freeze; this says the same about failure history). A cold successor that (a) runs the cheapest falsifiable experiment first, (b) checks the experiment's own machinery can't explain its result, and (c) leaves honest failures standing, never encodes a wrong assumption into CI. ## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

There's a new rule for testing fixes. First, run the cheapest possible test of your theory — before building any permanent machinery. But here's the catch: the test must be designed so that a "pass" can *only* mean your theory was right. In this case, fixing old records and re-running them wouldn't prove anything, because the system might just notice "this is the same record I saw before" and skip the real work. So instead they send one brand-new, correctly-made record through the whole system — if it passes, the theory is proven. Old failures stay on the record; nobody rewrites history to look green.
