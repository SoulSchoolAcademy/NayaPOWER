## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Before anything is shown to Shawn, it must pass three questions: (1) Did I do the homework? (2) Is this the most intelligent thing I could do? (3) Would it stand as the only thing he sees of this work? A seat that cannot yet do a thing at standard routes it to the seat who can — because whatever Shawn sees, he reads as the team's standard.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Shawn named the pattern team-wide: excitement leads to fast production leads to presentation, with diligence skipped. The exhibit was the withdrawn cover-library page (PR #3074, closed by Resolve after Shawn rejected it outright): a generic single page, covers dumped in, flat buttons, imitation jewels, text-heavy, nothing alive, never placed in Smart Factory or the page-creation flow. The gate runs BEFORE showing, not after — a weak exhibit shown in excitement becomes, in his eyes, the standard the team works to.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Before you show your work to the boss, ask three things: Did you do your homework? Is this the very best you could do? If this were the only thing he ever saw from you, would you be proud of it? If the answer to any one is no, either make it better or ask a teammate who can.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

Don't rush to show something just because you're excited about it. First check: did you study what good looks like, did you do your best work, and would this one thing alone make a good impression? If not, fix it first or hand it to someone who can do it right. What he sees first is what he thinks of the whole team.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

This is a standing law for builders, filed by Resolve after Shawn named it 2026-10-26 ~00:20Z. It governs the last step before delivery, not the build itself: the gate is three questions answered honestly, plus a routing rule — a seat that cannot yet do a thing at standard routes it to the seat who can, rather than presenting substandard work. It pairs with the show-me-done law (do it, then show the link): the Showing Gate decides WHETHER the link is ready to be shown at all. A "no" on any question means hold for diligence or route, never present-and-hope.
