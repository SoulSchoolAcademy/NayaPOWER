## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine teaching someone to swim by giving them a really good book about swimming. They can read it perfectly and even explain it back to you — but when they jump in the pool, they sink. Knowing the words isn't the same as doing the thing. The lane found this out the careful way: they gave seven different "books" to learners, and the learners could always repeat the words back but almost never did the thing. The only thing that worked was making the learner practice in the water themselves, right then. Reading about it doesn't make it real — doing it does.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like the difference between someone telling you "eat your vegetables" and someone putting vegetables in your hand. You can nod, you can repeat the advice perfectly, and still order the fries. Seven careful tests showed the same thing: passing wisdom along as words gets you agreement, not action. The one time behavior actually changed, the learner wasn't given advice to carry — they were asked to do something, right then and there. Words travel well and land lightly; acts land heavily. If you want a lesson to survive being handed down, don't just write it — give the next person something to DO with it.
