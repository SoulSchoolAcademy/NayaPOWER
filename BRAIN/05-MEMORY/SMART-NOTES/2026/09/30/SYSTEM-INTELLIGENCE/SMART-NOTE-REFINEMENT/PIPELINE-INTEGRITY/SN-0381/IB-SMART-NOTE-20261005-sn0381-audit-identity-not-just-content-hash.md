## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Two records can agree byte-for-byte on content and still disagree on identity — and an audit that only compares hashes will bless both. The Smart Note audit was bidirectional by content hash, so it could prove "the bytes match" while being blind to four identity-level failure classes: orphans in either direction between registry and capture store, captures missing their identity field entirely, and filenames that lie about the identity inside. The durable lesson: identity is the (SN-NNN, smart_note_id, filename) triple, and every pair of legs must be asserted bidirectionally — content-hash agreement is necessary but never sufficient for "the system knows what this thing is." The repair shows the method: strengthen the existing audit rather than building a second detector, and pin today's measured debt as a monotonic ratchet — enumerate the orphans by name, date them, and block all new drift without reddening the lane on legacy debt.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a library where every book has a title on the cover and a number stamped inside. The old audit just weighed the books to make sure none were missing pages — but it never checked that the number on the cover matched the number inside. So a book could be filed in the wrong place with the wrong number, and the audit said "all fine." The new audit checks the match: the cover number, the inside stamp, and the shelf label must all agree — in both directions. The books that are already mismatched get a list with their names on it, and no new mismatches are allowed.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like a filing cabinet where the old check just made sure every folder was full — it never checked that the label on the folder matched what was inside. Folders ended up in the wrong drawers, some had no labels at all, and one folder's label said one thing while the papers inside said another. The new check reads the label *and* the contents and makes sure they agree, in every drawer. The drawers that are already mixed up get an honest written list, and from today forward, nothing new goes in the wrong place.
