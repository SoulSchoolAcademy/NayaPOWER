## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a hall monitor who checks "did you say you did your homework?" — not "did you DO it?" Anyone can say the words and walk past. That's what happened: the checker looked for the right words, not the right action. The fix is simple: whenever you build a checker, you have to try to trick it on purpose — write a lie that should get caught, and a truth that should pass. If the checker can't catch your lie, it's not a checker, it's a rumor with a clipboard.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

A lock that opens for anyone who says "I'm allowed in" is a doorbell, not a lock. The team's checker had exactly this flaw: it confirmed the magic words were spoken but never checked the work was done. The new habit is older than computers — you test a lock by trying to pick it. Every review now includes a deliberate trick: one case designed to fool the checker, one honest case it must accept. Only the checker that catches the trick earns its name.
