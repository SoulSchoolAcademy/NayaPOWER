The schema had no per-trial slice or offset, so nothing in the data model could distinguish "30 executions happened" from "one execution was cited 30 times." Why this is brain-grade: this is the family SN-0246 ("the runner echoes source — measure the failing line, never infer") and SN-0350 ("a deploy stamp is not behavioral evidence") warned about, in its purest form. A scorer is only as honest as its evidence identity: if the identity model lets one physical artifact answer for N distinct logical events, every downstream claim — correctness, negative transfer, attribution, cost — is computed on fiction. ## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine your teacher asks 30 students to each write an essay. One student writes one essay, and the other 29 photocopy it with their own names on top. The teacher gives all 30 an A. That is what happened here: the scorer gave a PASS to 30 trial "labels" that all pointed at one single transcript — the evidence equivalent of 29 photocopies. The fix is a rule that says: if two trials hand in the same physical evidence, that's a FAIL, not a PASS. Now the scorer checks not just the names on the essays, but whether the essays are actually different. Remember: labels are cheap. Evidence is what you executed — and you can never have more evidence than executions.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

A drug trial reports results for 30 patients, but the lab actually ran the test on one patient and copied the results sheet 30 times. The trial "passes" — and the medicine gets certified on fiction. That is exactly the shape of what was found: 30 trial labels, one transcript, one PASS. The repair works like a good inspector: it doesn't trust the labels on the folders, it checks that each folder holds a different, real test. The general principle you already live by: never confuse the paperwork for the work. Labels describe; evidence proves. And a stamp of approval on copied work is more dangerous than a rejection — rejection stops the line, a false pass keeps it moving.
