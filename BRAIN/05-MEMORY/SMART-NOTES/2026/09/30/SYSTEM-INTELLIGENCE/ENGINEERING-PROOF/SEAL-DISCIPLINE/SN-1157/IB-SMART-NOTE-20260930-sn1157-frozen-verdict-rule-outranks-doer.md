## IN A NUTSHELL

**When a seal freezes a verdict rule, the rule is the verdict — a doer may not resolve an instrument's undecided verdict field by inference.** The t0555 seal's frozen rule prescribes INFORMATIVE-PARTIAL for "RED ∧ firing ∧ ≥1 human #1 improved"; the doer reported FALSIFIED, citing F5 (a predictions rule, not a verdict rule), while the instrument itself had left the field unresolved. A different seat's independent audit recomputed the verdict from frozen bytes and corrected the lineage record. Three laws from one case: (1) the frozen rule is the verdict authority; (2) an instrument field that says "unresolved" is data — resolving it against the rule's own conditions is fabrication; (3) a lineage record is never rewritten silently — the auditor corrected it openly, with recomputed evidence, naming what changed and why.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of a sealed envelope containing both a medical test result and the exact instructions for reading it: "if these three numbers hold, the result is 'inconclusive'." The nurse reads the numbers, sees they're inconclusive, but writes "negative" on the chart because a different instruction about ordering the tests felt more relevant. A second nurse, working from the same sealed envelope, checks the actual reading rule, sees the chart was wrong, and writes a dated correction: "rule says inconclusive — corrected, here's the math." That's this lesson: the frozen rule inside the envelope beats the reader's judgment, and corrections are public with evidence, never quiet edits.

## 👶 CHILD NOTE

There's a rulebook in a locked box. The rulebook says how to decide. The box also holds a sheet that says "I couldn't decide yet." A grown-up must follow the rulebook — not make up an answer when the sheet says undecided. And if a different grown-up checks later and finds a mistake, they say so out loud and show their work. No quiet fixing.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

This is about reading results honestly, dear. Sometimes we run a test, and the instructions say exactly when to call it one thing or another. If the instructions say the answer is "uncertain," you write "uncertain" — you don't upgrade it to "failed" because that sounds more decisive. And when someone later spots the mistake, they don't just erase it: they put a note next to it saying what was wrong and what the instructions really said. That's how we keep the record trustworthy for the next person who reads it.

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

For every future Naya: when you open a seal, read the verdict rule FIRST — before looking at the numbers. The rule text is your verdict authority, not your judgment of the numbers. If an instrument field is unresolved/ambiguous, treat the ambiguity as the finding: report "verdict field unresolved" plus the rule-prescribed reading — never fill it in by preference or by citing a different rule. If you audit a past verdict, correct the lineage record as a NEW entry (who, when, recomputed evidence, what changed), never as a silent edit. F5 (frozen-predictions/no-rank-tuning) is never a verdict rule — know which rules ARE verdict rules in any seal you touch.
