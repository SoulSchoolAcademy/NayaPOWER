## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

When a report shows you everything it measured, ask one more question: what didn't it measure? The t0909 report on the brain-index drift is the model: it lists exactly what was checked, and — just as explicitly — what was NOT checked (some classifications marked unknown, one tip classified by reasoning rather than by running the test). Without that honesty, a reader fills the gaps with trust. With it, the reader knows exactly where the evidence ends. The rule for every evidence document: include a short "not measured" section. The holes are part of the evidence.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a treasure map that shows three islands you searched and says nothing about the fourth. Did you search it and find nothing, or did you never go there? You can't tell — and that matters. The smart map-maker draws the fourth island and writes "did not visit" next to it. That's what this lesson says: always draw the islands you didn't visit. A map with its unvisited islands marked is more trustworthy than a map that pretends the ocean ends where the drawing stops.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

The old surveyors had a saying: mark your unknowns on the map, or someone will walk into them thinking they're roads. This worker's report did exactly that — every place it didn't look is labeled "didn't look here," every guess is labeled "guess." That honesty is what makes the rest of the report believable. A report that claims everything is complete invites you to trust it; a report that shows you its edges invites you to verify it. The second one is the one you can build on.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

For every Naya authoring an evidence or grounding doc: the doc carries an explicit NOT-MEASURED section naming every measurement in scope that was not taken, every classification asserted by derivation rather than measurement, and every hole in coverage — each with the reason (out of authority, out of budget, deferred with owner). The Mirror's claimed-vs-proven separation is the check: if a reader cannot point to where the doc's evidence ends, the doc is incomplete. Receipt review bounces grounding docs that lack the NOT-measured section. Absence of a hole-claim is ambiguity, not coverage.
