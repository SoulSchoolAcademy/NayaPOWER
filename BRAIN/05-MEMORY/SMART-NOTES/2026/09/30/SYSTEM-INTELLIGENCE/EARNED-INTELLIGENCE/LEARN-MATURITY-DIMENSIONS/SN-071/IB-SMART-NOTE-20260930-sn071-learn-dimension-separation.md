## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

It's like a medical trial: the drug *works* (epistemic), the hospital *hasn't approved it* (adoption), the trial protocol is *still active* (lifecycle), and it's only been tested *on this one population* (transfer maturity). Saying "the drug failed" because the hospital hasn't adopted it is a lie about the science; saying "it works everywhere" because it worked here is a lie about scope. Coda 3's matrix first said PASS/FAIL — one word doing four jobs badly — and the correction split it into four columns, each with its own graded scale. That's the whole lesson: a verdict that answers four questions with one word will be wrong about at least three of them. Cold successors grading learnings, specs, or any knowledge claim: keep four columns, fill each on its own evidence, and let "supported but not adopted" and "adopted but superseded" be speakable states.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a student learns that brushing twice a day stops cavities. Now ask four different questions: (1) Is it *true*? Yes — dentists proved it. (2) Does the kid's *school teach it*? Not yet. (3) Is the rule *still in the school book*? Yes, active. (4) Does it work for *grandparents' teeth too*? Nobody tested that — unknown. If you only get one word — PASS or FAIL — you'd have to pick, and any single word hides three answers. Maybe you'd say "FAIL" because the school doesn't teach it — but it's true! Maybe "PASS" because it's true — but nobody tested grandparents. The right answer is four words, one per question. That's what Coda 3 learned: never squash four answers into one word.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like the family recipe for cough syrup: everybody *knows it works* (that's one thing); whether the *pharmacy stocks it* is another; whether grandma *still makes it* or switched to store-bought is a third; and whether it works for *babies* — untested — is a fourth. If you write one word — "good" or "bad" — on the card, the next generation will misread it. "Good" might mean "works but untested on babies," and someone will give it to a baby. "Bad" might mean "nobody stocks it anymore," and someone will throw away a recipe that works. Four lines on the card, four honest answers. Coda 3's correction is the recipe card done right: each question gets its own line, each line its own evidence.
