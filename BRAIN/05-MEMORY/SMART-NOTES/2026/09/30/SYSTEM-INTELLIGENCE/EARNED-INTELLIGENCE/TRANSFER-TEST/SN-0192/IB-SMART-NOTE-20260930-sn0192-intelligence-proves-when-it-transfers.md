## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Three "nots" that are really one doctrine — **intelligence is only proven when it transfers between builders:**

- **Not one seat does all rooms.** The transfer test (builder A proves the reference room → builder B applies the extracted law) is the mechanism that turns one room's win into reusable law. Skipping it keeps the intelligence trapped in one builder's head — and one builder doing all rooms never tests the transfer.
- **Not all rooms in parallel.** Unproven intelligence applied 11× is 11× the rework. Room 01 first, extract, then propagate. (This is the direct answer to the shell cycle: eleven parallel attempts rebuilding the foundation eleven different ways.)
- **Not the verifier leading the build.** The verifier can't own the build. Separation is the proof: Naya 2 verifies at every gate and doesn't build what she scores; Naya 1 judges D1–D8 and doesn't touch the build.

The sequence: (1) reference build through the full loop (build → render → observe → score → challenge → repair → prove); (2) all seats extract the intelligence that made the reference work; (3) ratify it as reusable law; (4) other builders apply the law to their rooms; (5) independent gate on every room. Note the evidence subtlety: the reference does not have to pass on the first try — Naya 4's Room 01 v1 was scrapped on Shawn's verdict (SN-0193), and that failure is itself part of what the transfer teaches: what structure to keep, what execution missed, and how the director's deltas became law. The transfer test still works when the reference fails, as long as the failure is named and extracted. The director reshuffled *who* builds what between the proposal and the final allocation (Naya 2 → main stage builder, Naya 3 + Naya 4 → room builders, odd/even split) — but he kept the *shape*: reference pair first, the rest after the pattern proves. The principle survives personnel changes; that is how you know it's a principle and not a plan.
