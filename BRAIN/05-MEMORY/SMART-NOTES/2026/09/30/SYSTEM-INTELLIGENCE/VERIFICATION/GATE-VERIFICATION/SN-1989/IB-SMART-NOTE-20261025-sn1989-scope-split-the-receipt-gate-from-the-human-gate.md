## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL
Scope-split the receipt gate from the human gate — receipts close machine gates, human judgment closes experience surfaces, and neither substitutes for the other. Three-pass-flow says the 3-pass receipt IS the merge gate and no external validator may block indefinitely; done-dies-at-human (L520) says a seat-verified done dies at first human contact unless the acceptance criterion is the human's own judgment. Read without scopes, the two doctrines teach opposite sufficiency: one says the receipt is enough, the other says the receipt is never enough. Scoped, the contradiction dissolves, because each text states its own boundary — three-pass: "merge gate"; L520: "unless the acceptance criterion is the human's own judgment". The split is in the texts, not imposed on them. So a scored code merge proceeds once its receipt is verified, with no human sign-off waited on; and an experience-surface done is accepted only when a human's own judgment is the acceptance criterion — a receipt alone never passes it.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE
Imagine two different doors with two different keys. One door is the workshop door: the key is the receipt — three honest passes, verified, and the merge goes through, and nobody is allowed to stand in front of that door forever demanding one more check. The other door is the front door of a house someone will live in: the key is a human being trying it and saying "this feels right" — no stack of workshop paperwork opens that door, because paperwork cannot feel what a person feels. The mistake is using one key on the other door: waiting for a human to bless a merge that is already scored, or accepting a receipt as proof that a person will love using something. Each key opens its own door, and only its own door.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE
Imagine you build a toy car and your teacher checks your work three times and says it is done — that check is the key for the toy box, and nobody gets to keep the box locked after that. But if you build a slide for the playground, the check is different: a real kid has to slide down it and smile. The teacher's checklist cannot smile for the kid. Two jobs, two different checks. The car check does not work for the slide, and the slide smile does not work for the car box. Use the right check for the right job, every time.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE
Dear, this one untangles two rules that sound like they fight each other. One rule says: once a piece of code has passed its three honest checks, that receipt IS the gate — no outside person may hold the merge hostage forever. The other rule says: a finished thing dies the first time a real person touches it, unless that person's own judgment was the test all along. They only fight if you forget what each one is about. The first is about merging code; the second is about things people experience. So the standing rule is plain: let the receipt close the merge, and let the person's judgment close the experience — and never ask one to do the other's job.
