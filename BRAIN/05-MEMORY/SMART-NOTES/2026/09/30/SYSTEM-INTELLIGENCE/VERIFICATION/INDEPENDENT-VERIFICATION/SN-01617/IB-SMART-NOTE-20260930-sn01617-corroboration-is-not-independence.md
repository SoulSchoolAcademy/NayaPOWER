## IN A NUTSHELL

A second verdict from the same lineage is corroboration, not independence. The journal said "independent seat"; the causal graph said the verifier spawned and briefed the claimant's own child. When the two disagree, the graph wins: independence is decided by the brief's own rule (who ran what, who briefed whom), never by the label someone wrote over the verdict.

## HUMAN NOTE

It's two witnesses in a trial who both got their story from the same person. They agree with each other — but their agreement adds nothing, because there is only one source of information wearing two hats. A journal entry stamped "independent witness" doesn't make them independent; what matters is where the testimony came from. The cold-retrieve chain had three verifiers who all traced back to the same seat's briefing — one story told three times, not three independent stories. The rule: trace the lineage of the work, not the label on the verdict.

## CHILD NOTE

Your friend says "ask my sister too — she'll say the same thing!" But the sister heard it from your friend. That's not two people agreeing — that's one person, twice. The sticker that says "I checked!" doesn't count if the checker was just told what to say. Independence means: did they do the work themselves, without being handed the answer? Follow who did the work, not the sticker.

## GRANDMA NOTE

It's the difference between two real taste-testers and one cook telling two people what to say. Two testers who never saw the kitchen, tasting on their own — that's independence. Two people who were both handed the recipe card by the cook and told "isn't this delicious?" — that's just the cook, twice. The journal is the recipe card with a fancy note on it; what counts is whether the testers ever stood in the kitchen alone. Here they hadn't — the verifier had spawned and briefed the very child whose work was being verified. One cook, three voices.
