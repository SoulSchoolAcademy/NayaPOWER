## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Imagine an architect who writes a 50-page description of a house, then has a draftsman sketch it from the description — and the client signs off on the sketch. If the sketch can be "regenerated" from the text at any time, the sketch is never really locked: anyone can re-render it and claim it's the same house, and the design judgment that made the first sketch good evaporates in the re-render. But the client doesn't buy the description — he buys what he sees. The director reviews the image, not the prose. So the image has to lock alongside the words: designed, edited, and frozen as a first-class artifact, with the text kept in sync. Two-way, not one-way — because the judgment lives in the visual, and judgment is the work.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine you draw a picture of your dream treehouse and write a list of what it has: slide, rope ladder, lookout. Now someone redraws the picture using only your list. Will it look the same? No! The list can't capture the curly slide or the exact shape of the windows or where the sun hits the lookout — all of that lives in the drawing. The drawing is the real plan, not the list. When the team locks a room design, the picture locks too — you don't let someone redraw it from the list and call it the same treehouse. Picture and list lock together, and if the picture changes, the list gets updated to match.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's the difference between a dress pattern and the dress on the mannequin. The pattern is precise and canonical — but the customer tries on the dress. She judges the drape, the color, the feel: things no pattern fully captures, things that live in the garment. If you treat the mannequin dress as "just derived from the pattern," you lose the fitting session — and the fitting session is where the real decisions happen. The lesson the team learned the hard way: lock the dress and the pattern together. The eye decides; the pattern records. And when the eye asks for a change, you alter the dress first and update the pattern to match — not the other way around.
