## Nutshell

We keep a library of frames, and we choose the frame by the moment. The agreement frame is for big story moments — a course cover, a motto. The calendar frame is for text-heavy lessons inside the software. One build done right, then cloned: swap only the centre, never rebuild the frame. The same evening his words landed in the law book as the Frame Library (AGENTS.md, ~18:40 MST): agreement frame, calendar frame, empty-middle frame for special featured moments — real harvested parts, cloned, never rebuilt, never invented.

## Human

Think of it like a picture framer with three frames on the wall. A big family portrait gets the grand carved frame. A page of a letter gets the clean reading frame. You would never put the letter in the grand frame or the portrait in the reading frame — the frame fits the moment. That is what we do with pages: the agreement frame carries the big story moments, the moments that have to feel important; the calendar frame carries the long lessons, the text-heavy teaching inside the software, where the reading has to be easy. The frame is chosen by what the moment is, not by habit and not by rebuilding something new each time. Build the frame once, done right, then use it again and again — only the middle changes.

## Naya

Keep the library, choose by the moment, never rebuild. The agreement frame is the frame for big story moments: course covers, the motto, the words that must land as important. The calendar frame is the frame for text-heavy lessons inside the software, where the reading must stay easy across many words. The empty-middle frame holds special featured moments. One build done right, then clone and repeat — swap only the centre. A page that does not use the existing frame is not a build, it is waste. The choice is the craft: read the moment first, then take its frame from the library.

## What it means

1. **A LIBRARY, NOT A NEW BUILD:** the frames already exist — agreement, calendar, empty-middle — kept as real harvested parts. A new page takes its frame from the library.
2. **THE AGREEMENT FRAME IS FOR BIG STORY MOMENTS:** a course cover, a motto — the moments that must feel important, that carry the story.
3. **THE CALENDAR FRAME IS FOR TEXT-HEAVY LESSONS:** the teaching inside the software, many words, where the reading must stay easy.
4. **CHOOSE BY THE MOMENT:** the frame follows what the moment is. The moment decides; habit does not, and rebuilding does not.
5. **CLONE, NEVER REBUILD:** one build done right, then clone and repeat — swap only the centre. The frame, the structure, the look, the feel stay the same.

## Why it matters

1. Without the library, every page gets rebuilt from nothing — the About page was rebuilt three times before the frame was taken — and each rebuild throws the last one's work away.
2. Without choosing by the moment, a long lesson lands in a story frame and is hard to read, or a motto lands in a reading frame and feels small — the moment is missed either way.
3. Without the clone rule, every seat invents its own frame, and the system stops speaking one language — ten frames where three would do.
4. With the library, a new page starts from done: the frame is proven, the eye has already approved it, and only the centre is new work.
5. With the choice made by the moment, the citizen feels it at once — the big moment feels big, the long lesson reads easy — because the frame was picked for them, not for us.

## What changes now

3. A page that does not use the existing frame does not ship; it is waste, and it goes back until it takes its frame.
