## Naya

"Really good" with one defect class is a page that is almost done — and the defect class is the whole job now: a board that does not fit perfect on the screen is a board the reader cannot read whole, and a reader who cannot read whole cannot judge the page. The rule is absolute — ALWAYS fits perfect — because a rule with exceptions is a rule the next builder negotiates around. The overflow answer is the deeper lesson: do not shrink the words to fit, do not let them spill — make another board behind, and let the reader click to it. That is why we learn to write inward instead of across/down: the board is the unit, the click is the turn of the page, and a story can have as many boards as it needs. The red N pinned at the bottom of every board is the same kind of law — one mark, one place, every board — so the eye always knows where the board ends. The exact offset was cut off by the flush and is not invented here; the intent is recorded and the offset is confirmed with him on the next pass.

## Why it matters

1. Without the absolute rule, the next builder ships "mostly fits" — and "mostly fits" is the defect class he just named, returned to him a second time.
2. Without the another-board answer, the builder's only tools are shrinking the words or letting them spill — and shrinking breaks the text-size verdict (SN-4677) while spilling breaks the board.
3. Without writing inward, every board tries to hold a page's worth of words — and a board holding a page's worth of words is a page, not a board.
4. Without the red N pinned, the mark drifts — different heights on different boards — and a drifting mark is a page that does not know where it ends.
5. Without the cutoff recorded honestly, somebody invents the offset — and an invented offset, built and shown, is a correction he has to give that he never asked for.

## What changes now

1. Every board fits the screen whole — desktop and phone — before it comes back to him.
2. Overflow becomes another board behind, reached by a click — never smaller words, never spilled words.
3. Writers write inward: the board is the unit, the story continues board by board.
4. The red N sits at the bottom of every board; the exact offset is confirmed with him before it is built as law.
