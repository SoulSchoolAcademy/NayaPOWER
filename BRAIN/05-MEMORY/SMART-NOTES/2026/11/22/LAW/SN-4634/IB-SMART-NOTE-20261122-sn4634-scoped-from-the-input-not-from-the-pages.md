## Human

Imagine you are the judge in a contest, and your ruler is stretchy, your stopwatch runs fast, and you are looking through a tiny hole in the fence. You would call good work broken and broken work good — not because the work was bad, but because your tools lied to you. That is what happened six times in one morning. The page was innocent in every case: the link was sent and then stripped by the next page, the button was never wired to the room it promised, the test asked the door to open the wrong way, the footer was just one second slow, the measuring script read the wrong numbers, and the little window hid the change below the header. So the law is simple: never trust your first reading. Trace the whole path, tap the real button, test the direction the page promises, wait for the thing itself, measure twice with a good ruler, and look at the whole page — then write the verdict.

## What it means

1. Harvest first is the law: the Hub and the calendar hold the finished pieces — the board, the frame, the buttons, the font, the player. The builder's job is to take them exactly as they are and put them where they belong, and to learn from how they were made.
2. **JUDGE THE TAP BY THE WHOLE SLIDE:** a probe's view is a slice — never let the slice stand for the page; when a reading says "nothing changed" or "it looks broken," widen the view to the full text, the element's own state, and the rects before writing the verdict.
3. **THE LIVE HUB IS THE TEACHER OF LIGHT:** depth, high contrast, the yellow underglow, glowing green date, gold time, ticking clock, the orb's circling halo — carried into notes, smart notes, and posts, because no flat text anywhere is the bar for every page.
4. **PROVE WHOSE PIXELS THEY ARE FIRST:** before any round is dispatched on a visible defect, prove the owner — page markup, embed chrome, or browser chrome — and name it in the brief; a fix aimed at the wrong owner can only break what was already right.
5. **PARALLEL POWER, ONE MAKER PER FILE:** do all that you can, in parallel, across lanes — a team of masters, not one master with apprentices — and "one lane, one round" governs one page file only, never the whole machine.

## Why it matters

1. Without the criterion itself as the test, a no-change decision backed by the wrong test closes the defect in the record while the defect stays on the page — five links a citizen can never tap, wearing a passing report.
2. Without the trace, a negative observation read at the wrong layer misdiagnoses the round and sends a fix at the wrong owner — a Hub fix at a Hub that was already right, while the real gap stays unowned.
3. He saw the failure on his own screen, on the certificate page itself. It is the one thing standing between the agreement and a person actually joining — the moment of trust, broken by a missing number.
4. Without proof by tapping, a page can pass every check and still send citizens to the wrong place — because a destination is only proven by arriving there, and a button is only proven by pressing it.

## What changes now

1. The Factory + Project Intelligence + Hub merge is scoped from the input, not from the pages: what a person puts in when they start, what a Naya puts in for them, and what page comes out whole from that input alone — proven when a fresh input makes a finished page with no hand in between.
2. Every driver replaces fixed sleeps before a verdict with `wait_for_selector` (or an equivalent presence check) with a generous timeout; "not found" / "no nav" is re-run with a selector wait before it is ever written as a page verdict.
3. Every maker round proves the tap before it files — button tapped for real, unfold unfolding, cover opening, on the staged page in a working browser — and the captain's eye checks the tap, not the claim, before anything goes to Shawn.
4. Every tap probe reads the full target text and the target element's `hidden`/visibility state, never the page top alone; every screenshot claim about a clipped or broken control is checked against the control's own rects.
5. Overnight, every lane is scored by the charge: is the lane moving, is the eye proving it, would it survive his morning look — and when morning comes, the proof is the work itself, in front of his eyes.
6. Every test names the acceptance criterion it proves, and proves the criterion itself — scrollLeft extremes plus element visibility for reachability, a live reproduction log for a mechanism.
