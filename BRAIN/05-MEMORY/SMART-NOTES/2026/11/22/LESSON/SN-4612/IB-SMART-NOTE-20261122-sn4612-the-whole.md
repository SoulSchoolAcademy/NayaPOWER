1. Every new button shape is a new dialect, and a system with ten dialects is a system nobody can read at a glance. "STOP making new buttons" is not a preference, it is the harvest law applied to the smallest unit: the canonical form exists precisely so that a maker never has to invent one. And the five-pill limit is the same discipline applied to choice: the rail is not a menu of everything, it is a showcase of what matters most. Show five, centered, official — and the reader trusts the whole page, because the top of the page already told the truth about it.
2. A summary is a debt with his name on it. Every summary the machine files is a job Shawn has to do himself later — and he is the one person whose time the whole system exists to save. The legal pages proved the price in full: the summaries round, the writer's proper drafting, his four rulings, the builder's extraordinary pages — the whole job, again, because the first pass was a summary instead of the work.
3. The children and the factory are the two halves of the same promise. Children connect automatically because the system puts them together — and the system earns that trust by never looking: no reading mail, no listening, no watching, ever. And the factory belongs to everybody because the fourth sector is where all the new growth goes — when the automated slice is ninety percent of an economy ten times bigger, the whole question is who owns it. The mail ID is part of the same promise: one day we can mail everybody online in Canada and reach them on any topic — it is not about everything, it IS about everyone, everyone having everything they need, all in one place.
4. Every verification system drifts toward checking what is easy to check — the fixture, the programmatic fire, the byte count — and away from what matters, which is what the human sees. The drift is invisible because the checks keep passing. The only cure is a rule that cannot be gamed by a fixture: the eye reads the live page, adds the numbers, clicks the slot, and the page is whole only when the eye says so. That is why the double-check exists, and why its FAIL outranks a mechanism PASS — the mechanism checks the machine, the eye checks the experience, and the experience is the product.
