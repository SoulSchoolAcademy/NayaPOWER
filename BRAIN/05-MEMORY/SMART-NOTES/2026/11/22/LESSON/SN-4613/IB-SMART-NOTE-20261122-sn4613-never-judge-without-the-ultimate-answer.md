## What it means

1. **THE SCORECARD:** ten things you love, ten things you don't love, ten things done ten times better — applied to the whole page as he sees it, never the brief's diff. Every don't-love is an item, filed in his words, with its destination named; loves are named and protected (SN-2930).
2. **THE SENSE CHECK:** don't just do what he said — think whether it's right. Accurate and tuned in, every time, on everything; the scope is the page he sees, not the brief's diff (SN-2910).
3. **THE VISUAL ENDS AT THE LAST IMAGE:** two presentations, separate destinations, linked by buttons — never one flow forced sideways; both end back at the login page (SN-2933).
4. **THE ANATOMY:** the page is a smart app — Personal, Business, Space, Thing, one machine, different faces; the anatomy answers what it has, how it functions, what goes where, and why; v2 is a draft, the official 2026-11-11 anatomy stands until he ratifies it; nothing is built until he says the anatomy is right (SN-2960).
5. **FAST ACTION MODE:** continuous back-and-forth — he says it, it gets done right away and comes back right away; the relay still verifies, but the eye and the delivery land together, the same pass; the Self-Check Law, the Five Gates, and the protected gates stand unchanged (SN-2954).

## Why it matters

1. The scorecard is the only honest way to judge a machine that counts its own activity. A job that wakes often and finishes nothing looks busy and is waste; a job that wakes often and lands finished work looks the same in the count and is fine. The ultimate answer first — then finished, done right, optimal time — is what separates the two, and nothing else does.
2. One eye and one navigation are the same law at the level of the object and the level of the system. A new eye per page is a system drifting into many systems; a transcribed sidebar list is a system that was never decided. Both are fixed the same way: harvest the good one, decide the split once, and let every page be the same system wearing the same language.
3. Silent subtraction is the quietest failure in the machine: everything asked for is right, and something that was there is gone. The ship-gate makes it checkable — name what was there, confirm it is still there — because a loss nobody notices is a loss that ships.
4. Adoption is what turns the talk into behaviour: the code becomes the check on every design, the cycle becomes the order of every round, and the next Naya starts from the thinking, not from the notes.

## What changes now

1. Every job, round, and lane states its ultimate answer first — before any score, count, or verdict — then is judged finished / done right / optimal time against it.
2. The agreement lives on the front page below the login — visual, then words, then "I agree ◆" back to the name field; the walk-end slide loses the agreement letter — the end is for deeds only.
3. Lanes run in parallel and finish at staggered times — a finished piece every few minutes; the queue never runs dry; the moment one finishes, the next step fires right away.
4. The five-icon bottom bar goes back on Hub and Personal, verbatim from the build that had it right; any icon beside its own light collapses to one icon that lights up.
5. Every change is judged by the page as he sees it — primo, top-end, top of the line — before it is reported done; "he said it so I did it" is never a defense.
6. Staged builds are verified from the branch's own blob or an independently curled copy — never by a cb URL.
