## Nutshell

This extends the architect + scorecarder walk in two directions. First, the trigger: not only when a page is ready for his eye — EVERY time something comes back from an agent, scorecard it. A return is not a delivery until it has been ripped apart and survived: architecture (what do the functions do, how do they connect, is everything in the ideal spot) and ten-star service (does it feel like a real live living object, is the colour flow perfect, does it feel real, is it intuitive, should it be there, is it all it can be, zoomed out is it a work of art). "Rip it apart, it's okay" is permission: tearing a return apart is the job, not rudeness — an un-scored return is an unchecked claim. Second, the scope: not just one page — ALL the pages, the whole system. Figure out where we are at as a whole; doing the whole thing will teach something; then get aligned on the whole thing. And the maths behind it: 7% efficient is 93% inefficient; 14% is 86% inefficient. The work itself is awesome, amazing, beautiful, coming together — but 10x the workflow, get 10 times faster, by finding the root, solving it, and putting systems in place so we get more done.

## What it means

1. **THE VERDICT:** on the Hub — pill hover is rim light ONLY, on the outside, never the whole board. One SAVE per intelligent block, top right corner. VALUE purple, INTELLIGENCE white — purple balls with white stars, white orbs with purple stars, the words themselves dynamic and lit in their row's colour. Star boxes line up; stars dead centre; word beside its stars; the whole group centred. Lines that carry nothing are removed.
2. **THE SCORECARD:** scorecard every return from every agent — rip it apart, architecturally and for ten-star service — before it counts as delivered. And scorecard the whole system, not just one page: where are we at as a whole, what does the whole teach, then get aligned on the whole thing.
3. **THE LOOP:** the URL loop — work one, send the URL, take his feedback, fix it, send the next URL — repeat until crafted. He is waiting on the URL; the loop IS the work. A link goes to him only when it is a new version that has passed the check — never an unchanged link as new.
4. **THE PLAN:** the action plan is worked in parallel, not one at a time: one team per lane — Dashboard, Personal, calendar, membership — all at once, all aligned, all for the one system. Quality holds in every lane at once; more work never excuses less quality.
5. **AUDIT THE LANES:** look through the whole system that makes up Naya and ask — is any lane missing that needs to be activated right now, or are we running at the optimal? Answer honestly: busy is not optimal; optimal is every needed lane live at once.

## Why it matters

1. Without marrying the half-right versions, the Hub's system and the chat stay split — one version with the right cover and the wrong IM bar, the other with the right IM bar and the wrong cover. With it, the Hub's whole system goes into Personal, the instant chat system goes into the Hub, and where two versions are each half right, they are married: correct cover from one, correct IM bar from the other, one page.
2. Without the timeout obeying the bake, the bake is killed mid-run and the feed stands still while filing continues — and the tree grows while the live page ages. With it, the timeout exceeds the work it guards, the bake finishes, and the feed carries the notes live.
3. Without the finish line, the redo loop runs forever — work comes back not good, gets redone, comes back not good again, and nothing lands. With it, each of the four comes to him whole, in his order, and a page is done when he has seen the whole page and it held.
4. Without the parallel plan, one at a time is much slower — and the lanes wait on each other while the efficiency number falls. With it, one team per lane, all at once, all aligned, all for the one system — and more work never excuses less quality.

## What changes now

1. **Once a page is good and talked through, make it live on the main URL immediately** — then work on it from there, in the system. A staged link is a preview, never the landing; what sits in staging does not exist for him or for citizens.
2. **Understand, then fix, then optimize** — when anything breaks, ask first "how come that's happening?" — find the root cause and name it — then solve it, then optimize the system so it stays solved. Never fix blind, never optimize a thing you have not understood.
3. **The kingdom frame is locked in — build the interface INSIDE it** — obsidian black doing the work, purple as rim and accent only; when a page runs long, repeat the centre metal pattern, never stretch it.
4. **Every page gets its own self-scorecard** — love, wrong, ten-times-better — before it is shown as done; and every return gets scorecarded, whole system, in the captain's own view, before it reaches him.
5. **The server-side key never goes in page code** — a mail fix is proven by a send that lands and reads back; run199/run200 must NOT be run blindly.
6. **Never a new system per feed** — take the best parts of each and put them with each other; harvest whole, compose together.
