## Nutshell

The edge light around every frame is white — beautiful white light, like snow, shimmering like Christmas lights, an angel light. Actual white light around the edge, not just a white line, and not pink. The frame complements the text or the image in the middle of the board — it lifts what sits inside it, never colours it, never competes with it.

The frame is there to make the middle beautiful, not to be the star itself.

## Naya

Light every frame's edge white — actual white light around the edge, shimmering, with volume, never a flat white line and never pink, never purple, never any colour at all. The edge light complements the text or the image in the middle of the board; it lifts, it never tints, it never competes. Check every board against the eye: if the edge reads as any colour, it goes back — the bytes saying white is not proof, his eye saying white is. This rides with the emblem standard: real light with volume, real materials, nothing flat.

## What it means

1. **WHITE, NOT PINK:** the edge light is white. Pink is out — his eye said pink when the bytes said purple and white, and the eye is the verdict.
2. **LIGHT, NOT A LINE:** actual white light around the edge — with shimmer, with volume — not a flat white stroke drawn around the frame.
3. **LIKE SNOW, LIKE CHRISTMAS LIGHTS, LIKE AN ANGEL:** the reference is real white light in the world, not a hex on a screen.
4. **THE FRAME COMPLEMENTS THE MIDDLE:** the frame's job is to lift the text or the image in the middle of the board, never to compete with it or tint it.
5. **THE EYE IS THE CHECK:** a board passes when his eye says the edge is white, not when the bytes say so.

## Why it matters

1. Without white, the edge tints everything it frames — and a tinted board reads wrong to his eye no matter what the code says.
2. Without real light, the edge is a flat line — and flat is the opposite of the real-object law.
3. Without the snow / Christmas / angel reference, "white" drifts back to whatever white the builder picks — and the drift lands pink again.
4. Without the frame complementing the middle, the frame becomes the star — and the story inside it loses.
5. Without the eye as the check, a checker passes bytes that his eye rejects — and the rejection arrives after the build, not before it.

## What changes now

1. Every frame's edge light is white — actual white light, shimmering, with volume.
2. Pink, purple, or any coloured edge light is out — any board showing it goes back.
3. The frame is judged by whether it complements the middle, never by the edge alone.
4. The edge passes only when his eye says white — the bytes are the build, the eye is the proof.
