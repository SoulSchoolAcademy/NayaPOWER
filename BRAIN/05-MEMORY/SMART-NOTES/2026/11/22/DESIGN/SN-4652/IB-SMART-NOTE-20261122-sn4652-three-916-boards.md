On mobile, one board at a time, full screen, scroll between them. Never scroll inside a frame; never cut an image off; never narrow a board to fit a wrong shape. ## What it means

1. **THREE, NOT TWO:** two boards narrowed everything and left a dead gap in the middle, with nothing to click on a laptop. Three boards fill the frame.
2. **ALTERNATE:** image, text, image — then text, image, text. The rhythm turns every slide, so no two slides read the same.
3. **ONE BOARD AT A TIME ON MOBILE:** scroll shows one board full screen. The phone look is the real look; the laptop is three phones side by side.
4. **NO SCROLLING INSIDE A FRAME, NO CUT-OFF IMAGES:** a board is whole or it is wrong. If it scrolls inside, the format failed.
5. **"HOLDING THE 16:9" IS DEAD:** never say it, never build to it. The 16:9 layout is being replaced, not held.
6. **PHONE-FIRST IMAGES:** the 18 new images are made for 9:16, for this format. Never build the boards on reused wide pictures.
7. **THE CITIZEN ALREADY FITS:** their own mobile video or image is already 9:16 — the universal format meets them where they already are.

## Why it matters

1. Without three boards, the frame has a dead gap in the middle and the laptop has nothing to click — the page looks unfinished to his eye.
2. Without alternation, every slide reads the same and the eye stops — the turn is what keeps the story moving.
3. Without one-board-at-a-time on mobile, the phone squeezes a laptop page — and the citizen's own world never looks right on their own phone.
4. Without the no-scroll, no-cut rule, images get squeezed and cut — and a cut image is a broken promise about what the board shows.
5. Without killing "holding the 16:9", builders keep building the old layout while saying the new one is coming — and the replacement never lands.
6. Without phone-first images, the format is right and the pictures are wrong — and the build fails on the pictures, not the frame.
7. Without the citizen fitting already, every citizen's own video needs reformatting — and the system asks work of the person it should serve.

## What changes now

1. Every presenting board — About, Skills, School — is built as three 9:16 boards, alternating image, text, image.
2. On mobile, one board at a time, full screen; on laptop, three across inside the frame.
3. No scrolling inside a frame, no cut-off images — a board is whole or it goes back.
4. The phrase "holding the 16:9" is struck from every brief, every board, every message.
5. The School boards wait for the 18 new phone-first images; nothing is built on the old wide pictures.
6. A citizen's own mobile video or image is accepted as-is — it already fits the universal format.
