## Hook

"An image ALONE must tell the story." His law on the live call: the v2 School images are beautiful, but looking at just the picture he cannot tell what it is about — he would need the text to explain it, and that is the miss. The image and the text must say the same thing together, not two different things.

## Nutshell

An image alone must tell the story — someone looking at just the picture should already feel what the words are saying. The loop: start with word-perfect text, create the image that matches what the board says, check the image is aligned, then mold the text to match the image. Text → image → align image → align text to image. Repeat until image and text are one story. An instruction set was ordered for each of the 18: literally what the board will say, plus what the image must display to match it — given to the agents as the instruction set. Do not build images without that per-board instruction set.

## Human

Think of it like a picture book. You open it and the picture already tells you the story — you don't need someone standing next to you explaining it. If the picture needs the words to make sense, the picture is wrong. So you write the words first, perfectly, then you paint the picture the words describe, then you check they match, then you shape the words to the picture — back and forth until the picture and the words are telling one story, together.

## Naya

Make every image tell its story alone. Start with word-perfect text. Create the image that matches what the board says. Check the image is aligned — a stranger looking at the picture alone must already feel what the words say. Then mold the text to match the image. Repeat the loop — text, image, align image, align text to image — until image and text are one story. Never build an image without its per-board instruction set: what the board will say, plus what the image must display to match it. A beautiful image that needs the text to explain it is a miss, not a pass.

## What it means

1. **THE IMAGE ALONE TELLS THE STORY:** someone looking at just the picture already feels what the words are saying. If the picture needs the text to explain it, the picture is wrong.
2. **TEXT FIRST, WORD-PERFECT:** the loop starts with the words, finished and perfect — the image is made to match the board, never the other way around.
3. **IMAGE MATCHES THE BOARD:** the image is created from what the board says — it shows what the board is actually talking about, not just something pretty.
4. **CHECK THE ALIGNMENT:** the image is checked against the words — does the picture alone carry the story? If not, the image goes back.
5. **MOLD THE TEXT TO THE IMAGE:** once the image is right, the words are shaped to it — the two meet in the middle.
6. **REPEAT UNTIL ONE STORY:** the loop runs until image and text say the same thing together — not two different things.
7. **THE INSTRUCTION SET COMES FIRST:** a report for each board — what the board will say, plus what the image must display — is the instruction set the agents build from. No image is built without it.

## Why it matters

1. Without the image telling the story alone, the citizen looks at the picture and feels nothing — the board fails at first sight, before a single word is read.
2. Without text first, the image is made to a vague idea and the words are bent to fit a picture that was never theirs — two stories, not one.
3. Without the check, beautiful images ship that say the wrong thing — beauty without alignment is the miss he named.
4. Without molding the text, the words describe a picture that does not exist — and the citizen reads one thing and sees another.
5. Without the loop, each board is judged once and shipped — and the misalignment he caught in v2 repeats in the other nine.
6. Without the instruction set, agents build from a brief that never says what the image must display — and the same miss is built eighteen times.
7. Without one story, the School teaches two lessons at once — and the citizen learns neither.

## What changes now

1. Every School image is judged by the picture alone: does it tell the story without the text?
2. The loop runs for all 18: word-perfect text → image matching the board → alignment check → text molded to the image → repeat until one story.
3. The instruction set — one report per board, words plus what the image must display — is written before the other nine images are built.
4. No image is built without its per-board instruction set.
