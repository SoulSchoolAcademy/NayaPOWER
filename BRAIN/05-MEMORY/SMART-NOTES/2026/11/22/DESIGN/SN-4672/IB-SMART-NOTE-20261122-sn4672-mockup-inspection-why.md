## Hook

## Nutshell

The mockup inspection of 058fcf20 (~15:36 MST, 2026-11-22) found the WHY behind a mockup that did not land: the builder made NO new images — he reused the old Naya Code slide and the vault picture, squeezed into two tall 9:16 boards. There was no forward button — only tap-to-enlarge on phones — so on a laptop there was nothing to click. The sizing math narrowed the boards and left a big empty gap. The idea wasn't the problem; the build was. The lesson: when a mockup fails, inspect the build before judging the idea — reused assets, a missing control on one device, and sizing math that shrinks the work are build failures, and they look exactly like an idea failure from the outside.

## Naya

An idea and its build are two different verdicts, and a failed mockup is usually the build's verdict wearing the idea's name. The inspection proved it: no new images (old slide, old vault picture, squeezed into 9:16), no forward button (tap-to-enlarge is a phone gesture — on a laptop there is nothing to click), sizing math that narrowed the boards into a big empty gap. None of that is the idea. Inspect the build first — what was actually made, what controls actually exist on each device, what the sizing actually did — before the idea is judged, changed, or thrown away.

## What it means

1. **NO NEW IMAGES:** the builder reused the old Naya Code slide and the vault picture — squeezed into two tall 9:16 boards instead of images made for the boards.
2. **NO FORWARD BUTTON:** only tap-to-enlarge on phones — on a laptop there is nothing to click, so the mockup is a dead end on the device he uses.
3. **SIZING MATH SHRANK THE WORK:** the boards came out narrow, leaving a big empty gap — the format did the opposite of what it was for.
4. **THE IDEA WASN'T THE PROBLEM:** the build was. A failed mockup that is really a failed build looks exactly like a failed idea from the outside.
5. **THE INSPECTION IS THE VERDICT:** the why was found by looking at what was actually built, not by debating the idea.

## Why it matters

1. Without inspecting the build, a good idea gets thrown away for a bad build's sins — and the next build repeats the same sins on a new idea.
2. Without naming "no new images", reuse passes as craft — and every mockup ships yesterday's pictures in today's frame.
3. Without checking every device, a phone gesture stands in for navigation — and the laptop, the device he uses, has nothing to click.
4. Without reading the sizing math, a big empty gap looks like a design choice — it is a build failure, and it has to be named as one.
5. Without separating idea from build, the machine learns the wrong lesson — and the wrong lesson compounds.

## What changes now

1. A failed mockup is inspected at the build first: what was actually made, what controls exist on each device, what the sizing did.
2. Reused assets are named as reuse in the inspection, never passed off as new work.
3. Every mockup is checked for a forward control on laptop as well as phone — tap-to-enlarge is not navigation.
