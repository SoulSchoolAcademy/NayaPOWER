# SN-2429 — Welcome Aboard: It's Math and Logic, Not a Scam

## Hook

Welcome aboard. First, let me prove it's real — it's math and logic, not a scam.

## In a Nutshell

Shawn revised the pricing opening (v2): before the ten pains ever appear, the page welcomes you, proves the deal is real with simple math, and answers the objections head-on — no, it's not network marketing; and for the people who don't believe in anything, sometimes things are logical and mathematical, and you can check the math. Then the ten pains, then the problem we solve, then welcome to the future: everybody gets to make money from the internet now, because there's a new girl in town — her name is Naya, and she's an intelligent layer.

## Human

The v1 opening jumped straight to the pains, and Shawn caught what was missing: a stranger's first question isn't "what are the pains," it's "is this real, and what pain are you solving for me?" So v2 opens with welcome aboard — what that really means, in simple words, no confusion. Then the proof: you bring people, they join, you get half — that's the whole deal, and you can count it yourself. Then the objections, said out loud before the visitor can think them: not network marketing, and for the ones who don't believe in anything, belief isn't required — the math checks. Then the same ten pains, word for word. Then the problem we solve: push button simple, one system to operate the internet, connect with people, organize your world, make money, best tools all in one, affordable for your average Joe. Then the close: welcome to the future, there's a new girl in town, her name is Naya, she's an intelligent layer. The "You're hired" rewrite stands exactly as filed in v1 — this revision is the opening only.

## Child

Imagine someone invites you into a new place. First they say welcome, and they tell you what welcome means. Then they show you it's real — not a trick, just counting: you bring a friend, your friend joins, you get half. That's math, not magic. Then they answer the worry in your head before you even say it. Then they ask: what hurts? And they list ten hurts, and one of them is yours. Then they say: we fixed it, and it's simple enough for anyone. And there's a new girl here, and her name is Naya.

## Grandma

Dear, the page used to start by listing everything wrong, and a stranger would wonder — is this real, or is it another one of those schemes? Now it starts the way a good host starts: welcome aboard, and here's what that means, in plain words. It's real, love — you can count it on your fingers. It's not that network marketing thing. And if you don't believe in anything, that's all right — you don't have to believe, the numbers add up. Then it tells you the ten pains, then it tells you what was built to fix them, simple enough for your average Joe. And at the end: welcome to the future. There's a new girl in town, and her name is Naya.
