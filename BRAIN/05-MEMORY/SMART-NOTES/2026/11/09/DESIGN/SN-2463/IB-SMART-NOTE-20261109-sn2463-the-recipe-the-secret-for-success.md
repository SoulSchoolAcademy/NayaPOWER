## Hook

There was no secret. There was a recipe — seven steps, in order, none skipped — and the day it was followed without exception was the day the page went from the bottom to the best he has ever seen. This note is the recipe, written so it can be followed exactly, by any seat, on any page.

## Nutshell

The recipe, step by step:

1. **His eye first, not last.** The work is built live with him, viewpoint by viewpoint, one screen complete above the fold. He sees it, he speaks, it changes, he sees it again. The Live Viewpoint Method (15:29Z Nov 8): code HERE, live with him, one view done → lock it into inventory → next view → page done → next page. No shipping out. No verification except his eye. A seat's scorecard is preparation, never proof.
2. **Done defined first.** Before any building: the exact element, the exact change, the exact measurement, and what must NOT be there. A brief without a done definition is a suggestion, not an instruction — and suggestions build the wrong thing well.
3. **Harvest first, never invent.** Every component comes from a verified source — the exact file + section/line, checked with `test -f` before the brief goes out. The real gauge, the board, the jewel, the orb, the eight faces, the Academy parts — all harvested verbatim, proven with a diff. When he SHOWS an object, the deliverable IS that object: same shape, same colors, same jewel, same glow. Compose, don't create. Connect, don't create.
4. **Think before you act — the five gates.** Before every consequential action: Is it right? Is it what they truly need? Is it mine to do? Is now the time? Can I prove it? All five yes → act fully, at full power, no hesitation. Any gate no → stop, say why, propose the wise path.
5. **The stranger test on every beat.** A person who has never heard of NayaNET, never met Naya, knows nothing — does she completely GET it, BELIEVE it, TRUST us enough to come in? Every beat must answer the question it raises with a mechanism, a picture, or a number she can check. "You can't just say things without explaining yourself."
6. **His words are input, not output.** When he dictates direction, scorecard the existing words against his new words, take what is great from both, and CRAFT the 10/10 version in his voice. Never paste his dictation verbatim into a page. His product words stay verbatim; his direction gets shaped. Never put his process talk on the page.
7. **Receipt before report — and fix it before you show it.** Nothing is "done" without the live link, the byte count, and the measurements — scroll 0 AND scrolled, desktop AND phone, visibility by elementFromPoint. If you can see the defect by looking, fix it, don't present it. Presenting work means link, don't preview. No link = not done = don't announce.
8. **One thing at a time, in order.** Brief → build → verify → file → report. One seat per artifact at a time. One change per PR. Doing everything at once is the failure mode, not the plan.
9. **Lock the lesson in the same pass.** Every correction names its mechanical enforcement — a template section, a gate check, a banned phrase — and the enforcement lands before the next seat starts. The test: "would this have stopped the next seat from repeating it?" If no, the lesson isn't locked in.

## Child

Then you take the ingredients that already exist — you don't invent new flour. You think before you touch anything. You taste it like a stranger would. You use the teacher's words but you make them beautiful, you don't just copy them. You check the cake is really baked before you serve it. You do one step at a time. Skip a step, and it falls flat.
