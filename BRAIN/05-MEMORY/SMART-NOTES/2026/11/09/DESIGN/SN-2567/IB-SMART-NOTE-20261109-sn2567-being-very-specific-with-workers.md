## Human

Specificity is not a guarantee — it is a protection. A seat given vague instructions guesses; a seat given exact instructions can still miss the picture. Pass-2 proved it: the brief said the orbs must mount, the verifier measured them mounted 10/10, and his eye saw a mess. The lesson is not "specificity fails" — it is that specificity is necessary and not sufficient. What closes the gap is the live check: his eye on the rendered viewpoint, at real widths. So the brief must be as specific as it can be — exact element, exact change, exact measurement, what must not be there — AND the work must be judged by eye, never by the seat's own numbers alone. When work comes back messed up despite a specific brief, the answer is more specificity AND a live check — never less of either.
