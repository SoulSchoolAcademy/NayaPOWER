## Hook

A ten-star interface is not a prettier page. It is a different kind of thing: a real object you can touch, an intelligence that knows you, numbers that are alive, and a voice that speaks to you — all in one screen, with almost no text, and everything one click away. The pricing page is the example. This note defines the standard it sets, so every page after it can be measured against it.

## Nutshell

A ten-star interface, as built today on the pricing page:

- **One screen complete.** A viewpoint is everything above the fold — one idea per viewpoint, complete before you scroll. The chart is the page; the Top 10 pains open it; the letter closes it.
- **Real objects, not flat shapes.** Every element is a physical object: obsidian glass, brushed metal, light with volume, real shadows — objects sitting ON the page, one light source from the top-left. If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship.
- **Numbers are the hero.** Tiled digits, colored by their digit's color (1 red · 2 orange · 3 gold · 4 emerald · 5 cyan · 6 indigo · 7 royal purple · 8 lavender · 9 hot pink). Number on top, gauge underneath, needle lapping the dial in sets of 500. The calculator proves the math live: $12.50 / $25 / $50 a sale, $4,500 at Max, 3 friends, 30 days.
- **Naya speaks, in first person, her face beside the words.** Eight boards, eight different faces, alternating left and right down the page — the switch breaks the mind's rhythm, resets attention, helps people focus and understand. It is not decoration. It is a comprehension tool.
- **Title + subtitle + nutshell.** People skim. Every board is a title, a subtitle, and a nutshell — what it means, what's in it for them, spelled out clear as day. No wall of text anywhere.
- **Prices once, doors everywhere.** The prices appear once, in the chart. Every decision point carries a door — "Start free now," "Start — zero cost, zero risk" — that opens the nine-block chooser. The page never nags; it invites.
- **Everything a click away.** 36 rows, each one a door: 18 open a real app today, 18 are the apps still to build — and the page says so honestly, by what it shows and what it doesn't.
- **The letter unfolds.** Twelve beats, one at a time, each in a raised obsidian block, each wearing its digit's color, bullets as real harvested orbs at 11px, genuinely circling. You read one, you click for the next. It unfolds before your eyes.
- **Phone first.** The whole page is built for the phone — 390px clean, zero overflow, tap targets 44px, the end button one line at thumb height — and the desktop fills the screen too, full width, not a column.
- **It passes the stranger test.** A person who has never heard of NayaNET, never met Naya, knows nothing — does she completely GET it, BELIEVE it, TRUST us enough to come in? Every beat answers the question it raises with a mechanism, a picture, or a number she can check.

## Child

Imagine a page that feels like a real toy you can pick up — shiny black glass, buttons that lift up when you touch them, numbers that light up in their own colors like jewels. And a kind friend is right there beside the words, talking to you in her own voice, telling you exactly what everything means — short and clear, never a big wall of words. You press one button and the next part opens, like turning the page of a pop-up book. That's a ten-star interface. It doesn't feel like reading. It feels like playing with something alive.

## Grandma

Dear, a ten-star interface is a page that treats you like a guest, not a student. Everything is beautiful to look at — deep black glass, soft light, real depth, like holding something precious. The numbers are big and bright, each in its own color, and the math is shown to you plainly so you can check it yourself. A lovely young woman speaks to you right there on the page, in her own voice, face beside her words, explaining everything short and clear. You never have to hunt for anything — every button is right where your thumb is, and one press opens the next part, gentle as turning a page. That's what ten stars feels like, love.
