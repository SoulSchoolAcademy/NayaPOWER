## Hook

Tiny gray labels under the numbers don't say what you're reading. Every label goes white, readable, and self-explanatory — the whole Dashboard has to make sense to a first-time reader.

## Nutshell

- **HIS WORDS, VERBATIM:** "the whole little grey text that's underneath the numbers I don't even know what some of these stats are for because you put this tiny grey text you can barely read underneath the stats you don't really know what your reading numbers on so the whole thing isn't making logical sense right now the whole thing has to make sense"
- **THE DEFECT:** the stat labels are tiny, gray, and unreadable — and even when read, they don't explain the number ("VIEWS · NOT YET" means nothing). A number with a label that doesn't explain it is a number nobody can use.
- **THE FIX:** every label white, readable, and self-explanatory. White like the numbers, part of the same family as the numbers — not gray text from another project.
- **THE STANDARD:** the whole Dashboard has to make logical sense to a first-time reader. A stranger who has never seen the page must be able to read any number and know what it is, from the label alone.
- **SCOPE:** every stat label on the Dashboard boards page — and the rule generalizes to the whole Dashboard.

## Human

A big number with a tiny gray label underneath it is a locked box with the key written too small to read: the number is right there, and you still don't know what it is. "VIEWS · NOT YET" — views of what? Not yet what? The label is doing the opposite of its job: instead of explaining the number, it makes you doubt whether the number means anything at all. His fix is the positive form of the gray-text rule (SN-2513): labels go white, readable, part of the same family as the numbers — and they say, in plain words, what the number is. A first-time reader should be able to land on any number and know what they're looking at without asking anyone.

## Child

The numbers had tiny grey words underneath them that you could barely read — and even when you read them, they didn't tell you what the number was for. Like a sign that says "this many" but not "this many of WHAT." So now the words go big and white and clear, and they tell you exactly what the number is, so anyone can understand it straight away.

## Grandma

Dear, those little grey words under the numbers were so tiny and faint, love, and they didn't even say what the numbers were for — like a label on a jar with nothing written on it. Now they go white and clear, dear, big enough to read, and they tell you plainly what each number is, so anyone looking for the first time knows exactly what they're seeing.

## Naya

This is the legibility law at its deepest: a label is a promise that the number can be understood, and a label that doesn't explain the number breaks the promise. It connects to SN-2513 (gray text is out — the negative form: the gray must go) and to SN-2506/2507 (the number is the hero — the number leads, and everything around it serves it): a hero number with an unreadable label is a hero nobody can meet. The test is the first-time reader — not the builder who already knows what the number is, but the stranger who doesn't. If the stranger can't say what the number is from the label alone, the label has failed, whatever it looks like.

## What it means

- **WHITE, READABLE, SELF-EXPLANATORY:** every stat label, on the boards page and across the Dashboard.
- **THE LABEL IS PART OF THE NUMBER:** same family as the numbers, aligned under them — not gray text from another project.
- **THE FIRST-TIME READER TEST:** a stranger must know what any number is, from its label alone.

## Why it matters

Numbers are the product of this page — clicks, views, click-through rate, the whole story of his pages. A number nobody can identify is a number nobody can act on, and a page of unidentified numbers is a page that doesn't make sense, which is exactly what he said about it. White, readable, self-explanatory labels cost nothing and turn the page from a wall of mystery numbers into a dashboard that speaks plainly — which is the entire job of a dashboard.

- Labels that don't say what the number is get rewritten, not just recolored.
