## Naya

Two tens is a fork in the record (SN-2320's lesson: a number re-appearing under a new shape is a fork, not a revision) — and a fork on the page is worse, because the visitor must choose which ten to believe. The merge rule generalizes: whenever two lists claim the same number, they are one list with two faces — pain and solution, problem and answer. It is also the stranger test (SN-2443) applied to structure: a stranger reading two "top tens" asks which one is real; a stranger reading one ten, each item pain-matched to its fix, gets it. ## Why it matters

A page with two tens asks the visitor to do the merging in their head — and they won't; they will skim past both.
