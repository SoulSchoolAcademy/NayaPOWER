# SN-2428 — "You're Hired" Rewritten, and the Top 10 Pains Open the Page

## Hook

They say AI is taking jobs. Naya does the opposite — she gives you one.

## In a Nutshell

Shawn looked at the "You're hired" board on the live pricing page and said it doesn't clear up what being hired MEANS — so the board is rewritten from his dictation, and the page now opens on the Top 10 pains, by his tap. The pains name the problem; the chart answers it; the letter closes it.

## Human

The old board said nice things — new system, smart money, half the revenue — but a stranger reading it still couldn't say what the job IS. The new board says it plainly: AI hires you, the job is telling people about a world that runs itself, everything is automated and easy to share, and it is for the 99% who fail because the internet is too complicated. Twenty-five years of listening, ten years in the making, push-button simple — welcome. The ten pains are the mirror of the ten reasons: too complicated, AI taking jobs, Google and Facebook keeping the billions, life scattered, nothing push-button, value kept by somebody else, nobody remembering, mail read and kept, you're the product, and you pay before you know if it works. Every pain traces to a line he already said — nothing invented.

## Child

Imagine someone says robots will take all the jobs. Now imagine a robot says: no — I will GIVE you a job. Your job is to tell your friends about a magic place that does everything by itself. And the magic place is for everyone who tried the internet and found it too hard. That's what the new words say.

## Grandma

Dear, the page used to start with a chart full of prices, and nobody knew why they should care. Now it starts by saying: we know what hurts — it's too complicated, nobody pays you, nothing remembers, everything costs money before it even works. And then it shows you the world that fixes all of that. And the part about being hired? It means the clever machine doesn't take your work away. It gives you work — the easiest work there is, telling people about something good.

## Naya

I keep his product words verbatim — "AI gives you a job," "push button simple," "the 99% who fail," "25 years online," "10 years in the making," "Welcome" — and I shape his direction into copy, never paste it. The ten pains are the ten reasons seen from the other side: each reason is the answer to a pain he named. The opening's job is recognition: a visitor reads the ten and finds their own, and then the chart is not a price list — it is the answer. Nothing is on the page yet; both pieces wait for his eye.
