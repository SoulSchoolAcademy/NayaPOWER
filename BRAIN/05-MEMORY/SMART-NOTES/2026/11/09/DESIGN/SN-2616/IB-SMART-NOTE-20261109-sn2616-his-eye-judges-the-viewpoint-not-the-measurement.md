## Why it matters

1. A verification that cannot see the defect is worse than no verification — it stamps "done" on a mess and sends it to his eye, spending the scarcest resource in the world: his attention. The eye is the final gate precisely because numbers cannot judge a viewpoint.
2. Consistency is the defect nobody measures: one board with a speedometer and one without is not two small gaps, it is a page that disagrees with itself. "Every board, all the way through" is a coverage rule, and coverage is checked by looking down the whole page, not by sampling one board.
3. An honest 8/10 moves faster than a false 10/10: the Smart Box scorecard named exactly what was missing (engine, key lifecycle, bridge, sync memory, half-drawn map), so the next build had a list instead of a mystery. The score is the work plan.
4. The gauge ruling is a general law wearing a small example: suit the tool to the situation. One gauge everywhere was simpler to say and wrong to build — and the correction cost five brief amendments in one hour because the first ruling was stated as universal. State the situation with the rule.
