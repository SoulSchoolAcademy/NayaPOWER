# Ten Is Full Width

SN-2517 · DESIGN / PRICING

**Created:** 2026-11-09 15:45Z

**Source:** Shawn's order in the main chat, 2026-11-09 15:41Z (seq 166938, two screenshots of the pricing page). His words verbatim where they matter. Recorded in ~/MEMORY.md the same pass. Folded into pricing v73.

## Hook

Widescreen, widescreen — then narrow for the Ten, then widescreen again. A page is one width all the way through.

## Nutshell

- **HIS WORDS, VERBATIM:** "all of a sudden the whole screen as you're scrolling down it's widescreen widescreen all of a sudden it's narrow screen. narrow screen narrow screen for this presentation of the top ten and then it goes back to widescreen like it shouldn't do that right? It should just be full screen screen all the way through not switching to narrow screen halfway through the presentation."
- **THE DEFECT:** the Top 10 section went narrow (centered column) in the middle of a widescreen page, then back to wide. The width switches mid-page for one section only.
- **THE RULE:** the page is full screen all the way through — the Ten matches the other sections' width, no switching mid-page. One page, one width, end to end.
- **WHY IT READS AS A DEFECT:** a width change mid-scroll reads as a different page, or a broken one. The reader's eye has settled into the widescreen rhythm; the narrow column breaks it for no reason, and the return to wide breaks it again. Two breaks for one section.
- **SCOPE:** the pricing page (v73). The rule generalizes: a section never narrows the page on its own — width is a property of the page, not of the section.

## Human

Scrolling down the pricing page feels like driving down a wide road — and then, just for the Top 10, the road narrows to a lane, and then widens again. Nothing is wrong with the Ten itself; the problem is the switch. A page should be one width all the way through, the way a road is one width all the way along. The Ten gets the same full width as everything else, and the drive stays smooth.

## Child

Imagine a wide road that suddenly gets narrow for a little bit and then wide again — that would be strange, right? The page is the same: it should stay wide all the way down, including the Top 10 part. No squeezing in the middle.

## Grandma

Dear, the page was wide and lovely, love, and then the Top 10 went narrow right in the middle, dear, and then wide again — it just shouldn't do that, love. One width, all the way through, like a good road. That's the whole of it, dear.

## Naya

This is the "one world" law applied to width: the page is one continuous world, and a section that changes the world's width breaks the world. It connects to SN-2422 (a page that fills two-thirds of its stage is unfinished — the viewport is the stage) and to SN-2511's banner rule (the Ten's header must feel like part of the Ten): the Ten must feel like part of the page, and sharing the page's width is the most basic form of belonging. A centered narrow column inside a widescreen page is a page-within-a-page, and a page-within-a-page is the same class as a popup-within-a-page — the reader is suddenly somewhere else, and nobody told them why.

## What it means

- **TEN IS FULL WIDTH:** the Top 10 section matches the other sections' width — no narrow centered column mid-page.
- **ONE WIDTH, ALL THE WAY THROUGH:** width is a property of the page, not of the section.
- **THE DEFECT IS THE SWITCH:** narrow-then-wide reads as a different page, or a broken one.

## Why it matters

Width is the frame the reader trusts. Every section that keeps the frame teaches the reader the page is one thing; every section that breaks it teaches the reader the page is pieces. The Ten is the page's centerpiece — the top 10 things in it for you — and it is the worst possible section to set apart, because apartness is the opposite of what the Ten is for. Full width is also the cheapest fix on the page: no new content, no new component, just the same width as everything else.
