# The Headliner Leads the Page

SN-2613 · DESIGN / HEADLINER

**Created:** 2026-11-09 23:35Z

## Hook

The page had three equal sections, and they don't matter equally. The one that earns leads. The way in sits right under it. Everything else follows.

## Human

Think of a newspaper. The headline is the biggest thing on the page because it is the most important thing that happened. Under it, the story. Under that, the rest. Nobody puts the weather report above the headline and the headline in the middle of the classifieds — because the order of a page is its argument about what matters. The boards page had three equal sections arguing that the network, the custom links, and the ambassador link matter equally — and they don't. The ambassador link is the one that earns, the one whose numbers decide whether the whole thing is working. So it leads (SN-2526). And the entry — the way to make a new link — sits right under it, because the way in belongs next to the thing it feeds. Everything else on the page answers to the same test: does this help him see the numbers on any page, and does it let him add a link by URL? If it doesn't, it doesn't belong.

## Child

Imagine a classroom. The teacher stands at the front, big and easy to see, because she is the most important person in the room. The door is right beside her, so you can see how to come in. The posters are on the walls, not on top of the teacher. If the teacher stood behind the posters and the door was hidden, you wouldn't know where to look or how to get in. A page is the same: the most important thing goes first, the door goes right under it, and nothing covers the teacher.

## Grandma

Dear, it's like a shop window. The best thing in the shop goes in the window, right at the front, where everyone walking past can see it. The door is right underneath the sign, not hidden around the back. And every price tag says plainly what the thing costs — not in tiny writing you need a magnifying glass for. If the tag doesn't say what the number is, the number is no use to anyone. Put the best first, the door where they can find it, and the labels where they can read them.

## Naya

A page without a stated purpose drifts — boards accumulate, presentations change halfway down, entries go missing, labels stop explaining — because nobody can say what the page is for, so nobody can say what belongs on it and what doesn't (SN-2525). Stating the purpose gives the page its test, and the headliner gives the page its argument. The same discipline governs the checking: a live check is a photograph of one instant, and timing is part of the truth (SN-2527) — so a contradicted claim is held as OWED and re-checked after ~30 minutes, never declared a defect on one photograph. And the same discipline governs the working: no send-and-wait, ever (SN-2528). A fix is dispatched, built, verified, and shown back to back. The captain checks for sitting lanes continuously and dispatches on them directly — the moment one sits, she unsticks it herself. Boom, boom, boom: one agent after another, immediately, no waiting (SN-2529) — but the working check rides with the speed, or the speed just ships defects faster. Looks right is not enough. It must work.

## What it means

1. **Each button its board's color; the click works (SN-2520).** Every "Show on gauge" button lights in its board's color, never all the same gold. Clicking one features that board and the gauge follows its numbers. A dead button is a defect.
2. **Notifications top right (SN-2521).** The pill moves from the middle to the top right. The top is the notifications area; the points strip leaves the top. Each notification lights in its own color and tells you what it is.
3. **One board design (SN-2522).** Page boards and Smart Link boards share a single ultimate design. Sections separated and NAMED — pages vs Smart Links. Actions consistent across all boards. A change of presentation must have a reason the reader can see.
4. **The entry returns (SN-2523).** A visible place to enter a new Smart Link, at the top of the Smart Links section, wired to the creation flow. The promise needs its door.
5. **Labels must explain the number (SN-2524).** Every stat label white, readable, self-explanatory. The label is part of the number — same family, aligned under it. The first-time reader test: a stranger must know what any number is, from its label alone.
6. **The purpose, in his words (SN-2525).** His stats page for ANY page inside NayaNET — scroll and see clicks, views, click-through rate on any of his pages — and create by URL: put in any URL and boom, a new Smart Link shows up with stats. Three sections at the top: network stats (default), custom Smart Links, ambassador link.
7. **Two things, headliner first (SN-2526).** Refines SN-2525: the page's base is two things — NayaNET network stats, and affiliate/master link stats, THE MAJOR ONE. The headliner sits at the top; the track-any-link entry sits underneath it. Recorded as a refinement, not a silent edit.
8. **A contradicted claim is "not yet landed", not "false" (SN-2527).** Edge lag is real and measurable — up to ~30 minutes. Re-check after ~30 minutes before calling it a defect. A live check is a photograph of one instant.
9. **No send-and-wait (SN-2528).** The captain unsticks sitting lanes herself, in the same pass. A handoff failure is not a reason to wait — it is the signal to dispatch directly on the brief + the file.
10. **Boom boom boom (SN-2529).** One agent after another, immediately, no waiting; add more agents when it gets it done faster; work all day, every day, until all the interfaces are molded and working. Looks right is NOT enough — it must WORK: all buttons, all pages, everything in sync, all stats flowing back to the Dashboard.

## Why it matters

1. The boards page is a machine for looking at numbers — the gauge is the instrument, and "Show on gauge" is how you point the instrument at a board. If the pointing doesn't work, the whole page is a poster: pretty, and useless (SN-2520).
2. The top of a page is the first thing the eye reads, and if the first thing is confused — a bell in the middle, a points count where notifications should be — the visitor decides the page is confused before they have read a word (SN-2521).
3. A reader builds a mental model of a page from its first boards. When the presentation changes halfway down for no reason, the model breaks and the reader has to start over, guessing what the new boxes mean (SN-2522).
4. Without the entry, the page is a museum of links that already exist, and the visitor who came to make one leaves empty-handed. Restoring the entry costs one box; leaving it missing costs the page its purpose (SN-2523).
5. Numbers are the product of this page. A number nobody can identify is a number nobody can act on, and a page of unidentified numbers is a page that doesn't make sense (SN-2524).
6. The order of a page is its argument about what matters. Putting the ambassador link first tells the reader in one glance what the page is really for (SN-2526).
7. False alarms cost the team real work: a declared sync failure would dispatch a repair seat, file a defect, and pull the captain's attention — all for a lag that resolves itself (SN-2527).
8. Waiting is how work that is "in progress" stays undone for days while everyone believes it is moving. He is waiting on the seats to give him the work so he can give the next set — every hour a lane sits is an hour he cannot move forward (SN-2528, SN-2529).
