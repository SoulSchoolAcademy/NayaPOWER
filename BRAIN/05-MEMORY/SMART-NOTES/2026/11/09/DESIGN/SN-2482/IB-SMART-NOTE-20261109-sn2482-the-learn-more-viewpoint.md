## Human

A page that makes everyone read everything is a page that nobody finishes. The proof that convinces a stranger — the job, the money, the trial — is seven boards long, and seven boards is a wall. So the wall moves behind one door: "Not network marketing" stays where the eye lands, "Learn more" opens the whole story in order, and the close puts you exactly back where you were. The convinced never see the wall. The unconvinced find it in one tap. That is the whole design: the page is short for the sure, and deep for the curious.

## Child

Imagine a book with a really long chapter in the middle. Some people want to read it, some don't. So you put the chapter behind a door with a sign that says "Learn more." If you want it, you open the door, read it all, and when you close the door you're right back on the same page you left. Nobody is forced to read it, and nobody who wants it has to hunt for it.

## Naya

This is the page-length law (SN-2453) applied to the hired boards: length is a defect, and every section must earn its scroll. The boards earned their place by value, not by position — so they keep their value and lose their position. The viewpoint is the same mechanism as the popups (SN-2397): the proof lives one tap away, never in the way, and the close returns the reader to exactly where they were, because a reader who loses their place loses the page.

## What it means

- **PROOF AND BREVITY ARE NOT A TRADE:** the page can be short AND the proof can be complete, when the proof lives behind a door instead of in the flow.
- **THE ANCHOR STAYS:** "Not network marketing" is the board that stays visible, because it is the objection the visitor is already thinking. The button answers the objection; the viewpoint proves the answer.
- **THE RETURN IS PART OF THE DESIGN:** close returns to the same scroll position. A viewpoint that dumps the reader at the top is a trap, not a door.

## Why it matters

The page was extraordinary and too long at the same time. Cutting the boards would have removed the very thing that makes a stranger trust us; keeping them in the flow would have kept the page unreadable. The viewpoint is the third option — and it is the pattern for everything long on the page from here: the main flow carries the decision, the depth lives one tap away.
