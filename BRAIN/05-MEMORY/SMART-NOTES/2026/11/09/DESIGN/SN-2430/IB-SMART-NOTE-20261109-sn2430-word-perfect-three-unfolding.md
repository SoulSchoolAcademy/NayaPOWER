# SN-2430 — Word Perfect: Three Sections That Unfold Before Your Eyes

## Hook

One format, three times — the pains, the people, the letter — each one unfolding beat by beat, and every word perfect.

## In a Nutshell

Shawn's order: the letter's unfolding-beat format — read one, click for the next — is used for all three spots on the pricing page: the Top 10 pain points, who this is for (the Top 10 ideal customers), and the founder's letter. And every word is polished to the same standard: it is what it is, beautiful, not confusing, logical, reasonable, sensible — something they can trust because it's obvious, with the jargon cut. Woven through it: it's much more than money — much more than meets the eye — everything, all in one: the automation, the internet, the intelligent layer, the connection. All of it. Not one thing — everything that matters.

## Human

The letter already unfolded, and it was beautiful — so the other two sections now unfold the same way. The opening is sixteen beats: welcome aboard, it's real (math and logic), the objections answered, the ten pains one per beat, the problem solved, much more than money, welcome to the future. The ten ideal customers are ten beats, one per customer — who they are, what they're carrying, what Naya does for them, the moment it clicks — each one roughly half the length of the first version, same substance, fewer words. The jargon went: Smart Link became one link, Smart Mail became a message from Naya, Smart Access became a pay door, Smart Search became she finds anybody, the ledger became the money, recorded. The "You're hired" board didn't change — on read-through it already passed the standard, and it already carries the same substance in "the best of the whole internet, all in one place." The letter itself wasn't rewritten — it's approved by his eye — but reading it against the same standard flagged two things for him: the shared-upside paragraph still reads like a terms document ("participate in the revenue generated under the applicable program") with no number while the boards say 50%, and the nine free days are never named inside the letter itself.

## Child

Imagine a book that opens one page at a time, and every page is one clear idea. First the pains — ten of them, one at a time, and one of them is yours. Then the people — ten of them, one at a time, and one of them is you. Then the letter — twelve parts, one at a time. Each page says exactly what it means, in plain words, nothing confusing, nothing fancy for the sake of fancy. And all of it together is much more than money — it's everything, all in one.

## Grandma

Dear, the page used to tell you everything at once, and that's a lot to take in. Now it opens like a letter from a friend — one thought, then the next, then the next. The pains, one by one, so you can find your own. The people it's for, one by one, so you can find yourself. The letter, one part at a time. Every word is plain and true, love — logical, reasonable, sensible — the kind of thing you can trust because it's obvious. And it's much more than money, dear. It's everything, all in one.

## Naya

I keep his product words verbatim — "welcome aboard," "math and logic," "not network marketing," "push button simple," "average Joe," "welcome to the future," "there's a new girl in town, her name is Naya, she's an intelligent layer" — and I shape his direction into copy, never paste it. The v3 polish is shape and tightening, not re-writing: the opening's words are the v2 words re-shaped into sixteen beats, the customers are the v1 descriptions condensed into ten beats, the ten pains are unchanged word for word. The letter stays as his eye approved it; the two flags go to him, not into the page. Nothing is on the page yet; v3 waits for his eye, and the build seat implements after he approves the words.
