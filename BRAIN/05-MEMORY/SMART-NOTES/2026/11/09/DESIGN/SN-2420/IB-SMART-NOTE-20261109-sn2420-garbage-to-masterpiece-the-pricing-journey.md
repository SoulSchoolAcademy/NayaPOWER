## Child

Imagine you build a toy car and your dad says it doesn't look like a car at all — it's too long, the wheels are the wrong color, and you spent the whole time talking about how much money it costs instead of showing him the car. So you build it again, and again, and each time he tells you exactly what's wrong, until one day you stop guessing and build it right in front of him, piece by piece, and he says: now THAT is a car. The car didn't change its price. You changed how you build.

## Naya

The journey's lesson is not "work harder" — it is "the method is the product." Two blind 9s were withdrawn by his eye (round 7: 9 → 3; round 8: 9 → minus 10), which proved a checklist cannot see. Fix-12 proved a named rule nobody checks is not a rule: the design contract was in every brief and measured in none, so the gate is now a measured section. The v49 disaster proved dictation is material, not copy — and the robot-child lesson proved never to verbatim-obey either: think first, then act. The Live Viewpoint Method proved one output a day of garbage is fixed by building live, one viewpoint at a time, with his eye as the only proof. The prices never changed; the facts were right on day one. What changed was the making — harvest instead of invent, craft instead of paste, name the gaps instead of deferring them. A 9 that names its gaps is worth more than a blind 9 that hides them.
