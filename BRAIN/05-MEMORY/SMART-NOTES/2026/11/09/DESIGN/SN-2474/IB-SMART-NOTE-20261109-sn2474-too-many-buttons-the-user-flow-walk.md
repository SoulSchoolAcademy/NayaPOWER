## What it means

- **ONE DOOR AT A TIME:** a decision point carries one button, never two. Two doors at one point is a fork the user has to resolve — the page should have resolved it for them.
- **THE BOTTOM IS THE ENDING:** the "Everything, all in one" reminder closes the page, underneath the last button. A reminder placed mid-page becomes a cluster.
- **THE WALK IS THE TEST:** before a page is shown, walk it top to bottom as the user. The walk finds the doubles that a checklist misses.

## Why it matters

Every extra button is a question the visitor has to answer, and every question costs a little of their trust. The page's job is to carry them to one decision, not to offer them six. The flow walk is the cheapest verification there is — no tool, no seat, just the builder walking the page as a stranger — and it catches what the mechanics never will: too much of a good thing.

## What changes now

- The flow seat (v70) applies this rule: full button inventory, keep/cut/move per button, no two doors back to back, "Everything" to the very bottom, "not network marketing" below the gauge.
- Every future page gets a user-flow walk before it is shown: top to bottom, as the user, naming what should be here, what shouldn't, what needs to go, what needs polishing.
- The button inventory becomes part of the verification: a page with two doors back to back does not pass, however beautiful each door is.
