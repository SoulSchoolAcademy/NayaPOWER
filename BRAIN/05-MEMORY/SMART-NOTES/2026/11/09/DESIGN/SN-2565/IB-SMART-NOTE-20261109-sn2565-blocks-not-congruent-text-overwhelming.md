## Human

Congruence is what makes a page feel like one thing instead of a pile of things. When every board shares the same design, the same layout, the same spacing, the eye relaxes — the boards are parts of each other, and the page reads as one system. When they don't, the eye trips on every seam, and the page feels like it was assembled from different machines. And text that overwhelms is text that is not read: a mess of text is a board that failed to say its one thing clearly. Short, clear, scannable words are the difference between a board that informs and a board that is skipped. Together the two rulings are one standard — the page must look like one thing, and read like one thing.

## Child

Imagine a row of toy blocks, and none of them line up — different sizes, different gaps, all crooked. And each block is covered in tiny words, so many you can't read any of them. Dad says: "Make all the blocks line up the same — same shape, same spaces, all in a row — and make the words short and easy, so you can read them at a glance." Now the row looks like one thing, and the words are easy. That's the rule: congruent blocks, easy words.
