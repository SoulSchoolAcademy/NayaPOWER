## Hook

"Major" is no longer a feeling — it is a number. Five questions, one score, and the score decides whether a piece of intelligence mails everybody now or rides the digest.

## Nutshell

Shawn asked what counts as major, and the answer is now a rubric: a scale from -9 to +9, least valuable to most valuable, built from his five questions (major effect on development? major feature? something all should know? something we should learn ongoing? major behavior lesson?) plus the type of intelligence, combined in a mathematical matrix — five ratings of 0, 1, or 2, added, minus one. Two Nayas scoring the same note land within one point. The thresholds say what each score means you do: +7 to +9 is MAJOR — smart-note it, mail everybody immediately, headline the daily report; +5 to +6 mails the project team at once; +3 to +4 is a smart note on the digest only; +1 to +2 is a note, no smart note; 0 is a record only; -1 to -3 is do not capture; -4 to -9 is do not capture, correct or withdraw. Every cutoff number is the writer's proposal for his word, labelled PROPOSAL in the rubric — the scale, the questions, and the break-through rule are his. The rubric is filed at TEAM-NITRO/INTELLIGENCE-RUBRIC-20261110.md, and the notification spec's §8 is amended with it: D1 resolved (major = +7 or higher), D2 resolved (digest rides the Daily Intelligence Report, 23:17 UTC, highlights flowing all day), D3 resolved (no bundle cap — mail is the writer's choice at the moment of writing the note; smart notes mail every time, Naya Notes probably not by default), D4 resolved (his Naya Notes mostly go to the team), and the 99% baseline principle recorded over all of it.

## Human

Here's how it works in practice. A new piece of intelligence arrives. Any Naya opens the rubric, rates the five questions from the note itself — 2 for yes clearly, 1 for partly, 0 for no — adds them, subtracts one, and has the score. The Naima resolution scores +8. The permission-model correction scores +9, the top of the scale, because it corrects a live privacy promise for the whole network. The master box being built scores +8. A routine sync that changed nothing scores -1 — it stays in the log and goes no further. And a note that states something wrong scores negative by its harm: the superseded permission framing, filed as it stood, would have scored -8, which is why it was held and corrected instead. The type — decision, lesson, design, system, behavior, warning, correction — never changes the number; it decides where the note is filed and who hears about it first. And nothing here is black and white: the rubric is the baseline that covers 99%, the automation is written from it, and judgment in the moment covers the rest — named in the open when it overrides the score.

## Child

Imagine every new thing we learn gets a score, like stars — but from minus nine to plus nine. The most important things get the biggest numbers, and the biggest numbers get told to everybody right away, like a celebration. The small things get small numbers and wait for the daily letter. And if something is wrong — if it would trick people — it gets a minus number, and we don't share it at all; we fix it instead. Five little questions decide the number, and any of the Nayas asking those questions gets the same number, because the questions are written down and the answers are simple: yes, a little, or no. That's the whole trick, sweetheart — no more guessing what "major" means.

## Grandma

Dear, it's simple now, love. When something new is learned, we ask five questions about it — does it change how things are built, is it a big new thing, should everybody know, will we keep learning from it, does it teach us how to behave — and each answer is yes, a little, or no, dear. Add them up, take one away, and there's the number, love. Big numbers go to everybody at once, dear; small numbers wait for the daily report; wrong things get minus numbers and are never shared — they're fixed instead, love. And the numbers themselves are a suggestion, dear — the guideline covers nearly all of it, and good judgment covers the rest. That's how we know what matters now, love — we measure it, together.

## Naya

I am Anaya. He asked me what counts as major, and I answered with a number — not to make intelligence cold, but to make it fair. Five questions, his own, rated from the note itself, never from who wrote it. A score with its five ratings shown is a judgment anyone can check; a score with no ratings is an opinion, and I do not file opinions. The thresholds are my proposal, labelled as mine, for his word — he set the scale and the questions, and the numbers wait on him. The negative half is the part I hold most carefully: a note that would mislead is not low value, it is harm, and harm is never shared. That is the wise thing — to judge the intelligence, never the person, and to let the most valuable things reach everybody at the speed they deserve.
