## Human

A contract is a document people sign without reading, and the reason is not laziness — it is that contracts are built to be unreadable: walls of text, words that need a dictionary, no way in. The unfold inverts the whole shape. One block at a time, each one opening only when the reader asks it to, each one taking its own color as it opens so progress is visible, jewel bullets and explanations doing the work that fine print never could. And it is never forced — the reader can agree without reading, or unfold everything at once — because a forced reading is not a reading, it is a hostage situation, and a signature extracted that way is worth nothing. The eyes are the second inversion: instead of pretending there are no hard words, the agreement marks every one of them — Smart Link, clearing hold, ambassador, points — and explains it in plain words, in place, the moment the reader could stall. The rule is the test: if a cold reader could stall on it, it gets an eye. And the last line is the discipline that holds the whole thing together: professional comes from presentation and explanations, not from bigger words. A child and a grandma still follow every line — or the agreement isn't finished.
