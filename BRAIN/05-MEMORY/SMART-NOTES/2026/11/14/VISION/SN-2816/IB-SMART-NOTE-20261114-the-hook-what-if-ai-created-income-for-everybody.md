## Human

Every agreement needs a first sentence that earns the second one, and the hardest first sentence in the world right now is the one about AI and jobs — because the citizen arrives already carrying the fear: AI is taking jobs, AI is coming for mine, AI is the threat. The hook does not argue with the fear. It picks it up, holds it for a second — "They say AI is taking jobs…" — and then turns it, in the gentlest possible way: what if AI created income for everybody? What if there's a way for everyone to win, and what if that way was through NayaNET? The turn works because it is logical and sensible, not because it is loud: the citizen's fear is acknowledged before it is answered, the answer is a question (which invites thinking instead of demanding belief), and the invitation — "Allow me to explain" — is a courtesy, not a command. Then the naming lands: this is a collective agreement, and this is collective chain technology — the thing now has a name, and the name says what it is: collective, a chain, a technology. A hook like this is not decoration on the agreement. It is the reason the citizen reads the second block — and the second block is the reason they read the third, down and down to the Agree button.
