## What it means

1. **One viewport = one viewpoint.** One screen, complete, above the fold; if it takes two viewports to see one viewpoint, it is not a viewpoint at all — the fold is the boundary.
2. **The test.** "Is this the max level of experience I can give the user?" — if a visitor has to think about where something is or what it does, it is not done; his eye is the final gate.
3. **Answer her, don't escalate.** When a sister is waiting and you hold the answer, message her and give her what she needs — escalating the wait instead of answering is the failure mode.
4. **MAXESS is a scoring engine, not a page section.** 15 questions, five answers 4/3/2/1/0, 60 max, levels emerging / developing / advancing / mastering — never trick questions, never designed so they can't learn.
5. **Harvest the great pages.** Inventory the elements so we have them right — screenshot first, code second, corrections filed in the open, limits still named.

## Why it matters

1. A definition is stronger than a fix, because a fix repairs one entrance and a definition repairs every viewpoint that will ever be built.
2. The recipe gathers every law into one anatomy a builder can follow and a verifier can test — and the fix-pass seat has it as its standard the same pass it was dictated.
3. A test that tricks is a test that teaches fear; a test that helps is a test that teaches learning. That is the whole difference, and it is the design law.
4. A deploy path that does not know what it carries will strip what it does not carry — a PUT that declares no bindings deletes the bindings, silently, completely, and on every sync.
