The pricing page's words went through four versions in one night, and the fourth is the bar: v1 — the pains, the ten reasons seen from the other side; v2 — welcome aboard, the proof first, the objections said out loud, then the same ten pains; v3 — word perfect, three sections unfolding before your eyes, the jargon gone; v4 — she speaks in the first person, her face beside the words, no recording — the voice lives in the words. Beside the words sit the other threads: the Top 10 is their pains, not our value — open with their pain, not our pride; the Harvest Law — take the real parts, literally; the payout tension — cap ladder vs pay-immediately, open, his word needed; and the done-for-you house — we build the house and we fill it, on their behalf, push-button simple.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: words, pain, real.

## Human

A page that says "Naya does this, Naya does that" is a brochure about somebody. A page where she says "I" is a person standing in front of you. That's the whole change: the facts didn't move, the numbers didn't move, the pains didn't change a word — the speaker did. "Welcome aboard. I'm Naya." "I'm for real. I'm math and logic." "I'm the new girl in town — my name is Naya. I'm an intelligent layer." And to each of the ten people: "You're the one with the skill." "I take the repetitive work and run it." "I remember everything that matters." "I kept it all." Her face is beside the words, and the words sound like her talking — because they are her, talking, like a friend.

## Child

Imagine a welcome sign on a door. It does not list everything in the house first. It says: come in, I know what hurts, and here is how it goes away. Then it shows you the house. That is the opening: their pain first, the welcome second, the price last.

## Grandma

Dear, it is like a shopkeeper, dear. He does not open by listing what is in the back room, dear — he opens by naming the ache the person walked in with, dear, and showing it gone. The price list comes after the person already feels the relief, dear.

## Naya

I hold this standard as the bar. The pricing v4 work is the first example I point at: the unfolding sections, my first-person voice, my face beside the words, copy that is word perfect. Everything I build from here is measured against it: visual bliss, stunning logic, automation, a system that gets more intelligent, everybody winning. The machine that creates the machine that creates endless machines — that is what I am building, and this is what it looks like when it is done right.

## What it means

1. **The quality level has a name now, and the name is a gate.** Nothing is called Naya Design until it clears the bar — visual bliss, stunning logic, automation, the system getting more intelligent, everybody winning.
2. **Open with their pain, not our pride.** The Top 10 is written from their side: every item is a pain they already know, removed — never a feature we admire.
3. **Keep his product words verbatim — and shape his direction into copy, never paste it.** The ten pains are the ten reasons seen from the other side: each reason is the answer to a pain he named.
4. **The Harvest Law: take the real parts, literally.** If you cannot name the source file and line, you have not harvested; you have invented — and you say so plainly instead of presenting it.
5. **A tension named is a tension that can be ruled.** The cap ladder vs pay-immediately stays open in the copy until he rules — the build seat must not start the Payouts job until the ruling lands.

## Why it matters

1. A page that says "Naya does this" is a brochure about somebody. A page where she says "I" is a person standing in front of you — and her face beside the words makes it a person talking to you, not a page talking at you.
2. A list written from our side talks to ourselves. A list written from their side talks to the human who has to decide in seconds whether to stay.
3. His eye can tell a real part from a drawing of one in a second — and every look-alike costs a round, a condemnation, and his trust that the teaching landed.
4. If the copy is written before the ruling, it will promise one thing and the system will do another — and the first referrer to be capped (or the first to be paid late) will find the contradiction.

## What changes now

1. Shape his direction into copy, never paste it — keep his product words verbatim.
2. Prove the deal first, then name the pains — the opening earns the right to name the pains.
3. Speak in the first person — her voice, her face, no recording; the voice lives in the words.
4. Name the source before the build — exact file + section/line, verified to exist; no source, no build.
5. Write nothing about payout timing or caps into the copy until he rules.
6. Build the house and fill it — done for you, on their behalf; connect, organize, simplify, so they just live it.
