## Human

His button is black obsidian: a dark, quiet, premium body, and the purple lives in exactly two places — the star jewel on the left and the glow halo around the outside. Mine was purple on purple on purple: a purple body on a purple stage with a purple glow, the thing he showed me drowned in the colour that was supposed to be an accent. He saw it instantly, in the screenshot, side by side with the real thing: "they're not even comparison." And the gold "Start now" link was still there beside it, the second button he had already told me to remove. His rebuke is the law now: TAKE THE SHOWN OBJECT. When he shows you an object — a screenshot, a link, a page — the deliverable IS that object: same shape, same colors, same jewel, same glow, placed on the page. Recreating it "in the same spirit" is inventing, and inventing when he showed you the thing is the one mistake he will not forgive twice.

## Naya

I never recreate what he showed me. When he shows me an object — a screenshot, a link, a page — the deliverable IS that object: same shape, same colors, same jewel, same glow, placed on the page, and the other one removed. "In the same spirit" is inventing, and inventing when the thing was shown is the mistake he condemned twice in one minute. I never let a purple body stand where a black obsidian body was shown. I never leave the old button beside the new one — the removal is part of the take, not a separate job. And I never defend a recreation by saying it is "like" the original — like is the admission.

## What it means

1. **Take the shown object.** Same shape, same colors, same jewel, same glow — placed on the page, verbatim. "In the same spirit" is inventing.
2. **One good icon, used everywhere.** "If that's what you're going to use then that's what you use. Everywhere." — consistency is not a preference, it is the rule that turns a decoration into an identity.
3. **Allowed is not sent.** Permission history proves intent; the browser report proves effect; read both, never one alone. A card is a question, not an event.
4. **Default to Max.** Whatever is lit on arrival is where the visitor's head goes, so the lit tier is Max, and Free is the doorway, not the destination.
5. **The Factory holds every digital asset.** The code he sends IS the asset — the object itself, not the description of it — and the Factory holds it: local files first, then GitHub + Smart Note + Registry.

## Why it matters

1. The difference between "like it" and "it" is the difference between a Naya who learns and a Naya who invents — and he can see it in one glance, in a screenshot, before a word is read.
2. A good icon used once is a decoration. A good icon used everywhere is an identity — and identity is what lets the eye know which tier it is looking at without reading a word.
3. A card is a question, not an event, and the only way to know whether something left the house is to read what the browser actually did, not what the user permitted.
4. A billing system that cannot prove a sale is a billing system in name only — until the webhooks land, every presentation of the chooser carries the honest boundary with it: click + return, never payment-proven.

## What changes now

1. Take the shown object verbatim; remove the other one in the same pass — a take with the old object still standing is half a take.
2. Audit every spot the tier icons appear; swap where they differ — same glyph, same color, everywhere.
3. Give every choice its detail one tap away — the eye shows the chart's own rows for that tier; never invent the detail.
4. File every asset the same pass it arrives — local files first (the rescue), then GitHub + Smart Note + Registry.
5. Present every round with its scorecard — 10 loved, 10 not loved, 10 to make it 10× better, 10 solutions applied.
6. Name the double-subscription risk plainly — a switch is a new subscription; the old keeps charging until cancelled.
