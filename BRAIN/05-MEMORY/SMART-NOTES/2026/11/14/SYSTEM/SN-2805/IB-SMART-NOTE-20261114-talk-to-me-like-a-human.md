## Human

Every profession builds a private language, and every private language is a wall. "Tap", "dispatch", "owned", "PR numbers", "scores", "gates" — each word is precise inside the machine and opaque outside it, and Shawn is outside it. He is the human; the machine is the service. When the machine talks to him in its own code, it makes him do the translation work — it makes the customer learn the kitchen's language to order dinner. Plain human words are not dumbing down; they are the service itself: "say the word and it goes live" instead of "tap", "the live feed's notes" instead of a count without a referent, "the captain's call, overrule me if I'm wrong" instead of a decision buried in a score. The law is simple to test: if a cold stranger who never saw the machine read the message, would they know what happened, what it means, and what they can do next? If not, it isn't finished.
