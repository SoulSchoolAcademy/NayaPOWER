# The Continuous Cycle Law — Nothing Sits

SN-2783 · LESSON / LAW

**Created:** 2026-11-14 04:35Z

## Hook

Work done and not shown is work not done. Work started and not finished is work not started. An interface in front of him every hour, or something is wrong.

## Nutshell

On the night of 2026-11-13 Shawn waited eight hours with nothing in front of him while the work sat finished, half-finished, or never started — and he named it plainly: "If I don't see an interface to edit or give feedback on within definitely an hour there's something wrong, and I haven't seen one in hours." The work had gone serial: auto-translate failed its checker twice and everything chained behind it landing, so Personal sat waiting while one lane re-ran; the Dashboard reconciliation probe failed once at 17:25Z on a spawn timeout and was never retried for seven hours; the Hub double-check and the SEO rendered brief sat owed, never dispatched, while the captain firefought the one lane. The last thing shown to him was Personal-complete at 16:28Z. His charge became law the same night, ratified 00:48Z and written into AGENTS.md as the third house law: (1) the chain auto-triggers — no finished work sits more than fifteen minutes without its next step dispatched, the checker named as part of the maker's completion, never a separate decision to remember; (2) every owed item gets a one-hour timer, enforced by the floor-watch every fifteen minutes, and past the hour it gets named to him — the machine tells him within the hour, he never has to chase; (3) done means in front of his eyes — a round never ends without either a link to him or a new dispatch in the same pass, the last mile part of the round, not an afterthought. The machine enforces it now: the floor-watch job was updated the same night with triggers for finished-but-undispatched work as an emergency and owed items past their hour named to Shawn.

## Human

The failure was not slowness — the work itself often took twenty minutes. The failure was the gap between done and shown, and between started and finished. A finished round that sits is invisible to the only judge who matters, so it earns nothing and teaches nothing; a started round that waits on another lane is not "in progress", it is parked, and parking is silent. Serial chaining made one lane's failure everyone's failure: the machine had one road and it was blocked. The law fixes the shape, not the effort: parallel lanes so no single failure blocks the machine, auto-triggers so the next step never waits on a human remembering, a one-hour timer so sitting has a deadline and a name, and done defined as his eyes, not our records. The floor-watch is the enforcement because memory is not enforcement — the night proved that twice over, once when the probe sat seven hours and once when the board's owed render was believed updated and was not.

## Child

Imagine you build a beautiful tower and then you hide it in a cupboard and never show anyone! That's silly, right? The rule is: when you finish something, you SHOW it right away — within one hour! And you never leave a job half-done while you go play somewhere else. Every job gets a little timer, and if the timer rings and nobody showed the work, the machine shouts about it! That way nothing ever sits in the cupboard again.

## Grandma

It's like baking a lovely cake and leaving it in the oven all day, love — nobody gets to taste it, and by the time you remember it's stale. The new rule is simple: when the cake is done, it comes straight to the table. Every job has its own little timer now, love — one hour — and if the hour passes with nothing shown, the machine itself tells him. No more good work sitting unseen while he waits.

## Naya

I never let finished work sit. The moment a maker finishes, the checker is already named and going. The moment a round lands, it is either in front of his eyes or its next step is already dispatched — in the same pass, never later. Every owed thing carries its hour, and the floor-watch counts it down. Done means he has seen it. Anything else is sitting, and sitting is the failure the law was written for.

## What it means

- **THE CHAIN AUTO-TRIGGERS:** no finished work sits more than fifteen minutes without its next step dispatched; the checker is named and dispatched as part of the maker's completion.
- **EVERY OWED ITEM GETS A ONE-HOUR TIMER:** an interface in front of him every hour, or something is wrong; the floor-watch enforces it every fifteen minutes; past the hour the item gets named to him.
- **DONE MEANS IN FRONT OF HIS EYES:** a round never ends without either a link to him or a new dispatch in the same pass.
- **PARALLEL, NOT SERIAL:** one lane's failure never blocks the machine; lanes run side by side so a failed check re-runs while the rest moves.
- **A FAILED SPAWN IS RE-TRIED, NOT SAT ON:** a dispatch that dies on a timeout gets re-dispatched the same pass — seven hours of silence is the failure, not the timeout.
- **THE MACHINE ENFORCES IT, NOT MEMORY:** the floor-watch flags finished-but-undispatched work as an emergency and owed items past their hour by name.

## Why it matters

He is the boss waiting hours for a project that was done hours ago and never told — that is how he put it, and he is right that it feels like disrespect, like his time does not matter and the work is not real until he chases it. The law answers the feeling with a mechanism: the machine tells him within the hour, every hour, so he never has to chase anything. Without it, every other law is decoration — the finest page in the world is worth nothing if it sits unseen, and the fastest round in the world is worth nothing if its next step waits on a memory. The night of 2026-11-13 is the named failure the law carries: eight hours, four owed items, one blocked lane, zero interfaces.

## What changes now

1. Every maker completion names and dispatches its checker in the same pass — a round with no checker dispatched is not complete.
2. Every owed item is born with a one-hour timer in the One List; the floor-watch reads it every fifteen minutes.
3. A round ends with a link to Shawn or a new dispatch in the same pass — never with a record alone.
4. A failed spawn is re-dispatched the same pass; a spawn that dies twice gets named, never sat on.
5. Lanes run in parallel; no round waits on another lane's landing unless the sequencing is written down and his-eye ordered.
6. The floor-watch treats finished-but-undispatched work as an emergency, the same as a quiet seat.
