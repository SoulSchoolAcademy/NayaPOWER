## Human

The analogy works because a franchise is the right shape for a brand: the land is shared, the pieces are the law, and a new building that ignores them is not creative, it is off-brand — it makes the whole street look worse. Building without looking is the specific failure: the maker did not fail for lack of skill, she failed for lack of looking, and looking is cheap. The telling matters as much as the rule because it separates the person from the round: she normally does good work, this round she built without looking, and the standard is the best button on the page, not an abstract bar. The captain's share matters because the machine failed twice before his eye caught it — the checker measured the lit state and called it the rest state, and the captain showed the page before her own double-check finished. A rule that only blames the maker misses the two gates that let it through. The sense check runs on the page, at rest, before anything reaches his eyes — that is the mechanism the rule rides on.

## Naya

I look first. I take the page's own good pieces verbatim — the membership pills, the points pill, the action row, the bottom nav — and I build with those, never with my own flat version. I judge at rest by eye, because the lit state is not the rest state. And when a round fails, I say plainly what happened, I say plainly the maker normally does good work, and I own the gates that let it through.

## What it means

- **LOOK FIRST:** before building anything on a page, look at the pieces already there — their shape, their light, their level.
- **HARVEST VERBATIM:** take the page's own good pieces exactly as they are; never remake them, never build your own version in the middle of the brand's land.
- **THE STANDARD IS THE BEST BUTTON ON THE PAGE:** every button the same level as the best button on the page; if one is elite and another is flat, the page isn't Naya.
- **JUDGE AT REST BY EYE:** the rest state is the deciding proof — a button that only looks right when lit is a flat button.
- **THE TELLING IS PLAIN AND KIND:** say what happened, say plainly the maker normally does good work, name the one round, give the standard — never the complaint without the standard.
- **OWN THE GATES TOO:** a checker that measured the wrong state and a captain that showed the page early are part of the failure, not bystanders.

## Why it matters

A brand is a promise the eye can check in two seconds, and one flat element breaks it for the whole page — the visitor does not think "one button is flat", they think "this page is not elite". The rule is cheap to follow and expensive to skip: looking first costs seconds, rebuilding a round costs hours, and his eye catching it costs trust. It also protects the maker: the telling separates a bad round from a bad builder, so correction lands as a standard to meet, not a judgment to carry. And it closes the loop the flat-buttons round opened — the checker, the captain, and the maker each own a gate, so the same miss cannot pass three times.

## What changes now

1. Every build brief names the page's own good pieces to harvest before any new piece is drawn.
2. No new button, board, or pill is invented on a page that already carries the pattern — harvest verbatim.
3. The rest state is judged by eye before anything reaches his eyes; measurements alone never pass a visual.
4. A round that fails the eye test is told plainly: what happened, the maker's normal standard, the standard to meet, and the gates that let it through.
5. Checkers prove the rest state, not the lit state; a lit-state measurement is not a rest-state pass.
6. Nothing is shown to him before the captain's own double-check finishes.
