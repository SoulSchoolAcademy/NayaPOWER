This is the difference between an absence of evidence and evidence of absence, stated as a working law. A probe runs in an environment; when that environment cannot reach the thing being probed — the VM cannot reach Supabase — the probe's silence says nothing about the live system. It says something about the probe. "It cannot appear" is a claim about the world that a blind probe has no right to make — and when it is wrong, it is wrong in exactly the direction that hides a real defect from the person who needs to see it.

## Why it matters

Every verification in the machine runs in some environment, and every environment has a blindness. If probes are allowed to turn their blindness into certainty, real defects get certified away — the banner would have stayed "cannot appear" while Shawn stared at it on his screen. That is the exact failure the mission command forbids: being wrong with confidence, in the place where being right matters most. The law protects the whole verification chain: checkers, captains, and makers must all be able to trust that a verdict's "cannot" means the world cannot, not that the probe could not see. And the correction habit — own it, write it, re-diagnose — is what keeps one wrong claim from becoming a settled falsehood in the record.
