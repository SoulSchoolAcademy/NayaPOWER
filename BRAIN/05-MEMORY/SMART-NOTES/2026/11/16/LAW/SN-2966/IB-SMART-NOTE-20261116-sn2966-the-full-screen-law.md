## Human

A pop-up that uses a corner of the screen wastes the screen and insults the citizen's eyes: tiny text, hard to read, while the rest of the screen sits empty and black. The whole screen is there — use it. Max experience is not a luxury for big screens; it is the standard for every screen, regardless of how much information is on it. "Regardless of the information on the screen" is the key: a pop-up with three lines deserves the full screen just as much as a pop-up with three hundred, because the citizen's eyes are the same size either way. A small pop-up is a fail, the same as a flat button.

## Child

Imagine you open a present, but it's in a teeny tiny box in the corner of a giant room, and you have to squint to see it! Silly, right? The room is YOURS — open the present right in the middle, big and beautiful, so you can see everything! That's the law: pop-ups open big, almost the whole screen, with a little X to close them.
