## Human

Every system has a front door, and most of them are menus — lists of features, none of which is the thing itself. A calendar is different: it is time, and time is the one thing every citizen already understands. Click a day and you are IN that day — its dashboard, its feed, its news, its notes, its events — because a day is the natural unit of a life. The visitor view is the same door from the other side: not your world, but a brief of it — book a time, see what's coming up, send a message. That is how the system spreads: "communicate with me here" replaces email and Facebook not by being better at messages, but by being a place with a door you can point someone to. And "the feature everybody remembers" is a design brief, not a boast: each day lights up and feels alive, the menu is an extraordinary presentation of beautiful buttons and boards. If it does not look extraordinary on the screen, it is not done — however correct the timeline is underneath.

## Why it matters

The calendar is the answer to "how do they enter the system?" — not through a dashboard, not through a Hub, but through a day. It is also the answer to "how does it spread?" — a citizen points someone at their calendar and says "communicate with me here." Everything else in the system hangs off the day: the schedule, the wisdom, the reports, the feeds. If the calendar is ordinary, the system is ordinary; if the calendar is the feature everybody remembers, the system is remembered.
