## Human

A page that shows everything at once is a document. A page that unfolds one beat at a time is a presentation — and a presentation is a machine: each beat arrives, fits the screen, waits for the click, and the next one zooms in. The front page failed on all three counts at once: the face was bigger than the screen (the object did not fit its stage), the slides were illegible at phone width (the content could not be read), and scrolling revealed everything (there was no unfolding at all — just a long page pretending to be a presentation). The law fixes all three with one shape: one beat, almost full screen, perfectly formatted, button-folded, no scroll-reveal. The mechanic is the feeling of it: the image dials in as you arrive, the button glows beside it, the next page zooms into your viewport. That is what "mechanical, like a presentation, not just a web page" means — and it is the same law as the Unfold/Matrix Law (SN-2911), applied to the front page itself. "Push the envelope" is the bar above the law: not completely different, but so smooth, so beautiful, so well done that you have nothing but respect.
