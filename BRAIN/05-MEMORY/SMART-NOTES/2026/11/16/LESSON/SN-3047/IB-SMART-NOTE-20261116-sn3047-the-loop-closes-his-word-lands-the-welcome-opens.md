## Why it matters

1. A view you cannot leave is a trap, not a page — and a way back that doesn't work is worse than no way back at all, because it promises and fails.
2. "Implement right away" closes the gap fast action leaves open: the instruction doesn't sit, it moves — no sitting, no queue, no tomorrow — and naming the main thing keeps a fast day from becoming a busy day.
3. Without names, the bar is just a number line; with names, the bar is a story the citizen can tell about themselves — and a story they can tell is a story they come back to.
4. A single edit path is a guess about how the citizen thinks; three paths is the admission that citizens think differently — and the system serves all of them.
5. Without the test, the demographics list grows by accretion — every nice question gets added, and the citizen ends up interrogated. A question that matches nothing is a question that spies.
6. Every onboarding flow in the world asks questions; almost none of them says why. This one says why in the first breath: we ask so we can match, we match so we can show, we show so you can create, and it all ends in people.
7. A citizen who can't find home is lost in their own world. The Hub as the visible center means nobody ever has to ask "how do I get back?"
8. Mixed mail is lost mail. Separating by world at the front door means the citizen never has to sort, filter, or wonder — the page they opened already did the sorting.
9. A letter that is hard to read is a letter that is never read — and the collective agreement is the first thing a citizen signs, the promise the whole of NayaNET stands on.
