## Why it matters

1. A system is learned once and recognized everywhere — or it is not a system at all. The reader, the mirror, the clone trick and the scorecard are all the same promise from different angles: one way of showing things, one way of spreading wins, one way of judging pages. A citizen who learns the unfold in the deck already knows the reader in the certificate; a builder who learns the clone trick on a name already knows depth on a button.
2. The entering laws are the promise the showing laws make. "We got you" is only true if every way in is genuinely extraordinary — the name-only entry not a shortcut, the visual not a lesser path for people who don't read, the free walk not a dead end for people who don't pay. The moment any way in is second-class, the front door is a funnel with better manners.
3. The word is the interface before the interface. Calling a person's passion a "space" makes them translate themselves into our language before they can even say what they love — and a system that misunderstands what you love cannot match you with people who love it too. "Thing" is the word the citizen already uses; using it is the system speaking their language.
