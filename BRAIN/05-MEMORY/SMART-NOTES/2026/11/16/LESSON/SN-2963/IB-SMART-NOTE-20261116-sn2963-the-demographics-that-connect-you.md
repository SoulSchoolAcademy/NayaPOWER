## Human

This is the onboarding promise made visible: what you tell the system is how the system finds people like you. The more you tell it, the more it finds; if you don't set it, they can't find you; if you don't want to be found, don't set it. Every question is the citizen's choice — on or off, entered or skipped — and the feed follows: people into those things as connections, content about those things in the feed, people recommended. And the content source is the same choice: public content on a topic, setting or filter, just NayaNET content, or both. That is the customizable internet: it seeks to optimize for you, and you choose what "for you" means.

## What it means

- **THE FULL LIST:** where in the world (country, region, town) · church or organization · work · female or male · language · interests (scroll or type) · age · favourite brands · music · favourite people · favourite books.
- **EACH ONE ON OR OFF:** a menu shows them all; you go through them, enter what you want, turn on what you want, turn off what you don't.
- **WHAT IT DOES:** people into those things show up in your feed as connections; content about those things shows up in your feed; people get recommended to you.
- **THE CONTENT SOURCE:** public content on a topic, setting or filter — or just NayaNET content — or both. Your setting.
- **THE RULE:** if you don't set it, they can't find you. If you don't want to be found, don't set it.

## Why it matters

Auto-Match is only as good as what the citizen tells it — and a citizen who is asked everything at once, with no choice, tells it nothing. The menu with on/off switches is what makes the telling safe: each question is a separate, small, reversible choice. And the content-source setting is the same idea for the feed: the citizen chooses what the internet is for them, instead of the internet choosing for them. That is the customizable internet, and it only works if the citizen is the one customizing.
