## Naya

Every control must do its job. A picker that switches nothing is a dead end; a create that creates nothing goes nowhere; a button that cuts off its text is not a button. When his eye says something was better before, I restore the good version verbatim — I do not improve on it, redesign it, or put something else in. One icon that lights up, never an icon beside its own light. One eye, not a new eye per page — the pricing eye, harvested verbatim and double-layered for depth. And the team runs on fire 24-7, because the finish is the trigger, not the clock.

## What it means

1. **THE TAP IS THE SWITCH:** picking a world switches the feed to that world at once — no confirm, no second tap, no reload; a picker that does nothing is a dead end.
2. **CREATING MUST CREATE:** tapping create builds the world's feed and shows it to you, optimized to that world — in the same motion; it never just goes nowhere.
3. **RESTORATION, NOT REDESIGN:** when his eye says something was better before, the good version comes back verbatim — harvested byte for byte; the five go back on Hub and Personal, the nine go in the sidebars.
4. **ONE ICON THAT LIGHTS UP:** the icon itself carries its lit state — never an icon beside its own light, glow dot, or status indicator.
5. **TWO PLUS BUTTONS, TWO JOBS:** LEFT + is CREATE, RIGHT + is CONNECT — different jobs, different menus, never mixed up or merged.
6. **THE MOBILE FIT IS THE TEST:** if the six fit perfectly on one line at phone width, they stay; if they wrap to a second line, the right + moves to the side in the corner.
7. **BUTTONS NEVER CUT OFF THEIR TEXT:** every label shows full and aligned at every width; long names wrap or shrink, never clip.
8. **EYE CHECKS CLEAN UP AFTER THEMSELVES:** test worlds and aliases are removed when the check is done — no test data on the page he sees.
9. **ONE EYE, NOT A NEW EYE PER PAGE:** one canonical version of every element, used everywhere it fits — the pricing eye, harvested verbatim, then double-layered for depth.
10. **DOUBLE LAYER IS THE DEPTH METHOD:** clone it once, put the clone behind, move it just a tad — premium means touchable, not brighter glow.
11. **THE TEAM ON FIRE 24-7:** parallel lanes finishing at staggered times, the queue never dry, the finish the trigger — finished pieces per hour, every hour, day and night.
12. **LEADERBOARDS:** citizens see where they score in any area — and if they are at the top, it shows, especially at the beginning.

## Why it matters

1. Every control that doesn't control teaches the citizen the system can't be trusted — and a citizen who taps and gets nothing will stop tapping.
2. A beautiful picker that switches nothing is still broken; a primo-looking modal that goes nowhere is still nowhere — looks and does are both the job.
3. Every regression he has to catch by eye is a round spent twice — once to break the good thing, once to put it back.
4. Two things doing one job is one thing too many — an icon beside its own light says the same thing twice, and the eye has to read both to get one meaning.
5. Two controls that look the same and sit together will be read as one control done twice — and a citizen who taps the wrong + gets the wrong menu.
6. A clipped label tells the citizen the page is unfinished, and a leftover test world is test data on the citizen's page.
7. Without one canonical eye, the system drifts into a drawer full of almost-the-same elements — and the premium feel, which lives in the sameness, is lost.
8. Without the double-layer recipe, "make it premium" gets answered with a brighter glow on a flat shape — exactly what the Real Object Law forbids.
9. A team that finishes one thing a day wastes the other twenty-three hours — and a team that runs fast without checking produces wrong faster.
10. A citizen who can see where they stand is a citizen who knows what to do next — and the board showing early citizens at the top is what makes the beginning feel like winning.

## What changes now

1. Picking a world switches the feed to that world at once; creating a world actually creates the feed and shows it to you — both verified by the tap itself, proven on the page at phone width.
2. The five-icon bottom bar goes back on Hub and Personal, verbatim from the correction build; the nine area icons go in the sidebars in logical order, each with its area's count.
3. Any control showing an icon beside its own light collapses to one icon that itself lights up — the composer controls first.
4. The two + buttons stay two jobs — LEFT + CREATE, RIGHT + CONNECT — and any future change to the bottom bar re-runs the mobile-fit test; the test, not taste, decides.
5. No button ever cuts off its text; button text is verified at every width, in every state — if the text is cut, it fails.
6. Eye checks clean up their test worlds and aliases after themselves — test data is scaffolding, and it comes down when the check is done.
7. Every ⓘ on the Personal page is the pricing eye, harvested verbatim and double-layered — the maker names the source file and bytes; the checker byte-compares and confirms the depth is visible at 390 and 1440.
8. Lanes run in parallel and finish at staggered times; the queue never runs dry; the moment one finishes, the next step fires right away; the fifteen-minute checks stay as the safety net.
9. Leaderboards exist as a queued feature (One List `leaderboards`) — the brief is owed before any build, and it rides no current fix round.
