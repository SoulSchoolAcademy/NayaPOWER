## Why it matters

1. A quota wall looks like a system failure and is actually a plan limit — the distinction matters, because the fix is a purchase, not a rebuild, and the next time a 429 appears, the first question is the plan, not the code.
2. Without the Feed Law, every new card type finds its way onto the feed and the feed stops being a feed — three things is a test any builder can run in a glance.
3. A queue is a shelf, and a shelf is where finished work goes to wait — every hold is a decision the machine made for him without asking, and waiting is what exhausts him.
4. A template half-finished is worse than a page half-finished: every clone inherits the gap, and the gap compounds across every page sampled from it.
5. A "view your page" that opens a demo is a lie the citizen discovers in one tap — routing both links to the updated Personal makes the promise true by construction.
6. Without the back-button law, every new view is a gamble: does this one let me back out? A citizen who can always go back will always try the next thing; a citizen who got stuck once will hesitate forever.
7. A product without an anatomy is a pile of rounds: every seat builds what it thinks the page is, and the page comes out different every time.
