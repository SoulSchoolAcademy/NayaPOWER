## Human

The relay was built to protect him from unfinished work, and it became the thing that kept finished work from him. The visual agreement sat 10 hours. The sidebar round took hours. Not because the making was slow — because each verified step waited for the next queue, and the delivery waited for the whole chain, and the chain waited to be bundled with another round. Verification is not the delay; holding is. Fast action keeps the verification and drops the hold: the captain looks and the delivery goes in the same pass the report lands. He reacts, we fix, it comes back — that loop IS the work, not the overhead around it. "It's exhausting" is the diagnosis: waiting is the cost, not the making.

- **THE RELAY STAYS:** maker → checker → captain's eye still verifies — but the captain's eye and the delivery happen the same pass the report lands. No holding, no bundling.
- **NOT SUPERSEDED:** the relay itself, the Self-Check Law, the Five Gates, and the protected gates all stand — only the holding goes.

## Why it matters

A queue is a shelf, and a shelf is where finished work goes to wait. The machine's speed was never the making — the maker was fast, the checker was fast — it was the handoff between them, and the delivery held for the chain. Every hold is a decision the machine made for him without asking: "this can wait." Fast action takes that decision back: nothing can wait, because waiting is what exhausts him. The test of the mode is the clock between his word and his screen — minutes, not hours — and the proof is the same pass, not the next round.
