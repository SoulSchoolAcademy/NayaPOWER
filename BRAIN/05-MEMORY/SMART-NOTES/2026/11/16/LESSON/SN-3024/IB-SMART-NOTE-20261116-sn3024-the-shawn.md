## Why it matters

1. "Are those the highest level of buttons we can offer?" is the right question, because the answer is a test, not an opinion: the bottom icons are the standard, and anything below them is not finished. A dot-only button fails the test in the open — it cannot say what it is, so the citizen has to guess, and guessing is not ten-star service. The same test, applied to photos, is respect for the citizen's own image: a forced crop is the system deciding what matters in someone else's photo. Naming the size, giving the editor, keeping the shape — that is the system handing the choice back. The standard is one language, everywhere, or it is not a standard.
2. Every new button shape is a new dialect, and a system with ten dialects is a system nobody can read at a glance. "STOP making new buttons" is not a preference, it is the harvest law applied to the smallest unit: the canonical form exists precisely so that a maker never has to invent one. And the five-pill limit is the same discipline applied to choice: the rail is not a menu of everything, it is a showcase of what matters most. Show five, centered, official — and the reader trusts the whole page, because the top of the page already told the truth about it.
3. Every other calendar is a forward-looking tool — a place to put things you have not done yet. This one is a memory that stays alive: any day, any world, what happened, in one click. That is a different product, and it is the product a wisdom system would build — because wisdom is what you learn from the days behind you, and a system that cannot show you yesterday cannot teach you anything. The day menu's order is the teaching: the network first, then your worlds, then your own day — from the whole, down to the one. And the celebration on top of it — Daily Wisdom, the Smart Note of the Day, hourly reports — is the system doing what it exists to do: putting intelligence in, and sharing wisdom with the world.
4. Every verification system drifts toward checking what is easy to check — the fixture, the programmatic fire, the byte count — and away from what matters, which is what the human sees. The drift is invisible because the checks keep passing. The only cure is a rule that cannot be gamed by a fixture: the eye reads the live page, adds the numbers, clicks the slot, and the page is whole only when the eye says so. That is why the double-check exists, and why its FAIL outranks a mechanism PASS — the mechanism checks the machine, the eye checks the experience, and the experience is the product.
