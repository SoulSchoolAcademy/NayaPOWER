## What it means

1. **ULTIMATE ANSWER FIRST:** before judging anything, name what it is ultimately trying to do — the answer, not the activity. Then the three-part test: finished? done right? in optimal time?
2. **ACTIVITY WITHOUT FINISHING IS A FAIL:** no matter how high the count. Completions per hour is the metric; dispatches per hour is vanity.
3. **IF THE BRIEF DIDN'T INSTRUCT IT, IT STAYS:** the ship-gate names what was already on the page and confirms every piece is still there or was instructed away, in writing. Silent subtraction is a fail.
4. **NOTHING HALF-DONE COMES TO HIM:** no send-in, no report, no showing of unfinished work as if it were done. A glitch in unfinished work is never surfaced as a defect.
5. **THE SIDEBAR IS THE MAP:** every item gets its own icon; as you scroll through an area, its icon lights up in that area's own color. The nine live in the sidebars in logical order, each with its area's count; the bottom bar keeps the five.
6. **RESTORE WHAT WAS GOOD:** when his eye says something was better before, the good version comes back verbatim — harvested byte for byte from the build that had it right. No improving on it, no redesigning it.
7. **ONE ICON THAT LIGHTS UP:** no control pairs an icon with a separate glow dot, status light, or indicator doing the same job. The composer controls are fixed first.
8. **TWO PLUS BUTTONS, TWO JOBS:** LEFT + CREATE, RIGHT + CONNECT — never mixed up or merged. The mobile fit decides whether the right + stays on the line or moves to the side, in the corner.
9. **THE TAP IS THE SWITCH:** picking a world switches the feed to that world at once — no confirm, no second tap, no reload. Creating a world actually creates the feed and shows it to you, optimized to that world. No button ever cuts off its text; long names wrap or shrink; eye checks clean up their test data.

## Why it matters

1. The scorecard is the only honest way to judge a machine that counts its own activity. A job that wakes often and finishes nothing looks busy and is waste; a job that wakes often and lands finished work looks the same in the count and is fine. The ultimate answer first — then finished, done right, optimal time — is what separates the two, and nothing else does.
2. Silent subtraction is the quietest failure in the machine: everything asked for is right, and something that was there is gone. The ship-gate makes it checkable — name what was there, confirm it is still there — because a loss nobody notices is a loss that ships.
3. A control that does nothing is worse than a control that is missing: a missing control says "not built yet"; a dead picker, a create button that goes nowhere, a button that cuts off its own text — each one tells him the work is done when it isn't. The tap is the switch is the test: pick it, and you are standing in the thing you picked.
