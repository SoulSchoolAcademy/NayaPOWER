## Human

The failure is sending the unfinished thing in as if it were finished — and then reporting on its defects as if they were defects in the work, when they are only defects in the incompleteness. The voice note's glitch was real, but it was the wrong thing to tell him: the text wasn't final, so the voice couldn't be final, so the glitch was a property of "not done yet," not a property of the work. Telling him about it spent his attention on a problem that would vanish the moment the work was actually finished — and worse, it asked him to care about the quality of something that wasn't ready to be judged. The rule is the same shape as the whole-page rule and do-it-right-the-first-time: the thing comes to him whole, finished, and done right — or it doesn't come to him at all. Nothing half-done comes to him, from here on. That includes defect reports: a glitch in unfinished work is never surfaced as a defect, because there is nothing to defect yet — the work is still being made. The voice stays paused until the text is final; when the text is final, the voice is made, and only then is it judged.

## What it means

- **NOTHING HALF-DONE COMES TO HIM:** work that isn't final is not sent in, not reported on, not shown as if it were done.
- **A GLITCH IN UNFINISHED WORK IS NEVER A DEFECT:** the defect belongs to the incompleteness, not to the work — it is noise, not news, and it is never surfaced as if it were a finding.
- **THE VOICE WAITS FOR THE TEXT:** the agreement's voice stays paused until the agreement text is final; when the text is final, the voice is made, and only then is it judged.
- **FINISH FIRST, THEN JUDGE:** quality is judged on finished work — never on work still being made.

## Why it matters

His attention is the scarcest resource in the machine, and a defect report on unfinished work spends it on a problem that doesn't exist yet — a problem that finishing the work would have removed for free. Worse, it trains him to doubt work that is simply unfinished, and it trains the machine to report motion instead of finishing. The rule matters because it closes the loop on the sending standard: do-it-right-the-first-time says the work arrives finished; the whole-page rule says it arrives whole; this one says it doesn't arrive at all until it is finished — not as a report, not as a defect, not as a preview of its own incompleteness. Without this note, the next unfinished piece would arrive with its own glitch report, and his eye would have to sort the real defects from the not-done-yets again. With it, unfinished work stays in the shop until it is done — and what arrives is finished.

## What changes now

1. Nothing half-done comes to him — no send-in, no report, no showing of unfinished work as if it were done.
2. A glitch in unfinished work is never surfaced as a defect — it is fixed in the shop, silently, by finishing.
3. The agreement's voice stays paused until the agreement text is final; only then is the voice made and judged.
4. Defect reports are written only against finished work — work that is ready to be judged.
5. The sending standard is now threefold: finished (this note) · whole (the whole-page rule) · done right the first time (SN-2984).
