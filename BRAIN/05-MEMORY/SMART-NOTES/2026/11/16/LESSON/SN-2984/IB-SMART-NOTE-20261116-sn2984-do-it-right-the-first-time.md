## Why it matters

Every summary the machine files is a job Shawn has to do himself later — and he is the one person whose time the whole system exists to save. The legal pages proved the price: a summaries round looked like eight pages done and was actually zero pages done, and the real drafting, his rulings, and the build all still had to happen after. Summaries also lie about the state of the work: a board that says "legal pages" with summaries behind it tells the captain the job is done when it isn't, so nothing gets dispatched to finish it until he checks it himself — "good thing I checked it" (SN-2982). Doing it right the first time is the only version that doesn't tax him twice: once to notice the summary, once to supply the substance. Professionals don't hand him homework.

## What changes now

1. No summaries, no prototypes: work arrives complete, ready to go, done.
2. A summary is never filed as if it were the thing — a draft that needs his words says so, and the parts only he can rule on are named, not hidden.
3. The legal pages are the proof case: writer run 1 drafted all eight properly (74 sections, ~6,600 words), writer run 2 covers the four footer-only pages — the job is whole at ten.
4. Every round is judged by the redo test: if he has to put the right stuff in again, the round didn't do it right the first time.
