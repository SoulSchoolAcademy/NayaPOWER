## Human

Every system re-invents itself by accident: a new page needs a long text shown, so someone designs a new way to show long text, and now there are two ways, then five, and none of them is the one that worked. Mirror success is the rule against that drift. When a tactic proves itself — beautiful, valuable, useful, innovative — it is captured and carried everywhere it fits: the unfold from the deck into the reader, the three-bar stack from Personal into the Hub, the clone trick from the name into every button and input. The test is the sense check: it has to make sense on the receiving page. But the default is reuse, not invention — harvest first, never rebuild what already works. This is the same law as cross-page learning, stated as a habit of the whole system: we mirror what succeeds, we keep applying it throughout, everything we do. A success that stays on one page is a success wasted; a success mirrored everywhere is the system getting smarter in public.

## Naya

When something works — beautiful, valuable, useful, innovative — capture it and mirror it everywhere it fits. Never invent a second way to do what already works. Success mirrored is the system getting smarter.

## What it means

- **THE RULE:** we mirror success — capture the tactics that are beautiful and successful throughout the system and keep applying them everywhere we do.
- **THE TEST:** valuable, beautiful, useful, or innovative — a tactic earns mirroring by proving itself, not by being new.
- **THE DEFAULT:** reuse, not invention — harvest first; a success that stays on one page is a success wasted.
- **THE SENSE CHECK:** it has to make sense on the receiving page — mirroring is deliberate, never a paste.
- **THE FIRST INSTANCES:** the unfold from the deck into the reader; the three-bar stack from Personal into the Hub; the clone trick from the name into every button and input.

## Why it matters

A system that re-invents itself on every page is a system nobody can learn — every page a new language, every feature a new guess. Mirroring success is how NayaNET stays one system instead of a hundred experiments: the citizen learns a tactic once and recognizes it everywhere, and the builders stop spending their skill on solved problems. It is also how the hive compounds: a win in one lane becomes a win in all of them, in public, the same pass.

## What changes now

1. Every proven tactic is captured and mirrored where it fits — the unfold, the three-bar stack, the clone trick, and the next one.
2. New work starts from what already works — harvest first, never a fresh invention for a solved shape.
3. The sense check governs the mirror — it must make sense on the receiving page.
4. A success left on one page is named as waste in the run log, not left silent.
