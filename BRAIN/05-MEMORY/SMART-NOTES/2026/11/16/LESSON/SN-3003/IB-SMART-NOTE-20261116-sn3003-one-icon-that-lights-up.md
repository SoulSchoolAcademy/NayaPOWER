## Human

Two things doing one job is one thing too many. An icon beside its own light says the same thing twice — "here I am" and "I am on" — in two separate marks, and the eye has to read both to get one meaning. One icon that lights up says it once: the icon IS the light, the light IS the icon, and the state is the shape itself. That is why it is the better presentation — not because it is simpler to build, but because it is simpler to see. The rule generalizes beyond the composer: anywhere a control pairs an icon with a separate glow dot, status light, or indicator, the pair collapses to one — the icon carries its own lit state. The composer controls are first because that is where his eye caught it — the Make-a-post controls showed little lights beside the icons, and the lights were doing the icon's job. The same law rides with the sidebar rule (SN-3000 Sidebar Icons Light As You Scroll): the sidebar icons light in their own colors; the composer icons light as themselves — in both places, the icon is the light, never the icon plus a light.

## What it means

- **ONE ICON THAT LIGHTS UP:** the icon itself carries its lit state — the icon is the light.
- **NEVER ICON AND A LIGHT:** no control pairs an icon with a separate glow dot, status light, or indicator doing the same job.
- **THE COMPOSER IS FIRST:** the Make-a-post/composer controls lead — their little lights become the icons themselves, lighting up.
- **ONE MARK, ONE MEANING:** an icon beside its own light says the same thing twice; one lit icon says it once, and the eye reads it in one glance.
- **SIBLING RULE:** the sidebar icons light in their own colors (SN-3000 Sidebar Icons Light As You Scroll) — in both places, the icon is the light.

## Why it matters

Every doubled mark is a small tax on the eye — the citizen reads two things to learn one fact, and a page full of doubled marks feels cluttered even when nothing is wrong. The rule matters because it is a presentation law, not a taste note: one lit icon is measurably cleaner than an icon plus a light, and his eye said so on the live walk. It also matters because it prevents a whole family of clutter: any seat that adds a status dot beside an icon is adding a second mark for one meaning, and this note stops it before it is built. Without it, the composer would keep its little lights, and every new control would grow its own. With it, the icon lights up — and that is the whole control.

## What changes now

1. Any control showing an icon beside its own light collapses to one icon that itself lights up.
2. The Make-a-post/composer controls are fixed first — their little lights become the icons, lighting up.
3. No new control is built with an icon plus a separate light, glow dot, or status indicator.
4. The sidebar icons light in their own colors under the same principle — the icon is the light, in both places.
5. A control is verified by eye: one mark, one meaning — if you see an icon and a light, it fails.
