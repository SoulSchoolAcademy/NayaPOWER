## Human

Scorecarding is the method underneath the re-aim. Before you judge a job, a round, or a lane, you name its ultimate answer — what it is ultimately trying to do — and then you score it against that answer and nothing else. A job whose ultimate answer is "a page lands proven right, in front of Shawn's eyes, once" is scored on whether pages land proven right, not on how many times it woke. A job whose ultimate answer is "everything Shawn teaches, distilled into the system" is scored on whether the teaching is in the system, not on how many drafts it wrote. The discipline is in the order: ultimate answer FIRST, then the three-part test — finished? done right? in optimal time? — and "optimal time" is not fastest and not slowest, it is the right time for done-right. Keeping busy is a fail even when the activity count is high, because activity is not the answer — the answer is. This is the same pass as the finishing law (SN-2996 Optimize for Finishing and Producing Value): that law says what to optimize for, this one says how to judge it. Together they are the audit's frame, applied to the audit itself first — the audit scores each area against its ultimate answer before it scores anything else — and then to every job, round, and lane on the machine.

## What it means

- **ULTIMATE ANSWER FIRST:** before judging anything, name what it is ultimately trying to do — the answer, not the activity.
- **THEN THE THREE-PART TEST:** finished? done right? in optimal time? — optimal time is the right time for done-right, not fastest, not slowest.
- **KEEPING BUSY IS A FAIL:** activity without finishing is a fail no matter how high the count.
- **THE SCORE IS THE ANSWER, NOT THE EFFORT:** a job is scored on whether its ultimate answer landed, never on how many times it woke or dispatched.
- **SIBLING LAW, SAME PASS:** Optimize for Finishing and Producing Value says what to optimize for; this one says how to judge it — the audit's frame, applied to the audit first.

## Why it matters

Without the ultimate answer first, every score is arguable — a job can always defend itself with activity ("we woke 96 times, we dispatched 12 seats") and the human has no ground to say it failed. With the ultimate answer first, the judgment is mechanical: did the page land proven right? Did the teaching reach the system? Did the work finish, done right, in optimal time? If not, it failed — whatever the count says. This matters because it is the frame the performance audit now runs on, and the frame every future audit, review, and round will be judged by. A machine that cannot say what each part is ultimately for cannot tell whether any part is working — it can only tell whether it is moving.

## What changes now

1. Every job, round, and lane states its ultimate answer first — before any score, count, or verdict.
2. Each is then judged finished / done right / optimal time against that answer.
3. Activity without finishing is recorded as a fail, no matter how high the count.
4. Wake-up and dispatch counts appear only as cost against value produced, never as the score.
5. The performance audit (AUDIT-20261116.md) runs on this frame — ultimate answer first for every area it scores.
