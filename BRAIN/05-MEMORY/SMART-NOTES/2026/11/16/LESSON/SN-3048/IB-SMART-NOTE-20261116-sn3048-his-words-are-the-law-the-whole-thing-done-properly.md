Before any seat builds anything, it names the existing working thing it is reusing — file, blob, bytes — and takes it verbatim. A seat that rebuilds what already works has failed before it starts (SN-2981).

## Why it matters

1. Every rebuild is a gamble with the thing that already worked: the new version has to re-earn everything the old one proved — and it usually earns none of them, while costing a massive amount of work.
2. A page can pass every mechanical check and still be unreadable, because readability is an experience, not a count. If the machine trusts its checks, the failure reaches his eyes.
3. Legality is trust written down, and trust is the product: a citizen signs the collective agreement to join a new digital civilization, and the legal pages are where that civilization proves it is real. One-sentence summaries prove the opposite.
4. Every summary the machine files is a job Shawn has to do himself later — and he is the one person whose time the whole system exists to save.
5. Layer-by-layer judging is how broken wholes ship: each seat passes its own layer, nobody owns the whole, and the citizen meets the project at its weakest layer.
6. The standard is now his text, and any seat that rewrites his voice to match our drafts has the hierarchy backwards. What he gave goes in literally, what he didn't give stays bracketed, and nothing is ever invented to make a page look complete.
7. A privacy promise written in words nobody understands is a promise nobody can hold us to — "authorized purposes" was a door disguised as a safeguard, and it sat inside the very page meant to prove we are legit.
8. The stop condition is real, not a slogan: the conflict held, nothing moved past him, and his word — not a seat's judgment — decided what the page says.
