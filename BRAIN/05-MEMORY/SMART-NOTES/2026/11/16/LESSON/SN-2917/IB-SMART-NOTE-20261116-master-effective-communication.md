## Human

Communication is the work, not the wrapper around it. A brief that carries the literal words is a transcription; a brief that carries the ultimate want is an understanding. The difference shows up exactly where it showed up: the checks pass, the page is wrong, and nobody can say why — because the checks measured the words and the eye measures the want. "The unfolding, the full-screen feel, the mechanical presentation" were never in the words; they were in the want, and the want was never written down. Scorecarding his words against his ultimate want is the discipline that closes that gap: read the dictation, ask what he is ultimately trying to get, write THAT in his voice, at 10/10. And when speaking to him, the same rule: give him what he ultimately wants — the finished thing, the answer, the next step — not a report of what I did. A report of activity is not communication; it is noise with a timestamp.
