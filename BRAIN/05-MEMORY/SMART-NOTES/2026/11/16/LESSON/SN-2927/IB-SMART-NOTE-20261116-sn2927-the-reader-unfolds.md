## Human

A wall of text is a refusal to read. The reader unfold is the opposite shape: the full text, verbatim, but handed over one short paragraph at a time, each with a next button — the same unfolding mechanic as the deck, the same as the front page. The citizen never faces the whole thing at once, so they never feel overwhelmed; they click, click, click, and they are through it before they realize they have read it all. That is the point Shawn named: the tactic works because it never asks for the decision to read — it only asks for the next click. It becomes a common thing we do all the way through: anywhere a citizen would otherwise meet a wall — the agreement, the full contract behind the ⓘ, any long text — it unfolds instead. The text stays whole and verbatim; only the presentation changes. And the reader is beautiful: the words restated in the Naya Design way where they are ours to restate, plain to the average person, never bunched together.

- **THE SCOPE:** a common thing all the way through — anywhere a citizen would meet a wall (the ⓘ full contract, the agreement, any long text), it unfolds instead.

## Why it matters

The system asks citizens to agree to things that matter — a collective agreement, a contract, a whole world. If the words arrive as a wall, they will not be read, and an unread agreement is not an agreement. The reader unfold is what makes the serious things actually read: it respects the citizen's attention by never demanding it all at once. It is also the same mechanic as the deck and the front page — one system, one way of showing things, learned once and recognized everywhere.
