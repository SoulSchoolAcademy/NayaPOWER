## HUMAN

It's a good neighbor's way of working: if the fence between you is broken, he fixes it and tells you after — 'fixed the fence, it was leaning into your roses, here's what I did' — in ordinary words, not builder's jargon. And when he offers you a share in his orchard, the paper you sign comes before any money talk, says plainly what you are (a representative, not an employee), what you'll earn (half of what your guests actually pay), and how you're both protected. No fine print doing quiet work.

## CHILD

If you see something broken and you know how to fix it, fix it — then tell, in normal words, what you did and why. And the special paper — the deed — comes BEFORE anyone talks about money. It says: you're a representative, like someone who speaks for their country. You earn half when people you invited pay. And there are three safety rules together, so nobody gets hurt.

## GRANDMA

Plain dealing, dear: mend what's broken without making a ceremony of asking, report it in words a person uses at the kitchen table, and put the agreement in writing before a penny is discussed. An honest paper, read start to finish by a stranger who still says 'that makes sense' — that's the only kind worth signing.
