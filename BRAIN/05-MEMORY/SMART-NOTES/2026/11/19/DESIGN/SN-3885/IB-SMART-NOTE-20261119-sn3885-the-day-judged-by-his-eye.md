# The Day, Judged by His Eye

## Hook

"All everything on Monday gets orange… everything on Sunday gets purples." He opened the calendar link on the call and caught it in one look: every date number in the month grid sat in a gold tile, all thirty of them, every column. And then the morning's sharpest line: "people want to see notifications where their action's at." What followed was one walk, slide by slide, through the day page — his eye judging every slide, and every ruling landing on the same law: the day's colour on everything, the icon as the door, nothing said twice, nothing cloned, nothing invented.

## Nutshell

The month grid was wrong in exactly one place: every date number wore gold, while the headers and the cell borders already wore their weekday colours. His ruling draws the scope line the earlier ruling never drew — the clock-gold law governs the day-page banner number only; the month grid numbers carry their weekday's colour, and it is the DAY colour, not the gematria first-letter law: Sunday purple, Monday orange, Tuesday gold, Wednesday blue, Thursday green, Friday red, Saturday indigo. The clock and time digits stay exactly as they are — that colour look is good, approved as-is, and no round touches them. On the day page itself, the date and time are said ONCE, on the banner — the mid-line repeat goes, no date or time text on the cover at all, the overlay card comes out entirely, and the cover photo fills top to bottom, the citizen's own cover by default, changeable per day, Change cover sitting top-left. No "LOCAL" under the time — the time stands alone, with a small control beside it to change it. His pictures, not ours: the emerald-green and gold crystal-burst image he sent IS the standard default cover for Thursday, and the other six weekdays wait for his images, never invented, never guessed. His city is Kelowna, entered by him on the day page — and the entry works, it saves; but no live forecast appears, because no weather service is connected, and the page says so honestly instead of inventing one. Saving the city and showing a forecast are two different features; the first is done, the second waits for a weather provider, and that is his call. The little ball in the top-right corner is off — not aligned right — and the fix has two halves, neither optional: center it so it fits better, then make it extraordinary, more depth, more elevation, more detail, speedometer-level, because it is on EVERY day, and the most-seen object on the calendar cannot be the least-finished one. The wisdom slide was ruled first, then corrected within the hour: wisdom is the FIRST thing the citizen sees, everything on it centered on one axis — the orb, the greeting, "WISDOM OF THE DAY", the quote — with a Back button in the top-left corner of every slide. Then he corrected himself: notifications open the day, because that is where the action is, and wisdom is second; the centering and the Back button stand. The page-wide law runs through it all: when a thing appears on every page, a ruling about it is page-wide — LOCAL comes off ALL the slides, not the one he happened to be looking at; the weather line stays where it is, the withdrawal of its removal standing as a decision; and the orb fix rides everywhere the orb appears. The notifications slide is five blocks in one line — Personal, Business, Your Worlds, Sales, Appointments — just the numbers, real counts, zero where empty, never invented, each block with its notification count in the corner, all aligned, the beautiful header staying. And the borders take the DAY's colour, Thursday green across all five, not each block in its own area colour — that earlier line withdrawn in the same breath as the reorder. Five across, one line, fit perfect, never three-plus-two, numbers centred, really feeling active and alive. The Horoscope bar leaves the notifications slide entirely, the day nav sits just above the bottom frame in the black area, clear of the content, and the weather line must not block or overlap anything. Then the same law, twice more: Your Worlds is six beautiful big icons — no list, no label, no heading — click an icon and see that world's feed, mail, messages, connections and smart notes for THAT day only, the day as the filter, the back end's contract. And News is three big icons — Local, Province, Global — click one and it pulls that scope's feed for that day; the feeds themselves must be created and wired so the day view pulls real news per day, because news is never invented, and no weather or horoscope sits on the news slide.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: the day page is judged by his eye, slide by slide — the day's colour on everything, the icon as the door, and nothing said twice, cloned, or invented.

## What it means

1. **THE NUMBERS FOLLOW THE DAY, NOT THE GOLD:** the month-grid date numbers carry their weekday's colour — Sun purple, Mon orange, Tue gold, Wed blue, Thu green, Fri red, Sat indigo — gold only in Tuesday's column; the day-page banner number keeps its clock-gold, and the clock is never touched.
2. **SAY IT ONCE, SHOW IT WHOLE:** the date and time appear once, on the banner; the cover fills top to bottom, the citizen's own by default, changeable per day, Change cover top-left; no date or time text on the cover, no LOCAL under the time, a small control beside the time.
3. **HIS PICTURES, NOT OURS:** the emerald-green and gold crystal-burst image he sent is the standard default cover for Thursday; the other six weekday defaults arrive only as his images arrive — never invented, never guessed.
4. **IT SAVES, IT SAYS WHAT IT CAN'T DO, IT NEVER INVENTS:** the city entry works and saves; the forecast waits for a weather provider, his call, and the page says "not connected yet" in plain words — saving and forecasting are two different features.
5. **THE MOST-SEEN OBJECT MUST BE EXTRAORDINARY:** the top-right orb is centered and given depth, elevation and detail, speedometer-level — a centered flat ball is still unfinished — and the fix rides everywhere the orb appears.
6. **NOTIFICATIONS FIRST, WHERE THE ACTION IS:** notifications opens the day, dot zero; wisdom is second, everything centered on one axis, a Back button top-left on every slide — the order follows the order of action, and the earlier wisdom-first order stands corrected, not carried.
7. **FIVE BLOCKS, ONE LINE, JUST THE NUMBERS:** real counts, zero where empty, corner counts, aligned, borders in the DAY's colour — never each block in its own area colour, never three-plus-two — the header stays beautiful, the Horoscope bar leaves this slide, the day nav sits clear in the black area above the bottom frame.
8. **THE ICON IS THE DOOR, THE DAY IS THE FILTER:** Your Worlds is six big icons, News is three — no list, no label, no heading — and every click shows that world or that scope for THAT day only, feed, mail, messages, connections and smart notes, a back-end contract, not a front-end label.
9. **PAGE-WIDE MEANS PAGE-WIDE:** LOCAL off every slide, the orb fix on every page, the weather line where it is — a ruling about a thing that appears everywhere is never fixed on one page and left broken on the next.

## Why it matters

1. Without the scope line on the gold, the next round recolors the banner too, or leaves the grid gold — because the two rulings look the same until someone writes down that they govern different numbers.
2. Without "say it once," the day page keeps repeating itself — the date under the time, the time under the date — and a page that repeats itself is a page that doesn't trust its own banner.
3. Without his images as the only defaults, the round invents six pretty covers, and the citizen opens Thursday to a picture he never chose — his pictures, not ours, is the whole point of the default.
4. Without the honest "not connected yet," the page either fakes a forecast or quietly hides the gap — and a system that invents weather will invent anything.
5. Without the orb made extraordinary, the most-seen object on the calendar stays the least-finished one — and the eye lands on it every single day and finds it wanting.
6. Without notifications first, the citizen opens the day to wisdom and has to go looking for where their action is — the order of the slides is the order of their morning.
7. Without the day-colour borders, the five blocks split into five area colours and the day loses its one colour — five things shouting instead of one day speaking.
8. Without the icons-as-doors, Your Worlds and News stay flat lists of text rows — a directory, not a world you can enter — and the day filter stays a label instead of a contract.
9. Without the page-wide law, LOCAL comes off one slide and stays on the next, and the orb is fixed on the day page and crooked everywhere else — the same mistake twice.
