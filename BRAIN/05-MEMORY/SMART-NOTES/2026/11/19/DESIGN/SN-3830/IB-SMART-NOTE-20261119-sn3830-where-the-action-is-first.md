## Why it matters

1. Without the day colour on the numbers, the grid reads as one flat gold wash — and the weekday, which the headers and borders already know, is lost in the very numbers the eye reads first.
2. Without saying it once, the day page repeats itself and hides its own photo behind an overlay card — the citizen reads the date twice and sees the picture never.
3. Without his pictures as the defaults, the round invents six covers and the citizen's days open on images he never chose — a default he didn't send is a guess dressed as a standard.
4. Without the honest "not connected yet," the page either fakes a forecast or quietly patches a provider decision — and a citizen who catches one invented number stops trusting the real ones.
5. Without the orb made extraordinary, the most-seen object on the calendar stays the least-finished one — and every day opens on a small flat dot that says the page isn't done.
6. Without wisdom centered on one axis with a way back, the day's greeting scatters across the slide and the citizen who taps in has no door back out.
7. Without page-wide meaning page-wide, LOCAL comes off one slide and stays on the next, and the orb is fixed on one page and broken on another — the same mistake twice.
8. Without notifications first, the citizen opens the day and has to walk past the wisdom to reach the action — the page makes them look for the thing they came for.
9. Without just the numbers, the slide fills with "NOT CONNECTED YET" where counts should be — the citizen reads excuses instead of their own numbers, and a squished 3+2 block reads as broken.
10. Without the icon as the door, Your Worlds stays a directory of text rows — a list to read instead of a world to enter — and the day filter never reaches the back end.
11. Without real feeds behind the three news icons, the icons are a promise with nothing behind them — three beautiful doors that open onto invented news, which is the one thing the system must never do.
