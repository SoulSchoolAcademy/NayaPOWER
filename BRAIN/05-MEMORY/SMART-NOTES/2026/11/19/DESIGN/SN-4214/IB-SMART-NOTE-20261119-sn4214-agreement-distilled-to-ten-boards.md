## Ultimately What It Means

- **THE LENGTH LAW:** "If a presentation is going to be that long, it better be ultra compelling and interesting and beautifully written, so it keeps their attention — because otherwise it is way too long." Either distill it to its essence, or every sentence must hold attention. It applies to the agreement first, and to long presentations generally.
- **TEN BOARDS, 3–5 SENTENCES:** the decision. No long version in the walkthrough.
- **NO LEGAL NEED FOR THE LONG VERSION:** no lawyer, no statute, no legal need anywhere in the record. The agreement is a code of honour / shared promise, not a formal legal contract. The 240-sentence version exists only because the build brief said "keep every word verbatim."
- **NOTHING IS LOST:** the full word-for-word text stays in the public governance library for anyone who wants every word.
- **THE VOICE:** as if Naya is their friend — no jargon, straight up, analogies where they help. They get it, go "wow," respect it, and want to take part.
- **THE SHAPE:** picture boards (1–2 sentences) and text boards (3–5 sentences) alternating — picture, text, picture, text — never too much at once. Pictures harvested from the visual deck's existing pictures, never rebuilt. Captain decides the exact alternation and which picture goes where, judged as a real masterpiece.
- **SEQUENCED:** distillation round runs next on the walkthrough agreement file, after the in-flight frame round resolves — one lane, one round.
