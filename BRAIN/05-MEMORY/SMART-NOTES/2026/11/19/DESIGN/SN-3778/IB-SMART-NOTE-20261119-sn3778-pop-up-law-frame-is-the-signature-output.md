"Anything that has a pop-up now pops up and optimizes and fits INTO the frame, so it looks like it's meant to be there. No more little tiny pop-up things — always optimizing to the viewport, to that frame. That's our signature output type thing."

## The understanding

The frame is not one presentation among others — it is the signature output of the whole system, the shape everything takes when it appears. A pop-up that floats small and unframed breaks the signature: it looks like it landed on the page from somewhere else. The law makes the frame universal for appearing things: whatever pops up, pops up INTO the frame, optimized to the viewport, so it looks like it was meant to be there — because it was. The info card is the first proof: a small little pop-up becomes a full framed presentation of the person. After that, every pop-up — every card, every menu, every overlay — is judged the same way: is it in the frame, or is it a little tiny thing? The frame standard law already governs the top bar and the buttons; the pop-up law extends the same signature to everything that appears.

## The law

Every pop-up fits INTO the frame — optimized to the viewport, never a little tiny floating thing. The frame is our signature output type.

## Owed from this dictation

Every round that builds a pop-up proves it framed. The info card is the first proof; the settings menu presentation (SN-3756) already carries the frame as the standard for pop-ups, and this law makes it universal.

**No more little tiny pop-up things.**
