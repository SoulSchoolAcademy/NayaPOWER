# Every Feature Opens in a Frame

SN-3890 · DESIGN / THE FRAME STANDARD

**Created:** 2026-11-20 00:17Z

**Kinds:** Honor · Multiplication · Craft · Knowledge

## Hook

Settings was one crowded room — everything crammed into All Settings, nothing findable. The fix is a door, a frame, and a standard: Settings is a door that shows the house, every feature opens in a frame, and the frame is the standard for everything, every time.

## Nutshell

Settings opens on the person, not on the machine: its first entry is Set Your World — age, adult, male/female, age range, where they live, where they're from — the same onboarding data that powers auto-match, asked before anything is tuned. And Set Your World asks one question per frame — each question its own frame, multiple choice, click next through them all until answered — the flow harvested from the walkthrough, frame by frame, because one question per frame is the unit of attention and the citizen never faces ten questions at once. The spectrum runs all the way through Settings: each block turns a new colour on hover, never one colour per section, so the eye always knows where the hand is. But All Settings was too crowded to hold it all, so the house splits into rooms: Notifications gets its OWN area, never inside All Settings, and so do cover buttons, with the cover-button destination mapping living in the Cover Buttons area. The Settings button itself becomes a menu of areas — Set Your World first, then Notifications, Cover Buttons, Common Settings — nothing crammed into All Settings anymore, and even Common Settings gets its own area, because "common" is still a room, not a drawer. The menu is long enough for all areas and scrolls; each entry is a beautiful obsidian button lighting its own colour, with a raised high-level icon like the Hub sidebars; Set Your World first because who you are comes before what you tune; entries include install-app-on-page and public on/off. Underneath it all sits the standard: THE FRAME IS THE STANDARD for every pop-up and feature display, frame by frame — the zoomed-in viewpoint — so every feature opens the same way and the citizen always knows where they are. The messenger lives by it too: the instant messenger sits in an EXPANDABLE frame — scroll down and the frame goes down until it's completed — because "nothing back" is not a version, and the overhaul is quite an overhaul. Smart Mail is a framed letter: every mail piece feels like a real manuscript in a framed beautiful letter — extraordinary, high contrast, raised boards, text like the intelligent boards — studied against what elite messengers do and built ten times better, because elite bubbles carry who/when/seen/reactions and ours carried none of it. The three open questions from that study are answered in one breath: seen is the reader's face orb, labels sit at a comfortable reading size (17px board text governs), and voice notes get 1.5×/2× speed controls — show the person, not the mechanism. And the colours that carry it all stay in their settled sequence: the Dashboard sidebar flow was misaligned — crown chakra, then crown chakra again, then blue, purple missing, the same duplication down the sidebar — so the flow runs the spectrum in order, never repeating a colour back-to-back, never skipping one, the same sequence on the Dashboard and the Hub, aligned. Magenta and purple are kept BOTH — dropping magenta would collapse the Hub into the Dashboard, because magenta IS the Hub — each made the most beautiful rich version of itself, high contrast to the other at a glance, the Dashboard taking the points purple as its anchor and the Hub staying magenta.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: one frame, one viewpoint — every feature, every time.

## What it means

1. **SETTINGS OPENS ON THE PERSON:** Set Your World is the first entry — who they are, before anything is tuned — because the onboarding data powers auto-match and the system must know them first.
2. **ONE QUESTION, ONE FRAME, NEXT:** Set Your World asks one question per frame, multiple choice, click next until answered — the walkthrough's flow harvested verbatim, never a long form, never a new pattern.
3. **SETTINGS IS A DOOR, NOT A ROOM:** click Settings and the menu lists each area — Set Your World first, Notifications, Cover Buttons, Common Settings — and even the remainder gets its own area, because "common" is still a room.
4. **TOO CROWDED IS TOO CROWDED:** Notifications and cover buttons each get their own area, never inside All Settings — split the room, don't shrink the furniture.
5. **THE FRAME IS THE STANDARD:** every pop-up, every feature display, is a frame — the zoomed-in viewpoint, one thing at a time — and any new display that opens outside a frame is out of alignment, not a variation.
6. **THE FRAME GROWS WITH THE CONVERSATION:** the instant messenger sits in an expandable frame — scroll down and the frame goes down until it's completed — because a frame that cannot grow interrupts the talk.
7. **MAIL IS A LETTER YOU CAN HOLD:** every mail piece is a real manuscript in a framed beautiful letter — high contrast, raised boards, text like the intelligent boards — studied against the elite and built ten times better.
8. **SHOW THE PERSON, NOT THE MECHANISM:** seen is the reader's face orb; labels sit at a comfortable 17px; voice notes get 1.5×/2× — "Yes, do that" is the whole approval, no more deliberation.
9. **ONE SEQUENCE, NO REPEATS, NO GAPS:** the sidebar colour flow runs the settled sequence in order on every sidebar, on every page — and magenta and purple are both kept, each the most beautiful rich version of itself, high contrast at a glance.

## Why it matters

1. Without Set Your World first, Settings opens on the machine — toggles and knobs before the person — and the system asks the citizen to tune what it does not yet understand.
2. Without one question per frame, the citizen faces ten questions at once in a long scrolling form — and the onboarding rhythm the walkthrough and the calendar already proved is thrown away for a new pattern.
3. Without the menu of areas, All Settings stays the crowded room — and the citizen digs for the one thing they came to change, which is the opposite of what the system promises.
4. Without the split, Notifications and cover buttons stay buried in All Settings — a whole system and a whole mapping, hidden inside a drawer.
5. Without the frame as the standard, every feature invents its own opening — and the citizen has to relearn where they are on every page, instead of always knowing.
6. Without the expandable frame, the messenger cuts the conversation off or loses the thread — and "nothing back" stays the verdict, because no visible change is no version.
7. Without the framed letter, mail stays rows in a list — and the study's gap (no who/when/seen/reactions) stays unfilled, because nobody named what the elite already do.
8. Without the three calls, seen stays a checkmark, labels stay a squeeze, and voice notes stay one speed — and the citizen reads a mechanism instead of a person.
9. Without the settled sequence, the sidebar repeats a colour and skips another — and every page that copies it inherits the break, because the sidebar is the system's spine.

## What changes now

1. The Set Your World build takes the walkthrough's frame-by-frame flow verbatim — one question per frame, multiple choice, click next — with the spectrum answering each hover.
2. Settings becomes a menu of areas — Set Your World, Notifications, Cover Buttons, Common Settings — each its own area, reached from the menu, nothing left in All Settings.
3. The Settings menu ships long enough for all areas, scrollable, obsidian buttons lighting their own colours, raised icons, Set Your World first, install-app-on-page and public on/off included.
4. The frame standard governs every pop-up and feature display from here on — any new display outside a frame is out of alignment.
5. The messenger lane builds the expandable frame, the framed letter, the face-orb seen, 17px labels, and 1.5×/2× voice-note speeds — one messenger lane, whole.
6. The flow-fix round lands the sidebar sequence on the Dashboard and the Hub together — no repeats, no gaps — with the exact magenta and purple shades proven on the page by his eye.
