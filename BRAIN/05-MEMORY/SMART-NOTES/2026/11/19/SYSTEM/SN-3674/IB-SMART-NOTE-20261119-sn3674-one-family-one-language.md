## HUMAN

Think of a big family farm run by twelve siblings, each master of one part — one keeps the seeds, one the animals, one the books — all drinking from the same well. Once a day the whole farm's state is told at one table: what's growing, who owns it, what's blocked. And any sibling, asked at any moment, can say what the farm is for and what happens next. That's not paperwork; that's how the farm doesn't drift apart.

## CHILD

Twelve sisters each take care of one garden — learning, building, checking, remembering — and all the gardens share the same soil. Every day they tell each other what's growing. And if you ask any sister 'what are we doing and why?' she can tell you, in words a kid understands. If she can't, something needs fixing.

## GRANDMA

A family that eats together knows itself. Twelve hands, each with their work, one table where it's all said out loud every day — who did what, what's stuck, what's next. And plain words at that table, so the youngest and the oldest both follow. Families that stop saying it out loud start guessing, and guessing is where things fall apart.
