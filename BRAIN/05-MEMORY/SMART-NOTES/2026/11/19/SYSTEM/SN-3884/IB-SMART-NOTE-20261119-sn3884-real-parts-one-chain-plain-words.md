## Why it matters

1. Every invented part doubles the maintenance and halves the quality, every guessed number quietly lies to a citizen, and every unproven join breaks at the worst moment.
2. Citizens never meet the mechanism; they meet the surface — a system whose proofs stop at the byte count will feel broken while reporting green.
3. Trust is built at exactly these seams: a citizen routed wrong, paid on an arbitrary clock, or shown a bake proof dressed as a live one learns the same lesson — that the system's words and its facts are different things.
4. Underrating is the quiet killer: a citizen who thinks it is a new AI never looks for the network, and a free member promised the powered tier feels deceived at the first limit.
5. Without the definition stated, most intelligent thing is a slogan and the scorecards have no top — with it, the whole system has a compass: teach before powering, verify before claiming, and let harm end the argument.
6. Systems rot by replacement: each new version quietly discards what people loved about the last — and this family is the counter-law, keeping belonging, beauty, and the day's calendar as the one door everybody already understands.
7. Everything the system knows is worthless at the exact point it is misunderstood — by the brief, by the builder, by the page, by the citizen — and this family guards that point end to end.
