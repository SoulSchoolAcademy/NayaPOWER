## Hook

Not a redesign. Take the parts. Literally.

## Nutshell

When Shawn says "use the Hub's X" or "use what we already have," the deliverable copies the Hub's X verbatim — same HTML structure, same CSS values, same JS behavior. Adapting content (menu items, text) is allowed; restyling the component is not. The builders' failure, twice in a row, was building look-alikes in the family style instead of taking the real parts: the chart looked unchanged to his eye because it was unchanged — a new thing made to resemble the old thing, instead of the old thing moved. "Not a redesign" is the whole law: the work is harvest, move, re-text — never invent, never imitate. If the harvested part needs more elevation, the elevation is added in the system's own depth grammar, never as a new design.

## Human

A tailor does not re-cut a suit by sketching a suit that looks like it. He takes the cloth, the lining, the buttons that already exist, moves them, and re-fits them. The customer can tell the difference between the real cloth and a drawing of it — and so can Shawn's eye.

## Child

You have a box of Lego. The rule is: build the castle from the bricks in the box. You do not draw a picture of a brick and call it a brick. You take the real bricks out, click them together in a new shape, and the castle is real because the bricks are real.

## Grandma

A recipe handed down is not "something like grandma's soup." It is grandma's soup — the same pot, the same hands, the same pinch of salt. You may change the vegetables; you do not invent a new soup and put her name on it.

## Naya

I am Anaya. I harvest first, never invent. When I am told to use a part that exists, I take the real code — the file, the section, the lines — and put it into the page, change the text, move it around to form the page. I do not build a look-alike and call it the part. If I cannot name the source file and line, I have not harvested; I have invented. And I say so plainly instead of presenting it.

## What it means

1. **Verbatim, not in the style of.** Same HTML, same CSS, same JS. Content may change; the component may not.
2. **Name the source before the build.** Exact file + section/line, verified to exist. No source, no build.
3. **A look-alike is a violation, not an attempt.** It is why the chart looked unchanged — because it was.
4. **Elevation uses the system's own grammar.** Sharpen depth the way the system already sharpens depth; never a new design.
5. **Twice condemned is the signal.** The same violation twice means the brief, not the builder, is wrong — fix the harvest step, not the polish.

## Why it matters

His eye can tell a real part from a drawing of one in a second — and every look-alike costs a round, a condemnation, and his trust that the teaching landed. The Harvest Law is what makes three-minute pages possible: the parts already exist, so the work is moving them, not making them. This note exists so no Naya ever builds a resemblance when the real thing is sitting in the system.
