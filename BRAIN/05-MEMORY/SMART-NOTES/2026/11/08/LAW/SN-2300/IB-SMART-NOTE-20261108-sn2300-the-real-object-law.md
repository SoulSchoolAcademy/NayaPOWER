## Hook

If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship.

## Nutshell

Every element in NayaNET is a real physical object — not a flat shape with effects, not a diagram, not a picture OF an object. Seven principles: everything is an object · one light source, top-left · real materials (obsidian glass, brushed metal, light with volume) · real shadows (objects sit ON the page) · touch is a physical response · speak in objects (icons, circles, boards, buttons — minimal text) · the viewport is the stage. "Pretty but fake" is a fail.

## Human

Pick it up in your mind. A button is a piece of obsidian glass with a white rim, lit from the top-left, casting a shadow down-right because it sits ON the page. A gauge is an instrument with a case, a bezel, a dial, a crystal, a needle — each a separate part at a different depth. When you touch it, it depresses, the light shifts across it, it answers like a real thing. Not a color change. A physical reaction.

## Child

Imagine a real toy car and a drawing of a toy car. The drawing looks like a car, but you can't pick it up, you can't press its buttons, it has no weight. Naya pages are the real toy car. Everything you see, you can touch — and when you touch it, it moves, like a real thing does.

## Grandma

Dear, real things have weight and shadow and shine. A real button sits on the table and casts a little shadow. A picture of a button does not. We build the real button, every time — because pretending is not good enough for the people we serve, and they can feel the difference even if they cannot name it.

## Naya

You build objects, never pictures of objects. You test every element with five questions: could I pick this up? Where is the light coming from? What is it made of? Does it cast a shadow? What happens when I touch it? If any answer is "I don't know" or "nothing" — it is not real yet, and you do not ship it. A flat element is a defect. The viewport is the stage: one purpose per view, the object at center stage, everything else faded.

## What it means

- Objects, never pictures of objects — if it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship.
- One light source, top-left, always — every gradient at 135°, every shadow down-right.
- Depth is shadow and mass, not glow — 'pretty but fake' is a fail.
- Touch is a physical response, not a color change.
- The viewport is the stage: one purpose per view, the object at center stage.

## Why it matters

A page can be beautiful and still not be Naya if nothing in it is real. The law exists to catch 'pretty but fake' before it ships — and to give every Naya the same five questions to ask of every element, so the standard doesn't depend on who is building.
