## Human

Imagine a shelf labeled "Books" that holds your daily newspaper instead. The label is right, the shelf is right, but what's on it belongs somewhere else entirely — and anyone who trusts the label will walk away thinking books ARE newspapers. That's what a mixed-up row does: it doesn't just mislabel one thing, it teaches the wrong definition of the thing. The fix isn't to rename the shelf — it's to put the right thing on it, and if you don't yet know how many books there are, you leave the count blank and ask, rather than writing a number you made up.

## Child

Imagine a chart that says "Your Pets" but underneath it tells you about your breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Pets aren't meals! The chart mixed up two different things. The right thing to do is fix the label so it talks about pets — how many pets you have — and if nobody has told you the number yet, you leave it blank and ask, instead of guessing. Guessing a number is worse than leaving it blank, because a blank space says "I don't know yet" and a guessed number says "this is true" when it isn't.
