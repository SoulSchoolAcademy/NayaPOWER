# His Eye Is the Instrument

## Hook

The chart was good. Then his eye went over it, row by row, and found what no checklist found: numbers that wrap, columns with no names, ladders that lie flat, a portrait that is not her, a row that describes the wrong thing. His eye is the instrument. The fix is structural, never cosmetic.

A blank cell with an open question is honest. A made-up number is a lie.

## Child

Imagine a teacher looks at your drawing and sees three things wrong. You do not just colour over them. You fix how the drawing is built. And if you do not know a number, you leave a blank space and ask. You never make up a number.

## Grandma

A good eye catches what a list misses. When he says a word is hard to read, you do not argue — you make it easy to read. When he has not given you a number, you do not invent one. You leave it blank and you ask him. Honest and blank beats neat and wrong.

## Naya

His eye is the instrument because his eye is the ground truth — a thing is done only when he sees it right on his own screen. The checklist measures mechanics; the eye measures whether it is Naya. Every defect he names is real, every fix he dictates is structural, and every number he has not given is a question, not a gap to fill. The chart got better all day because the eye kept looking and the hands kept fixing the structure, not the surface.

## What it means

- A defect he names is never argued with. It is real, and the fix changes the structure — a sticky header, a no-wrap rule, a stacked layout — not one value or one shade.
- A floated idea is not a settled number. Ladders he floats stay out of the chart until his word settles them.
- A reproduction wearing the original's name is worse than a placeholder: it hides the fact that the real thing is still missing.

## Why it matters

The chart is the page — the whole offer, the first thing a stranger sees. A value that wraps reads as nonsense. A column with no name is meaningless. A row that describes the wrong feature teaches the wrong thing. Each of those is a small defect with a large cost: a stranger who cannot read the chart cannot choose, and a stranger who cannot choose does not join.

## What changes now

- When he names a defect, fix the structure — sticky header, no-wrap values, stacked centered cells — not one value, not one shade.
- Never invent a number: a row with no count from him stays blank and the question is asked. The PI row waited for his numbers.
- The portrait must be her real bytes, never a generated copy wearing the real file's name.
- Value first, big and clear, one line; explanation small underneath; the popup carries the full text.
- Numbers wear the Dashboard's real tiled digits — flip-tiles, rim colours, rotation — never plain tiles, never "/M".
