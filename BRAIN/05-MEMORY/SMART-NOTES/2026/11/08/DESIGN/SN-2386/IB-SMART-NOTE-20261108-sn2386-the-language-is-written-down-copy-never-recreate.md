# The Language Is Written Down — Copy, Never Recreate

SN-2386 · DESIGN / LAW

**Created:** 2026-11-08 23:30Z

## Hook

The design language used to live in one man's eye. Now it lives in writing — the Playbook, the Intelligence Report, the Act, the Block Rules, the Inventory, the Harvest. There is no excuse left for recreating what already exists.

## Nutshell

Ten notes lock the language in: the Playbook is the how, the Intelligence Report is the why, the Clarity Confirmation Act is the spec, the Intelligent Block Rules are the laws, the Inventory is the organised whole, the Anatomy is the test, the Harvest is the parts themselves. And the pattern they all exist to kill is the recreate pattern — builders retyping a description and producing a lower-quality copy of a part that was refined by his eye over a year. The law is one line: take the code itself, byte for byte. Copy, never recreate.

## What it means

- The design is no longer taste held in one head. It is a written language with a vocabulary (the parts), a grammar (the laws), and a test (the anatomy).
- The recreate pattern is the single most expensive habit in the system: a year of refinement, lost in one retyping.
- "Only the chart is good" is not an insult — it is the method: keep what is truly good, throw away the rest, build from the good part.

## Why it matters

Before these notes, every builder had to guess what "Naya design" meant, and every guess came back a little wrong. Now the guess is gone. A builder who reads the language and copies the parts cannot produce a look-alike — the failure mode is named, the fix is named, and the test is six questions long.

## What changes now

- Read the Playbook, the Intelligence Report, the Act, and the Block Rules before any design work — the activation habit, every time.
- Copy harvested parts byte for byte and change only the text inside. Never restyle, never rebuild "in the style of".
- A rebuild that is a look-alike gets thrown away except what is truly good — the chart was the only good part, and it became the page.
- Score your own work before anyone else sees it. His eye is the instrument, not your 9/10.
- Build one viewpoint at a time, live with him, until he says it is good — then it locks into the Inventory.
