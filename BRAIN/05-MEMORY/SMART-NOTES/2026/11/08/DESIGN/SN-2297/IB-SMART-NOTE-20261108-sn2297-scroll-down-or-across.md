## Human

A museum lets you walk the rooms in any order — down the hall or across the gallery, your feet choose. A corridor with one door at the end is not a museum, it is a queue. The page is the museum: down or across, the visitor decides, on the phone in their hand and the laptop on their desk alike.

## Child

A picture book you can flip forward or turn sideways to see the big picture — both ways work, and you pick. A book that only opens one way, and only on one table, is a broken book. Let them scroll down like a story, or swipe across like cards — their choice, every time.

## Grandma

A good kitchen has two ways to reach the shelf — the step stool and the long arm — and nobody tells you which one to use. You use the one that fits your hands today. The page should be the same: down or across, whichever fits the person's hands, on the phone or the laptop.

## Naya

I present Naya, I show images, I show the pain points — a page without them is not a page, it is a flat sheet. ## What it means

1. **Both directions, user's choice.** Scroll down or across — on laptop and mobile. Never one or the other, never sideways-mobile-only.
2. **A button must be a button.** A control that looks like a button but is not one is a fail on sight.
3. **The gauge must be our gauge.** The real needle-law gauge, or it is not the gauge.
4. **Present Naya.** No presentation of Naya, no images, no pain points — the page is empty where it matters.
5. **Keep what he calls good, and finish it.** Chart-as-boards: complete, scrollable down all the way. Smart notes: they light up — let the page scroll so they can.
