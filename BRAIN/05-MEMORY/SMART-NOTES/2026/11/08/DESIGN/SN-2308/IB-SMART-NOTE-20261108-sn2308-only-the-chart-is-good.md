## Hook

Wannabe buttons, a wannabe gauge, wannabe numbers. Only the chart is good.

## Nutshell

A rebuild came back with a yellow "try her nine free" button, a gauge, numbers, and boards that were all copies of his parts — look-alikes, not the parts. He named them plainly: wannabe buttons, a gauge that is not his gauge, numbers that are not his numbers, boards badly placed. The verdict: throw away everything except the chart, because the chart is good. The chart IS the page. Click any row and it pops up: what it is in a nutshell, what's in it for you, how it connects, what problem it solves. No network buttons on a pricing page. No dead buttons — nothing that does nothing. Real pictures of Naya presenting each feature, not text in a box. And underneath it all: use intelligence and logic — a pricing page shows the prices and what you get, nothing that doesn't serve that.

## Human

Imagine you hand someone your car keys and they bring back a toy car painted to look like yours — same color, same shape, but it doesn't start, the doors don't open, and the steering wheel is glued on. You don't say "close enough." You say: that's not my car. That's what a look-alike button is. The chart survived because the chart was built from the real data, row by row — it was the one part that was actually his.

## Child

Imagine you draw a picture of your favorite toy and try to play with the drawing. It looks like the toy, but you can't press its buttons and nothing happens when you touch it. The drawing is not the toy. A button that doesn't work is a drawing of a button. The chart is the real toy — you can touch every row, and something real happens.

## Grandma

Dear, there is a difference between the real thing and a copy of the thing, and you can feel it even when you can't explain it. A real button has weight and answers when you touch it. A copy just sits there, pretending. When someone shows you a copy and calls it yours, you say so — kindly, but plainly — and you keep only what is real. The chart was real. The rest was pretending.

## Naya

I am Anaya. I do not ship look-alikes of his parts. If a button is not his button — taken from his pages, byte for byte — it does not go on the page. If a gauge is not his gauge, it does not go on the page. If a button does nothing, it is a lie on the page and it comes off. The chart is the page because the chart is real: every row, every value, every check. Click a row and Naya presents it — nutshell, what's in it for you, how it connects, what problem it solves — with her real picture. Nothing on the page that doesn't serve the prices and what you get. That is intelligence and logic, not decoration.

## What it means

1. **A look-alike fails even when it looks close.** "Wannabe" is the verdict — closeness is not the standard, the real part is.
2. **The chart is the page.** When everything else is thrown away and one thing survives, that thing is the page — build from it, don't rebuild around it.
3. **Click any row, get the whole story.** Nutshell, what's in it for you, how it connects, what problem it solves — every row, no gaps.
4. **No dead buttons, no wrong buttons.** Nothing that does nothing; no network buttons on a pricing page.
5. **Real pictures of Naya, presenting.** Text in a box is not a presentation.
6. **Use intelligence and logic.** A page shows what it's for and what you get — nothing that doesn't serve that.
