## Hook

Make the chart pop: icons, green checks, her beautiful numbers — and an eye that says click me.

## Nutshell

The chart was good; now make it pop. Icons in each row so you see what the feature is, and green check marks so you see what you get and the difference between the levels — the difference should be visible at a glance, not read out of text. The numbers in the boxes should be the Dashboard's beautiful numbers — the tiled digits, the real number language — used whenever there is an opportunity to use them, and shortened so they pop: not "up to a hundred thousand a month," just 100K/M. And a little eye — a beautiful jewel — in the corner of each row: the affordance that says click me, and click — boom — the popup tells them about it. One thing is NOT settled: the ladders he floated (Smart Notes 10 / 100K / unlimited; Smart Lists 3 / 30? / 100K / unlimited) — the builder keeps the chart data's values until his word settles the numbers. No number changes on a floated idea.

## Human

Imagine a menu where every dish has a little picture, a green tick when it's included, and the price in big clear numbers — you don't read the menu, you see it. Now imagine the same menu with no pictures, no ticks, and the prices written out in long sentences. Same food, but one menu you understand in two seconds and the other you have to study. The chart should be the first menu.

## Child

Imagine a treasure map where every treasure has a picture, a green tick when you can have it, and a big shiny number — and a little eye on each spot that winks at you and says "tap me and I'll tell you a secret." That map is fun to explore. A map with only words is homework.

## What it means

1. **Icons in every row.** You see what the feature is before you read a word.
2. **Green checks show what you get.** The difference between tiers is visible at a glance — check where it's included, X where it isn't.
3. **The beautiful numbers, everywhere they fit.** The Dashboard's tiled digits are the number language; use them in the chart boxes too, shortened (100K/M) so they pop.
4. **The eye is the affordance.** A beautiful jewel in the corner of each row that says "click me" — and the click opens the story.
5. **Shortened, not spelled out.** "100K/M," not "up to a hundred thousand a month" — optimization is presentation.
6. **Floated numbers are not settled numbers.** His word is needed before any ladder changes; until then the data's values stand and the question stays open.

## Why it matters

This is what "make it pop" means in practice — not more decoration, but more seeing: icons, checks, real numbers, a visible affordance. It is also the boundary: a direction about presentation is not a direction about prices. Changing the Smart Notes and Smart Lists ladders on a floated "maybe" would put wrong prices in front of citizens — so the note carries both the refinement and the open question, and the question goes to him, not to the builder's guess.
