## Why it matters

The difference between a pitch and a letter is the difference between being told and being welcomed. A statement on a page asks for attention; a letter from the founder gives it — sealed, signed, framed like a plaque, funny, warm, real — and it earns the reading line by line instead of demanding it all at once. And the ending is the whole point: a letter that ends with the button is a door, not a speech — the reader never has to go looking for what to do next, because the doing is right there, where the feeling is warmest. That is what "show, don't tell" means in practice: the form does the persuading, the words do the welcoming, and the button does the inviting. Get any one of the three wrong — the wrong form, the wrong order, the button anywhere but the end — and the beautiful words wreck themselves. Get them right, and the introduction is the experience.
