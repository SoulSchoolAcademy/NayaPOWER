## Why it matters

Round 16 is the harvest law applied to icons: the page already had the answer, at the top, and the failure was that the answer didn't travel. Every re-invented icon is a small tax on the reader's recognition — each new mark forces the eye to re-learn what a tier looks like, and a page that makes the eye re-learn in every section is a page that never lets recognition become instant. The canonical-icon rule is also the cheapest fix in design: no new drawing, no new decision, just an audit and a swap. And it compounds with round 14 — color says which period, icon says which tier, and together they let the chooser be read at a glance, with no thinking at all. The next builder who draws a fresh icon for a tier that already has one is making the round-16 mistake, and the audit — "is this the canonical icon, same glyph, same color?" — is the check that catches it before his eye has to.
