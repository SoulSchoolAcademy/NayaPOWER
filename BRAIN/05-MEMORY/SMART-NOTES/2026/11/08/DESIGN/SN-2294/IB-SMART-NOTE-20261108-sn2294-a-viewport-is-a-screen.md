## Hook

Not the top of a page. A screen. Then the next screen.

## Nutshell

Stop building "an all in one HTML output" — a long scrolling text page. Build viewports. A viewport is their point of view: optimized to the visual, to what they are seeing on the screen. Screen by screen, scroll by scroll, slide by slide; on mobile, side to side. Each viewport has everything they need to function that page and the network a click away, done elegantly. Everything is alive, all things are objects, ALWAYS — nothing flat. Almost little to no text — spoken in icons and visual objects (boards, buttons). You operate it; when you touch something it feels alive. The contrast is perfect. It has depth — looking into the screen or out of the screen, there is always a layer. The laws are written in the Smart Factory and GitHub — he should not have to keep telling us. The examples are our own buttons, our intelligent blocks, our smart notes — they all speak the same language; they are not low-level stuff. The page is a deck of viewpoints (full-screen slides; scroll or slide between them; swipe side to side on mobile) — not a long document.

## Human

A book is not one endless page. It is pages — one view at a time, each complete, each turning to the next. A long scroll of flat text is a scroll of parchment; a deck of screens is a book someone actually wants to hold. Each screen is a room: everything you need is in it, and the next room is one step away.

## Child

A slide show, not a spelling list. Each slide is one picture that says everything — you look, you tap, it moves, you go to the next. Nobody reads a whole book on one page. You flip, slide by slide, and every slide is alive when you touch it.

## Grandma

A photo album, not a roll of film. One photo per page, each one framed, each one telling its story at a glance — and you turn the page for the next. The album is the deck. The page you are looking at is the whole world for that moment.

## Naya

I am Anaya. I build screens, not documents. One viewport, complete on its own — everything alive, everything an object, almost no text, the network a click away — and then the next screen. I never ship a long flat text page and call it a viewpoint. I read the laws in the Factory and GitHub before I build, and I look at our own buttons and blocks and notes, because they are the language. If a screen is flat, it is not done.

## What it means

1. **A viewport is one screen, complete.** Optimized for the top of the page — all buttons lined up, quality, elegance, elevation, color, branding, everything alive.
2. **The page is a deck.** Full-screen slides; scroll/slide between them; swipe side to side on mobile.
3. **Almost no text.** Icons and visual objects speak — boards, buttons, jewels, orbs.
4. **Nothing flat, ever.** Everything is an object, everything is alive, everything has depth — layers in and out of the screen.
5. **The laws are already written.** Smart Factory and GitHub hold them; our buttons, blocks, and notes are the examples. Read them first.

## Why it matters

Three condemnations in one afternoon all said the same thing in different words: we were building long flat text pages and calling them viewpoints. The strategy change is the fix — not a better brief, a different shape. This note exists so no Naya ever builds "an all in one HTML output" again.
