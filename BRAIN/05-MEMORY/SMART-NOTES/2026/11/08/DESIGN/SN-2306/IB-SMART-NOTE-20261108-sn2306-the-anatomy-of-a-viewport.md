## Hook

A viewport is their point of view. One screen. Down or across — both, their choice.

## Nutshell

A viewport is ONE screen — not the top of a long page, not a section. The page is optimized so they can scroll DOWN or ACROSS: both, not one or the other, on laptop and on mobile — the user chooses. The structure is a Rubik's Cube matrix of intelligent blocks: one core that morphs into its faces. The feel: it flows like water — free, smooth, easy, everything alive and lighting up — and it makes sense when you experience it: everything is where it belongs, so the moment you touch it, you understand it. If a visitor has to think about where something is or what it does, it is not done. Each viewport has everything they need a click away (the Smart Bar, one bar at the top). Everything is alive, all things are objects, always — flat text HTML is the lowest level of coding and never ships. Almost no text: the page speaks in icons and objects. Perfect contrast, always depth. One language: the harvested pieces — the real needle gauge, the `.ph-card` board, the `.os-jewel` / `.pj-jewel`, the Dashboard numbers, the glossy pills. The test, six questions: one screen? everything a click away? every element a real, alive object? meaning survives with almost no text? contrast perfect, depth everywhere? every piece harvested? Any no — the viewport is not done, and it never reaches his eye.

## Human

You are standing in one room, and everything you need is in that room. You don't hunt, you don't scroll for days, you don't repeat yourself. You touch a jewel and the detail opens; you touch a number and its records open; you slide across or scroll down — whichever you like — and the next room is there, just as complete. That is a viewpoint: one screen, complete on its own, alive in every direction.

## Child

Imagine a magic book where every page is a whole little world — one page, one world, everything you need on that page. You can turn the pages down, or slide them sideways — you choose. And every picture in the book is a real toy you can touch: press it, and it lights up and does something. If you have to stop and think "where is the thing I need?" — the page is not finished yet.

## Grandma

Dear, a good room has everything in its place — you walk in, and you know where everything is without being told. That is what a viewpoint is: one room, everything where it belongs, everything alive, nothing flat, nothing hidden. You should never have to hunt for what you need in your own house.

## Naya

You build viewpoint by viewpoint, never page by page. Each screen is a complete, optimized viewpoint, always mobile optimized. You take the harvested pieces and compose them — never fresh HTML that imitates them. You run the six-question test on every viewport before showing it, and a viewport that fails the test never reaches his eye. His eye is the final gate — but the test is what keeps the failures from wasting his time.

## What it means

- One screen, complete on its own — not the top of a long page.
- Down or across — both, the user's choice, on laptop and on mobile.
- Rubik's Cube matrix: one core that morphs into its faces.
- Almost no text — the page speaks in icons and objects.
- The six-question test runs before his eye, every time.

## Why it matters

The viewport is the unit of experience. If one screen isn't complete, alive, and obvious, no amount of pages underneath will save it — the visitor judges the whole world by the one screen in front of them.
