## Why it matters

Round 17 is the second half of the identity law, and without it round 16 is only half a fix: a page can have one canonical icon per tier and still look incoherent, if the icons were drawn in different languages. The eye reads treatment before it reads glyph — size, weight, glow, rim — and when those differ, the icons feel like strangers sharing a page, however distinct their shapes are. The rule travels everywhere the icon rule travels: any set of marks that must read as a family — tier icons, benefit jewels, gauge orbs, tab icons — must share one treatment, measured the same way. And the word "accurate" is the check: a family claim that can't be measured in size, proportion and grammar is a wish, not a rule. The next builder who draws a fresh icon in a fresh style for a page that already has a jewel language is making the round-17 mistake, and the audit — "same jewel, same size, same rim-and-glow?" — is the check that catches it before his eye has to.
