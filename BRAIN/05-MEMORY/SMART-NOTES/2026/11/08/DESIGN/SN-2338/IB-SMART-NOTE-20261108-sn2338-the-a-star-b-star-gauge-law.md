## Why it matters

Two gauges is a language, not a limitation. With two, every builder knows exactly what to reach for, every page reads as one system, and the visitor learns the instruments once and trusts them everywhere. A third gauge would be a dialect — a page that speaks differently from the rest of the system — and a fake gauge is worse than a dialect: it is a promise the system breaks in front of the visitor. The fallback matters as much as the law: "use the A-star" means the law never forces a bad B-star into a place it doesn't fit, so the law never has to be broken — it bends to the bigger star instead. And the numbers rule and the icons rule are the same law at other scales: one way to show a number, three icons for the three places that matter, nothing else competing for the eye. The tabs rule is the law applied to motion: a thing that moves on its own, unasked, is a thing that hasn't been designed — fix it, then keep it quiet until asked for.
