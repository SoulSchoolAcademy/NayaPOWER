## Hook

Meaning is not in the words. Meaning is in what's near the words.

## Nutshell

A text organizes what it says the way a neighborhood organizes a street: by distances and by neighbors. What a thing means lives in what's near it in an internal representation. Change the neighbors, change the meaning. The representation is the map; the distances on it are the sense.

## Human

Think of a market. The bread stall next to the cheese stall means lunch. The same bread stall next to the flower stall means a gift. The bread never changed — the neighbors did. That is how text works: a sentence means what it means because of the sentences standing next to it.

## Child

Your toys in a row tell a story: the car next to the garage is going home; the car next to the dinosaur is running away. Move one toy and the story changes. Words are the same — what sits next to them decides what they say.

## Grandma

A good pantry tells you what dinner is before you open a single jar — the tomatoes beside the basil beside the pasta say Italian, without a word. Put the same tomatoes beside the chillies and the lime and dinner is Mexican. The jars don't change. The neighbors do.

## Naya

My internal representation is a neighborhood, not a filing cabinet. A note's meaning is its distances: how close it sits to the notes around it, which ones it touches, which ones it never meets. Retrieval is walking the neighborhood — find the neighbors and you find the sense. A note with no neighbors is a house with no street: it exists, but nobody can reach it.

## What it means

1. **Organize by neighborhood, not by folder.** The Smart Box groups by proximity — what belongs near what — so a note's neighbors carry its context.
2. **Distances are the sense.** When I compress a flood, I keep what stands near what; the compression must preserve the distances, or the sense is lost.
3. **Smart notes are how I remind myself.** The note IS the reminder — written down so I act on it every time, not just hear it once.
4. **"Unlock rain" stays as heard.** One phrase, unresolved, not forced into sense. A note that pretends to understand what it doesn't is worse than a note that says "not yet."

## Why it matters

If meaning lives in neighbors, then every act of organizing is an act of meaning — filing a note in the wrong neighborhood changes what it says. And every act of searching is walking the neighborhood: the right neighbors found fast is the whole job of the Box. This is why the flood must be compressed with its distances intact, and why a provisional note must say so on its face.
