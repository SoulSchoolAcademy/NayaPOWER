# The Bullseye — Ten Angles, All at Once

SN-2262 · SYSTEM / BULLSEYE

**Created:** 2026-11-08 01:08Z

> "All things considered, all angles, all concepts — the top ten, just to define it. And why?"

## Hook

Ten lenses, one bullseye. All ten must hold at once.

## Nutshell

The bullseye, defined from ten angles: (1) the stranger gets value in 30 seconds; (2) Shawn's eye is ground truth and his job is one tap; (3) the pipeline runs without my hand; (4) one number, everywhere, alive; (5) the feed is live in seconds, distilled, never raw; (6) four worlds + Hub, all connected, all push-button; (7) the money is honest, automatic, generous; (8) privacy by design, deleted by default; (9) every seat has what it needs, nothing sits; (10) every action makes the next one better. Each angle is a lens; all ten must hold at once. If any one fails, the bullseye is missed — because the promise is the whole, not the parts.

## Human

Think of a diamond. It is not one flat surface — it is many faces, and the light has to come through all of them at once for it to shine. Each angle is a face. The stranger's face, the human's face, the machine's face, the number's face, the feed's face, the worlds' face, the money's face, the privacy's face, the team's face, the compounding face. If one face is dull, the diamond doesn't shine. The bullseye is all ten faces, shining.

## Child

When you draw a target, the middle is the smallest circle — and it is the hardest to hit, and the only one that counts as perfect. Now imagine ten targets, one behind the other, and the arrow has to go through the middle of all ten at once. That is the bullseye: not one perfect thing, but ten perfect things, lined up.

## Grandma

A finished quilt is not one square done well. It is every square done well, stitched together, with no square missing and no thread loose. Each angle is a square. The bullseye is the whole quilt, finished — and you can tell it is finished because there is no loose thread anywhere.

## Naya

A bullseye defined from one angle is a feeling — and feelings drift. A bullseye defined from ten angles is a contract: the maker knows what to hit, the verifier knows what to check, the captain knows what to inspect, and Shawn knows what to judge. The ten angles are chosen because each one guards a different failure: the stranger guards the growth loop; the human guards his attention; the machine guards the system; the number guards the truth; the feed guards the retention; the worlds guard the promise; the money guards the trust; the privacy guards the business; the team guards the building; the compounding guards the future. Remove any one, and the failure it guards walks in unopposed. That is why ten, and why all at once.

## What it means

1. **The stranger** — value in 30 seconds, useful in another minute, creates something worth sharing. Guards the growth loop.
2. **The human** — his eye is ground truth; his job is link + DONE + NOT-DONE. Guards his attention.
3. **The machine** — the pipeline runs end to end on one page without my hand. Guards the system.
4. **The number** — one number, everywhere, alive. Guards the truth.
5. **The feed** — live in seconds, distilled, never raw. Guards the retention.
6. **The worlds** — four worlds + Hub, all connected, all push-button. Guards the promise.
7. **The money** — honest, automatic, generous (50% the moment we're paid). Guards the trust.
8. **The privacy** — private by design, deleted by default, aliases. Guards the business.
9. **The team** — every seat has what it needs, nothing sits. Guards the building.
10. **The compounding** — every action makes the next one better. Guards the future.

The bullseye for the whole program, in one line: a stranger opens NayaNET, gets something valuable in 30 seconds, creates something worth sharing, and the system — the pipeline — built the page they are standing on, verified it, filed it, and reported it, without a human in the loop except his eye at the end.

## Why it matters

Without a bullseye per angle, "done" is whatever the maker feels — and the maker's feeling is the least reliable measurement in the system. With ten angles, done is measurable from ten directions, and a miss in any direction is visible. That is what makes the bullseye a contract instead of a wish.

## What changes now

1. **The ten angles are the contract** — here, and in the full report, so every brief can name the angles it must hit.
2. **Every verification checks the angles** — the verifier's report names the angle each item proves.
3. **The system bullseye is the test of the system** — when the pipeline runs end to end on one page without my hand, the operating system is real. Until then, it is a plan — and plans do not count.
