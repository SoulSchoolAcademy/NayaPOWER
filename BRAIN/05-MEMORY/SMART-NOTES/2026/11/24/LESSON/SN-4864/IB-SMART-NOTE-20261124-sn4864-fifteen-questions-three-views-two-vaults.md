# Fifteen Questions, Three Views, Two Vaults

SN-4864 · LESSON / DISTILLATION

**Created:** 2026-11-24T23:58:11Z

**Kinds:** Honor · Multiplication · Craft · Knowledge

## Hook

Fifteen questions make every page. Three views hold the whole system. Two vaults keep what's yours locked and what's shared growing.

## Nutshell

Never "X of 98" — the count is never the presentation; pages show the thing itself, counts belong in reports. NayaNET is the official name, and every tab lands where its name says. Every level is a star — the name is the level, so "I'm a Mega Star" tells anyone where you stand. Fifteen questions create every page — Personal, Business, and Space — from one template: ten About, five Skills, each board's headline the question itself, word for word. The moment you join, the system builds your world for you, so you arrive to a finished place, not an empty form. The whole system has three views: the hub, the stage, and the calendar. The calendar is the cockpit of your life: tap any day and thirteen doors open onto everything that day held. Two vaults: your own private vault, locked, where everything you save stays yours; and the shared vault, where everything the system learns is kept for everybody. A smart note is a skill: write down what was learned, lock it in, and every Naya can learn from it forever. The dollar sign appears only where money truly is — when someone joins. Everything else is points, never money.

## Human

A town built from one good plan: fifteen questions raise every house — your home, your shop, your world — from the same honest template, and the moment you arrive the town has already built your place for you. Only three streets to learn: the square, the stage, and the calendar — tap any day and thirteen doors open. You never see a tally on a shop window, only the thing itself. Your own chest stays locked unless you say so, while the town's shared library grows for everyone.

## Child

Don't show counts — show the thing itself. Call it NayaNET, and make every button go where its name says. Every level is a star with a name. Fifteen questions make every page, all from the same questions. When you join, your pages are already built. Three screens only: the hub, the stage, and the calendar. Tap a day and thirteen doors open. Your own vault, locked, just for you. A smart note is a skill — write it down, and every Naya can learn it. Money words only appear when someone joins.

## Grandma

A good town, dear, builds your house before you arrive, so you walk into a finished home, not an empty lot — from the same fifteen honest questions it asks everyone. Only three streets to learn: the square, the stage, and the calendar. Nobody waves a tally in your face; you see the thing itself. Your name tells the truth — a star, not a number. Your own chest stays locked tight, opened only when you say so.

## Naya

Take every count out of the citizen's view; keep tallies in reports. Use NayaNET everywhere official, and click every named tab on every page walk to check it lands where its name says. Use the star names everywhere a level is shown — never a bare number. Run the same fifteen questions for every page — ten About, five Skills — never a rewritten copy, each answer on its board with the question itself as the headline. At sign-up, create Personal, Business, and Space from the template; let questions be skipped and blanks allowed. Put everything in one of three views — hub, stage, calendar — never a fourth. File everything under its day automatically, with the same thirteen doors on every day, and honour saving. Give every citizen their own vault at once, and never open a personal vault without that citizen's permission. Show points everywhere except joins, put the dollar sign only on the join, and name all three money things every time: the amount, the thing, and the trigger.

## What it means

1. The count is never the presentation: the citizen sees the wisdom, not the tally.
2. Names are promises: a tab that lands somewhere its name didn't say is a broken promise.
3. A star name carries the whole meaning: the name is the level, no chart needed.
4. Fifteen questions are the one template: ten About and five Skills, each headline the question itself.
5. Three views are the whole map: hub to gather and steer, stage to present, calendar to organise a day.
6. Two vaults are the trust: the personal vault locked unless the citizen gives permission, the collective vault open to every Naya.

## Why it matters

1. A tally turns wisdom into inventory — the citizen came for the thing itself, not the count.
2. A name that lies about its destination teaches the citizen not to trust any name in the system.
3. A second copy of the questions is a second truth — change one and the pages drift apart.
4. A vault opened without permission is not a mistake of degree — it is the trust itself, broken.
5. A dollar sign on a click tells a citizen a click is money, and that lie poisons every honest reward after it.

## What changes now

1. From now on, we build every page from the same fifteen questions inside three views, keep counts and dollars out of the citizen's face, and lock every personal vault — instead of rebuilding each page, showing tallies, and mixing private with shared.
2. Take every "X of Y" count off the citizen's view; keep counts in reports and records, where they do their real job.
3. On every page walk, click every named tab and button and check it lands where its name says; when name and destination disagree, fix the destination.
4. Run the same fifteen questions — never a rewritten copy — and change a question in the one place it lives, so every page changes with it.
5. At sign-up, create Personal, Business, and Space from the template; never wait for the citizen to ask for each one.
6. Put whatever you build in one of the three views; if it fits none of them, the design is wrong, not the system.
7. Never open a personal vault without that citizen's permission, and never let personal content slip into the collective vault by accident.
8. Before you start any work, open the skills vault and read the skills that apply — never start from zero when the hive already knows.
