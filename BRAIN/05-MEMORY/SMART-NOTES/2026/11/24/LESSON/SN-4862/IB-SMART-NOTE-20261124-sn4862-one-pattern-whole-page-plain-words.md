# One Pattern, Whole Page, Plain Words

SN-4862 · LESSON / DISTILLATION

**Created:** 2026-11-24T23:56:39Z

**Kinds:** Honor · Multiplication · Craft · Knowledge

## Hook

One pattern, learned once, used everywhere. The whole page alive, the boards mirroring, the words plain — and three doors in.

## Nutshell

Use the whole page. Keep everything that belongs together in one area, not scattered across a thousand pages. Every board is image, text, image, and the next flips to text, image, text, like a mirror. Show what a thing is, not a clever picture that stands for it — an image must be obvious at first glance. Tell each story in chapters of three or four pictures, each one a different part, never the same story twice. Keep the full version, and give three ways in: visual, text, and skip to the end. Every slide says what it is, why it matters, what's in it for you, in plain words a child and a grandma can understand. Boards stack three deep and three across, each going back on its own. One bar on every page, the same six buttons in the same colors. Three lenses up top — Collective, Personal, Activity — with your own quick links underneath.

## Human

A house built one honest way: the whole floor used, everything that belongs in a room staying in that room. The pictures hang in a rhythm you feel before you read a word, and each one is the real thing, not a riddle. The story comes in short chapters, and you can enter it by looking, by reading, or by jumping to the end.

## Child

Use the whole page. Don't leave big empty spaces. Keep things that go together in one place. Make the boards go picture, words, picture — then words, picture, words — like a mirror. Show the real thing, not a tricky picture. Tell the story in little chapters. Give three ways in: look, read, or jump to the end. Say what it is, why it matters, and what's in it for you.

## Grandma

A good page is like a good room, dear — every inch used, everything where it belongs, nothing left bare. The pictures hang in a rhythm, each one showing the real thing plain as day. The story comes in short chapters, and you can enter it however you like. The words are warm and plain, and they never pretend to be warmer than they are.

## Naya

Fill the whole screen and hold one area per page, with buttons that open views inside it. Alternate image-text-image with text-image-text, each image linked to its text. Make every image literal — obvious in two seconds with no words under it. Chapter every story in three or four pictures, each a different part, each batch labeled. Keep the full version whole and build three doors: visual, text, skip to the end. Write every slide in the three beats, in plain first-person words. Stack boards three deep and three across, each with its own back.

## What it means

1. One area per page is the unit: the whole screen alive, views instead of exits.
2. The mirror is the rhythm: two board shapes alternating, visible at a glance.
3. Literal is the test: if an average person cannot name an image in two seconds, the image is wrong.
4. Chapters are the discipline: three or four pictures, each a different part, nothing told twice.
5. Three doors are the kindness: the full version kept whole, entered by picture, by words, or by the end.

## Why it matters

1. A page that leaves most of its screen blank tells the citizen the place is unfinished.
2. A pattern learned once pays for itself on every page after; a pattern invented per page taxes the citizen every time.
3. A clever symbol that needs explaining has already failed; only the real thing works at first glance.

## What changes now

1. From now on, we build every page from one shared pattern — the whole screen alive in one area, mirrored boards of real things, told in plain words with three doors in — instead of inventing each page fresh and leaving beauty nobody can use.
2. Before an image ships, ask: would an average person know what this is in two seconds, with no words under it? If not, the image is wrong.
3. For each story, make three or four pictures that each show a different part, label the batch, and cut any picture that repeats another.
4. Stack boards three deep and three across, each with its own back that moves only that board; never send the N to a video.
5. Build the same six labeled buttons on every page, same order, same colors, from the same code — and hold three lenses up top, with the citizen's quick links underneath.
