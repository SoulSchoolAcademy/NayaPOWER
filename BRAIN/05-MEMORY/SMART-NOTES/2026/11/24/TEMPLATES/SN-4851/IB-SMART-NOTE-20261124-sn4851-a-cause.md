## Nutshell

You help people, run a charity, or build a movement — by naming exactly what hurts and who it hurts, delivering the first help you can, and growing the help until the world is different because you acted.

## Human

Think of a village well that runs dry. One person can carry water from far away, but the village stays thirsty. Then ten people dig together, and the well fills again — and stays full, because now everyone knows how to keep it. This project is the digging: who hurts, what help looks like, and every person who picks up a shovel.

## Child

Imagine you see a hurt dog on your street. First you give it water. Then you find its owner, or a vet, or someone who can help. Then you tell your friends, and they help too. One dog, one kind act, and then more people helping more dogs. This project remembers every dog, every kind act, and every friend who joined in.

## Grandma

It's like the food bank at the church. It started with one neighbour leaving a tin on the step. Then another neighbour, then a shelf, then a whole room, and now nobody in the street goes hungry at Christmas. This project keeps the food bank's story: every tin, every family fed, and the day it became something the whole street does together.

## What It Means

A cause is not a feeling — it's a wrong with a name, a person it hurts, and a first help you can deliver this week. The mission names the change and the hurt: what, and who, right now. The goal names the first help delivered — one person helped, one dollar raised, one thing fixed. The vision names the different world: what is true when the cause has done its work. The helped people are the proof, and the proof is what turns caring into change.

## Why It Matters

Most caring dies as a feeling because nobody turned it into a first act — the hurt stayed far away, the help stayed vague, and nothing happened. A project makes the first act small and near: one person, one dollar, one thing fixed, this week. A small help delivered is worth more than a big feeling felt. And a delivered help, filed where others can see it, is what turns one person's caring into a movement.

## Real-World Example

A citizen's street has no safe place for children to play, and cars speed past. Her mission: "Slow the cars and give our children a safe place to play." Her goal: "A petition signed by every house on the street, delivered to the council." Her vision: "Children playing outside again, and drivers who know this street is theirs too." Her links hold the council page and the petition; her notes hold every signature and every meeting; her people hold the neighbours, the councillor, and the parents. The day the council paints the first crossing, the project shows every signature that made it happen.
