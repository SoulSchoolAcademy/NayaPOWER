## Nutshell

You take an idea and turn it into a real business — one problem, one customer, one sale at a time — until the business stands on its own and pays for itself.

## Human

Think of opening a little bakery. First you know one thing: people in your street want fresh bread and nobody sells it. You bake a loaf, a neighbour buys it, then ten neighbours, then you need a bigger oven. The business is just that chain — a real problem, a real person, a real sale — repeated until it holds itself up. This project keeps the chain in order: the problem, the people, the numbers.

## Child

Imagine you make lemonade and sell it on your street. First you need lemons, sugar, and cups. Then you need people who are thirsty. Then you need to charge enough to buy more lemons. If people keep coming back, you have a business. This project is the lemonade stand, written down — what you sell, who buys it, and whether it works.

## Grandma

It's like planting a vegetable garden to sell at the market. You don't plant everything at once — you plant what people want, you tend it every day, and on market day you see what sells. The next week you plant more of what sold and less of what didn't. This project is the garden book: what you planted, what sold, what to plant next.

## What It Means

Starting a business is not about having an idea — it's about proving the idea with a real person paying real money. The mission names the problem and the person. The goal names the first proof: one sale, one customer, one working day. The vision names what the business gives when it's running — freedom, income, something useful in the world. Everything else — the name, the logo, the plan — comes after the proof, not before it.

## Why It Matters

Most business ideas die as ideas because nobody asked a real customer to pay. A project forces the honest question early: who is this for, and will they pay for it? Asked early, the answer is cheap. Asked late, after months of building, the answer is expensive. This template puts the customer first and the building second, so the business is real before it's big.

## Real-World Example

A citizen loves fixing bicycles and everyone in her building brings her theirs. Her mission: "Fix bikes for people in my neighbourhood who can't afford the shop." Her goal: "Ten paying customers in the first month." Her vision: "A small workshop where neighbours drop off bikes in the morning and pick them up fixed by evening." Her links hold the parts suppliers and the prices; her notes hold every repair and what it cost; her people hold her first ten customers. The tenth sale is the proof the business is real.

## How It Connects

This template is the learning template pointed at the world: instead of learning a skill for yourself, you learn what people need and build it for them. The skeleton is the same — mission, goal, vision, links, notes, people — but the proof is outside you: a customer, a sale, a person helped. That outside proof is what makes a business different from a hobby.

## How to Apply

Pick this template, then answer its three questions in your own words. Add your first link (a competitor's page, a supplier, a tool). Write your first note (the problem you saw, in one paragraph). Add one person (the first person who would pay for this). Then go ask that person — the project keeps their answer, and their answer tells you what to build next.

## What Changes Now

1. Your idea has a home — one project, not a dozen scattered thoughts.
2. Your problem and your person are written down, where you can't dodge them.
3. Every customer conversation and every number files into the same place.
4. Your first sale has a date on it — a finish line you can actually reach.
5. The business grows by proof, not by hope: each sale teaches the next step.

## What's In It For You

You get to find out — cheaply and early — whether your idea is a real business, before you spend months building the wrong thing. And if it is real, you have the record of how it became real: the first customer, the first sale, the first month it paid for itself. That record is the business's memory, and it never forgets.
