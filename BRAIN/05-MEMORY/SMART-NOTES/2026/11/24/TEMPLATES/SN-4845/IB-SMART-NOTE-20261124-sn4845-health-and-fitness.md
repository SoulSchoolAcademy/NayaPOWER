## Nutshell

You get fit, lose weight, or run a race — not by wishing, but by one small habit kept every day, logged where you can see it, until your body feels like yours again.

## Human

Think of training for a race the way you'd fill a jar with coins. One coin a day looks like nothing. But the jar fills, and one day it's heavy in your hand. Every workout, every good meal, every early night is one coin. This project is the jar: it keeps every coin where you can see it, so on the days you feel like quitting, you can look at how full it already is.

## Child

Imagine you want to run all the way around the park without stopping. First you run to the first tree, then to the second tree, then halfway, then all the way. You don't run the whole park on the first day — you run a little farther every day. This project remembers every tree you reached, so you always know you're farther than yesterday.

## Grandma

It's like tending a garden after a long winter. You don't shout at the soil — you water a little every day, you pull the weeds, and one morning there's a green shoot. Then another. By summer the garden feeds you. This project is the garden diary: what you did each day, what grew, and the morning you realized you felt strong again.

## What It Means

Health is not a single decision — it's a thousand small ones, kept. The mission names what healthy means for YOU, not for a magazine. The goal names the first finish line you can actually see — a race date, a weight, thirty days of a kept habit. The vision names the ordinary week on the other side: what you eat, how you move, how you sleep, how you feel. The log is the proof, and the proof is what keeps you going.

## Why It Matters

Most fitness plans die in week three because nobody kept the record — the person only remembers the days they failed, never the twenty days they didn't. A project keeps the whole record: every workout counted, every meal logged, every weigh-in filed. On a bad day, the record says the truth: you have already done more than you think. That truth is what keeps people going.

## Real-World Example

A citizen wants to run a 10K race in six months, after years of not running. Her mission: "Feel strong in my own body again, and finish a 10K." Her goal: "Run 5K without stopping in eight weeks." Her vision: "Saturday mornings I run with friends, then cook a good breakfast." Her links hold the training plan and the race page; her notes hold every run and every weigh-in; her people hold her running partner. The morning she runs 5K without stopping, the project shows every run that got her there.
