## Nutshell

You get out of debt, save, invest, or earn online — by naming the first number that proves it, tracking every dollar where you can see it, until money stops being the worry.

## Human

Think of a leaky bucket. You keep pouring water in and it never fills, because there's a hole at the bottom. The first job isn't pouring faster — it's finding the hole. Then the bucket starts to hold water, and one day it's full. This project finds the hole with you: where the money goes, what you can close, and the day the bucket finally holds.

## Child

Imagine you get pocket money every week and you want a bicycle. If you spend it all on sweets, the bicycle never comes. If you put one coin in a jar every week, the jar fills, and one day you ride the bicycle home. This project is the jar: every coin in, every coin out, and the day you can see the bicycle getting closer.

## Grandma

It's like paying off the house, one payment at a time. Nobody pays it off in a day — you pay a little every month, and the pile of what you owe gets smaller and smaller, until one day the house is yours. This project is the payment book: what you owe, what you paid, and how much smaller the pile is than it was.

## What It Means

Money is not about being rich — it's about what money does for you, in order. First the hole (debt), then the cushion (savings), then the growth (investing, earning). The mission names the first job. The goal names the first number that proves it — a debt at zero, a savings balance kept for three months, a first dollar earned online. The vision names the life on the other side: what you do when money is a tool, not a worry. The numbers are the proof, and the proof is what keeps you honest.

## Why It Matters

Most money plans die because the numbers live nowhere — in your head, on a bank statement you never open, in a drawer. A project puts every dollar where you can see it: in, out, owed, saved, earned. Seen numbers can be changed. Unseen numbers just grow. This template makes the numbers visible, and visible numbers get smaller (debt) or bigger (savings) on purpose.
