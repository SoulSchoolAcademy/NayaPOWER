## Nutshell

You build a group, a club, a channel, or a world of your own — by gathering the first few people who care about the same thing, meeting again and again, until the community lives on its own and belongs to everyone in it.

## Human

Think of starting a fire. You don't begin with a log — you begin with a spark, a little dry grass, and you blow gently. Then a twig catches, then a branch, and one day there's a fire big enough to warm a whole circle of people. This project tends the fire: the first spark, the first few who gathered, and every meeting that kept it burning.

## Child

Imagine you start a club at school for kids who love drawing. First it's just you and your best friend, drawing at lunch. Then two more kids join, then five, and soon the whole table is drawing together. This project remembers every kid who joined and every picture you drew, so the club never forgets how it started.

## Grandma

It's like starting a knitting circle. At first it's three neighbours and a pot of tea. Then the neighbours bring their friends, and the friends bring their daughters, and one day the whole hall is knitting and talking. This project keeps the circle's story: who came, what you made, and the day it became something nobody owned and everybody loved.

## What It Means

A community is not a page or a channel — it's people who keep coming back for each other. The mission names the who and the why: who it's for, what brings them together. The goal names the first gathering — the first members, the first room full. The vision names belonging itself: what people get when the community is alive on its own, without you pushing it. The gatherings are the proof, and the proof is what turns a group into a home.

## Why It Matters

Most groups die after the first meeting because nobody kept the thread — no next date, no record of who came, no story of what happened. A project keeps the thread: every gathering filed, every member story kept, every idea for the next one in the same place. People come back to a place that remembers them. This template makes the community remember, so the people keep coming back.

## Real-World Example

A citizen loves birdwatching and wants a local group. Her mission: "A morning walk where anyone can come and see birds together, no expertise needed." Her goal: "Ten people on the first walk." Her vision: "Saturday mornings, a friendly crowd with binoculars, and new people joining every week." Her links hold the group page and the bird guides; her notes hold every walk and every bird seen; her people hold her first ten walkers. The morning the eleventh person shows up — someone nobody invited, who just heard about it — the community is alive on its own.

The skeleton is the same — mission, goal, vision, links, notes, people — but the people ARE the project, and belonging is the product. If they keep coming back, it's real; if they don't, it's a poster.

## How to Apply

Pick this template, then answer its three questions in your own words. Add your first link (your group page, your channel, the place you'll gather). Write your first note (why this community, and who it's for). Add one person (your first member — start with one). Then hold the first gathering, even if it's just the two of you, and file what happened. The project grows one gathering at a time, and the fire catches.
