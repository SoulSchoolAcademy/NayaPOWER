## Nutshell

You make a move, a renovation, a wedding, or a big trip — one decision at a time, with everyone involved in the same place, until the day arrives and you're living in it.

## Human

Think of planning a wedding. A hundred little decisions — the place, the food, the music, the guests — and if they live in five different heads, something gets forgotten. Put them in one place and the whole family can see the same list, the same dates, the same costs. This project is that one place: every decision, every date, every person, where everyone can see it.

## Child

Imagine your family is going on a big trip. First you pick where to go, then you pack the bags, then you get the tickets, then you go. If everyone knows the plan, nobody gets left behind. This project is the plan on the fridge: where, when, who's coming, and what to pack.

## Grandma

It's like fixing up the old house so the grandchildren can visit. You don't do it all at once — you fix the roof, then the kitchen, then you paint the front door. Each job finished is a room ready for family. This project keeps the jobs: what's done, what's next, what it cost, and who helped.

## What It Means

Family projects are different from solo projects because the work is shared — and shared work needs one shared truth. The mission names what you're making together and why it matters to the family. The goal names the first thing done — the first decision that makes it real. The vision names the day itself: what it feels like when you're living in it. The list is the proof, and the proof is what keeps everyone moving together instead of apart.

## Why It Matters

Most family plans fall apart not because people don't care, but because the plan lives in different heads — Mum thinks the wedding is in June, Dad thinks July, and nobody booked the hall. A project puts the truth in one place where the whole family can see it: the dates, the decisions, the costs, the people. One truth, seen by everyone, is what keeps a family project from becoming a family argument.

## Real-World Example

A family is renovating the kitchen before the new baby arrives. Their mission: "A kitchen where we can all cook and eat together, ready before the baby comes." Their goal: "The cabinets in and the floor down by March." Their vision: "Sunday dinners in a kitchen that finally fits us all." Their links hold the quotes and the tile samples; their notes hold every decision and every cost; their people hold the builder, the electrician, and grandma, who is paying for the fridge. The day the floor goes down, the project shows every decision that got them there.
