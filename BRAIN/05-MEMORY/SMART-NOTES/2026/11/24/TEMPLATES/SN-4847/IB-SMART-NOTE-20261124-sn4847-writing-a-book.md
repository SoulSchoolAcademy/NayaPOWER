## Nutshell

You write a book, a course, or a body of content — by saying what you need to say, one finished piece at a time, until the whole thing exists and can help the people it was written for.

## Human

Think of building a stone wall. You don't lift the whole wall at once — you lay one stone, then another, and you step back to see the line. Some days you lay ten stones, some days one, but the wall only grows stone by stone. This project is the wall: every chapter a stone, every draft a layer, until one day there's something standing that wasn't there before.

## Child

Imagine you want to tell a really long story — so long it needs a whole book. You can't tell it all in one breath. You tell one chapter, then another, and you write each one down so you don't forget. This project is the book of your story: one chapter at a time, until the story is all there.

## Grandma

It's like knitting a blanket for a new baby. You don't knit it in a day — you knit a row when the house is quiet, and the blanket grows row by row. Sometimes you drop a stitch and have to go back, but the blanket remembers every row. This project keeps your rows: the chapters, the drafts, and the day the blanket is finished and warm.

## What It Means

Writing a book is not about being a writer — it's about having something to say that someone needs to hear. The mission names the saying and the hearing: what, and for whom. The goal names the first finished piece — one chapter done, one lesson taught, one draft complete. The vision names the reader on the other side: who it helps and how, when the book is out in the world. The pages are the proof, and the proof is what keeps you writing.

## Why It Matters

Most books die as a first page because nobody kept the thread — the idea lived in the writer's head, the draft lived nowhere, and the book quietly never happened. A project holds the thread: every chapter filed, every idea kept, every reader's note in one place. The book grows where you can see it, and a book you can see is a book you can finish.

## Real-World Example

A citizen has taught cooking to her neighbours for twenty years and wants to write it down before it's lost. Her mission: "Save the recipes and the stories, for my grandchildren and anyone who loves to cook." Her goal: "Ten chapters drafted." Her vision: "A book on the shelf that keeps our kitchen alive." Her links hold her outline and the books that taught her; her notes hold every chapter draft; her people hold her first readers — her daughter and her neighbour. The day the tenth chapter is drafted, the book exists, even before it's printed.
