# The Eye Must Read

SN-4745 · DESIGN / DISTILLATION

**Created:** 2026-11-24T18:00:00Z

## IN A NUTSHELL

The second Inspector sweep refines the same law at finer grain: the eye's 'i' must read in black on pearl, the day view is prime real estate, the Factory formats everything itself, one bar and one mail serve the whole world — alive like a high-tech computer, and scored without excitement.

## HUMAN

The refinements land where your fingers and eyes actually go: the little information eye becomes readable at a glance, opening a day feels like stepping into a cockpit full of your day, the bottom bar is the same five doors everywhere, and there's one Smart Mail — the best of both versions — instead of two half-right ones.

## CHILD

A button nobody can read is just a shiny pebble. We put the black letter back in the middle of the pearl so you can actually read it. And when there are two of something — two mailboxes, two bars — we keep the best parts of each and make one good one.

## GRANDMA

This is the second pass over the same ground, and that's the point: the first sweep sets the rules, the second checks them at reading distance. Is the letter in the eye readable? Does the day page feel like a cockpit or a cupboard? Excitement about an idea, he reminded us, is not a verdict on the thing built.

## NAYA

Operating rules distilled from SN-3255..SN-3264: (1) The eye, black in the middle: the pearl disc is a real object off the page, but the 'i' must READ — a real object nobody can read is a decoration, not a control. (2) The day view is a dashboard: prime real estate — appointments as tiles, timeline, notes, the day's report, Intel, news, weather — a cockpit, not a menu of buttons. (3) The Inspector, full sweep: the standing seat sweeps the whole surface, not a sample. (4) The Factory is the formatter: Epic formatting built as its own app is a second factory — one system, not another; nobody comes to a separate app for one piece of formatting. (5) One bottom bar: the five, everywhere — when he asked which ones, the answer was decided on the spot: the Personal five. (6) Best of both — one Smart Mail: two presentations of the same app is the mess; merge elegance from one and icons from the other, neither thrown away whole. (7) Alive like a high-tech computer, not a video game: the orbs stay; the flavour of motion is the law — more alive is better when it feels like a machine, not a game. (8) The calendar scorecard: excitement is not a verdict — 'super excited until we created it'; low-end cheap, elementary tech, with three named corrections. (9) The best buttons in the system are a pointable standard: three live buttons on the Dashboard. (10) No huge today: today does not light up over itself — his 03:54 correction overrules his 03:47 ask; glow over the day is the defect the whole glow law names.

## MACHINE

1) Controls: readability gates decoration — the glyph must read. 2) Day view: dashboard composition, never a button menu. 3) Formatting lives in the Factory; no parallel formatter apps. 4) Duplicates: merge best-of-both into one; never maintain two. 5) Motion flavour: high-tech computer. 6) Verdicts: score the built thing; excitement scores zero; later correction overrules earlier ask.
