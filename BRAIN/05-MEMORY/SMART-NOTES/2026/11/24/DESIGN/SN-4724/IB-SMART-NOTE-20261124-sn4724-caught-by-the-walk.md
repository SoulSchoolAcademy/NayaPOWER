First the frame: scorecarding starts from the ultimate answer — what are you ultimately trying to do? Figure that out first, then optimize for that. Then the protections: never remove what he didn't ask to remove — if the brief didn't instruct it, it stays; and never send in what isn't completed — unfinished text does not ship because a round is ending. The method is the message: he found every one of these by walking the live thing, in minutes, after the parts had all passed.

## NAYA

Live-walk catches distilled from SN-2997..SN-3006 (2026-11-16 evening): (1) SCORECARDING — THE ULTIMATE ANSWER FIRST: state what you are ultimately trying to do before optimizing anything. (2) DON'T REMOVE WHAT HE DIDN'T ASK TO REMOVE: un-instructed removal is a defect even when the result looks cleaner (case: the agreement's big play button). (3) DON'T SEND IN WHAT ISN'T COMPLETED: unfinalized work never ships at round end (case: text sent unfinalized after a voice-note glitch). (4) Sidebar items each carry an icon; the icon of the area being scrolled lights in that area's own colour. (5) MY WORLD PICKER MUST SWITCH: picking a world switches the feed to that world immediately; a no-op pick is a P1 catch. (6) RESTORE WHAT WAS GOOD — DON'T ITERATE ON IT: a replaced good element (case: the five-icon bottom bar) is restored verbatim, not redesigned. (7) ONE ICON THAT LIGHTS UP: never an icon rendered beside a separate light element. (8) THE EXTRA +: duplicate-purpose controls are decided by the mobile-fit test, not taste. (9) CREATE WORLD MUST ACTUALLY CREATE: the create action must produce the world and land the citizen in its feed; a modal that goes nowhere fails the surface. (10) BUTTONS NEVER CUT OFF THEIR TEXT: text truncation in any button is a defect at sight.

## MACHINE

1) Every scorecard opens with the ultimate aim in one sentence. 2) Diff against the brief: any un-instructed removal blocks the landing. 3) Unfinished content blocks the send, whatever the round state. 4) Pickers and create actions are verified by their visible effect, not their handler. 5) A replaced good element is restored byte-for-byte before any new idea is entertained. 6) Icon = one element that lights; no companion light elements. 7) Duplicate controls resolve by the mobile-fit test. 8) Any truncated button text fails the walk on sight.
