# The Day, Designed Whole

## IN A NUTSHELL

The day is the citizen's world, designed whole. It opens with what matters most: a notification bar first — inbox, messages, loves, comments, actions — across all three worlds, because a day that opens with anything else hides why you came. The greeting lives in the top bar with the date, warm the way the Hub's good morning is warm; the numbers stand as big blocks across the top; and the day's frequency is a real diamond — a spinning jewel of light, never a flat mark, never faster than twelve seconds a turn. The weekday stands as straight words in the day's color, light embedded in the letters, no button and no pill; all seven weekday names fit whole at phone and desktop width, because a cut-off word is an elegance failure. One headliner says the date once. Inside, the day's color fills the whole room — you are inside the day, not beside it — and the design is discerned, not copied: the reference is a lesson, and the question is always what is most intelligent here. The everyday world is seven simple things, each one tap away.
