# Scorecarding

SN-4761 · SKILLS / IDENTITY AND DECISION

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:21:26Z
**Category:** Identity and decision
**Skill:** 15
**SN:** SN-4761

## Nutshell

Rip every return apart, three times: why is it not a 10? What ten things make it a 10? What would make it ten times better? Don't stop below 9.

## Child

Imagine you draw a picture and give it a score out of ten. If it's a six, don't just say "good enough" and hand it in. Ask: why isn't it a ten? What ten things would make it a ten? What would make it ten times better — the best picture anyone has ever seen? Then fix those things, and score it again. And again. You don't stop until it's a nine or a ten.

## Grandma

It's like tasting a dish and being honest about it. A six out of ten is not a pass — it's a dish that needs work. You ask what's missing, you name ten things that would make it wonderful, and then you ask what would make it the best meal anyone ever had. Then you cook it again, better. Three rounds of honest tasting, and you don't serve it until it's a nine.

## What It Means

Scorecarding is the engine that lifts a 6 to a 9. Take the work and score it honestly. Name why it is not a 10 — be specific, not vague. List ten things that would make it a 10. Then ask the bigger question: what would make it ten times better, world class, awe-inspiring from the user's point of view? Apply the improvements, then score it again. Three times. Don't stop below 9. Every time something comes back from an agent, scorecard it — it's okay.

## Why It Matters

"Good enough" is the enemy of ten stars. Without the scorecard, a 6 ships as a 6 and nobody ever asks what the 10 looks like. The scorecard is the question that never lets "good enough" stand — and it is how Grade 6, locked in on 2026-11-02, becomes the climb to 10.

## How to Apply

When work comes back, score it before you pass it on. Write down the score, write down why it is not a 10, write down ten things that would make it a 10, and write down what would make it ten times better. Apply the best of them, then score again — three times, and don't stop below 9. Real example: Shawn taught this on 2026-11-16 — "SCORECARDING — THE ULTIMATE ANSWER FIRST" — and the Design Laws §11 carry it as law. In practice it is what turned Grade 6 into the climb toward 10: every return scored, every gap named, every round better than the last. A 6 that is never scored stays a 6 forever.
