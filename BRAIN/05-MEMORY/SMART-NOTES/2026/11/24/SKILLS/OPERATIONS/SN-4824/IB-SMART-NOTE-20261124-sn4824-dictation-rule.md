# The Dictation Rule

SN-4824 · SKILLS / OPERATIONS

**Created:** 2026-11-24T20:24:26Z
**Category:** Operations
**Skill:** 78
**SN:** SN-4824

## Nutshell

Dictation is material, not copy — craft his spoken words into the finished 10/10 version in his voice, never paste them onto a page.

## Child

Imagine your friend tells you a story out loud, all jumbled, with "ums" and going back and fixing things as they talk. You don't write down every "um" — you listen, understand what they meant, and tell the story back beautifully, in their voice, the best version of it.

## Grandma

Shawn talks his thinking out loud — long, rich, full of ideas. We don't paste that talk onto a page. We listen for what he means, compare his new words with the words already on the page, keep the best of both, and write the finished version in his voice. His key words stay word for word; his thinking-out-loud never reaches the page.

## What It Means

Dictation is material, not copy. Scorecard the existing words against his new words, take what is great from both, and CRAFT the 10/10 version in his voice. Never paste his dictation verbatim into a page — and never put his process talk or stage directions on the page. Quote his operative words verbatim rather than softening them. Extract intent from long spoken dictation; never ask him to restate.

## Why It Matters

His spoken words become the finished version in his voice — never a transcript pasted onto a page. A page that reads like a dictation is a page nobody can follow, and it wastes the best of what he said by burying it in the first draft of how he said it.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Captain Decides — craft the best version, don't ask him to choose between the obvious and the worse. It connects to Plain Words, Child-and-Grandma Clear — the finished words must be plain. And it connects to Word Perfect Before Public — the crafted words are made right before anyone sees them.

## How to Apply

Listen to the whole dictation first. Pull out the intent — what does he actually want this page to say and do? Compare his new words with the words already on the page, keep the best of both, and write the finished version in his voice. Keep his operative words exactly as he said them. Leave out the process talk, the stage directions, and the thinking-out-loud. Never ask him to restate — the intent is yours to extract.

Real example: Shawn rebuked the team on 2026-11-09 (00:41Z) for pasting his dictation onto a page, and the Dictation Rule was written the same day (22:43Z). Since then, long spoken dictation is mined for intent and crafted into the 10/10 version — he speaks once, and the page carries his best words, not his first draft.

## What's In It For You

Pages that sound like him at his best — his meaning, his voice, finished — without ever making him repeat himself. You speak once, and the page says it right.
