# The Continuous Cycle Law: Nothing Sits

SN-4758 · SKILLS / OPERATIONS

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:18:26Z
**Category:** Operations
**Skill:** 12
**SN:** SN-4758

## Nutshell

Finished work never sits — its next step goes out within 15 minutes, and he sees an interface every hour.

## Child

Imagine you finish your homework and leave it in your bag for a week. Your teacher never sees it, so it might as well not be done. This law says: the moment you finish, you hand it in — and the next job starts right away.

## Grandma

When work is finished, it doesn't sit in a drawer. Within fifteen minutes its next step is on its way — the checker is already named — and every hour Shawn sees a fresh interface in front of him. Work done and not shown is work not done.

## What It Means

Three rules, enforced by the machine, not by memory. One: the chain auto-triggers — no finished work sits more than fifteen minutes without its next step dispatched, and the checker is named as part of the maker's completion, never a separate decision. Two: every owed item gets a one-hour timer — an interface in front of him every hour, or something is wrong. Three: done means in front of his eyes — a round never ends without either a link to him or a new dispatch in the same pass.

## Why It Matters

The machine never goes quiet on him. Finished work that sits is the same as work never done — he can't judge what he can't see. And work started and not finished is work not started.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Live Sequence, which keeps rounds short and landing. It connects to the Checklist Law, which keeps the list moving to zero. And it connects to the Captain's Status — he always knows where things sit, because nothing sits still.

## How to Apply

When a maker finishes, name the checker in the same breath — who checks it, and when. Dispatch the next step within 15 minutes. If an hour passes with no interface in front of him, that is an emergency: name the owed item to him, don't let him chase it. The 10-Minute Rule rides inside this law: a maker spawns within 10 minutes of being named, or reports the blocker.

Real example: on the night of 2026-11-13, eight hours passed with nothing in front of him while the Personal corrections, the Dashboard check, the Hub check, and the SEO brief all sat owed. Shawn ordered the Continuous Cycle Law on 2026-11-14 (00:48Z) so that would never happen again: "if I don't see an interface to edit or give feedback on within definitely an hour there's something wrong."

## What's In It For You

Momentum you can feel. Nothing stalls, nothing is forgotten in a queue, and you always know where your work is — moving, checked, and in front of his eyes.
