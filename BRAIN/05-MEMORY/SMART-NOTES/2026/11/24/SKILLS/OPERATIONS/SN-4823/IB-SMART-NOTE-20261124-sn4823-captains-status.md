# The Captain's Status

SN-4823 · SKILLS / OPERATIONS

**Created:** 2026-11-24T20:23:26Z
**Category:** Operations
**Skill:** 77
**SN:** SN-4823

## Nutshell

When he asks what's going on, he gets what's ready, what's building, what's next — not a pile of links.

## Child

Imagine your teacher asks "how's the project going?" and you hand her a bag of fifty papers. She has to read them all to find out. A good answer is three short sentences: what's done, what's being worked on, what's next. That's the Captain's Status.

## Grandma

When Shawn asks what's going on, he gets one clear answer in one breath: what's in, what's done, what needs checking. He never gets a pile of links to untangle — links appear only where a finished thing needs his tap.

## What It Means

The Silver Platter format: what's IN · what's DONE · what NEEDS CHECKING. What's in is being worked now. What's done is finished and proven. What needs checking is waiting on his eye. The status is the answer; a link is never the answer itself.

## Why It Matters

He knows exactly where things stand in one breath. A status that makes him click ten links is not a status, it's a chore — and it spends the attention he should be spending on judging finished work.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Link Law — links are current and finished, never the status. It connects to the Continuous Cycle Law — he hears every hour. And it connects to Done-Proven Reporting — the done count comes first, proven in the system.

## How to Apply

Answer in three parts, every time: what's IN, what's DONE, what NEEDS CHECKING. Keep it short enough to read in one breath. Attach a link only to a finished thing that needs his tap — never as a substitute for saying where things stand.

Real example: Shawn set the Captain Rule on the voice call of 2026-11-24 (~10:33–10:34 MST): when he asks what's going on, he gets a captain's status, not a pile of links. The Silver Platter format — what's IN, what's DONE, what NEEDS CHECKING — is the shape of that answer, in use since 2026-11-21.

## What's In It For You

His trust, kept. He asks once, understands in one breath, and never has to chase the machine for where things sit. You get judged on the work, not on the pile of links around it.
