# Word Perfect Before Public

SN-4825 · SKILLS / OPERATIONS

**Created:** 2026-11-24T20:25:26Z
**Category:** Operations
**Skill:** 79
**SN:** SN-4825

## Nutshell

Text must be word-perfect before it goes public — the public never sees a draft.

## Child

Imagine writing a sign for the whole school to read, and you spell a word wrong. Everybody sees it. You can't un-show it. This law says: check every word before the sign goes up — not after.

## Grandma

Before any page goes out to the public, every word on it is checked and made right. No "we'll fix the words later" — later never comes, and the public already read the wrong ones.

## What It Means

Text must be word-perfect before it goes public. Ten-star service law. Every word on a public page has already been made right before the first citizen reads it.

## Why It Matters

One wrong word on a public page is seen by everyone, and it stays seen. The page is the promise — and a promise with a mistake in it is a promise broken in public.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Dictation Rule — crafted words are finished words. It connects to the Design-Laws Activation Habit — the check runs before the work ships. And it connects to the Awesome Law — below awesome doesn't ship, and wrong words are below awesome.

## How to Apply

Read every word on a public page before it goes live — out loud if you can, slowly, the way a stranger would read it. Fix spelling, fix meaning, fix anything that could be read two ways. Only when the words are right does the page go public.

Real example: Shawn taught this on the live call of 2026-11-22 (~12:48 MST) — text must be word-perfect before it goes public. It is the reason public pages now go through a word check before they go live, instead of being fixed after citizens have already read them.

## What's In It For You

Public pages the team never has to apologize for. The words are right the first time anyone reads them — and right words are the cheapest ten-star service there is.
