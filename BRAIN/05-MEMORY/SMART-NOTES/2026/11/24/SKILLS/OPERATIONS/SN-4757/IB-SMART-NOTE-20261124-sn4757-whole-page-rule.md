# The Whole-Page Rule

SN-4757 · SKILLS / OPERATIONS

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:17:26Z
**Category:** Operations
**Skill:** 11
**SN:** SN-4757

## Nutshell

A project comes to him whole or not at all — one message, one link, every item proven.

## Child

Imagine you build a whole puzzle, but you show your friend just one piece and say "look, I finished!" Your friend still has to wait for the rest of the puzzle. The Whole-Page Rule says: finish the whole puzzle first, then show it — all at once.

## Grandma

When a page has ten jobs on it, we don't show Shawn one finished job while the other nine are still open. We finish the whole page, prove every job on it, and then it comes to him in one message with one link.

## What It Means

Never show a single finished item alone when the rest of the list is still open on the same page. A one-item round on a page with nine open items is the wrong shape. The round's bullseye is the whole list, and the project arrives whole — or it does not arrive at all.

## Why It Matters

His attention is never spent assembling a finished project out of scattered pieces. He sees a page once, whole, and judges it once — instead of spending his time telling us about the nine things we haven't shown him yet.

## How It Connects

It is the Checklist Law's twin: the list is worked to zero, and the whole page is what comes back. It connects to the Link Law too — the one link he gets is current, finished, and proven.

## How to Apply

Even if one item on a page finishes early, keep it with the rest until the whole page is done. Then send one message, with one link, and every item on the page proven against it. If the rest of the page is still open, the finished item waits — it does not go to him alone.

Real example: on 2026-11-13 Shawn said, "you did one thing and sent it back." The points pill — one item of his Personal list — was shown alone while the plus, the lines, the feed, and the membership were still undone. The Whole-Page Rule stops that: the one thing waits until its brothers and sisters are done, and they all arrive together.

## What's In It For You

Fewer trips back to him, and every trip counts. You see the finished page once, whole, instead of collecting pieces and wondering what's still missing.
