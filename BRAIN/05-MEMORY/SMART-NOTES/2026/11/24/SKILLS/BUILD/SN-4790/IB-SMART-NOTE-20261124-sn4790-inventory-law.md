## Nutshell

Keep a list of every part of every page, and check it every time — a missing part is a fail, caught before his eye, not after.

## Child

Imagine you have a puzzle, and you count the pieces before you start: one, two, three… all one hundred. When you finish, you count again — if a piece is missing, you know straight away! Without counting, you'd never know a piece had gone missing until someone noticed the hole.

## Grandma

Dear, it's like the pantry list on the fridge. You write down everything that's supposed to be in there — the flour, the sugar, the salt. Before you bake, you check the list against the shelves. If the sugar is gone, you know before you start, not halfway through the cake.

## What It Means

Keep inventory of every element that makes up each thing — the Hub, the Personal, every page. Know every part of it, the anatomy of all of it, and always be checking: is it all there? A missing part is a fail, caught before it reaches his eye, not after. Parts come only from the official parts registry — a part that isn't in it is a fail, never invented.

## Why It Matters

Things stop drifting backwards and getting forgotten. A page is a whole made of named parts — if you don't know the parts, you can't know when one has gone missing. The frame going missing from the Hub unnoticed is the proof of why it matters.

## How It Connects

It is the checklist behind Harvest First, the Frame Library, and Compose, Never Create — the inventory is what tells you which parts exist to harvest and compose. It guards the No-Redesign Law: the inventory says what is supposed to be there, so an unasked removal is caught. And it serves Verify the Rendered Experience: the render check walks the inventory, part by part.

## How to Apply

List every element of each page, by name, from the official parts registry. Before anything goes to Shawn, check the list against the page: is every part there, in its place? A missing part stops the round. A part not in the registry is never used. Keep the list current — when a part is added or changed, the inventory changes with it.

## What's In It For You

For Shawn: pages stop losing pieces silently — what he saw yesterday is still there today. For us: the missing part gets caught in the workshop, not on his screen. The real-world proof: Shawn charged this on 2026-11-21: "sometimes you just go backwards or you forget stuff." The Hub frame went missing unnoticed — nobody had a list that said the frame was supposed to be there, so nobody noticed it was gone. The inventory is that list.
