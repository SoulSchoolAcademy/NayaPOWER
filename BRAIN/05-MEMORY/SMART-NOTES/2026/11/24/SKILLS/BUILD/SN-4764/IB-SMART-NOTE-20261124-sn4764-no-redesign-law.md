# The No-Redesign Law

SN-4764 · SKILLS / BUILD

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:24:26Z
**Category:** Build
**Skill:** 18
**SN:** SN-4764

## Nutshell

Never redesign his pages. Change only what he named — don't remove, move, or touch anything else.

## Child

Imagine your friend asks you to colour one flower red in their drawing — and you decide to redraw the whole picture your own way. They'd be upset! They only asked for one flower. You colour that flower, and you leave everything else exactly as they drew it.

## Grandma

Dear, it's like someone asking you to hem a dress. You hem the dress — you don't restyle the sleeves, move the buttons, and change the colour while you're at it. They asked for a hem. You do the hem, beautifully, and the dress stays their dress.

## What It Means

Never redesign his system or pages. Don't remove what he didn't ask to remove. Don't move what he didn't ask to move. Don't touch what he didn't ask to touch. Change only what he named. If a law and a brief conflict, the law wins and the designer stops and names it.

## Why It Matters

His pages stay his pages. Every change is a change he asked for — never a surprise redesign he has to discover and undo. A quiet "improvement" nobody asked for is not a gift; it is work he has to spend his time reversing.

## How It Connects

It is the guardrail around Harvest First and the Frame Library: we reuse his parts and change only what he named. It pairs with the Sense Check — the sense check catches what the brief missed, this law stops the designer from "improving" what the brief never mentioned. And it serves His Eye Is the Final Gate: his eye expects his page, not a stranger's version of it.

## How to Apply

Read the brief, list exactly what it names, and change only those things. Everything else stays exactly where it is, exactly as it is. If something in the brief would break a design law, stop and say so — the law wins. If you see something else that needs fixing, name it to the captain; don't fix it silently inside someone else's brief.

## What's In It For You

For Shawn: he never opens a page and finds something moved, removed, or restyled that he never asked for. For us: no wasted rounds undoing surprise redesigns. The real-world proof: the law dates to 2026-11-01 (No-Redesign + Read-Before-Design Gate), sharpened by the Change-Only-What-He-Named Rule on 2026-11-22. The failure it stops is the quiet redesign — a maker "tidying" a page and moving a button he placed deliberately.
