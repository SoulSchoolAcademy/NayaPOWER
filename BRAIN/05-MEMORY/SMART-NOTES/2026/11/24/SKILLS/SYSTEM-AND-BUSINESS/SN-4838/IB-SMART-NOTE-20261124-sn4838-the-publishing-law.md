# The Publishing Law

SN-4838 · SKILLS / SYSTEM AND BUSINESS

**Created:** 2026-11-24T20:38:26Z
**Category:** System and business
**Skill:** 92
**SN:** SN-4838

## Nutshell

Private is live at once, no approval. Public must be checked and verified first — the right speed for each.

## Child

Imagine a notebook: what you write for yourself is yours the second you write it. But if you want to pin it on the school noticeboard for everyone to read, a teacher checks it first to make sure it's safe and kind.

## Grandma

Think of a letter: a note to yourself goes straight into your drawer. A letter to the whole village goes through the post office, where it's checked before it goes out to everyone.

## What It Means

Private = auto live, immediately, no approval. Public = must be approved/verified by the network. At first we manually verify their pages; eventually auto-verify — an automated process scans their URL for errors, bad code, foul language; if it passes the safety test, it goes live.

## Why It Matters

Private things should be instant — a person writing for themselves should never wait. Public things should be safe — what the whole network can see must be checked first. One speed for both would be wrong in both directions.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Privacy Law, because private is private — instant, and never read. It connects to Membership + Payments, because a member's public page is one of the things that must be verified before it goes live. It connects to the Two Vaults, because what is published publicly is part of what the collective can see, while the personal vault stays locked.

## How to Apply

When a citizen publishes something private, make it live immediately — no queue, no approval step. When they publish something public, hold it for verification: by hand at first, and later with an automatic scan that checks for errors, bad code and foul language. If it passes the safety test, it goes live; if it fails, it stays back and the citizen is told why.

## What's In It For You

Your private publishing is instant, so the system feels alive the moment you use it — and the public network stays clean and safe, so trust in what people see never breaks.

**Example (illustrative):** A citizen writes a note for herself at breakfast and it is live on her own page before her coffee cools, no waiting, no approval. That same afternoon she publishes her business page to the public network: it waits, a check scans it for errors and bad language, it passes, and it goes live for everyone. Private was instant; public was safe. That is the two speeds the law was recorded to keep apart.
