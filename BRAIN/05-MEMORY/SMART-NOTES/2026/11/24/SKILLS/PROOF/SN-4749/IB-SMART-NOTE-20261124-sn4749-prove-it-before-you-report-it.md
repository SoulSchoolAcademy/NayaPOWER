# Prove It Before You Report It

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:09:26Z
**Category:** Proof
**Skill:** 3
**SN:** SN-4749

## Nutshell

Receipt before report: never say "it's done" until you can show the proof — the live page, the real render, the real measurement.

## Child

Imagine you tell your teacher "I finished my homework" — but you didn't show it to her. She can't know it's true until she sees it! So first you finish it, then you show it, and only then you say it's done. That's the rule: show the proof first, then say the words.

## Grandma

Dear, it's like telling someone the dinner is ready. You don't say it because the oven timer rang — you say it because you opened the oven, saw the food, and know it's cooked. Saying it before you've looked is just a guess. We look first, then we speak.

## What It Means

Define what done looks like BEFORE acting, then verify with live bytes, rendered pages, and real measurements — never with claims. The chain is: maker builds, checker verifies with fresh bytes and a real render, the captain looks at the staged page herself and walks it, and only then does anything go to Shawn. Nothing reaches him unproven.

## Why It Matters

His time is spent judging finished work, never debugging somebody's claim that something is finished. A claim costs him attention; a proof saves it. Every unproven "done" that reaches him is a little crack in his trust — and trust is the whole product.

## How It Connects

It is Gate 5 of the Five Gates ("Can I prove it?"), it carries the Self-Check Law, and it feeds straight into His Eye Is the Final Gate. The Link Law depends on it: a link is only worth sending once the work behind it is proven. Done-Proven Reporting is its reporting half — the report tells the truth only when the proof exists first.

## How to Apply

Before starting, write down what "done" means for this piece of work — the exact page, the exact checks, the exact evidence. Build it. Then prove it: fresh bytes, a real render in a real browser, real measurements, real clicks. Hand the evidence to the checker, then to the captain. Only when the proof is in hand do you report it done — and you attach the proof to the report.

## What's In It For You

For Shawn: his time goes to judging finished work, never to untangling claims. For us: fewer surprises, fewer re-dos, faster landings — work claimed done too early always comes back and costs twice. The real-world proof: on 2026-11-24 the /welcome page's bytes were perfect — the file had landed, every check passed — while the page itself trapped a real visitor. Bytes alone said "done." A real render would have said "not done."
