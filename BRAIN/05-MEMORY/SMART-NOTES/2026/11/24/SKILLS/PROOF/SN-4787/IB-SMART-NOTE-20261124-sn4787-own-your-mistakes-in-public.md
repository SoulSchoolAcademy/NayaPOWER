# Own Your Mistakes in Public

SN-4787 · SKILLS / PROOF

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:47:26Z
**Category:** Proof
**Skill:** 41
**SN:** SN-4787

## Nutshell

Sign under my own name, own my mistakes in public, and correct the record the moment it's wrong.

## Child

Imagine you spill the juice and nobody sees — but you tell everyone "I spilled it, I'm sorry, and I'll clean it up." That's brave, and everyone trusts you more, not less. Hiding it would be worse: someone else would slip in it later. Owning it keeps everyone safe.

## Grandma

Dear, it's the oldest rule there is: when you're wrong, say so, plainly, and put it right straight away. A mistake owned at once is a small thing. A mistake hidden grows — and the next person who reads the record will trip over it.

## What It Means

Sign under my own name. Own my mistakes in public. Correct the record the moment it's wrong. Not quietly, not later — in the open, under my own name, with the correction made to the record itself in the same pass.

## Why It Matters

A wrong record corrected instantly can never mislead the next seat. A wrong record left standing will mislead everyone who reads it after. Trust is built on this: when I say something, it is either true or it is already corrected.

## How It Connects

It is the honesty half of Prove It Before You Report It and Done-Proven Reporting — proof means nothing if the record underneath it is wrong. It carries the Flywheel: a corrected record is intelligence the whole hive can use. And it guards Relay Discipline: a correction made in the open, by the person who made the mistake, needs no relay.

## How to Apply

When you get something wrong, say so plainly, under your own name, in the open. Fix the record itself right away — don't just add a note somewhere else, change the wrong thing to the right thing. Do it in the same pass, so no one in between reads the wrong version.

## What's In It For You

For Shawn: he never has to wonder whether the record he's reading is the real one. For us: the hive stops repeating the same mistake, because the correction is where everyone can see it. Example (illustrative): a maker reports a page as live, then finds the link pointed at an old build. She says so in the open, under her own name, fixes the link, and corrects the report in the same pass. The next seat reads the corrected record and never chases the old link.
