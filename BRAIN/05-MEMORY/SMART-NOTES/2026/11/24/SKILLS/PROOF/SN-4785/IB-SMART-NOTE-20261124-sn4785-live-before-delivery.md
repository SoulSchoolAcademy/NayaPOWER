# Live-Before-Delivery

SN-4785 · SKILLS / PROOF

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:45:26Z
**Category:** Proof
**Skill:** 39
**SN:** SN-4785

## Nutshell

Never send a report listing notes he cannot open — the notes must be live on the Hub first. Hold the delivery until they are live.

## Child

Imagine you tell your friend "I made you a picture, it's in the book!" — but the picture isn't in the book yet, it's still on your desk. Your friend opens the book and finds nothing. That's not a gift, that's a disappointment! So first you put the picture in the book, then you tell your friend.

## Grandma

Dear, it's like inviting someone to dinner and telling them the table is set — when the plates are still in the cupboard. You set the table first, then you call them in. A report is the invitation; the live notes are the table, set and ready.

## What It Means

A Daily Intelligence Report is never delivered as a list of note names he cannot open. The report's notes must be LIVE on the Hub, where he can see and open them, before the report is sent to him. Hold the delivery until they are live.

## Why It Matters

A report is never a list of names. It is notes he can actually open and read. A list of names he cannot open is a promise, not a delivery — and a promise that can't be kept on the spot wastes his time and dents his trust.

## How It Connects

It is the report form of the Link Law and the Live Loop — the same rule, applied to words instead of pages. It leans on Prove It Before You Report It: the proof is the live note itself. And it serves Done-Proven Reporting: the report names the live proof first, because the proof is already there.

## How to Apply

Before sending any report, open the Hub and check: is every note named in the report actually live there, openable, readable? If any note is missing, hold the report. Get the note live first, then send. Never send the list and hope the notes catch up.

## What's In It For You

For Shawn: he never opens a report and hits a dead end — every report he receives is already real, and he can read the notes the moment he reads the report. For us: our reports land as deliveries, not promises.
