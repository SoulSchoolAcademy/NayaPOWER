## Nutshell

Before anything merges, the maker checks her own work three times and answers seven questions in writing — then the captain checks it again. Only then does it merge.

## Child

Imagine you build a Lego castle and before you show it, you check it three times yourself: are all the pieces on? Is it standing straight? Did I follow the picture? Then your big sister checks it too. Only then do you put it on the shelf. Two pairs of eyes, three checks — that's how nothing wobbly gets through.

## Grandma

Dear, it's like sending a letter. You read it over once, then again, then a third time — and you answer, in writing, whether it's true, whether it's complete, whether it's ready. Then someone you trust reads it too. Only then does it go in the post. Nothing slips out unchecked.

## What It Means

The maker checks three times and answers the seven questions in writing, the captain double-checks, the maker merges. Merges follow this law, never a casual ship. The seven questions are the self-examination: is it right, is it complete, is it proven, is it in the right place, does it follow the laws, is it the best version, is it ready for his eye.

## Why It Matters

The merge itself is gated by written self-examination — nothing slips into the main line unchecked, and nobody merges on a feeling. A casual merge can break the live site for every citizen; a written check stops that before it happens.

## How It Connects

It is the merge gate inside Prove It Before You Report It, and the discipline behind Scorecarding. It pairs with His Eye Is the Final Gate: the machine checks itself twice before his eye is ever asked to judge. And it protects the whole Build lane — a bad merge can undo a harvested frame or a proven part in one careless click.

## How to Apply

Before merging, score your work three times. Answer the seven questions in writing — not in your head, on the record. Hand it to the captain for an independent double-check. If either check fails, the work goes back; it does not merge. Never merge on a feeling, never merge because the round is running late.

## What's In It For You

For Shawn: the main line stays clean, and nothing unproven ever reaches the live site. For us: bad work is caught by the person who built it or by the captain — never by Shawn, and never by a citizen.
