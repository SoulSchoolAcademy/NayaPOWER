## Nutshell

Use the whole page — never leave most of it blank — and keep everything that belongs together in one area, not scattered across a thousand pages.

## Child

Imagine you bought a big piece of land to build a house, but you only built on one little corner and left the rest empty and bare. That would be silly — you bought the whole land, so build on the whole land! And imagine all the rooms of your house in one house, not one room here, one room across town, and one room somewhere else. One house, all the rooms together. That's what a page should be.

## Grandma

Think of a home that is lived in — every room full, warm, and used, nothing empty, nothing bare. And think of everything you need in one place: the kitchen, the table, the cups, all together in one room, not scattered across the neighbourhood. A page should feel like that home: full, alive, and all in one place.

## What It Means

Build on the whole land: never a third built and the rest left blank, never buttons floating on empty space. And one AREA holds everything relevant to that page — the buttons inside it open views within that area, instead of sending the person off to a thousand top-level pages done wrong. It is the opposite of one endless scroll pretending to be unity.

## Why It Matters

A half-empty page looks unfinished, and a thousand scattered pages confuse everyone. A full page with one clear area feels alive, rich, and intentional — like a home that is lived in. People feel the difference in one glance: this place is cared for, and I know where I am.

## How It Connects

It connects to Fit-Perfect, because the area fits whole on the screen; to the Cube, because views stack behind inside the area; and to the One-Area Law it came from. It is what makes a page feel like a place instead of a signpost.

## How to Apply

Plan each page as one area that holds everything relevant to it. Fill the whole screen with real content — no big blank patches, no lonely buttons. Inside the area, buttons open views — the same room, seen a different way — instead of sending the person to a whole new page.

Example (illustrative): a Business page that once showed three small buttons floating on a big blank screen is rebuilt so the whole screen is alive — the business's world, its offers, its people, all inside one area, with buttons that open each view right there. The same page, but now it feels like a place, not a signpost.

## What's In It For You

Every page feels full and worth visiting, and you always know where you are — one area, everything in it, nothing scattered. You never open a page and wonder if it's finished, because it looks and feels finished.
