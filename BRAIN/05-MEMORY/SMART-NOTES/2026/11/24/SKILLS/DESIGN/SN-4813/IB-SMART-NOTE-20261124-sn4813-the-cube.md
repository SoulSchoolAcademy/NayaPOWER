## Nutshell

Tap the N or the board and the next full board sits directly behind it — three layers deep, three across — and going back on one board never resets the rest.

## Child

Imagine a cube made of cards, like a Rubik's Cube. You tap a card and a whole new card appears right behind it — same size, same look, just the next one. You can go three cards deep, and three cards across. And every card has its own little back button: if you go back on one card, only THAT card goes back — the rest stay exactly where they were. Nothing ever resets. One tap, one card. Easy!

## Grandma

Think of a stack of beautiful photograph boards, one behind the other. You tap the top one and the next board slides into view — whole and complete, looking just the same. Each board has its own little button to go back, and pressing it only takes that one board back — it never knocks the whole stack over. Simple, steady, and you always know where you are.

## What It Means

Tap the N or tap the board, and the next FULL board sits directly behind, looking exactly the same, same formatting, no difference — three layers deep, three across. The slots alternate image and text as you go in. Every board gets its own little back or close button, and boards are INDEPENDENT: back on one board moves only that board back one level, never all boards, never resets the presentation. And the N never goes to a video.

## Why It Matters

The tap itself is the innovation. A whole presentation becomes one gesture, learned in a second — tap, and the next board is there, whole and familiar. Nothing to learn, nothing to get lost in. And because the boards are independent, nobody ever loses their place by tapping back.

## How It Connects

It connects to Fit-Perfect, because every board fits whole; to the Mirror Pattern, because the alternating image-text rhythm runs as you go in; and to the One Bar, because the N is the tap that opens the cube and the bar is what brings you home.

## How to Apply

Stack boards three deep and three across, each one a full board in the same format, alternating image and text. Give each board its own back button that moves only that board. Tap the N or the board to go in; tap back to come out — one board at a time. Never let back on one board touch the others, and never send the N to a video.

You can operate a whole presentation with one gesture you learn in a second — tap, and the next board is there. You never lose your place, because going back only ever moves one board. It feels like a real object in your hands: solid, simple, and always doing exactly what you expect.
