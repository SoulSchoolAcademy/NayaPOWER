# Auto-Optimization of the Viewport

SN-4802 · SKILLS / DESIGN

**Created:** 2026-11-24T20:02:26Z
**Category:** Design
**Skill:** 56
**SN:** SN-4802

## Nutshell

As you scroll, the page turns to frame exactly what you're looking at — perfectly, like a machine turning to face you.

## Child

Imagine a toy camera that follows you around the room, always keeping you in the middle of the picture. Wherever you go, the picture fits you perfectly. Our pages are like that camera: wherever you scroll, the page frames what you're looking at.

## Grandma

Think of a good stage play: the spotlight always finds the actor who is speaking, and the stage turns so you can see them properly. You never have to lean or stretch to see what's happening. Our pages are a stage like that — the light and the frame follow your eyes.

## What It Means

Auto-Optimization of the Viewport means: as the citizen scrolls, the view zooms into their viewport, into their point of view. Each page fits perfectly above the fold where they are viewing — the page arranges itself around the person looking at it. It feels MECHANICAL: you see the screen coming into your viewport and then going away, like a machine turning. The matrix turns like a Rubik's cube and the viewport follows your gaze. Shawn made this official on 2026-11-18 at about 22:25Z — "make it official" — and it lives in Design Laws §9.

## Why It Matters

What the citizen is looking at is always perfectly framed — never half off the screen, never lost below the fold. A page that fits where you are feels alive; a page you have to chase feels broken. The person is the centre, and the page arranges itself around them.

## How It Connects

It connects to Fit-Perfect + No-Scroll (every screen fits whole in its frame), to the Cube (the matrix turns, and the viewport follows), and to Mobile First (the phone screen is where the framing matters most). The viewport is the stage; the page performs on it.

## How to Apply

Design each screen to fit whole in the viewport it will be seen in, and let the view follow the scroll: as the citizen moves, the screen zooms into their point of view and frames the piece they are looking at, then turns to the next like a machine. Test it by scrolling yourself — if anything you need is cut in half at the edge of the screen, the framing has failed. Example (illustrative) — a citizen scrolling a long page on her phone does not find the next board cut in half at the bottom of the screen; the view turns and frames it whole, like a machine turning a cube to face her, and she keeps reading without ever adjusting anything herself.

## What's In It For You

You never lose your place and never hunt for the thing you were reading. The screen meets you where you are, every time — framed, whole, and waiting.
