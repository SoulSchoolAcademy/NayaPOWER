## Nutshell

Every board is image, text, image — and the next board flips it to text, image, text, back and forth like a mirror, so the pattern itself tells the story.

## Child

Imagine a row of tiles that goes red, blue, red — and the next row goes blue, red, blue. They match, but flipped, like looking in a mirror. That's the mirror pattern. Every board is picture, words, picture — and the next one is words, picture, words. Click a picture and you get its words; click the words and you get their picture. Back and forth, like a game.

## Grandma

Think of a quilt where the squares alternate — one row light, dark, light, the next dark, light, dark. The pattern is pleasing to the eye before you even study any single square. That's how these boards work: the rhythm is beautiful on its own, and the story unfolds inside it, one square at a time.

## What It Means

Every board is one, two, three: image, text, image. The next frame is text, image, text on the outsides — back and forth, reverse matching, like a Rubik's Cube or tic-tac-toe, keeping symmetry, looking beautiful. Click an image and it goes to that text; click that text and it goes to that image — two mirrors, every board one or the other of these two.

## Why It Matters

Symmetry is something the eye can feel even before the mind names it. A mirrored layout looks beautiful and tells the story in layers — the pattern itself does part of the talking, so the reader understands the shape of the story before reading a single word.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Cube, because boards stack behind each other in the same pattern; to the Chapter Method, because each chapter follows the same rhythm; and to the Real Object Law, because the pattern makes the boards feel like real things in a real arrangement.

## How to Apply

Lay out every board as either image-text-image or text-image-text, and alternate as you go. Link each image to its text and each text to its image, so the two mirror each other. Check the whole sequence at a glance: the rhythm should be visible before any word is read.

## What's In It For You

You get presentations with a rhythm your eye trusts, so the story lands before a single word is read. The pattern is always there, steady and beautiful, and you always know where you are in the story — because the mirror shows you.
