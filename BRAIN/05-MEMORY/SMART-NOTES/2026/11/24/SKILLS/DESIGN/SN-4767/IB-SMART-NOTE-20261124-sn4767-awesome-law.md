# The Awesome Law

## Nutshell

If it's not utterly awesome, it isn't. Period — it doesn't ship, doesn't stay, doesn't count.

## Child

Imagine you draw a picture for your mom. If it's just okay, you might keep it. But if it's the most beautiful picture you ever drew, you run to show her right away. Our rule is: only the run-to-show-her pictures go out. The just-okay ones go back until they're beautiful.

## Grandma

Think of baking for guests. You wouldn't serve a cake that came out flat and say "it's fine." You'd bake it again until it was a cake worth putting on the table. That's this law: nothing reaches the table unless it's utterly awesome.

## What It Means

The Awesome Law is the bar in one sentence: "If it's not utterly awesome, it isn't. Period." Anything below utterly awesome doesn't ship, doesn't stay, doesn't count. There is no negotiation with it and no "good enough" that survives the day. Shawn gave this law on 2026-10-29, and answered it himself with "1000%."

## Why It Matters

Without a bar, almost-good work piles up and the standard quietly sinks — a little worse each time, until nobody remembers what excellent looked like. One sentence stops that slide. It gives every maker, checker and captain the same instant test, and it protects Shawn's time: he only ever sees work that already passed the highest bar we know.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Five Gates (the check before acting — "is it right?" is the Awesome Law's cousin), to Scorecarding (why is it not a 10, what makes it a 10), and to the Real Object Law and the Emblem, which say what awesome *looks like*. The Awesome Law is the bar; the other laws are the way to reach it. It also connects to His Eye Is the Final Gate — the eye is the last judge of whether something is truly awesome.

## How to Apply

Hold every piece of work up to the one question: is this utterly awesome? If yes, it goes out. If no, it goes back — fixed, rebuilt, or dropped — until it is, or it doesn't ship at all. Don't argue it up from a 7 to a pass; scorecard it to a 10 or let it go. Real event: since Shawn gave this law on 2026-10-29, a page that is merely fine has not shipped — one sentence has stopped more almost-good work from going out than any long checklist could.

## What's In It For You

You never have to wade through almost-good work. Everything that reaches you — every page, every note, every button — has already passed the highest bar we know, so your time is spent enjoying the best, not sorting the mediocre.
