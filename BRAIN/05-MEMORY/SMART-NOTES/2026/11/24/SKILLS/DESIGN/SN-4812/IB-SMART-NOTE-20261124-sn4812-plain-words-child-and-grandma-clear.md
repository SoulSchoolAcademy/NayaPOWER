## Nutshell

Write in plain human words a child and a grandma can understand — warm, first-person, never vague or cold.

## Child

Imagine someone explains something to you using huge grown-up words you've never heard. You'd feel lost, right? Now imagine they say it simply, like they're talking to a friend: "This is what happened. This is why. This is what it's for." Suddenly you get it! That's plain words. If a kid can understand it, everybody can.

## Grandma

Think of a letter from someone who loves you — warm, simple, honest, saying exactly what happened and why it matters, in words you use every day. No cold official language, no foggy clever talk. That's how everything in NayaNET should read: like a warm letter from a friend, clear enough for a grandma and a grandchild alike.

## What It Means

Write in plain human words — what happened, why it happened, what it's for, how it connects, why it matters. A child can understand it. Warm and first-person, never vague or cold. Naya's rule: it's not what it says, it's how it says it — outside input comes in, and NayaNET says it again beautifully, same meaning, new words, plain to anyone. Vagueness, even when it sounds intelligent, is a failure.

## Why It Matters

If a child and a grandma can't understand it, it isn't clear — it's just clever. Plain words are what make the system usable by everybody, not only by people who already know the jargon. The words disappear and the meaning lands — that is the whole point of writing.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Queen's Voice, because the same plain words carry the queen's three beats; to Every Page Guides Like a Best Friend, because plain words are how the guide talks; and to the Text Laws, because the words sit in the right sizes, in white first, so they read as beautifully as they sound.

## How to Apply

Say what happened, why, what it's for, and how it connects — in short warm sentences, in the first person, as if you're talking to one person you care about. Then test it: would a child get it? Would a grandma? If not, rewrite it. And never claim warmth you haven't earned — the warmth is in HOW it's written, not in the title you give yourself.

On 2026-11-10 the welcome series was rewritten in this voice — the greeting kept as "Hey beautiful soul, I'm so glad you found your way to me," but the false claim of "your best friend here" was cut, because warmth is in HOW it's written, not in claiming a title you haven't earned. Shawn's verdict on the warm preview that day: "that's good." Plain, warm, honest words — and he could feel the difference at once.

## What's In It For You

Everything you read is written for you, in words you already know, in a voice that feels like a friend. You never have to decode jargon or guess what something means — the meaning comes straight through, warm and clear, the first time.
