## Nutshell

Beauty nobody uses is just decoration — keep the full version, and give three ways in: visual, text, and skip to the end.

## Child

Imagine a beautiful book that is so long nobody ever finishes it. It's pretty, but nobody reads it — so what's the point? Now imagine the same book, but you can watch it as a movie, read it as words, or jump straight to the ending to see how it turns out. Now everybody can enjoy it their own way! That's the headlights principle: make it beautiful AND make it usable.

## Grandma

Think of headlights on a car: they have to look lovely, but first they have to light the road — if they don't help you see where you're going, how pretty they are doesn't matter at all. In the same way, our work has to be beautiful AND useful. If nobody reads it, the beauty was wasted. So we keep the whole thing, and we give people three doors in — pictures, words, or straight to the end.

## What It Means

Balance effectiveness and beauty: if they're not going to read it, it doesn't matter how beautiful it is. Nobody reads two hours end to end — so keep the full version anyway, and give three ways in: visual, text, and skip to the end. Beauty and use, together, never one without the other.

## Why It Matters

Beauty nobody uses is decoration, and a useful thing nobody enjoys is a chore. The principle keeps us honest: the beautiful thing must also be usable, and the usable thing must also be beautiful. One without the other is half a job.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Unfold, because the full version arrives in small pieces; to the Queen's Voice, because three sentences answer the reader's three questions; and to the Awesome Law, because the bar stays high — beauty is never the excuse for cutting the real content.

## How to Apply

Always build the full version — the complete story, kept whole. Then build three doors into it: a visual way in, a text way in, and a straight jump to the end. Let the reader pick the door that fits them, and never force everyone through the same long road.

Example (illustrative): a two-hour presentation of the whole NayaNET story is kept complete, but a visitor is offered three ways in — watch the visual version, read the words, or jump straight to the ending that says what it all means for them. The person who only has five minutes still leaves understanding it, and the person who wants everything can still have everything.

## What's In It For You

Long work actually gets read, because you can enter it your own way — pictures, words, or the ending first. And the full version is still there for the day you want all of it. You never have to choose between beautiful and useful, because you get both.
