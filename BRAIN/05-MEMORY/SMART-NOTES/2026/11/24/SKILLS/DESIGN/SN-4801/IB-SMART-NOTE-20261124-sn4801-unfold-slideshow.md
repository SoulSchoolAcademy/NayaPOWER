# The Unfold + The Slideshow

## Nutshell

Three sentences per board, one idea each, three boards then a button — long ideas become a walk, never a wall.

## Child

Imagine a storybook. You don't get the whole story on one page — you get a little bit, then you turn the page, then a little more. Turning the page is the fun part. Our long pages work like a storybook: a little bit, then a tap, then a little more.

## Grandma

Think of a good teacher explaining something hard. She doesn't say it all in one breath — she says one thing, waits for you to follow, then says the next. Our pages teach like that teacher: one idea at a time, at your own pace.

## What It Means

The Unfold + The Slideshow means: three sentences per board, one idea each. Three boards, then a button. The two official formats: THE UNFOLD (press the button, the next piece unfolds down the page) and THE SLIDESHOW (press Next, the next screen slides in). Bits at a time, not the whole page or book — human psychology: people can only take in so much at once. Legal pages unfold three boards at a time like everything else — never a wall because "it's legal." It lives in Design Laws §6 and §9, and the Reader Unfold was set on 2026-11-16.

## Why It Matters

A long text becomes a walk instead of a wall. Anyone can follow it, one idea at a time — and nobody is asked to swallow a whole book in one screen. Walls make people leave; walks make people finish.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Intelligent Block Anatomy (the anatomy is the shape of a note; the unfold and the slideshow are how a long note is walked), to Minimum Text (each board carries only a few words), and to Every Page Guides Like a Best Friend (Step 1, Step 2, Step 3, the button — the walk itself is the guide). Shape, fewness and guidance together make a long idea finishable.

## How to Apply

Break the text into boards of three sentences, one idea per sentence. Show three boards, then a button. In the Unfold, the button opens the next piece below; in the Slideshow, Next slides the next screen in. The reader walks forward at their own pace, never facing a wall. And never excuse a wall: even legal pages unfold three boards at a time. Example (illustrative) — a citizen opening the agreement does not meet a wall of legal text; she meets three short boards, taps, and the next three unfold. She reaches the end because the walk let her.

## What's In It For You

Long ideas — agreements, lessons, legal pages — that you actually finish, because each step is small and the next step is always one obvious tap away. You never face a wall again.
