# The Intelligent Block Anatomy

## Nutshell

Every smart note is built on the same spine, in the same order, with every part truly filled — so a child and a grandma can both understand it in seconds.

## Child

Imagine a story told in steps: first the tiny version, then the easy version, then what it means, then why it's important, then how to use it. Every story is told the same way, so you always know where to look. That's the anatomy — a story-shape that never changes.

## Grandma

Think of a good recipe card: it always starts with the name of the dish, then what it is, then why you'd make it, then how it connects to the rest of the meal, then the steps, then what you get at the end. You never have to hunt for the steps — they're always in the same place. Our smart notes are recipe cards like that.

## What It Means

The Intelligent Block Anatomy is the spine every smart note is built on, in this order: Nutshell (the Human note, ONE section, truly short) → What It's Not → Child → Grandma → What It Means → Why It Matters → How It Connects → How to Apply → What's In It For You. Every section is filled with real words — a placeholder is a hole, never a section. "See more" appears only when there is more. The format is a system format, shaped by the machine itself, never pasted in by hand. It lives in Design Laws §16.

## Why It Matters

It is how intelligence becomes understandable to a child and a grandma. It is the core content format of the whole network — the way every lesson, every skill, every idea is told. Without the anatomy, notes drift into long walls of text that only the writer understands. With it, any note can be opened by anyone and understood in seconds.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Queen's Voice (three sentences: what it is, why it matters, what's in it for you), to Plain Words (child-and-grandma clear), and to the Unfold and the Slideshow, which are the two ways a long note is walked through bit by bit. The anatomy is the shape; the unfold and the slideshow are the walk. It also connects to the Design-Laws Activation Habit — the anatomy is one of the laws a seat reads before building.

## How to Apply

Write a note by walking the spine, in order, and never skipping a section. Start with the Nutshell — one or two sentences, the whole thing in a nutshell. Then say what it is not, then explain it for a child, then for a grandma, then what it means, why it matters, how it connects, how to apply it, and what's in it for the reader. Read each section back and ask: is this real text, or a placeholder wearing a section's name? Real event: on 2026-11-21 Shawn corrected a note round because the why-it-matters, how-it-connects and how-to-apply sections had been left as placeholder text — and on the call on 2026-11-24 he re-affirmed it, adding that the stars must be candy-apple purple, not light pink. The anatomy only works when every section is truly filled.

## What's In It For You

Anybody — a child, a grandma, a stranger walking in cold — can open a smart note and understand it in seconds. Intelligence stops being locked up in long pages and starts being something anyone can use, including you.
