# The Real Object Law

SN-4752 · SKILLS / DESIGN

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:12:26Z
**Category:** Design
**Skill:** 6
**SN:** SN-4752

## Nutshell

Every thing on a page is a real physical object you could pick up and touch — not a flat picture of one.

## Child

Imagine your toys. A toy car is a real thing — you can hold it, it has shiny paint, it casts a little shadow on the table. Now imagine a drawing of a toy car on paper. It looks like a car, but you can't pick it up. Our pages must be like the toy car, not the drawing.

## Grandma

When you look at our pages, every button and board should feel like a real object sitting on a real table — a polished stone, a glass tile, a brass button. One light shines from the top-left, like sun through a window, and everything casts a soft, true shadow. You can tell just by looking that you could press it.

## What It Means

The Real Object Law means nothing on a page is a flat shape with fancy effects, and nothing is a picture *of* an object. Every element is built as an object with real materials — obsidian glass, brushed metal, light that has volume. One light source, top-left. Real shadows, so objects sit *on* the page. Touch is a physical response: press in, light shifts, lift on hover. If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship. Shawn set this law on 2026-11-01, and made it canonical for all of NayaNET forever (SN-0859). It lives in Design Laws §1 and §4.

## Why It Matters

It is the difference between a page that looks like software and a page that feels like a real thing in your hands. People trust what feels real, and they enjoy touching it. A flat page is looked at and left; a real page is picked up, pressed, and stayed with. The whole feeling of NayaNET — premium, alive, worth coming back to — rests on this one law.

## How It Connects

It connects to everything else we build, because it is the base rule underneath all design. The Deep Palette gives objects their colors. The Object Anatomy gives them their construction — black inside, white as a real object in the middle, light and lift outside. The Emblem sets the quality bar. The Glow Law tells the light where it may sit. No design skill stands without it, and every page is judged against it first.

## How to Apply

When you build or judge any element, ask: does this look like a real thing I could touch? Check the light — is it coming from the top-left? Check the material — does it look like glass, metal, jewel, or just a flat colored shape? Check the shadow — does the object sit *on* the page? Press it — does it press in and out, does the light shift, does it lift on hover? If any answer is no, build it again as a real object. Real event: since Shawn set this law on 2026-11-01, flat shaded circles and pills have been sent back and rebuilt as real things — that is the law doing its work.

## What's In It For You

Pages that feel like beautiful real things instead of flat computer screens. You pick them up, you press them, and they answer like real things do — and that feeling is what makes you want to come back tomorrow.
