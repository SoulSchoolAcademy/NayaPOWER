# Minimum Text, Speak in Icons

SN-4797 · SKILLS / DESIGN

**Created:** 2026-11-24T19:57:26Z
**Category:** Design
**Skill:** 51
**SN:** SN-4797

## Nutshell

Say as few words as you can — let the pictures, icons and objects do the talking.

## Child

Imagine a sign that says "STOP" in big red letters — you understand it in one second. Now imagine a sign that says a whole paragraph about stopping. You'd never finish reading it before you walked past. Short signs win. Our pages are short signs, not paragraphs.

## Grandma

Think of the pictures on a remote control: play, pause, louder, quieter — you know them without reading a manual. If the remote came with a page of instructions for every button, you'd never use it. Our pages work like that remote: the picture tells you what to do.

## What It Means

Minimum Text, Speak in Icons means: say as few words as you can; the visuals speak the language. Too much text is a FAIL — way too much text, not enough contrast, not enough icons, not enough separation is terrible, whatever the counts say. If text is there, it looks extraordinary; if it doesn't, it doesn't stay. It lives in Design Laws §2.4.

## Why It Matters

A page a child can read at a glance beats a page that explains itself in paragraphs. People do not read walls of words — they look, they tap, they move on. Words that nobody reads are not communication, they are clutter. Fewer words, well shown, is what makes a page usable by anybody cold.

## How It Connects

It connects to the Text Laws (the few words that stay follow the 25/19/17 scale in white), to the Real Object Law (objects speak instead of paragraphs), and to the Unfold and the Slideshow (long text is walked through bit by bit, never dumped as a wall). Fewer words plus real objects plus a bit-by-bit walk is how a page becomes understandable in two seconds.

## How to Apply

Cut words until only the ones that must be there remain. Let icons, objects and pictures carry the meaning. Give what is left room to breathe — contrast, separation, size — so each word looks extraordinary. Then look at the page with fresh eyes and ask: could a child understand this at a glance? If the answer is a wall of text, cut again. Real event: on 2026-11-24 the Messenger rebuild was rejected by Shawn's eye as "a pile of text" — the page had the right words and still failed, because there were far too many of them and not enough icons, contrast and separation. The count of words was never the test; the glance was.

## What's In It For You

Pages that anyone can understand in two seconds — a child, a grandma, a stranger walking in cold. Less reading, more doing, and never a wall of words standing between you and what you came to do.
