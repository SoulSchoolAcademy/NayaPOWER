## Human

The question he asked — "are we missing anything?" — is the question of a builder who knows the big shape is right and is hunting for the small thing that makes the big shape usable. The research answers it precisely: the big things are won already, and the miss is not a feature, it is a view. A CRM's power is not its database, it is that one person, opened, shows everything: what you know about them, what you shared, what you owe them next, and when. Without that view, the pieces exist but the human has to assemble them every time — notes here, scheduler there, ledger somewhere else — and assembly is friction, and friction is where relationships go quiet. The three gaps are really one gap stated three ways: the person as the atom, with their history, their next step, and their standing, in one place, in the morning. "Haven't spoken in a while" is the most humane view in the whole report — it is not a metric, it is a kindness, a system that notices a friendship going quiet before the human does. And the what-not-to-learn list is the guardrail that keeps the learning honest: we take the human layer and refuse the surveillance layer, because the moment we log opens or read mail to track a relationship, we have become the thing our privacy law exists to prevent.

## Child

On their page you write little notes about them, all the fun things you did together, and what you want to do next — like "say hi to Sara, it's been three weeks!" And every morning, the book shows you who you haven't talked to in a while, so nobody gets forgotten. That's what the best helper-books do!

And in the morning it gently says "you haven't rung Sara in three weeks — why not say hello?" — like a friend reminding you, not an alarm going off. That's the kind of care that keeps friendships warm.

## What it means

- **THE VERDICT:** we are doing it right on the big things — the match, auto-organization, the vault, the backdating calendar. No CRM has the whole.
- **GAP 1 — NOTES ON A PERSON + THE PERSON TIMELINE:** private dated notes on a connection, the whole shared history in one place, a "haven't spoken in a while" view (last-contacted, oldest first).
- **GAP 2 — THE DATED NEXT STEP:** every connection and every promise carries one next step with a date; the day view shows what's due this morning; Naya reminds like a friend, not a red alert.
- **GAP 3 — THE BOARD:** where everything stands — who you're talking to, who went quiet, what's booked, what's owed. The morning list IS the whole system in one place.
- **WHAT NOT TO LEARN:** enterprise weight · add-on sprawl · paid automation walls · surveillance logging (open-tracking, auto-reading mail — against our privacy law, full stop).
- **QUEUED:** `person-notes-on-connections` + `person-next-step-morning-list` — both ride the Living Calendar build + Personal landing whole.

## Why it matters

Relationships are the product — the match brings people together, but the notes, the next step, and the board are what keep them together. A system that matches brilliantly and then lets the friendship go quiet has done half the job. The one-person view is the missing half: it turns a list of connections into a set of relationships with a past and a future, and it does it without any surveillance — the notes are the citizen's own, the reminder is a friend's nudge, the board is their own morning. That is the CRM lesson worth learning, and the surveillance lesson worth refusing, in one move.

## What changes now

1. Notes on a person become a real capability: private dated notes on any connection, with the whole shared history in one place.
2. Every connection and promise carries one dated next step; the day view shows what's due this morning.
3. The relationship board shows where everything stands — talking, quiet, booked, owed — and the morning list is the whole system in one place.
4. The "haven't spoken in a while" view ships as the most useful view, oldest-first.
5. No surveillance logging is ever added to get there — no open-tracking, no mail-reading, ever.
