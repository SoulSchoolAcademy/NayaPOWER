# No Line May Parrot a Backstage Instruction

SN-2863 · LESSON / SYSTEM

**Created:** 2026-11-15 00:31Z

**Paired Naya Note:** NN-20261115-no-line-parrots-a-backstage-instruction

## Hook

The agreement does not tell people it "feels official." It IS official.

## Nutshell

Shawn, reading the staged agreement (00:31Z), called out one line in the deed: "Between NayaNET and you — written out, so it feels official." His correction: **"so it feels official" is his backstage instruction to the captain echoed back to the citizen** — "you're telling them what I'm saying to you... that makes no sense." The agreement does not say it FEELS official; it IS official. The line was rewritten in her voice, stating the fact plainly. **Standing rule from this:** no line may parrot a backstage instruction. If a line reads like something said to the maker, it does not ship. The captain owned the miss plainly to him, and the correction went into the running v4 build as addendum #4. The same rule later governed the Official words: the candidate's "must be published and the payment system proven operational before we can promise a specific payday" was dropped as backstage language, and the fix round's item-1 ruling kept the two stiff Official phrases only because they are his ratified words, not because they read well.

## Human

This is a small line with a large lesson. A backstage instruction is a note from the director to the maker — "make it feel official" is a direction about the effect to achieve. When that direction leaks into the text itself, the citizen reads the scaffolding instead of the building: they are being told what the maker was told, and the spell breaks — they can hear the two of us talking behind the curtain. The test is simple and mechanical: read a line and ask who it sounds like it was said to. If it sounds like it was said to the maker, cut it and state the fact instead. "Written out, in plain words, so we both know where we stand" states the fact; "so it feels official" narrates the instruction. The rule generalizes beyond the agreement: any citizen-facing text — a board, a button, a letter — that echoes a brief, a correction, or a design note is a leak, and leaks do not ship. The captain owning it plainly is part of the lesson too: the miss was named in the same message as the fix, not hidden behind the correction.

## Child

Imagine your teacher tells you "make your drawing look happy!" — and then you write on the drawing "my teacher told me to make this look happy!" That's silly! You just draw it happy. The words on the page should talk to the person reading them — not repeat what someone whispered to you while you were making it!

## Grandma

It's like this, love — if I ask you to make the tea taste lovely, you don't serve it saying "she told me to make it taste lovely." You just make lovely tea! The words we show people should speak to them directly, not repeat the little notes we passed each other while we were working. If a line sounds like it was written for the maker, it doesn't go out.

## What it means

- **THE RULE:** no line may parrot a backstage instruction. If a line reads like something said to the maker, it does not ship.
- **THE PROOF:** "so it feels official" was cut from the deed; the line now states the fact — written out, in plain words, so we both know where we stand.
- **THE TEST:** read a line and ask who it sounds like it was said to. Said to the citizen, it stays. Said to the maker, it goes.
- **THE SCOPE:** every citizen-facing line — boards, buttons, letters, not just the agreement.
- **THE OWNERSHIP:** the miss was owned plainly to him in the same message as the fix.

## Why it matters

Trust is a feeling the words create, and nothing kills it faster than hearing the stage directions. A citizen who reads "so it feels official" learns two things at once: that someone told us to make it feel official, and that we are telling them about being told. The agreement's whole job is to make a stranger feel this is real — and real things do not announce that they feel real. The rule is cheap to apply and catches a whole class of leaks: brief language, correction language, design-note language, all of it stays backstage where it belongs.

## What changes now

1. Every citizen-facing line is read with the backstage test before it ships.
2. A line that echoes a brief, a correction, or a design note is rewritten to state the fact, or cut.
3. The deed carries the plain fact, never "so it feels official."
4. Backstage language in a words source (like the candidate's payment-system sentence) is dropped at the merge, not carried into the build.
