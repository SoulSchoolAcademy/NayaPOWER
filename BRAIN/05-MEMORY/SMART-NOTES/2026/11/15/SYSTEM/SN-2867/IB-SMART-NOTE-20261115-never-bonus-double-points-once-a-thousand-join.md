## Human

"Bonus" and "double points once a thousand join" describe the same reward, and the difference is everything. A bonus is a gift — given, free, extra, unearned by anything in particular. "Double points once a thousand join" is a milestone — earned by the collective act of a thousand people joining, arriving exactly when the thing that earns it happens. The word "bonus" tells the citizen the reward is spare change handed out; the milestone wording tells them they are part of a thousand founders building something together, and the doubling is the moment the thousand is complete. The timing rule is the same lesson in time: pay the reward up front and it is a bribe for joining; pay it at the thousandth join and it is a celebration of arriving. A reward before its milestone is spent before it is earned — and a system that pays before the milestone teaches its citizens that milestones do not matter. The word and the timing are one rule: the reward follows the achievement, in language and in time.

## Why it matters

Words are the contract a citizen reads before they join, and "bonus" is a word that shrinks the thing it names. A thousand founders is not a promotion, it is the founding of the thing itself — and the doubling of points at the thousandth join is the moment the founding is complete.
