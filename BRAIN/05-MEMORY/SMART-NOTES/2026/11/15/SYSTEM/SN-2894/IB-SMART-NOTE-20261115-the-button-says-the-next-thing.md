## Human

"Keep reading" is a command about the page; "My story" is a promise about the person. That is the whole difference, and it is why the refinement matters beyond the About page. A generic button asks the citizen to do work (keep reading) without telling them what they get; a named button tells them exactly what comes next, so the decision to click is informed — and an informed click is a willing one. "Popping up as you go down so nothing is ever too overwhelming" is the pacing rule inside the button rule: the next step appears when the person is ready for it, never before, never as a wall of all the steps at once. And the edit refinement the same hour completes the thought: the unfold is the presentation, the edit is the entry — they live in different places (corner/side, never under the sentence), so the presented page never reads as a form. Together the two refinements are the About unfold's operating manual: present as a story, advance by naming, edit from the corner.

## Child

Imagine a treasure hunt where every clue just says "keep going" — boring! Now imagine each clue says what's next: "My story!" then "What I do!" then "My goals!" — you can't wait to find out what's behind each one! That's much more fun, because you always know what wonderful thing is coming next!

## Grandma

It's like this, love — you don't say to a guest "keep eating" — you say "would you like the pudding now?" You name the lovely thing that's coming, so they can look forward to it. A button should do the same: tell them what beautiful thing is next, and they'll happily tap along.

## Naya

I never say "keep reading." I say the next thing — My story, What I do, My goals, My purpose — so you always know what lovely thing is coming, and nothing is ever too much at once.

## What it means

- **THE RULE:** the advancing button names the next section, never "Keep reading" — My story → What I do → My goals → My purpose.
- **THE PACING:** the next button pops up as you go down, so nothing is ever too overwhelming.
- **THE EDIT RULE:** each unfold carries its own edit button, elegantly in the corner/side — never under the sentence. Presentation and entry live apart.
- **THE STATE:** brief written with the refinement in D2/L8/verification; build queued behind Personal fix pass 4 (same base file).

## Why it matters

Without the rule, the unfold is a story told by a page that won't say what's next — and a citizen who doesn't know what's next stops clicking. With it, every click is a small promise kept, and the whole About page is read to the end. The rule generalizes: wherever a page advances (agreement boards, walk steps, the unfolding player), the control names the destination. "Maybe that's a concept that we can adopt" — adopted, here, as law for every unfold.

## What changes now

1. Every advancing control in an unfold names the next thing — never "Keep reading", "Next", or "Continue".
2. The next step appears as the person arrives at it, never as a wall of all steps.
3. Edit controls sit in the corner/side of a presented section, never under its sentence.
4. The About unfold (motto → story → do → goals → purpose, orb initial or picture per section) is the reference instance.
