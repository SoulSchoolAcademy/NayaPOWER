## What it means

- **THE RULE:** pay-when-paid by default; no fixed payout clock, no heavy restrictions at the start.
- **THE ACCOUNT:** three states, always visible — PENDING (made) · OFFICIAL (active) · PAID — how much made, how much official, how much pending, how much paid.
- **THE PRACTICE:** citizens often get paid around day 10, because that's when we get paid — do not stop that.
- **THE CHANGE RULE:** a stipulation comes only if needed to protect the system, and only with a notification to citizens — "we had to evolve this, this is what we're doing now."
- **THE WORDS:** Step 8 carries "When we get paid, you get paid. Often around day 10." with PENDING / OFFICIAL labels. The candidate's "can't promise a payday yet" and PENDING / PAID do not survive.

## Why it matters

A payout rule is a promise about money, and money promises are where trust is won or lost. A fixed clock would have made us promise a date we do not control; pay-when-paid promises only what is true — that the citizen is paid when we are — and the account proves it in three states a child can read. The restraint is part of the promise: starting light says we trust the citizen, and changing only on a real problem, with notice, says we will keep trusting them. That is the agreement's whole posture in one line.
