## THE NUTSHELL LAW

A nutshell is three, four sentences max. Never a ton of sentences. A book-length nutshell is not a long nutshell — it is **an error in the system**, and it tells him there are no rules put on the smart notes. The cap is a rule, enforced, not a guideline.

His words: *"In a nutshell is like massively too long. In a nutshell should never be a ton of sentences. In a nutshell should be like three, four sentences max."*

## THE OFFICIAL FORMAT

The official list, in order — nothing else in the official list:

1. in a nutshell
2. child note
3. grandma note
4. what it means
5. why it matters
6. what's in it for you

The board he was shown is NOT the official one — *"these are not the official ones… this is unaligned smart notes, not official."* The learning/lesson layer ("the adaptive learning they call it — what was to be learned") is okay to have, but it is NOT one of the official ones.

## THE BOARD MUST BE ALIVE

The inner board aligned perfectly into the frame — moved up a little and in a little. The orb flowing. The text glowing, lit up — *"which I showed you last night you're supposed to do… it was not lighted up."* An undynamic board that is not quite aligned is not done. The old one he walked in the browser last night, where the top text looked alive, is the contrast — that is the bar.

## THE STARS AND THE ORB — REAL OBJECTS, NOT DEAD ICONS

- **Rate Intelligence:** dead center, beside it a white pearl orb — the same spinning orb as the DM initials, but lit up white outside, flowing nice white in the center, a little diamond in the dead center. A white pearl diamond spinning of light; it looks like a real orb. It replaces the diamond that is "dead and not alive."
- **The stars, same concept:** purple ones with purple flow of light in the middle, spinning around, a really bright white star in the middle. Intelligence ones with white light flowing around, the purple star really evident in the middle. **Purple for value, white for intelligence; make the purple stars bigger** — probably twice as big, at the perfect sweet spot where it looks just real. Each one with a little light around it, lit up at the back, behind it — flowing light going around it.
- **Contrast, exact (his example):** a rating of 7/10 lights exactly 7 stars brighter; the other 3 stay unlit. Same for any rating — 3 lights 3, 8 lights 8, 10 lights all 10. The lit/unlit difference must be definite and evident at a glance.
- **Remove:** a random welcome button that shouldn't be there, and a little down arrow — both go.
- **Flowing light on every circle:** every circle — child, grandma, what-it-means, the sidebar ones, all of them — gets a slow flow of light travelling around its outside edge, always flowing, like the glowing circled icons at the bottom of the old board.
- **Headline + date alive:** the headline and the date line (Friday 20 November 2026) must carry the same living light they had on the old board — dynamic, lit up, beautiful — not plain flat text.
- **The design-contract standard (his order):** all text always dynamic and lit up, everything alive like real objects, elevation everywhere, every circle alive and flowing — written into the design contract as the standard, so he does not have to keep repeating "don't do flat text / make it alive / make sure there's elevation."

## WHY IT MATTERS

A board that shows unaligned notes in a non-official format with a dead diamond and a book-length nutshell is three failures wearing one shape: the format is wrong, the rules are missing, and the thing is not alive. The Nutshell Law is the rule that was missing — without it, the system has no rules on the notes at all, and he can see that at a glance. The official six is the fix in one line. The orb and the stars are the Real Object Law applied to the board's smallest pieces: if it doesn't look touchable and alive, it doesn't ship. The positions are the professional standard — centered counts, COMMENT under the N, SHARE INTEL in the corner — and a misaligned 0 is a visible unprofessionalism, not a nit.
