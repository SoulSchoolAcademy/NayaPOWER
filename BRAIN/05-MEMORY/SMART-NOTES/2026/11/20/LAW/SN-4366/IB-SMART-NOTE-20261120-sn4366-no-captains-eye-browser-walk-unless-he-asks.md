A checker that passes 12/12 in bytes while his eye still finds live defects (fix-1d proved it: checker 6/6, his eye still found the defects) is not a gate; it is a rehearsal. The eye walk's real failure is deeper than cost: a seat that only verifies what is there never thinks ahead with him — it never says "we should do this." So the rule changes from capping the walks to stopping them: the staged page goes straight to him, and his eye is the check.

## What it means

1. His eye is the scarcest thing in the machine, and spending it is not the waste — spending tokens to imitate it is.
2. A preview is a copy; only the live page at the address he opens is the thing itself.
3. A staged link proving bytes is not the live address proving itself. "Live" means the address he opens serves the page — verified by opening it, not by the build existing somewhere.
4. When the last change wrecks what was good, the fix is restoration, not another iteration on the wreckage.
5. Answer a storage question with a verified split: check what is actually stored, what is actually missing, and what the block actually is — then say which half needs the new home and which half doesn't.

## Why it matters

1. Every eye walk burns the very tokens he was asking about, and then asks him to spend his own eye anyway — the walk pays twice and proves once.
2. He judges what he opens. Every round that ends on a staging card instead of the live page costs him a round of judging a copy — and risks a rollback that takes away the version he liked best.
3. He asked for evidence it's done right, and his own test is the address: the preview screenshot shows what it SHOULD look like, but the live page does not present like that at all.
4. A 630-page bill of restrictions teaches a citizen that the system suspects them; a bill of rights teaches them that the system serves them.

## What changes now

1. No captain's-eye browser walk is dispatched from here on unless he asks for one. A finished build reaches him as a staged link with its checker verdict in bytes — and the render proof is his eye, named as the gate, never claimed before he looks.
2. Iterate the agreement page LIVE so he judges the real thing.
3. Never report a page live until the address has been opened and read back — bytes, size, sha — at that address.
4. Every check that does happen covers mobile AND desktop, always both.
5. When a change makes the page worse in his eye, restore the good version verbatim — do not improve on it, redesign it, or put something else in.
6. When the finished build lands, deploy it to nayanet.app/agreement by the map's recipe, then verify by opening the address itself.
