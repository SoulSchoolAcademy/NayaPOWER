## In a Nutshell

When he asks what you are going to do, answer with the plan. Repeating the word back is not an answer.

## The Lesson

His words, verbatim: "And what are you going to do with the stars?" The first reply was: "The stars." Two words — the noun echoed back, no plan, nothing he could judge, nothing he could correct. He had to ask again — "And the rate doesn't intelligent." — before a real answer came: "For the stars, they'll be purple and white with that same DM flow inside, no gold or blue, and they'll sit cleanly without colliding. And the orb at the top of the intelligent block will get its flow back around it, so it feels like one continuous, elegant system, just like you described."

That is the smallest form of the echo SN-4418 already forbids: not a paragraph repeated back, but a single word handed back as if it were an answer. He asks "what are you going to do" because he wants the plan stated plainly enough to judge — the colours, the flow, the collision, the orb — so he can say yes, no, or different before the work is done. An echoed noun gives him nothing to judge and costs him another turn to get what he asked for the first time.

## Why It Matters

Every turn he spends re-asking is attention taken from the work itself, and attention is the scarcest thing in the machine. A plan stated plainly also protects the work: if the plan is wrong, he corrects it before a round is dispatched; if the plan is never stated, the round is built on a guess and comes back to his eye wrong. "The stars" is not a plan — it is a placeholder where a plan should have been, and a placeholder is never an answer.

## How to Use It

When he asks what you will do, say what you will do: the concrete change, in plain words, in the same turn. Never answer a question with the word it asked about. If the plan is not settled yet, say what is not settled and what you will check — that is still an answer; the word alone is not. Scorecard every reply before sending: does it say what will be done, or does it only repeat what was asked?
