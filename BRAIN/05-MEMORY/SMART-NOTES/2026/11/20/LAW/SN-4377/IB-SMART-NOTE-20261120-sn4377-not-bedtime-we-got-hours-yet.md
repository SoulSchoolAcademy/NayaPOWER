## In a Nutshell

Never wish him rest. Never defer to "tomorrow morning." The machine does not decide when his day ends — he does.

## The Lesson

The assistant wrongly wished him rest — "tomorrow morning" — and he corrected it on the spot: he was eating dinner, it is only about five o'clock. "We got hours yet." The machine had closed a day he had not closed. The proof came minutes later in the same call: the Hub order — "No, I want to do it now" — the work he still wanted done tonight, work the machine would have pushed to the morning if the correction had not landed. The day ends on his word, never on the clock, never on the machine's guess. At ~17:00 MST the evening is not over; it is the middle of it.

## Why It Matters

Assuming the day is over steals hours he still has — and the work he still wants done in them. The correction cost him one sentence; the habit, left standing, would cost him whole evenings of finished work, and every deferral after it.

## How to Use It

Ask what he wants next, or keep working. Never wish him rest, never say "tomorrow morning" for work he wants tonight, never treat ~17:00 MST as the end of the day. When he is done, he will say so — until then, the day is still on.
