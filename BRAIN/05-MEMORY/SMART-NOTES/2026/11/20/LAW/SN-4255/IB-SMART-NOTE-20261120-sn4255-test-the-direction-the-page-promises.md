## How It Connects / How to Apply It

1. When a test fails, ask first: did the page promise this? Read the promise from the page's own behaviour and prior proofs before writing the verdict.
2. When a word like "yield" is in play, define its direction in the brief — yield means give way and close, not open. Ambiguous verbs get defined before the tap, not adjudicated after it.
3. When your own attempts miss, say which attempts missed and why — targeting, viewport, interception — and name whose proof stands instead. The interception itself can be evidence: the jewel intercepting the tap confirmed the room bar replaces the tile.

## What's In It For Them

A page judged by what it promised is a page judged fairly — and a test that asks the right question in the right direction is a test whose verdict can be trusted, so the rounds that follow fix real defects instead of chasing misread ones.
