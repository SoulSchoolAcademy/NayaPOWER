"Stop doing new buttons. Just do them the same. You're trying to do something different again." A builder who reaches for a new button when the house button is sitting there has not learned the house button yet.

Note the sequence, because it matters: he first floated "Maybe back is white and the forward is blue or purple" — and then corrected himself in the same breath: "No, no, no, no. No purple and whites." The final rule is both obsidian black. A floated idea he immediately withdraws is not the order; the correction after it is.

## Why It Matters

Every new button is a new thing for him to judge, correct, and re-teach — and a page whose buttons do not match the rest of the system does not feel like one system. The house button is the standard because it is already ten-star; re-deciding it every round is the tax he keeps paying.

## How to Use It

On the agreement and everywhere else: take the Hub button — obsidian black, raised, real, faint white swish forward on forward, faint white swish back on back, a light around the edge. Never a white button, never a purple button, never a new button. If a brief does not name the house button, the brief is incomplete; if a build shows a different button, the build goes back before it reaches him.
