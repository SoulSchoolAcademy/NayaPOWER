## In a Nutshell

Slides do not have to alternate image-text rigidly. The pattern is: image + a little text → an all-text slide (no image, no video) that continues the story of that image → back to image + a little text. Never scroll down to read. Every frame stays in the frame; they just click the button, never folding down.

## The Lesson

The rigid image-text-image-text alternation was never the rule — it was a habit mistaken for one. A slide can be all text when the story needs it: the text continues what the image started, and the next slide returns to image. What is never allowed is the scroll: "You don't want to have them scroll down to read. If you're making them scroll down to read and then you've done then it's not right. I want them to every every frame so they stay in the frame. So just click in the button they never folding down." A presentation that makes the reader scroll to finish a thought has broken its own frame. The click is the only movement; the frame is the whole world of that slide.

## Why It Matters

A scrolling slide is a slide that does not fit — and a presentation full of them teaches the reader to leave the frame instead of staying in it. The continuation slide keeps the story moving without breaking the frame or repeating the image.

## How to Use It

On the agreement and every presentation after it: image + a little text, then a text-only continuation slide where the story needs room, then image again. If text overflows a frame, it becomes the next slide — it never becomes a scroll. Which slides become text-only continuations is decided from the chapter text in the slide round; v28 (live now) is not restructured on this alone.
