## What it means

1. **THE RULE:** a list retold is not a list finished. Repetition is not progress — the count that matters is items proven on the page he sees. "Done" means the whole list, on the page, today — never the round finished, never one item shown while nine sit open. This is the Checklist Law and the Whole-Page Rule, restated by his own correction: the page comes to him whole or not at all.
2. **THE FIX (ordered):** whole-list rounds, prove only what changed. One brief carries the whole list, so one proof covers the list instead of one proof per item; each check proves the changed lines and spot-checks the rest, instead of re-reading all 10 MB. And every dispatch names its next step, so no seat sits idle waiting — the Continuous Cycle Law, applied to the token bill.
3. **THE SHAPE CHANGE (the fix, made today):** the completion coordinator carries the WHOLE list in one brief — About, Skills, calendar, Settings, Connections, Invite, gate, DM rail, Smart Tabs, nine notes — and nothing returns until all of it is done. One brief, one list, one return. That is the cure for a day spent redoing one page.
4. **THE RULE:** when a build is a mess, the fix is not a round of patches on the mess. A patch keeps the shape that made it a mess; a rebuild keeps only the flow. The old code is not the starting point — the citizen's steps are.
5. The first number is not the number. Twenty-three was true as far as it went — 23 means heaven, and he said so while labeling. But the full set, once it was in front of him, said 27: net, art, God, Lord all equal 13, and slide 13 itself — "All 13" — and love is 27, so 13 plus 14 is 27, and three nines is 27 which is light, and 27 is his daughter's birth date, nine times three, completion, graduation. None of that was designed in advance. "I didn't plan it. It just did."

The order is the story: the problem first, the answer second, the person third — never the system before the person. ## What changes now

1. **Run whole-list rounds:** one brief, one list, one proof — a page proven once per list, not once per item.
2. **Scope every check to the change:** the diff is the proof's subject; the rest of the page gets a spot-check (regression sweep on the seams the change touches), never a full re-read.
3. **Never queue a list behind a single round that can go back:** carry the whole list in one brief, and let nothing return until all of it is proven on the page.
4. **When he repeats an ask, treat the repetition as the signal:** the list is still open. Put every item on ONE checklist, in his words, and work it to zero — never show one item alone while the rest sits.
5. **One wake, one job:** a seat wakes for exactly one job and finishes it — never a wake that reads the whole context to decide what to do.
6. **Start the rebuild from the citizen's steps in order,** one screen per step, never an overload — and prove each step lands before adding the next.
