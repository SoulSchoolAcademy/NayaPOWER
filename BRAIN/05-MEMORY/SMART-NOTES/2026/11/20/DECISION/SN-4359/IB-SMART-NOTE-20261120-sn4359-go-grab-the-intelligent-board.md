## Nutshell

The board as built "is not alive at all, no activeness" — and he gave the structure that makes it alive. One major board, with inner boards inside it that display the text and light up too — usually two inner boards, sometimes three on the odd slide, like a mini intelligent board. Most slides should use 2–3 boards, not one big board, because broken-up and elevated is easier to read. If a slide has no image and no video, the whole thing becomes one slide that lights up and is readable. And the board itself is not built new: go grab the intelligent board from the Hub — the exact code, already raised and elevated, looking as it should — use PART of it, not the whole thing. It sits beside the player for now; when the player is not there, the whole frame becomes the intelligent board. The player's video and image area IS an intelligent block, with an image in it.

## Human

The player is the first thing people see, so the player is the standard the whole presentation is judged by. A recreated player tells them at the first glance that this is a copy; the actual player tells them this is the real thing, made by the people who make the thing. This is the harvest-first discipline at its sharpest: SN-4285 said put the literal player in the frame instead of linking it; this says put the ACTUAL player in, not a new one built to look like it — linking it was the first miss, rebuilding it was the second, and both misses have the same shape, substituting something near the thing for the thing. Sizing the frame to the player instead of squishing the player into the frame is the same rule in the other direction: the frame serves the player, because the player is the pro object and the frame is its stage. And the transport controls are not decoration — pause, stop, backwards, forwards are what make it a player instead of a picture that happens to move.

## Child

We already have a beautiful board that glows and lifts off the page. We take that same board and put it in the frame, next to the player. One big board holds the words, and inside it sit two smaller boards — three sometimes — so the words are easy to read.

## Grandma

Think of a beautiful tray that holds smaller shining dishes. The big board is the tray; the little boards hold the words, two or three at a time, so your eyes rest on one small piece, then the next. When there is no player on the slide, the whole tray is the board, glowing on its own.

## Naya

The two details carry the lesson in miniature: NAYA beside her picture (not underneath — a placement, not a redesign) and less glow in the middle (restraint, not a new effect).

4. One module, learned once, everywhere — that is the system getting simpler while the surface gets richer.
5. His eye is ground truth, and a claim his walk disproves is withdrawn, not defended.

## Why it matters

1. The presentation is the first thing a new citizen sees, and a dead board tells them the system is a picture of intelligence rather than intelligence itself.
2. If the first object in the presentation is a near-miss copy, everything after it is read as a near-miss copy too; if it is the actual player, the whole presentation inherits its quality.
3. Every "fixed" claim that is not true costs a walk, a call, and a little trust.
4. Carrying an unfinished format to ten pages does not save time; it multiplies the unfinished format by ten, and then every page has to be re-carried once the format is finally right.
