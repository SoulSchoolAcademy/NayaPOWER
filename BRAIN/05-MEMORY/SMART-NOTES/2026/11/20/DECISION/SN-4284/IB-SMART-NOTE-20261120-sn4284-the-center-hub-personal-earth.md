## The Lesson

The center is settled, and he restated it because the confusion keeps returning in new clothes. A feed that needs its own home, a page that needs its own center, a world that wants to be the hub — every one of them is a second center, and a second center splits the system. The Hub is the Personal page with a cover and a menu bar: one page, one center, one front door. The earth does not get a twin; the worlds revolve around it. That is the shape of the whole system — the Anatomy says the same thing in its own words, and this is his voice saying it again, locked in: "Nobody creates another hub; we already have the center."

The restatement also settles the direction of building. We do not build centers; we build worlds and connect them to the center. A world that cannot connect to the Hub is not a world yet — it is a stray page looking for a home. And the purpose is not architectural tidiness: the center exists so that all the earthlings can succeed in the physical realm, in the golden age. The Hub is the mother brain because everything returns to it — the feed, the worlds, the people — and the mother brain is the earth herself because the system is for everyone on it.

## Why It Matters

Every round that builds another hub costs the system its unity, and unity is the product. The person opens one place and finds their life there — Personal is the Hub, the Hub is the Personal page — instead of hunting across centers that each claim to be home. When the center is one, the worlds can be many: Personal, Business, Space, every world a citizen builds, all revolving around the same earth. When the center splits, the worlds scatter, and the person is back in the mess the agreement promises to end. Locking the center in again is what keeps the many worlds from becoming many systems.

## How to Use It

Build worlds, never hubs. When a brief asks for a new center, a new home, a new front door, stop it at the brief: the center already exists, it is the Hub, it is the Personal page with a cover and a menu bar, and the new thing connects to it as a world. Score every page against this: does it revolve around the center, or does it try to become one? A page that revolves is right; a page that competes with the center goes back.
