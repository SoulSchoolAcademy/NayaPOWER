## The Lesson

The player is the first thing people see, so the player is the standard the whole presentation is judged by. A recreated player tells them at the first glance that this is a copy; the actual player tells them this is the real thing, made by the people who make the thing. This is the harvest-first discipline at its sharpest: SN-4285 said put the literal player in the frame instead of linking it; this says put the ACTUAL player in, not a new one built to look like it — linking it was the first miss, rebuilding it was the second, and both misses have the same shape, substituting something near the thing for the thing. Sizing the frame to the player instead of squishing the player into the frame is the same rule in the other direction: the frame serves the player, because the player is the pro object and the frame is its stage. And the transport controls are not decoration — pause, stop, backwards, forwards are what make it a player instead of a picture that happens to move.

## Why It Matters

"Look at the quality of their player" is the bar he set, and quality is the first thing the eye judges. If the first object in the presentation is a near-miss copy, everything after it is read as a near-miss copy too; if it is the actual player, the whole presentation inherits its quality. The playlist going is the same subtraction as the bottom bar (SN-4287): what does not serve the story goes, and what replaces it — the red N and the button — serves the story. The frame fitting the player is what makes it look professional and aligned instead of cramped: a pro object needs its own measurements, not the leftovers of a frame drawn first.
