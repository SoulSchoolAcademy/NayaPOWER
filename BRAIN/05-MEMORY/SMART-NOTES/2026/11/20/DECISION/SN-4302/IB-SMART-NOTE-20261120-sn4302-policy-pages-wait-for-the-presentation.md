## In a Nutshell

After the presentation is done, go into each policy page and put the frame around the text, formatted with the same formatting as the presentation. But wait until the presentation is done — the formatting has to exist first.

## Child

First we finish the big story and make it beautiful. Then we take that same beautiful frame and put it around the words on each rules page — Terms, Privacy, and all the rest. We do the story first, so we know what beautiful looks like.

## Grandma

You finish the pattern before you sew the rest of the quilt. The presentation is the pattern — the frame, the boards, the glowing words. Once it is right, each rules page gets the same pattern wrapped around its words.

## What It Means

Sequence is the decision: formatting is created once, on the presentation, and then carried — not invented fresh on ten pages. Doing the pages first would mean ten different guesses at the format instead of one finished standard.

## Why It Matters

Formatting the policy pages before the presentation is done would bake in whatever the format happens to be today — and the format is still being fixed. Waiting means the pages inherit the finished thing, once, instead of being redone.

## How It Connects

The footer links on the first frame point to these pages; the presentation's frame, board, and manuscript font become their frame, board, and font. One formatting, from the story outward to the rules.

## What's In It For You

When you open any rules page, it reads like the story you already loved — same frame, same glowing boards, same clear words. The rules feel like part of the welcome, not a different place.
