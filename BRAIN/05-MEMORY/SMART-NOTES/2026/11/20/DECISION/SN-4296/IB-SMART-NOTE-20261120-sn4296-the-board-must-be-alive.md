## The Lesson

Aliveness is not a finish applied at the end; it is the structure. A single big board with text on it is a poster, however pretty — it does not move, does not light up, does not answer a touch. Two or three inner boards inside one major board give the light somewhere to travel and the eye somewhere to land, and each board can light up on its own. The second half of the lesson is the harvest law, restated by him in this call because it keeps being broken: get the pieces from the Hub AND the calendar and learn from them when doing this — do not recreate. The Hub's intelligent board already exists, already raised, already elevated; the work is taking part of it and putting it in the frame, not building a board-shaped thing from scratch.

## Why It Matters

The presentation is the first thing a new citizen sees, and a dead board tells them the system is a picture of intelligence rather than intelligence itself. Every slide that reads as flat text in a flat frame is a slide that fails the Real Object Law in front of the one person whose eye decides. And every recreated board is a second copy of a thing that already exists — it drifts, it breaks differently, and the next builder has to fix two boards instead of one. One board, taken whole from the Hub and used in part, is the only way the presentation and the feed stay the same object.

No image, no video — the whole thing becomes one lit-up slide you read. The player's picture area is an intelligent block with the video or image in it. When a piece already exists in the Hub or the calendar, take it — "they keep recreating when they shouldn't. Over and over."
