## In a Nutshell

The video must sync to the visual player — and the visuals must react to the audio, not just play alongside it.

## The Lesson

His order: the video must sync to the visual player; the visuals must react to the audio, not just play alongside it. Playing alongside is two things happening at the same time; reacting is one thing answering the other — the picture moves because the sound moved, the light rises because the voice rose. A player that only plays alongside is a slideshow with a soundtrack; a player whose visuals react to the audio is alive, and alive is the standard of the whole network — every element a real object, alive on hover, moving when it is touched and when it is spoken to. The sync is the proof: if the visuals do not change with the audio, they are not synced, whatever the timestamps say.

## Why It Matters

The walkthrough is the front door — name first, ten questions, agreement, world reveal, then hired — and its pages carry video and audio that must feel like one performance, not two files started together. A citizen who hears a voice and sees a picture that does not answer it feels the gap even if they cannot name it; the gap is the difference between a presentation and a machine that is listening.

## How to Use It

When building a video/audio player, bind the visuals to the audio: the picture, the light, the motion must answer the sound as it plays. Test it by ear and eye together — mute the audio and the visuals should go still; play it and they should move. Never ship "plays alongside" as "in sync."
