## In a Nutshell

The text must feel like a story being told — written with the right grammar, like it is being written and spoken to them. His example of what's wrong: slide 1 says welcome, slide 2 is "Hi, I'm Naya" plus a whole bunch of text, slide 3 is one line "welcome to your world" — welcome appears TWICE and the spacing of the words across slides is uneven. Fix the duplication and the word spacing so it flows as one story.

## The Lesson

A story told out loud never says hello twice, and it never puts a whole paragraph on one breath and a single line on the next. The slides are beats of one telling: each slide carries its fair share of the words, in order, in spoken grammar. Uneven spacing is not a layout problem first — it is a pacing problem: the words were poured, not told.

## Why It Matters

He reads it as a listener, not a reviewer. A repeated welcome tells him the teller lost the thread; a crammed slide tells him the teller is rushing. The presentation's job is to make the reader feel spoken to, one slide at a time — and the words are the story, not the decoration around it.

## How to Use It

Rewrite the slides as spoken beats: one greeting, once, at the start; each slide one thought, roughly even weight; read it aloud slide to slide and cut wherever it stumbles. No slide carries a whole bunch while the next carries one line.
