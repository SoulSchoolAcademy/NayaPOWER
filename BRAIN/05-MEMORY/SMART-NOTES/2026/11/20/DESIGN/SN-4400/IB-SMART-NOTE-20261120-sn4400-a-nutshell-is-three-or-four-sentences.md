## In a Nutshell

A nutshell is the whole thing in three or four sentences — if it takes a paragraph, it is not a nutshell, whatever the label says.

## The Lesson

His words, on the board labelled "in a nutshell": "the in nutshell is not in a nutshell." The board carried more than a nutshell holds, so the name lied about the thing. Small adjustments only, not a redesign. A nutshell earns its name by its size: the whole idea, distilled, in three or four sentences, on a board that sits where it belongs and glows no brighter than it needs to.

## Why It Matters

"Nutshell" is a promise to the reader: this is the whole thing, small enough to hold. A long board under that label breaks the promise and teaches the reader to distrust the labels — the next board's label means less. The fix is not a rewrite of the idea; it is a cut to the size the name already promised.

## How to Use It

When a board is called a nutshell, count the sentences: three or four, and the whole idea inside them. More than that — cut it until it fits, or rename the board to what it actually is. Fit is part of the promise too: up a tad, in a tad, light down a little — small moves, not a redesign.
