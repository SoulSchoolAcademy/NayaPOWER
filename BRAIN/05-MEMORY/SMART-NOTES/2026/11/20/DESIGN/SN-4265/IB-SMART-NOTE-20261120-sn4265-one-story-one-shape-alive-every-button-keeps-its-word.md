## Hook

He walked the feed with his eye one evening, and everything he saw came back to one verdict: the story must be the same in every room, the shape must be the same on every board, the page must be alive under the hand — and every button must land where it promised. A beautiful page with a button to the wrong place is a broken promise.

## Nutshell

A smart note is six boards in a fixed order, each lighting up in its own colour, the nutshell short by rule. The agreement is ten boards, picture and text alternating, in a friend's voice a child and a grandma both understand — because it is a shared promise, not a legal contract, and no long version is legally needed. The date border glows live again and the orb's ring circles it, both restored, not redesigned. The messenger must be truly connected — a real conversation per person, their feed and matched content beside it — and the organizers must work, New showing what's new and All showing all. Stars must read lit at a glance, and the comment panel must be clean and elegant, input at the top, five kind suggestions beneath. The introduction is the video: one frame above the fold, video left, audio right, one button into the agreement — and no test-mode note anywhere in it, because citizens read it after launch, when the system is working. Every button is clicked through on every eye walk, and the whole story carries, unchanged, into Personal, Business, and Space.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: one story, one shape, alive — and every button keeps its word.

## What it means

1. **ONE SHAPE FOR EVERY NOTE:** six boards, fixed order, fixed lights, nutshell short by rule — a paragraph-length nutshell is a massive shell, and it looks strange on the page. Simplicity wins; the human-note board is gone, magenta is gone, purple stands.
2. **THE PROMISE FITS IN THE HEAD:** ten boards, 3–5 sentences each, picture and text alternating, in a friend's voice — because nobody joins a promise they cannot hold in their head, and no lawyer or statute anywhere in the record needs the long version.
3. **ALIVE IS RESTORED, NOT INVENTED:** the glowing date border and the circling orb flow existed before and were lost — harvest the living versions and put them back. Flat text is the failure state; a lit star that barely differs from unlit fails the control.
4. **CONNECTED MEANS A CONVERSATION HAPPENS:** percentages on a card are not the proof; a DM that opens a panel not knowing who the person is proves the messenger is a placeholder. The match becomes real only inside a real conversation, with their feed beside it.
5. **THE ORGANIZER IS THE PROMISE:** New empty and All incomplete is not a content problem — it is the auto-organizers not working, and on the first screen the feed cannot be trusted. What is seen for a second and then gone is still broken.
6. **EVERY BUTTON KEEPS ITS WORD:** click through every one, on every eye walk and every checker, and a page passes only when each lands current and relevant — not stale, not a dead end, not the wrong place. The check rides the walk that is already happening.
7. **WRITE FOR THE DAY THEY READ IT:** a test-mode note in the introduction would be nonsense to anyone reading it after launch — the system is in test mode only because we are, pre-launch. Cut it everywhere live, and sweep to zero.
8. **ONE STORY, THREE ROOMS:** the introduction's concepts — vault, connecting notes, storefront, Team Naya, community spaces, agreement and Law of One, privacy by default, free to join, your data yours — carry into Personal, then clone whole into Business and Space. A different story inside is a citizen misled.
9. **THE VIDEO IS THE START:** the introduction script is the video's narration, and the video is the first thing they see — watch it, listen to it, or click straight through — in one frame, with one button, and Naya's real voice from the video itself.

## Why it matters

1. Without the six-board anatomy, every note invents its own shape, and the citizen has to learn the page again with every note — instead of the light telling them where they are before they read a word.
2. Without the ten-board agreement, the promise stays a giant book nobody finishes — and understanding is what makes them go "wow," respect it, and want to take part.
3. Without the live border and the circling orb, the page feels flat even when the words are good — and flat boards get scrolled past while live boards get lived in.
4. Without a connected messenger, the match stays a number on a card — percentages with no conversation — and strangers never become connections.
5. Without working organizers, new work vanishes from view, and citizens learn their own work will not be seen either — a feed that loses things loses the people who feed it.
6. Without stars that read at a glance, nobody is invited to rank — and the feed never learns what is truly valuable; without an elegant comment panel, nobody is invited to speak.
7. Without the button check on every walk, a beautiful page quietly sends citizens to stale pages, dead ends, and the wrong rooms — and the expensive way to find out is from a citizen who already clicked.
8. Without the test-mode cut, a citizen reading after launch meets a warning about a world that no longer exists — and doubts a system that is working.
9. Without the same story in all three rooms, the front door makes a promise the inside breaks — one home, never three different houses with three different rules.
