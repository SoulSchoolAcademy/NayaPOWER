## Hook

Clicking play must do something. The video plays first of all — it must play on open, not wait for a tap.

## Nutshell

"Yeah, so right now none of the video, the video and the images are not playing. So you got to figure out why they're giving you the video and it's not playing. And why I'm giving you the images and they're not displaying." The order is not "put the video in again" — it is figure out WHY. Why is the video they are giving us not playing, and why are the images he is giving us not displaying? The cause is the job; the placement is the easy part. This follows the same day's earlier corrections (SN-4295, SN-4309): the player was corrected twice and the placeholder was still standing in for the real link — putting it in again without knowing why it fails is how a correction gets "done" three times and still fails his eye.

## Grandma

A play button that plays nothing is a broken promise, not a missing feature. A correction that adds is not the same as a correction that restores. ## Naya

A thing that will not play has a reason, and the reason is the work. Re-placing the same video in the same spot and hoping it plays this time is not a fix — it is a repeat with a new timestamp. The diagnosis comes first: what is handed to the build, what the build does with it, where it breaks — named, in order, with proof. Only then does the placement mean anything, because only then can the walk prove it plays.

## What changes now

1. Before the next placement, trace the failure: the video file or link as given, what the player receives, what the browser does with it, where it stops — and the same for the images he sent. Name the cause in writing, fix the cause, then place the video and the images and prove them playing and showing on the walk. Never report "the video is in" until it plays on open in front of his eye.
2. Wire the deck play to audio plus video together; wire video play to audio through the visual; wire pause both ways; autoplay the video on open.
3. Crop the orb out of the image he sent (screenshot 47a0acbb…): dark nice deep rich red ball, nice bright gold N in the middle. Red glow at about half strength. Purple glow on ENTER leads. No white glow in the center of ENTER.
4. Put her picture big, almost twice the size, in the center so her face looks like it is talking to them. Put the little red N at the bottom. Do not put her picture in both the corner and the box — choose its one place (top right corner or the box area, as he posed it) and keep the N at the bottom.
5. Never put her picture in the audio player; keep the voice light, the way it was. On the text screens, grow her picture 25–50% bigger max and judge by the one test: does she feel present there?
6. Three buttons — back icon, Next in the middle, forward icon; all obsidian in the middle with a beautiful icon that pops. Next carries the flowing edge-light and the purple glow behind it; back and forward are elegant arrows with no glow. No bottom bar, no slide count anywhere.
