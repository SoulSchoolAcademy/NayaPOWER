## The Lesson

A layout is a proportion with a job for every third. One third is the player, two thirds is the picture, and when the player leaves, its third becomes the one board — the space is never dead. Mobile is the same order in a stack: picture first, board under it. The mistake to avoid is treating the player-less pages as leftovers; they are the same frame with the board taking the player's place.

## Why It Matters

Without the proportion, the player floats in a corner and the picture area fills with her photo instead of the video, and the later pages have a hole where the player was. With it, every slide has the same shape — player and picture, or board and picture — and the reader never has to relearn the page. The mobile rule protects the same shape on the screen he actually walks: picture above, board below, nothing competing side by side at 390.

## How to Use It

Build the frame as one third player at the edge plus two thirds video area beside it. On every page after the first, put the presentation bar — ONE intelligent board — in the player's third. On mobile, stack: video or image above, board just underneath. Do not put her picture in the video area — the actual video goes there. Do not leave the player's third empty on later pages.
