## The Lesson

His question, on call 65eef2c9: "What share on the card?" He did not understand the share/approve card — the step that was supposed to let him approve sharing the agreement. The record is plain about it: the share step needs plain words, not a card he has to decode.

A card is a machine's way of asking: a title, a button, an implied action, and the citizen is expected to work out what it means. Plain words are a person's way: "Share this agreement with [name]? Yes / No." The card failed in the same way "earn half" failed on the same board — it asked him to do work the words should have done. He asked the question out loud because the card did not answer it: what share, on what card, to whom, and what happens when I say yes?

## Why It Matters

Every step he has to decode is a step where he can decode it wrong — and the share step is the one that sends his work out into the world. A misunderstood share is a share that goes to the wrong place, or doesn't go at all, or goes before he's ready. The approval card is supposed to protect him; a card he can't read protects nothing. Push-button simple means the words say the action: who, what, where, and what happens next — in one sentence, before any button.

## How to Use It

Never present a decision as a card alone. Every share, approve, or send step leads with one plain sentence: what is being shared, with whom, and what happens when he says yes. The buttons underneath say yes and no in the same plain words. If he has to ask "what share on the card?" the step has already failed — rewrite the words, not the card.
