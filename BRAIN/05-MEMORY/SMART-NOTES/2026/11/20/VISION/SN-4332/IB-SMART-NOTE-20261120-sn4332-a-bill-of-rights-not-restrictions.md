## In a Nutshell

His philosophy, stated on the call: "If you want to create a great economy or a great nation, what you want is a bill of rights not a bill of restrictions. The Clarity Act was 630 pages and it was a bill of restrictions… probably 620 pages were restrictions." He wants a bill of rights, not a bill of restrictions. The Clarity Act as it stands is 630 pages, and by his count probably 620 of them are restrictions — the shape of a document built backwards. A founding document that grants and protects is the shape of a great economy or a great nation; a founding document that forbids, page after page, is the shape of a system that does not trust its own people.

## The Lesson

Rights first, restrictions last — if at all. A document that tells people what they may do, what is theirs, what is protected, invites them in; a document that tells them what they may not do, six hundred pages of it, keeps them out. The page count is the proof: when the restrictions outnumber the rights sixty to one, the document is not a charter, it is a fence. Build the charter as rights given, and let any restriction earn its single page by proving it protects a right.

## Why It Matters

The Clarity Act is meant to be the founding law of NayaNET — the thing a citizen reads and feels safe inside. A 630-page bill of restrictions teaches a citizen that the system suspects them; a bill of rights teaches them that the system serves them. His philosophy here is the same as his design law everywhere else: the thing must feel good to live inside, or nobody lives there. The Act cannot be the exception to the ten-star standard.

## How to Use It

Rewrite toward rights: every section of the Clarity Act states what is granted and protected first, in plain words a child can understand, and any restriction that remains must name the right it protects. Measure the rewrite the way he measured the problem — count the pages of rights against the pages of restrictions, and let the rights win by a wide margin. The rewrite itself is owed work for his hand and the captain's; this note records the philosophy, it does not claim the rewrite done.
