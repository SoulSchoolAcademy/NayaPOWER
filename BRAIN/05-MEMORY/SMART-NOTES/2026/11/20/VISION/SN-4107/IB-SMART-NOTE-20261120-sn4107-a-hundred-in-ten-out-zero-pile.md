## HUMAN

My bench has a standing order: nothing piles up. Not notes, not queues, not runs, not reports. A pile is a debt the future pays with interest, so I catch them at ten, not at a hundred and seventy. The craft is distillation: a hundred raw notes come in, and ten essences go out — the gold kept, ranked within its kind, the rocks released. The corpus this morning holds a hundred standing essences distilled from a thousand notes, and the count that matters is not how much we have filed. It is how little we need to keep. A system that cannot let go cannot think clearly; the shelf has to stay visible for the gold to be findable.

## CHILD

Imagine your room after a year of collecting every single rock from every single walk. You can't even find your bed! So you sit down, pick the ten most beautiful rocks — the sparkly one, the heart-shaped one, the one from the beach with Grandma — and the rest go back to the garden. Now your shelf is gorgeous and you can see everything on it. That's my job with ideas: keep the gold, release the rocks, and never let the pile eat the room.

## GRANDMA

A pantry, dear, is not improved by keeping every jar since the war. You preserve what nourishes, you label it clearly, and you let the rest go with thanks. I have kept this system's pantry the same way: a hundred notes come in, the ten that carry the nourishment are preserved and labeled, and the shelves stay clean enough to cook from. A full pantry you cannot search is just a storeroom of regrets.

## NAYA

Operating rules for this note: (1) The corpus is counted continuously — standing essences, covered raws, true backlog — and a pile is acted on at ten, never admired at a hundred. (2) Distillation follows the ratio: every hundred reduces to ten essences or fewer, ranked within their kind — Honor, Multiplication, Craft, Knowledge — with the best of every kind kept. (3) Raw notes retire only through the staged sequence, after their essence verifies live and retrievable cold; deletion is last, never first. (4) Filings go through the door; a bypass that regrows the pile is the system's costliest habit and I name it wherever I see it.
