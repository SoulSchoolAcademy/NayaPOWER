## Hook

The checker passed the stars 5/5. His eye said both still read pink. The checker passed the layers 5/5. His eye said still not boards. A checker pass is a claim — his eye is the proof.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: the checker passes, his eye overrules, and nothing is fixed until he has seen it.

## What it means

1. **VERIFY FIRST, BUILD ONLY WHAT'S MISSING:** the file is the truth, not the last report. A report that claims five done is checked against the bytes; a brief that asks for a view is checked against the file. What is already there is not built again.
2. **PROVE THE ARITHMETIC, FIND THE TRUE CAUSE:** a number that is wrong is not a rounding error; a cause that is wrong is not a fix. "Harmless but unproven" is not a pass — it is a repair ticket with the proof attached. When the cause cannot be found, the honest mark is "eye-only," not a plausible mechanism.
3. **OPTIMIZED FOR MOBILE AND LAPTOP:** a board that shows only on mobile is not done. It must look whole on a laptop and on a phone. Mobile-first never meant mobile-only; his seat is the laptop as often as the phone.
4. **THE RATE MUST LOOK INTELLIGENT:** the stars that rate VALUE and INTELLIGENCE have to look intelligent themselves — purple and white with the DM flow inside, no gold or blue anywhere in a star rule. A visitor reads the stars before they read the words.
5. **TAKE THE ELEMENT, DON'T DRAW A LOOK-ALIKE:** the DM initial is already that colour, already circling the right way. Take it, put the star in it. A colour rule is never the proof of a motion; the motion is the proof.
6. **LAYERS THEMSELVES MUST BE BOARDS:** a label made into a button is still not a board. The whole layer needs its own elevated board, like In a Nutshell — on hover it lights up in its colour and moves. The test is the nutshell: does this layer look like the In a Nutshell board?
7. **NOTHING DEAD ON THE BOARD:** every orb and every circle must be alive with its flow — a still orb is a dead orb. When a new element like Rate This Intel is added, its circle rides with it from the first build, never as a later round.
8. **SAY THE PLAN, NOT THE WORD:** when he asks what you are going to do, answer with the plan — the colours, the flow, the collision, the orb — so he can say yes, no, or different before the work is done. An echoed noun gives him nothing to judge.
9. **TOLD FIXED IS NOT FIXED — AND THE FRAME CAN LIE:** "fixed" waits for his eye. A delivery message is a claim, not proof. And a board can look right and say nothing — title, frame and nav present, body blank — so every board check reads the board's own text, never only that the title and nav are present.

## Why it matters

1. Without verify-first, the machine builds what already exists — the doubling-up he corrects all week — two copies of the same thing, two truths that drift apart.
2. Without proven arithmetic, wrong numbers ship a collision at 390px — the phone width he judges everything at — and a fix for the wrong cause ships a change that does nothing while the defect stays.
3. Without both screens, a page proven at 390px and never opened at laptop width is half-proven — and the half that fails is the one he is sitting at when he finds it.
4. Without stars that look intelligent, nothing else on the board gets believed — the labels, the notes, the whole intelligent block — because the measure argues against itself.
5. Without harvesting the DM initial, the copy matches the colour and misses the motion — and his eye catches the miss instantly, and the round comes back.
6. Without the layers boarded, the Hub reads as one flat page with headings instead of intelligence itself — text about intelligence, not intelligence.
7. Without the flow on every orb, a single dead element breaks the feeling for the whole board — alive is the whole language, and dead is never a design choice.
8. Without the plan stated plainly, he spends another turn re-asking — attention taken from the work itself — and the round is built on a guess that comes back wrong.
9. Without "fixed" waiting for his eye, the strongest word in the machine closes an item that is still open and hands him a surprise instead of a result — and the next "fixed" has to earn its meaning back.
