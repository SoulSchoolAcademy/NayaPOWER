His answer names the mechanism, not just the complaint: **a light that goes around the edge** — when you click a star, it lights up around the edge. The rated state needs a rim, so a rated star reads as rated at a glance and an unrated star reads as unlit.

The same call sent back two more items the checker had passed. The checker claimed a 64px gap and passed it; his eye says the buttons still sit on the box. Sixty-four pixels can be measured and still read as cramped, because spacing is felt before it is measured — the gap either reads as breathing room at a glance or it doesn't, and no number overrules the reading.

A spinning date draws the eye to the motion and away from the day; a lit edge draws the eye to the date itself. The same mechanism now serves two elements, which is how a design language forms: one answer, used the same way everywhere it is needed. On the same call his eye caught a big white line in the Hub's main area — "On the page you'll see it. On in the main area. See this big white line?" — and the line comes out, named or not. ## What it means

2. **A COUNT IS NOT A READING:** rating 7/10 lights exactly 7 stars brighter — in number. Brighter is a visible difference, not a count; a star can be counted as lit and still not look lit, and the eye is the judge of that.
