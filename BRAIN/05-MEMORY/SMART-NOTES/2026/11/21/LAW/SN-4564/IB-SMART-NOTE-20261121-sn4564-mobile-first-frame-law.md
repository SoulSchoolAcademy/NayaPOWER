## The Lesson

**MOBILE-FIRST FRAME LAW (his order, 2026-11-21 ~12:47 MST):**

**MOBILE FIRST, THEN LAPTOP.** Every frame is built mobile-first, then laptop. The phone is the first surface, not the afterthought — the frame is designed for the hand, then stretched to the desk.

**THE 9:16 PAIRING.** The 9:16 vertical frame gives more room to write and pairs as roughly a third/two-thirds with the text board beside it on a laptop; on mobile it folds to stacked blocks — video, then the next block. One frame, two arrangements: side by side on laptop, stacked on mobile.

**TOGGLE BETWEEN BOTH, GREAT ON BOTH.** Every page must be easily toggled between mobile and laptop and look great on both. The toggle is part of the page, not a test tool — the citizen flips it and the page holds.

**THE GATE.** If a design does not convert well both ways, we do NOT build the page that way — only frames optimized for mobile first that still look great on laptop ship. A design that is beautiful on one surface and broken on the other is not a design; it is half a page.

## Why It Matters

He looks at the pages on his phone first — that is where his eye is, and that is where the citizens live. A frame designed laptop-first and squeezed onto a phone is a frame that scrolls inside itself, clips its own words, and hides its own buttons — every failure in the calendar walk is a laptop-first frame meeting a phone. Mobile-first is the order that makes the frame fit where it is actually seen, and the laptop is the second surface, not the first excuse.

## How to Use It

Build every frame mobile-first, then laptop. Pair the 9:16 vertical frame roughly a third/two-thirds with the text board beside it on laptop; fold to stacked blocks on mobile. Make every page easily toggled between mobile and laptop, great on both. Do not ship a design that does not convert well both ways.
