## The Lesson

**BAR RULE — NOT STICKY (his rule, 2026-11-21 ~11:24 MST):**

**SHOWS ONLY WHILE SCROLLING DOWN.** The bottom icon bar is not a fixture. It appears while he scrolls down and it is gone the rest of the time. On most pages it isn't there at all. That is the rule: presence is the exception, absence is the default. A bar that sits on the page is a bar in the way of the frame — and the frame is the thing he is looking at.

**SUMMONED, NEVER STUCK.** When it is not showing, a small corner button pulls it up — or it can sit at the end of a page. The bar is summoned when wanted, never stuck where it is not. The corner button is the whole mechanism: small, out of the way, always findable. The end of the page is the other home: finish the page, find the bar there. Either way, the bar never claims space the frame should have.

**SUPERSEDES THE OLD WORDING.** This replaces the older law — "the page icon bar fades away while scrolling down and returns about a second after scrolling stops" — and the membership render language built on it ("fades + floats back ~3s"). The old wording had the bar returning on its own after a pause; the new rule has it showing only while scrolling down and otherwise gone until summoned. The difference is who decides: the old bar came back by itself; the new bar appears while he moves and waits to be called the rest of the time. Every brief, every bar build, and every render claim from here uses the new rule. The fade/float-back wording is retired — do not cite it, do not build to it, do not claim it as a pass.

## Why It Matters

The bar is on every page — ADD · PERSONAL · BUSINESS · WORLDS · ECOSYSTEM · CONNECT — so a wrong bar rule is wrong everywhere at once. The sticky bar, the fading bar, the bar that floats back uninvited: each one takes space from the frame and attention from the thing he came to see. The not-sticky rule gives the frame the whole page and gives him the bar exactly when he is moving through it — and a small corner button for every other moment. One bar, the same everywhere, showing only while scrolling down: that is the bar he described, and it is the bar every page carries from here.
