## Hook

"Three main parts run the whole system, and everything expands from them." From those three, one composer, one bar, one set of lists — and one finish line: his hands, his eye. "There's no open note. Just you need to put it on the main."

## Nutshell

Three main parts run the whole system, and everything expands from them: (1) the Hub — the central hub, where the intelligent notes go; (2) the Universal Hub — the Personal, one page that can be personal, business, space, whatever; (3) the Dashboard — the stats and the engine and the Smart Ledger, the whole heart of the technical world. From the three, anyone can get to the central Hub, get their stats, see what is going on, and create endless worlds — and nothing is a fourth main part; everything is a face of one of the three. The universal bottom bar belongs on the MAIN pages — Hub main, Personal main, Dashboard main — one bar, the same everywhere: ADD · PERSONAL · BUSINESS · WORLDS · ECOSYSTEM · CONNECT, with the plus button, network notifications showing there. It floats up a couple of seconds after scrolling stops, then you tap ADD or go to any world — and the Dashboard already has it, so it is the harvest source, taken byte for byte, never rebuilt. ADD pulls up a post composer that looks like an intelligent block, social part at the bottom: type whatever, add images, it comes out beautifully, and ADD asks "Which feed?" first. Images auto-format up to NINE — 1 full width in its own frame, 2 side by side, 3 across, 4 four-corner grid, 5–9 in even rows of three (9 = 3×3) — no manual layout, the block arranges them. The block comes Naya framed, standard: a setting lets the citizen choose framed or unframed, and DEFAULT IS NAYA FRAMED — separate from the unformat toggle, which is about the boards inside, while framed/unframed is about the outer frame. Then the test plan, in his words: (1) get the icons on the Hub; (2) end/finish on the Personal; (3) TEST 1 — he tests it himself on Personal AND on the Hub, making a post himself, with an image, coming out in the proper formatting — if it works, that is good; (4) TEST 2 — he gives me images and I auto-format them on the Hub itself. Both tests are his hands, his eye, and no round is done until they pass. The link rule guards his attention the same way: only send links that have actually been EDITED and are ready to be viewed — never a link he already reviewed, never a link to something unedited — because a link counts only as a NEW edited version awaiting his eye. And the names must be true: the voice system hears "Naya" as "nine," so when he says "Naya notes" the transcript shows "nine notes" — two different things, never to be confused. Naya notes are the full-deal notes; Nine Notes are the nine vision drafts (Heaven on Earth, Once You Go You Don't Go Back, and the rest). That trap settles the "posting options" fragment — he said "Smart Notes, Naya notes, and a smart post," not a fourth kind of thing — and the nine in A17 is nine IMAGES, a different nine entirely. The calendar entry screen that says "NAYA NOTES" must say "YOUR NOTES": the citizen writes their own notes here, not Naya — whatever they want, saved to their Smart Vault, auto-organized, all their notes for that day sitting there, back to any day to check that day's notes, images uploadable too. "MAKE BOTH NOTES" is ruled out, do not build it — the Naya→AI→Naya round-trip cannot send from Naya to the AI and back to Naya. Smart Lists adds "Your Notes" as a fifth list — the four as built, exactly as built, plus Your Notes, which opens the citizen's own notes by day — because people post manually sometimes and may want to input their own notes sometimes. And underneath both sits the full concept: a Smart List IS the feature — lists of notes, contacts, worlds, your notes — the tools live WHERE the citizen inputs, a PLUS button to create a list, tag the item to a list right there, images, everything saved to the Smart Vault for that day; AUTO-ORGANIZE — they input, we organize; and YOUR WORLDS in the main Hub is the TABLE OF CONTENTS for all the smart lists — go there, find all your lists, tap the list you want, it opens and shows every item in that list. Simple access is the point. Still open: where he saw "nine notes" on screen is to be pinned down — ask him which screen before fixing — and the journal wording ("journal your day in your words," "keep for this day," "what was this day?") is forming, not settled, do not build until he settles it.

## Why it matters

1. Without the Trinity, every new thing looks like a fourth app — and a builder who knows the three knows where a thing belongs before building it.
2. Without the bar on the main, the way in everywhere is missing where the citizen actually stands — on the open note it chases a view instead of anchoring the page.
3. Without the composer, the citizen has to learn two shapes — and without "Which feed?" first, a post has nowhere to go.
4. Without auto-format, every citizen has to arrange a grid by hand — and one image deserves its stage just as nine deserve their 3×3.
5. Without framed as the default, the signature frame stops being the standard — and without the citizen's choice, the standard becomes a lock instead of a default.
6. Without his tests, acceptance is a checker PASS — and a round that never reaches his hands never finishes.
7. Without the link rule, every re-sent link spends his attention for nothing — and teaches him that links can be ignored.
8. Without the Naya/nine distinction, one misheard word becomes a phantom category — a builder builds a thing that never existed, while the real thing, Naya notes, goes unfiled.
9. Without YOUR NOTES, a screen called NAYA NOTES tells the citizen Naya wrote these — and Naya did not. The name is the promise: theirs to write, theirs to keep, theirs to go back to.
10. Without the list as the feature, the tools live somewhere else and the lists live nowhere findable — a list nobody can find is not a list, and simple access is the point.
