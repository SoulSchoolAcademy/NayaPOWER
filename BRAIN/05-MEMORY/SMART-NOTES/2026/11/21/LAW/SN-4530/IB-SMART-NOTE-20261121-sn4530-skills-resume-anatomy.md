## In a Nutshell

The Skills page does not need ten questions — it needs to be done right so the message gets across: this is what I have to offer, this is my ability, my skills, my service, my offering to the world. It follows the anatomy of a résumé: the top is who you are — name, phone number, and the rest — then the statement: this is what I'm looking for, and this is what I have to offer. On the Skills page the headline comes first: "ultimately this is what I have to offer and this is what I'm looking to do for you." Then the experiences. A business says simply "here's my services." A person says "this is what I'm good at" — sports, technology, AI, dance, whatever it is. And it is not necessarily selling: "connect with me, I'm good at XYZ."

## Why It Matters

A Skills page built as ten more questions would bury the one thing it exists to say. The résumé anatomy puts the message first: who you are, then the statement of what you offer and what you are looking to do for them — and everything else, the experiences, the list of what you are good at, sits under that headline. A stranger landing cold sees the offer before they see the detail, and they know in seconds whether to connect. And because the page does not have to sell, it can welcome: a dancer, an athlete, a kid good at AI — all of them get to say "this is what I'm good at" and "connect with me," without having to turn themselves into a store.

## How to Use It

Build the Skills page as a résumé: top — who you are (name, phone, and the rest); then the headline statement — "this is what I have to offer and this is what I'm looking to do for you"; then the experiences, if they want to list them. A business: "here's my services." A person: "this is what I'm good at" — sports, technology, AI, dance, whatever it is, in their own words. End in connection, not necessarily a sale: "connect with me, I'm good at XYZ." Do not force ten questions onto the Skills page — its test is whether the message gets across. Take them at their word (SN-4529): no proof demanded at join.
