## What it means

1. **A REPEATED LESSON IS AN UNLEARNED LESSON:** if he has to say "stop making new" again, the last time it was said nothing changed mechanically — the repetition IS the evidence.
2. **A LESSON COUNTS ONLY WHEN BEHAVIOR CHANGES MECHANICALLY:** the next builder cannot make the same mistake — not discouraged from it, unable to.
3. **THE QUESTION IS THE TEST:** "Haven't you learned this lesson already?" is the question the loop asks itself after every correction — will he have to say this again?
4. **PROOF BEFORE HIS EYES:** the fix round proves the frames match before he sees it again — same frame as the agreement, fitted whole, no new design — measured, not claimed.
5. **THE COMPOUND IS THE MECHANISM:** inventory + brief-template + Project Intelligence + GitHub, same pass — that is what makes the lesson stick across builders.
6. **FAILURE IS NAMED PLAINLY:** if he has to say it again, the compound did not run — say so in the daily log and fix the loop, never silently file it again.

## Why it matters

1. Without the test, the loop files the correction and moves on — and the next builder makes something new again, because nothing stopped them.
2. Without the mechanical change, the lesson lives only in words — and words he has already spoken, over and over, at the cost of his patience.
3. Without proof before his eyes, the fix round comes back to him unproven — and he has to spend his own eye re-checking the frames, which is the very work the round owed him.
4. Without the compound, each correction reaches one place — a note, a template — while the builder works from another, and the lesson never reaches the hands that need it.
5. Without naming the failure plainly, the loop can keep failing quietly — filing the same lesson again while he keeps saying it again, and nobody ever says the compound did not run.
