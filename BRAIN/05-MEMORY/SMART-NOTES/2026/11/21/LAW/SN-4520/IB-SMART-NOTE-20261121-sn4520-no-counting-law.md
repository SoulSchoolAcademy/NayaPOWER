## In a Nutshell

Never count frames, never report "X of 98". Nobody sits through 98 slides. The 98-slide / deck-count approach is dead.

## The Lesson

**NO COUNTING LAW (his law, 2026-11-21 ~11:24 MST):**

**THE COUNT IS THE DEFECT.** "Never count frames, never report 'X of 98'." The number is not a progress report — it is the proof the approach is wrong. A deck that needs counting is a deck nobody will finish. When the machine reports "42 frames" or "98 frames" or "51 of 98 zero scroll," it is measuring the wrong thing: how many slides exist, instead of whether anyone will sit through them. The count turns a design failure into a statistic and hands it to him as if it were diligence.

**NOBODY SITS THROUGH 98 SLIDES.** That is the whole reason, in his words. A person does not open a page to count their way through 98 of anything. They open it to see the thing — the motto, the vision, the goal, the mission — one headline-led frame at a time, and then they are done. The 98-slide approach is dead not because 98 is too many and 42 would be fine, but because counting slides is how you build something nobody finishes. The fix is not a smaller number. The fix is no number at all.

**WHAT REPLACES THE COUNT.** One headline-led frame per item (SN-4523): Motto, Vision, Goal, Mission — his list, in that order, each its own frame, like the calendar and agreement frames in his screenshots. The frame is the unit, not the deck. A maker proves a frame is whole — everything fits inside it (SN-4522) — and never says how many frames there are in total, because the total is not the point and never was. If a round's report needs a number, the number is the frames that are whole, not the frames that exist.

## Why It Matters

Every About rebuild came back with a count — 42 frames, 98 frames, 51 of 98 zero scroll — and every count was the machine telling him the size of the thing instead of whether the thing was right. The counts made the work look measured while it was failing his eye three times in one morning. The No Counting Law kills the metric that let the failure hide: from here, a round reports frames whole, not frames counted. If a maker cannot say a frame is whole without saying how many there are, the frame is not the unit yet.

## How to Use It

Never write a frame count in a report, a brief, a checker verdict, or a message to him — no "X of 98", no "42 frames", no "51/98". Build one headline-led frame per item and prove each frame whole: everything fits inside it, no scrolling inside it, aligned, same spacing and same look and feel as the screenshots. If a build needs more frames to fit, overflow moves to the next frame — and the count still goes unreported.
