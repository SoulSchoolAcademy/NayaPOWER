# The Agreement Fix Round

SN-4455 · LAW / THE AGREEMENT FIX

**Created:** 2026-11-21 08:59Z

**Kinds:** Honor · Multiplication · Craft · Knowledge

## Hook

"Why do I have to keep telling you how to do buttons?" The button already exists — obsidian black, raised, real, with a faint white swish — on the Hub, the Collective, the Personal, the Activity button. That button. Stop doing new buttons.

## Nutshell

He had to say it again, and the saying is the law: "Stop doing new buttons. Just do them the same." The agreement's buttons kept coming back white, or purple, or a new invention each round — and the correction is not about this button's colour, it is that the button already exists, decided and proven, and a round that invents a new one is doing different work from the work asked. Both buttons are obsidian black, raised, real, with a faint white swish — forward swishing forward, back swishing back, a light around the edge — and the floated idea he withdrew in the same breath ("Maybe back is white and the forward is blue or purple") is not the order; the correction after it is ("No, no, no, no. No purple and whites"). Small, too: the Hub's own buttons, arrow-only, just above the fold at the bottom of the page, so forward is always clickable without scrolling — a button that takes up the whole page forgot its job. The beautiful red N lives with the text: centered under "Naya — create, learn, grow together," centered at the bottom of each slide board, in the text box only, with breathing room between the text and the bottom edge — never jammed against it — and only one red N ever, the one he picked; the crystal N stays in the corner exactly as it was, never taken by the red N. The board itself breathes wider, ~15–20% on desktop, so the N gets its room and the buttons fit just below it — and the phone is a question, not an assumption: prove the 390px layout and report exactly what happens, desktop-only widening if the phone breaks. The show moves again: the voice orb flows in a circle as she speaks and the black-and-blue background rays flow in a circle behind the frame — both were there before, both are still now, restored as they were, never redesigned, because a page that used to move and now stands still has gone backwards. The legal bar sits just below the frame, in the page flow — scroll down, see it, click any of its ten links, that is it — small, slim, quiet, never sticky, never overtaking the page, and "don't miss anything up" while fixing it. And the slides themselves got their pattern: image and a little text, then an all-text slide that continues the story of that image, then back to image — never scroll, every frame stays in the frame, click the button, never folding down. Under it all runs the standing law he named the magic of the system: bring everything back to its essence, consistently, always, keep only the best and what is needed, let the rest go — you do not enter a race carrying a pile of extra stuff. And the two conduct laws that guard the whole round: prove it on screen before you deploy — source-verified is not screen-proven, and a failed render stops the deploy instead of shipping a page whose corner logo is wrong — and never invent a rule and put his name on it: "nothing goes live until it matches your picture exactly" appears nowhere in his words, and his actual rule is live-first, finished work live immediately as a freeze point, because it is safer live than on a computer where it can be lost and he cannot access it.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: take what already works, give it room to breathe, prove it on a real screen, and never invent what he did not say.

## What it means

1. **THE HOUSE BUTTON IS THE ONLY BUTTON:** obsidian black, raised, real, faint white swish, a light around the edge — the Hub / Collective / Personal / Activity button, used everywhere, always the same. Never white, never purple, never new.
2. **SMALL ARROWS ABOVE THE FOLD:** the Hub's own buttons, arrow-only, sitting just above the fold at the bottom of the page, so forward is always clickable without scrolling. A button that takes up the whole page forgot its job.
3. **THE RED N LIVES WITH THE TEXT:** centered under the saying and at the bottom of every slide board, in the text box only, with breathing room between the text and the bottom edge. Only one red N ever — the beautiful one he picked. The crystal N stays in the corner exactly as it was.
4. **THE BOARD BREATHES:** widen the slide board ~15–20% on desktop, so the N gets its room and the buttons fit just below it. The phone is a question: prove the 390px layout and report exactly what happens; desktop-only widening if the phone breaks.
5. **MOTION IS THE SHOW:** the voice orb flows in a circle as she speaks; the black-and-blue background rays flow in a circle behind the frame. Restore both as they were — never redesign the motion, never ship a page that used to move and now stands still.
6. **THE LEGAL BAR SITS BELOW, QUIET, NEVER STICKY:** just below the frame, in the page flow — scroll down, see it, click any of its ten links, that is it. Small, slim, quiet, never overtaking the page, never fixed to the viewport.
7. **TEXT-ONLY CONTINUATION SLIDES, NEVER SCROLL:** image + a little text, then an all-text slide continuing the story of that image, then back to image. If text overflows a frame it becomes the next slide — it never becomes a scroll.
8. **ESSENCE, CONSISTENTLY:** bring everything back to its essence, always — keep only the best and what is needed, let the rest go. That is the magic of the system: speed, effectiveness, usefulness, and a system kept at high performance because it is never weighed down.
9. **PROVE IT ON SCREEN BEFORE YOU DEPLOY:** source-verified is not screen-proven. The claimed artwork must be seen resolving on screen before the deploy runs; a failed render stops the deploy instead of shipping a page whose corner is wrong.
10. **NEVER INVENT A RULE:** a rule that appears nowhere in his words is not his rule. His actual rule is live-first: finished work goes live immediately as a freeze point, because it is safer live than on a computer where it can be lost and he cannot access it.

## Why it matters

1. Without the house button, every round invents a new button for him to judge, correct, and re-teach — and a page whose buttons do not match the rest of the system does not feel like one system.
2. Without the small arrows above the fold, the reader has to hunt for the next button, and the moment they hunt, the flow breaks.
3. Without the red N's home, the mark lands in the wrong room or pressed against an edge — clutter on one side, a mistake on the other — and the page he called "really well done" stops looking done.
4. Without the wider board, the N has no room because the board has no room — and the fix becomes a smaller mark instead of the space the mark needs.
5. Without the motion restored, the page stands still where it used to live, and stillness where there was life reads as broken.
6. Without the legal bar reachable by scrolling, a bar that exists in code and not on screen is the same as no legal bar — the citizen scrolling down finds nothing to click.
7. Without the continuation pattern, a slide that does not fit becomes a scroll, and a presentation full of scrolls teaches the reader to leave the frame instead of staying in it.
8. Without essence as a habit, the system keeps everything and slows under its own weight — the same way his memory at ~900K characters a wake was not optimal.
9. Without the render gate, a build can be right in its files and wrong on the screen, and the defect he already corrected twice ships live.
10. Without the no-invention law, a guess stated as his order steers the whole machine wrong — work held back, deploys frozen, and him having to correct his own agent about his own words.
