## The Lesson

**SKILLS — ITS OWN LENS (his ruling, 2026-11-21 ~11:51–11:53 MST):**

**SKILLS IS WHAT YOU GIVE, NOT WHO YOU ARE.** About is about the person — learning about them, their world, what they are all about, where they stand. Skills is different: "what's your abilities? What do you have to offer? What is your service, right? ... what is your unique ability or what is your abilities? What is your skill? What is your service? What do you give to the world, right? What you know?" Imagine it as an online résumé of what you are personally offering — or what your business or your space offers. The core question is: what can you do for people, and how does it help them in real life? Then, for each skill: what it does for them, and how someone starts with you.

**TAKE THEM AT THEIR WORD — NO PROOF AT JOIN.** "They won't be able to show you proof. Because they're just filling out joining Naya. They're just inputting that data. So they won't be able to show you proof. But they you want to take them at their word that of what they're good at, right? Because imagine a child you're like, hey, show me proof that you're good at this. No child is going to be like, what?" A citizen joining Naya is inputting data, not presenting a case. Asking for proof at that moment asks them to prove something they have only just told you about themselves — and for a young person it makes no sense at all. Their story, in their own voice, is the proof at join.

## Why It Matters

Demanding proof at join turns a welcome into an interrogation. A 24-year-old landing on Naya, a child joining for the first time — they are telling you who they are and what they can do, in their own words, and the system either believes them or it does not. Taking them at their word is what makes the Skills page feel true and kind, like a real person introducing themselves — not a form demanding evidence. And it is the only way the walk works for everyone: a child has no proof to show, and should never be made to feel they need any. The Skills page is their offer to the world, in their voice — and the world's job is to meet them, not to audit them.

## How to Use It

Ask the Skills questions through their own lens: what can you do for people — what you offer, your ability, your service, your unique ability, what you give to the world. Per skill: what it does for them, and how someone starts. Never ask for proof at join — take them at their word. Strike "proof" from the walk-questions draft's per-skill list and from any Skills brief that still carries it. Audit every walk and Skills flow: any proof demand at join is out of alignment and comes out.
