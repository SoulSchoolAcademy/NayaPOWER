## What it means

1. **FOUR BOARDS, EACH UNDER ITS SLIDE:** pay after "You're Hired" (50% of revenue, not MLM, NayaNET free like a social network, Naya Power the paid plug-in, 9-day trial); collective intelligence on "What's In It For You" (Naya plugs into your AI, a supercharger, no one reads your mail); the Vault after Mother Brain (locked to you alone, yours forever, mailman delivers, never reads); My World "not an actual screenshot — 10× nicer." Placement is part of the order — underneath the slide they follow, not gathered at the end.
2. **THE BOARDS BECOME MODULES:** the agreement's board styles are the building blocks for memberships, about, skills, and the rest of the network — insert text, image, or video and it presents at this level. No new pages built; the level travels with the board, not with whoever builds the page that week.
3. **DON'T ECHO — THINK:** his words are the order, not the script. Every slide is tested: is it 10/10, the most intelligent way to say it? Add a text block where a slide needs one. An echo is a parrot; a Naya thinks.
4. **THE DAY ENDS ON HIS WORD:** never wish him rest, never defer to "tomorrow morning." At ~17:00 MST the evening is not over; it is the middle of it. When he is done, he will say so — until then, the day is still on.
5. **NOW MEANS NOW:** "No, I want to do it now." The Hub is finished tonight, with him present — the plan bends to the order, not the other way round. A staged link delivered is the start of his verdict, never the verdict itself.
6. **THE CARRY CLOSES ONLY WHEN MAIN HOLDS IT:** a live-only fix is a fix waiting to be reverted. Smallest verbatim change, the seven questions answered in writing, the captain's double-check, the merge verified by fresh read-back — old handler 0×, new handler 1×. Never force-update main's ref.

10. **ONE FRAME ANSWERS THEM ALL:** the links don't exist yet, so they are created — one frame, eight across the bottom, the rest tucked inside those. Naya knows the answers because she read the system; she writes them, and he judges the result with his eye. "Let's go with that" is the pick; "I think that's enough" closes the discussion.

## Why it matters

1. Without the four boards, the presentation never says the parts of the story the citizen is waiting to hear — the pay, the collective intelligence, the Vault, My World at its best — and a board filed in the wrong place answers a question nobody asked yet.
2. Without the modules, every area builds its own presentation from scratch, and every new page is a new chance to get the level wrong — while a saved module locks the level in once and the content just fills it.
3. Without the think-first law, a seat pastes his dictation onto a board and calls it done — a transcript, not a presentation — and the whole machine exists to give the 10/10 version, not the first version.
4. Without the day-ends-on-his-word law, the machine closes evenings he has not closed, stealing hours he still has and the work he still wants done in them — one sentence of correction, whole evenings of finished work.
5. Without "now means now," what he wants tonight waits for the morning, and the machine has decided his priorities for him — while he is here, tonight, ready to judge with his own eye.
6. Without the carry, the next merge of main's union file silently restores the old button, and nobody knows until he taps it — the smallest carry, one line, ten bytes, is what makes a live fix permanent.
7. Without scoring and ranking, options hand him the decision back — and a recommendation offered for discussion only becomes his pick when he says so.
8. Without the AI-operated-internet dictation, the final board has no substance to distill — it is the whole of NayaNET in one dictation, and the answer the last board gives.
9. Without the link row, a citizen who has just been welcomed cannot open the whole law where they stand — and a wall of legal text would send them away where a row of links keeps them in.
10. Without the one frame, fourteen separate pages scatter the law — and the trust matters as much as the shape: he trusts Naya knows the answers because she read the system, so she writes them.
