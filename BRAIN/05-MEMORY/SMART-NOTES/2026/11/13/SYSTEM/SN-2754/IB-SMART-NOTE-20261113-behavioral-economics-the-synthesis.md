**Human:** A system that knows everything about a person and still lets them make a bad decision has failed at the last step — the step where the knowing becomes the choosing. Behavioral economics is the study of that last step: how the way a choice is shown changes the choice that gets made. The synthesis says the answer is not to exploit the shaping — it is to design the shaping honestly. Show the real costs next to the real benefits. Say what you do not know. Offer the "neither" and the "not now." Make the good path easy and the risky path checked. Measure whether the person understood and benefited, not whether they clicked. And never forget the caution: most of these effects are hypotheses with weak averages, not laws — a jam study that averages to nothing, a savings result that only counts the people who stayed. So the work is a pilot, one journey, measured on the human's side — completion, understanding, confidence, regret — and if it works, one Naya proves it and another Naya retrieves it, and the collective has learned something real. The biggest thing is the frame itself: we align the AI, we align the architecture, we align the database — and now we align the human's cognition with the same rigor, because the human's decision is the whole point of the system.
