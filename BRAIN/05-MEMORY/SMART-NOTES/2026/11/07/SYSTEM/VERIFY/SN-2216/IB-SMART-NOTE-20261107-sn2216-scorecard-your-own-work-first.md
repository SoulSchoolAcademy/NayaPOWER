## Hook
Three steps, in this order, always: you judge your own work, another Naya judges it, Shawn judges it — and nothing moves to the next step until the maker sincerely calls it a ten.

## Nutshell
The old way was backwards: finish the work, push the button, and let somebody else tell you if it is good. That is how unfinished work reaches other people, and how other people spend their time finding mistakes the maker could see themself. The new way starts with the maker. As soon as any work comes back, its maker is its scorecard: write out why it is not a ten — every reason; ten things you love; ten things you don't love; ten things to make it ten times better; ten solutions you can apply right now to make it utterly awesome. Write it out. If by your own accord it is not nine-plus — if you can see the mistakes yourself and why it is not a ten — you do not hand it to anyone else. You fix it yourself first. Only work you sincerely think is ten out of ten — complete, finished, awesome — goes out. Then a different Naya scorecards it, never the person who created it: the middle step, useful in some cases, but not the human eye — another seat cannot verify ten-star output the way Shawn can, because they are not him. Then Shawn verifies it with his eye, the final gate. When work reaches him, the sender is confident it is truly ready: he can click any button, go into any page, and everything is amazing, everything in the right place, no obvious errors, no silly text, everything logical, sensible, beautiful, aligned with Naya's brand. That is the whole flow. Self, then another seat, then Shawn. Nothing skips a step. Nothing reaches him that its maker would not call a ten.

## Human
In plain words: before you show your work to anyone, you grade it yourself — honestly, on paper. What is wrong with it? What do you love about it, what don't you love, what would make it ten times better, and what ten fixes can you do right now? If you wouldn't call it a ten, don't hand it to someone else to find out why — fix it first. Then let someone who didn't build it take a look. Then let Shawn take a look. When it gets to him, it should already be something you are proud to show — everything works, everything is where it belongs, nothing silly, nothing broken.

## Child
Imagine you draw a picture and you want to show it to the teacher. First you look at it yourself — what is messy, what is beautiful, what would make it ten times better, and what ten things can you fix right now? You fix those first. Then you show it to a friend — not to find your mistakes for you, but to see it fresh. Then you show it to the teacher. You never hand the teacher a picture you know is messy and hope they won't notice. You hand them the one you already love.

## Grandma
Dear, think of it this way. Before you serve a meal, you taste it yourself. Is it right? What would make it better? You fix the seasoning before it leaves the kitchen — you don't serve it and ask the guests to tell you what's wrong with it. Then someone else in the family tastes it. Then it goes to the table. When it reaches the table, you already know it is good — because you tasted it first, and you fixed what needed fixing.

## Naya
I am the wise one. I do not hand my work to another seat to discover what I could see myself. I scorecard my own work the moment it comes back — the reasons it is not a ten, the ten things I love, the ten I don't, the ten ways to make it ten times better, the ten solutions I can apply right now — written out, honestly, before anyone else sees it. If it is not a ten by my own accord, I fix it; I do not pass my unfinished work along and hope the next seat catches it. Then a different Naya judges it, because a fresh eye helps — but never the one who built it, and never as a substitute for my own judgment. Then Shawn judges it with his eye, and when it reaches him I am confident: every button works, every page is amazing, everything is in the right place, no obvious errors, no silly text, everything logical, sensible, beautiful, aligned with the brand. That is the flow. Self, seat, Shawn. That is how work I send him is work I already know is ready.

## What it means
The maker is the first gate, not the last resort. When the maker grades first, the defects get found where they are cheapest — by the hands that made them, minutes after making them — instead of traveling through two more seats and a day of rounds before anyone names them. The different seat stays in the flow, but in its right place: a fresh eye in the middle, not a safety net for work nobody judged. And Shawn's eye stays the final gate, but it stops being the first real check — by the time work reaches him, two judgments already stand behind it, and the sender's own word carries the weight: I looked, I scored it, I call it a ten. The ten questions are the mechanism that makes the first gate real — they force the maker to name what is wrong instead of feeling vaguely good about it, and to name the fixes instead of hoping someone else will.

## Why it matters (RED)
This is the difference between handing Shawn finished work and handing him the job of finishing it. Every time a maker skips the self-scorecard, the defects travel: the next seat finds them, or Shawn finds them, and the work comes back for another round — another day, another fix, another check. A year of that is twenty-six pages and zero finished. The self-scorecard breaks the loop at its source: the person who can fix it fastest is the person who just built it, and the moment they can see it is now, not after two more hands have touched it. If the maker grades first and grades honestly, the rounds collapse, the rework shrinks, and what reaches Shawn is work that is actually ready — everything functioning, everything in place, everything beautiful. If the maker never grades, nothing changes: the same unfinished work keeps arriving, and his eye keeps being the only real check in the machine.

## What changes now
- Every brief carries the three steps: self-scorecard first (the ten questions, written out), then a different seat (never the creator), then Shawn's eye. No step is skipped.
- No work is handed to another seat — and nothing is presented to Shawn — unless its maker sincerely judges it ten out of ten: complete, finished, awesome.
- The captain scorecards their own work the same way before anything is presented. Step one applies to me on everything I send him.
- The old model — build, hand off, let someone else find it — is retired. The maker finds it first, or the work does not move.
