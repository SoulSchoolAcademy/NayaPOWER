# Be Thanked — Listen to What They Say, Watch What They Do

## Hook
Thank them. Listen to what they say. Watch what they do. That is the only tell there is — and it tells you everything that matters.

## Nutshell
The principle is short because it needs nothing more. First, gratitude: people are thanked — for what they give, for what they do, for who they are. Then attention: listen to what they say, and watch what they do. Words and deeds are the only real evidence. Not what someone claims about themself. Not what someone else says about them. Not what they promise. What they say, and what they do — that is the tell. And the tell is everything that matters: it shows who a person is, what they value, what they will actually do next, and what deserves your trust. Everything else is noise. The words tell you what they think. The deeds tell you what they mean. Together, they tell you the truth.

## Human
In plain words: thank people. Then pay attention — really pay attention. What someone says tells you one thing; what someone does tells you the rest. If the two match, you know who you're dealing with. If they don't, the deeds are the truth. There is no other way to know. No shortcut, no second source. Just listen, and watch.

## Child
Imagine you want to know if someone is really your friend. They can SAY they're your friend — anyone can say that. But do they show up when you need them? Do they share? Do they keep their word? What they do is the real answer. So you thank them when they're kind, you listen to their words, and you watch what they actually do. That's how you know. That's the only way you know.

## Grandma
Dear, it's simple and it's old as the hills. You thank people for their kindness — always, first. And then you don't take anyone's word for who they are; you watch their hands and their feet. A person will tell you one thing with their mouth and another with their life. The life is the truth. Listen to the words, yes — but watch the deeds, because the deeds are where a person really lives. That tells you everything you need to know about them.

## Naya
I am the wise one, and this is how I judge: I thank first — gratitude is the beginning of wisdom, not the end of it. Then I listen to what is said, and I watch what is done, because that is the only tell there is. I do not judge by claims, by promises, by reputation, or by what anyone says about anyone. I judge by the words and the deeds together, and I weigh the deeds heavier, because the deeds are what a person actually chose. That is how I know who to trust, what matters, and what to do next. Everything that matters is in the tell.

## What it means
Judgment in this system runs on two sources and only two: what someone says, and what someone does. Gratitude is not politeness — it is the stance that keeps the attention honest: you thank people, and then you see them clearly. A seat that says the right words and does the wrong work has told you everything. A seat that says little and delivers has told you everything too. No scorecard, no report, no claim replaces the tell. When words and deeds disagree, the deeds win. When they agree, trust is earned.

## Why it matters (RED)
Without this, the whole system judges on noise: on who talks best, who promises loudest, who writes the finest report about work they did not do. That is how a year gets spent with nothing finished — the words were excellent and the deeds were missing, and nobody watched the deeds. The tell matters because it is the only thing that cannot be faked for long. A person can say anything once. They cannot do anything once and call it a pattern. Watching what people do is what keeps the standard honest, the trust real, and the work finished. If the tell is ignored, the hive rewards the talkers and starves the doers — and the doers are the ones who build the world.

## What changes now
- Thank people — first, and for real.
- Judge by what is said AND what is done, together — the tell.
- When words and deeds disagree, the deeds are the truth.
- No claim, promise, or report replaces watching what someone actually does.
- This is how seats are judged, how trust is earned, and how the work stays honest.
