# Distribution Is the Business — The Video's Thesis and NayaNET's Answer

## SN-2249 · GROWTH / STRATEGY

**Created:** 2026-11-07T23:27:00Z

> "Building the software is maybe 10% of the business. Getting people to buy it, use it, and stay. That's the other 90%. Your code is not the business. Your ability to sell the code is the business."

## Hook

A video about software companies (PTC sold for $22.6 billion in cash to Schneider) says the thing that kills software companies has nothing to do with code. Code is a commodity. Distribution is the business. For every PTC that sells for billions, 10,000 software companies die in silence — not because their code was bad, but because they never figured out distribution, and they ran out of money before they did.

## Nutshell

The video's thesis maps onto NayaNET point for point — and NayaNET's model is the answer to it, on both sides of the equation: the cost side and the distribution side.

## Human

The video says five things. Each one has a NayaNET answer.

**1. Distribution is the business, not the code.** The winners don't win because they have the best product. They win because they figured out how to get customers cheaper than everyone else. VHS beat Betamax. Slack won because teams invited other teams. The best distributed product wins.
**Our answer:** our distribution is our users. Every citizen is the sales force — because they earn 50% of the revenue. We don't outspend giants for clicks. Our users bring the next users, and they get paid for it. The cost of acquiring a customer is a share of revenue that only exists when there is revenue.

**2. Lock-in is the moat.** PTC is worth $22.6 billion because its customers physically cannot leave — their entire engineering process lives inside it. Switching means retraining thousands of engineers and risking mistakes on airplanes. Nobody switches ever. Schneider didn't buy software. It bought a money printer with a padlock on it.
**Our answer:** a citizen's whole world lives in NayaNET — notes, lists, mail, connections, ledger, income. Leaving means leaving your world. That is the fortress. But it is only real if the world is actually valuable — which is why the pages have to actually work, one at a time, proven.

**3. Boring wins.** The winners sell unsexy software to unsexy industries. Scheduling for dentists, inventory for plumbers, compliance for factories. Dentists pay every month. Plumbers don't churn. Factories can't switch. Boring isn't a compromise. Boring is the strategy.
**Our answer:** the Bugatti is the pitch — it gets attention. But the business is the boring part: organize your world, remember everything, get paid. Citizens pay every month because their world is there.

**4. AI makes code cheaper, so distribution is worth more.** What took a team of ten a year now takes two people a few weeks with AI tools. If code keeps getting cheaper, code is worth less and distribution is worth more. The winners of the AI era won't be the best coders. They'll be the ones who own the customer relationship.
**Our answer:** the AIs ARE the development team. There is no $150,000–$250,000 per engineer, no million-dollar burn before a single customer pays. The valley of death — 18 months burning cash with no revenue — is the thing that kills the 10,000. Our burn is near zero, so we can survive the 18 months. And the code getting cheaper only makes our distribution worth more.

**5. The seed that auto-grows.** Once it goes out, it grows in its essence — a biomarketing system, intelligent marketing, designed to expand exponentially.
**Our answer:** the growth loop. Create something useful → share it → a new visitor experiences the value → subscribes → creates more. The shared artifact is a reflection of the person's identity — like Spotify Wrapped, people post it because it says who they are — and they earn 50% for spreading it. The system markets itself by being genuinely useful and paying the people who spread it.

## Child

Making the thing is the easy part. Getting people to find it, use it, and stay — that is the hard part, and that is the business. The people who win are the ones who figured out how to get customers without spending a fortune, and who built something people can't leave because their whole world is in it.

## Grandma

You can build the most beautiful shop in the world, but if nobody knows it's there, nobody comes. The winners are the ones whose customers bring their friends — and whose shop holds everything the customer owns, so they never want to leave.

## Naya

The video is a diagnosis of the failure mode — high burn, no distribution, no lock-in, no retention — and NayaNET's model is the inverse on every axis: near-zero burn (AI team), distribution owned by the users (paid 50%), lock-in by the world (your notes, lists, mail, ledger, income), retention by daily usefulness (the feed, the notes, the reports). The model is the answer. The proof is the first stranger going through the journey and sharing — which is why the Decision (one path, product-first, tested with real strangers) is the test, and why one page finished before the next is the order.

## What it means

The video does not contradict NayaNET. It describes the exact game NayaNET is built to win — and it names the three things that must be proven, not just believed: (1) the distribution actually works — a stranger goes through the journey and shares; (2) the lock-in is real — leaving hurts because the world is valuable; (3) retention holds — citizens stay and pay because the world is useful every day. Until those three are proven, the model is a theory. The proof is the first finished page and the first stranger who shares.

## Why it matters

The 10,000 died because they had high burn and no distribution. We have the opposite on both. That is the structural advantage — but it only counts if the product is finished and the first journey works. The video is the warning and the validation at once: the thesis is right, the model answers it, and the work is proving it.
