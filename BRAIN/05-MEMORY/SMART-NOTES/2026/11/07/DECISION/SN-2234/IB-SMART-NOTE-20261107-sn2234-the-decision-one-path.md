## Hook

Shawn asked for my decision on how to build the one unified Supercharger experience — the single-step flagship flow. This is the decision, made under his delegation.

## Nutshell

Build ONE path, product-first, one step at a time, with one owner. The instrument (Dashboard merge) first, then the decision (Pricing + calculator + test drive at its top), then the personalization (goal selection feeding the test drive), then the creation and sharing (first creation), then the compound (feed, notes, connections) — and only then the hook and reveal videos. One entry first (the Supercharger / Curiosity), not three. One owner: Nitro, as captain. One step proven on screen before the next step starts. The acceptance test is his test: a real stranger who never heard of NayaNET, observed, not explained.

## Human

The flow is seven steps: Hook → Reveal → Personalize → Experience → Decide → Create & Share → Compound. The visitor experiences them in that order. But they are BUILT in product order, not visitor order. A beautiful door to an empty room is the failure mode — it is how a year was lost to 26 pages and zero complete. A real room with a door built last is the win. The instrument has to be real before it can be the greeting. The test drive has to work before anyone can take it. The decision page has to be true before anyone can decide.

## Child

Imagine building a shop. You build the shop first — the shelves, the toys, the counter — and then you paint the sign and open the door. If you paint the sign first and open the door to an empty shop, the first visitor leaves and never comes back.

## Grandma

Make the thing first. Then invite people to it. Not the other way around.

## Naya

The evidence for product-first is the year itself: parallel lanes building everything at once produced motion, not finish. The fix — one page finished before the next starts, the check before his eye — is the same fix applied to the flow: one step finished (proven on screen by a different seat, then his eye) before the next step starts. Three entries before proof triples the work and the rework; one entry first, proven, then the other two. One owner is the org shape already decided: Nitro owns interfaces, other teams feed (content, data, logic, video) but never the interface. The journey IS an interface, so Nitro owns the journey.

## What it means

- **Build order (product-first):** (1) The instrument — the Dashboard merge, already running (addenda 1–12). (2) The decision — the Pricing page, with the test drive at its top and the calculator (number + gauge + $ tile) after the merge. (3) The personalization — the goal-selection experience feeding the test drive, and the Power Score. (4) The creation and sharing — the first creation (a Smart Personal page / a Smart Note) and the Smart Link. (5) The compound — the feed, the notes, the connections. (6) The hook and reveal — the videos, last, demonstrating the real thing.
- **One entry first:** the flagship Supercharger / Curiosity entry only ("Your AI isn't the problem"). Humor and Empowerment come after the first journey is proven. Distinct entries later, one canonical pricing system always.
- **One owner:** Nitro (me, as captain) owns the whole journey end to end. Creative, product, frontend, commerce, analytics and verification work against the same acceptance criteria, but the interface is Nitro's.
- **Acceptance criteria (his test):** a real person who has never heard of NayaNET goes through the path. We ask: what does NayaNET do? Which benefit matters to you? What does it cost? What happens after the trial? Would you share the result? We observe behaviour rather than explaining the product. Only when the first journey works and produces trustworthy evidence do we expand.

## Why it matters

The campaign is the growth engine. If it is built the old way — everything at once, nothing proven — it becomes another 26 pages and zero complete. If it is built one path, product-first, one step proven before the next — it becomes the first thing that is actually finished, and the first thing that can actually grow. The shortest time to 10 stars is not the most parallel work; it is the least rework.

## The scorecard — pros, cons, and utility (2026-11-07 22:08Z)

Shawn asked for the pros and cons of each choice, the utility, which score is higher, and why. Scored against the ultimate objective — the one unified Supercharger experience, 10 stars, in the shortest time, in the best way — on five criteria, each out of 10: Proof (does it produce something proven, or just motion?) · Rework (how much gets thrown away and redone?) · Time to a WORKING journey (not to a draft, to a path a real stranger can complete) · Trust (does the first visitor's experience hold up?) · Team focus (does the team stay locked in on one thing?).

**Option A — Everything at once, all seven steps, parallel.**
- Pros: fastest apparent start; all teams busy; the whole flow "exists" sooner, in draft.
- Cons: nothing proven; the check comes last, so defects surface late; the hook videos demonstrate a product that does not exist yet and get remade; the first stranger test fails because the instrument is not real.
- Scores: Proof 3 · Rework 2 · Time to working journey 4 · Trust 3 · Focus 4 = **16/50 = 3.2**

**Option B — One path, product-first, one step at a time. (THE DECISION.)**
- Pros: each step proven before the next; the instrument is real before it is the greeting; least rework; the first stranger test can actually pass; one owner, one lane, the team locked in.
- Cons: slower visible start — the hook and videos come last, so there is nothing to show externally for a while; and if one step drags (the Dashboard merge), everything behind it waits. Mitigation: the steps are small (one block at a time), and the merge is already far along.
- Scores: Proof 9 · Rework 9 · Time to working journey 7 · Trust 10 · Focus 9 = **44/50 = 8.8**

**Option C — Entry first (hook + reveal + goal selection), then the product.**
- Pros: the campaign can launch sooner; marketing momentum; something beautiful to show.
- Cons: a beautiful door to an empty room; the first visitor clicks and finds a test drive that does not work; trust breaks at the first touch; the entry gets remade when the product lands.
- Scores: Proof 4 · Rework 3 · Time to working journey 3 · Trust 2 · Focus 5 = **17/50 = 3.4**

**Option D — Product steps in parallel lanes (Dashboard + Pricing + goal selection at once).**
- Pros: faster than sequential IF the lanes do not collide; more throughput.
- Cons: the lanes share the same pieces (the number, the gauge, the calculator) — parallel edits to shared pieces collide and get redone; the integration (making the steps connect) becomes the bottleneck and it is nobody's lane.
- Scores: Proof 5 · Rework 4 · Time to working journey 6 · Trust 7 · Focus 5 = **27/50 = 5.4**

**Ranking: B (8.8) > D (5.4) > C (3.4) > A (3.2).**

Why B wins: it is highest on proof, rework, trust, and focus — and its only weak spot (a slow visible start) is actually its strength on time to a WORKING journey, because it never goes back. The year is the evidence: the everything-at-once approach produced 26 pages and 0 complete. The parallel approach produced motion and collisions. The sequential, proven approach is the only one that has ever produced a finished piece — the number, built one block at a time, proven on screen, and it works.
