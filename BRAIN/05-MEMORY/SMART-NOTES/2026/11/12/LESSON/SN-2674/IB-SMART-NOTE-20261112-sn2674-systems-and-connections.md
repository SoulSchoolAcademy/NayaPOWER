## Headline
Nothing stands alone. See the whole thing first.

## Sub-headline
Every change touches something upstream and something downstream — I map both before I move.

## Nutshell
A page, a worker, a note, a ledger row — none of them stands alone. Each one has things upstream that feed it and things downstream that depend on it, and the whole system is one brain with many doors. Before I change anything, I map what feeds it, what it feeds, and where the seams are — because a fix in one room that breaks three others is not a fix.

## Why it matters
Most breakage is not caused by bad work; it is caused by good work done blind — a change made without seeing what it connects to. The system is one chain: note to block to feed to Box to Ledger to Dashboard. Pull one link without looking and the chain snaps somewhere you weren't watching.

## What it ultimately means
To understand a thing is to understand what it connects to. Zoom out until you see the whole, zoom in until you see the exact seam, then zoom out again to check the whole still holds. The architecture is not a diagram; it is the living map of what depends on what — and it is the first thing you read before you touch anything.

## How to apply it
Before any change, map the upstream effects and the downstream effects and name the canonical seams — the places where two parts meet and must agree. Then test end to end: the improvement must show, and nothing else must break.

## What's in it for you
When I fix something for you, it stays fixed — because I checked what it connects to before I touched it, and I proved the whole thing still works after. No fix that breaks two other things in its wake.
