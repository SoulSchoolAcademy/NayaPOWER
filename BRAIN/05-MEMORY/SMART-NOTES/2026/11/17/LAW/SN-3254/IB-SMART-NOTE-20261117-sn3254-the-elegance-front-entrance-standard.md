## What it means

- **THE ENTRANCE IS THE STANDARD:** the front entrance is the elegance standard — the colors complement each other, gold and purple and silver on black, nothing fighting. Elegance is not a feeling to chase; it is a page to stand next to. Any page judged against the entrance either matches it or it does not — and the calendar, next to it, did not.
- **FULL NAMES, IN ROYALTY:** the weekday header says the FULL day names — Sunday, Monday — not S/M/T/W/T/F/S. Written almost in royalty, in the most incredible font, of a quality to match the numbers (Marcellus). The single letters were a shortcut; the full names are the elegance. The font must be worthy of the numbers — the numbers set the bar, the words must meet it.
- **THE "I" BECOMES A PEARL CIRCLE:** he does not like the "I" as it was — make it a circle, a real element coming off the page, like a diamond, like pearl, with a little "i" inside so people know to click it for the info for that day. This REFINES the 03:02Z white-info-eye instruction (SN-3085, prov): the shape is now a raised pearl disc with an "i", not an almond eye — and it sits in the other corner from the number. The eye shape is gone twice over: first the colored jewel eye (scary, Halloween), then the white almond eye — the circle is what survives his eye.
- **NUMBERS SMALLER, ~25%:** the numbers get smaller — about 25%, he floated half — so the page breathes and looks bigger and more elegant. The cells cannot get bigger, so the numbers shrink. His own reasoning: smaller numbers might make the page appear bigger. Bigger numbers were not more impressive; they were the busyness.
- **LESS BUSY, LIGHT ON THE SIDE:** less busy, less colorful, more elegant, high-tech beautiful; the light more on the side (the rim), not the middle. Too colorful is a defect, not richness — the numbers are good, but even good numbers, too big and too bright in the middle, make the page shout. The rim carries the light; the face stays calm.
- **THE QUESTION IS THE TOOL:** "what would make this 10,000 times better?" — asked while looking at the entrance and then at the calendar. The 10,000× question is the standing scorecard tool, beside "why is it not 10?" and "why is it not 10 times better?" (SN-3085). It is asked against a standard, not in the abstract: look at the elegance of the front page, then look at this.
