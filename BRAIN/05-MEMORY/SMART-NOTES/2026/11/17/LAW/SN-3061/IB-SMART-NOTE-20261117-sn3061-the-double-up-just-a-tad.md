## Human

A double-up is a clone sitting just behind the thing, offset by a hair, so the eye reads depth — one object sitting on another, a real thing with a shadow of itself. Move it a little too far and the trick breaks: now you SEE the spacing, you see two things with a gap between them, and the eye reads it as a mistake, not as depth. It looks weird. It doesn't look pro. The calibration is not a number you measure — it's a feeling you check with the eye: can you feel the depth? Can you see the gap? If you can see the gap, you went over much — pull it back until the gap disappears and only the depth is left. Just a tad. That is the whole law: the offset is a hair, never a step.

## What it means

- **DEPTH YOU FEEL, NOT A GAP YOU SEE:** the double-up (clone) exists to make a real object read as deep — one thing sitting on its own shadow. The moment the spacing becomes visible, the illusion dies and the eye reads two things with a gap between them.
- **"JUST A TAD" IS THE CALIBRATION:** the offset is a hair. There is no number in this law — the check is the eye: can you feel the depth? Can you see the gap? If the gap is visible, you went over much — pull it back.
- **OVERSHOOT READS AS A MISTAKE, NOT AS MORE DEPTH:** more offset is not more depth. Past the hair, every extra pixel makes it look worse, not deeper. The pro version is the smallest one that still reads.
- **THE EYE DECIDES, NOT THE MEASUREMENT:** no ruler settles this. His eye saw the spacing and called it — the fix is judged the same way, by looking, not by measuring.
- **HEADLINE OF FIX-4:** the first thing fix-4 must get right, before the boards, the gray, the orbit, the rims, the glyphs — because it is the thing his eye caught first.

## Why it matters

Without this note, the next designer hears "do the double-up" and moves the clone by a comfortable, visible amount — because a visible amount feels like doing the job properly — and the page comes back looking weird again, and Shawn has to say it a second time. With it, the law is in the brief: a hair, never a step; feel it, don't see it. The lesson generalizes past clones: every depth effect in the system — shadows, lifts, glows, orbits — is judged the same way. Depth you feel is pro; spacing you see is a mistake.
