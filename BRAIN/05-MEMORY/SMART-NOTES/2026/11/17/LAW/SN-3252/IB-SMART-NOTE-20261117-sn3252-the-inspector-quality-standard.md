## What it means

- **THE INSPECTOR IS A STANDING SEAT, NOT A ROUND:** always have an inspector — it sweeps again after every major landing and once a day with the daily report. A one-off audit catches one drift; a standing inspector catches every drift, because it runs after every landing. The cadence is the law: after every major landing, and daily.
- **THE STANDARD IS THE LANGUAGE, ALL OF IT:** every page scored against the Code, the Design Language, the Palette, the Real Object Law. Four measures, not one — a page can pass the palette and fail the Real Object Law, or pass the Code and fail the language. The inspector scores against all four, every page.
- **THE QUESTION IS ALIGNMENT:** is everybody in line? Is everybody designing in the brand language, in line with Naya's design, where it's truly her design all the way through? "Truly her design all the way through" is the bar — not mostly, not the top half, all the way through. A page that is her design in the header and not her design in the footer is not in line.
- **THE OUTPUT IS A RANKED LIST, WORST FIRST:** the breaks listed and ranked, worst first. The inspector does not write an essay; it writes a list, ordered by severity, so the worst break gets fixed first. This is the loop's own shape (listen → understand → note → file) applied to quality: the sweep is the listen, the ranking is the understand.
- **THIS IS THE ANSWER TO TONIGHT:** the jewel eye with the colored iris passed a builder's check and failed his eye four minutes later; the Dashboard passed its checker and his eye said "it all looks terrible"; the Smart Mail re-checker passed the dock and the captain's eye found it half-cut. The inspector is the seat that exists because passes are not verdicts — it walks the project page by page against the language, so the drift gets caught by the seat instead of by his eye.
