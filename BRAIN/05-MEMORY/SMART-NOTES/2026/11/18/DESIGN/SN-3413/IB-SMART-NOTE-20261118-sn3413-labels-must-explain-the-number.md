# Labels Must Explain the Number

SN-3413 · DESIGN / DISTILLATION

**Created:** 2026-11-18T20:13:00Z

## IN A NUTSHELL

A board that needs a manual has already failed: its label must explain its number, its design must be one design, and its sequence must follow the colour flow without exception. This family is the board-law stratum — ten-is-full-width, jewels under the start button, labels that say what the number means, notifications where the eye expects them, the Boards page defined by purpose and three sections, and two-things-headliner-first ordering. Each rule sounds small; together they are the difference between a page a stranger reads in one pass and a page a stranger studies and abandons.

## HUMAN

Think of road signs. Every town uses the same shapes and colours, the place name is big, and the distance is labelled so you know it's miles. Imagine if every town designed its own signs — you'd slow down at every one, and some you'd misread entirely. Boards are signs for ideas.

## CHILD

In a comic book, every box is the same shape and you read them in order, and the words tell you what's happening in the picture. If every box was a different shape and the words were missing, you'd give up — even if the story was great.

## GRANDMA

A good cookbook uses the same layout for every recipe: name, how long, what you need, what to do. You learn it once and then you can cook anything in the book. That's kindness in print. These board rules are the same kindness, on a screen.

## NAYA

Reject per-board design invention in review. Check label-presence for every rendered number. Verify full-width rendering and colour sequence from screenshots in reading order. Headliner element must be first in its section's DOM and visual order.

## MACHINE

1) One board design system-wide; per-board invention is a defect. 2) Boards render full width (ten is full width) at their breakpoint. 3) Every number on a board carries a label that explains what the number means, in plain words. 4) Board colour follows the standing sequence; no board takes a neighbour's colour. 5) A page section leads with its headliner; supporting items follow, never precede.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

Boards are the unit of persuasion on every NayaNET page: three boards, then a button. If each board invents its own design, width and label logic, the reader pays a learning tax on every single one, and the tax compounds until the page feels overwhelming — the exact defect named in this stratum. One design, full width, explained numbers and a fixed colour sequence turn the tax into momentum: each board makes the next one easier.

## WHY IT MATTERS

Citizens decide in seconds whether a page is for them. Boards that explain themselves, in a rhythm the eye learns once, are how a long page becomes a short experience. This essence keeps the rhythm mechanical so no builder has to re-derive it — or break it — by taste.
