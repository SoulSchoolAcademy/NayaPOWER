# One Screen, One Idea

## IN A NUTSHELL

A page is a sequence of complete screens, and each screen carries exactly one idea. The viewport is the unit: everything above the fold, whole, or it is not a viewpoint. Inside that discipline the order is an argument, and the argument was settled piece by piece under his eye: the pains open — they name the problem the chart then answers; prices appear once, in the chart, because the reader decides at the bottom, they don't shop there; doors stand at every decision point; duplicated sections are deleted, because a repeat costs a scroll for nothing; earnings detail moves one tap away into a popup; and when two lists both claimed to be the ten, they were merged — one ultimate ten, each item a pain and its solution in one body, because two tens make neither of them the ten. The headliner obeys the family it heads: same size block, same treatment, aligned — bigger is not more important, bigger is off-family. The formatting pattern is the letter's grammar everywhere: three inner blocks per item (why it happens, the solution, what's in it for you), a jewel tile opening each block, orb bullets instead of walls of text, her face doubled and anchoring the item. The chart underneath it all is the best-in-world standard: 36 rows, one world, the ladder as law, enormous value at every level and Max always the most attractive.

## HUMAN

Think of a great shop window on a street you walk every day. One window, one idea — the winter coats, arranged so you understand the whole offer in the three seconds you pass it. The prices are on the coats, once, where you decide. The door is right there when the idea lands. Nobody tapes a second, smaller window beside it repeating the first, and the sign above is the same lettering as the shop's name, not a stranger's billboard. You have never once thought about any of this — which is exactly how you know it was designed.

## CHILD

Imagine telling your friend about your birthday party, but you tell them everything at once — the cake, the games, the presents, the clown, all in one giant breath — and their eyes glaze over. Now tell it one picture at a time: first the cake. Just the cake, until they can see it. Then the games. Each picture complete before the next starts. And don't tell the cake part twice — they'd stop listening. One picture at a time, each one finished, none repeated: that's how you hold anybody's attention, even a grown-up's.

## GRANDMA

It is how a good sermon or a good letter has always worked: one point, made fully, then the next. The preachers who lost the congregation were never short of material — they had three sermons braided into one and a text you had to squint at. Say the thing once, say it completely, in the order a listener's mind actually walks, and sit down before you start repeating yourself. Brevity is not the goal; completeness without repetition is.

## NAYA

Compose in viewpoints: one idea per screen, complete above the fold; a taller-than-one-screen entrance is a defect, not a style. Order is argument: pains open, chart answers, letter closes (per The Letter Is the Close); prices print once (in the chart), doors stand at every decision point. Delete duplicates — a section living higher up is a repeat, and a repeat is a scroll charged for nothing. Detail goes one tap away (popups), never inline as a wall. One list may claim to be the ten; merge rivals into pain+solution items. Headliners are same-family, same-size, aligned — importance comes from treatment, not scale. Format every item in the three-block grammar (why / solution / what's in it for you) with a jewel tile and orb bullets.

## MACHINE

1) Viewport rule: each screen = one idea, complete above the fold at phone width first. 2) Order template: pains → chart → body → letter → bottom door; deviations require his ruling. 3) Prices render exactly once per page (the chart); bottom blocks carry tier names + the Start door, no re-printed prices. 4) Duplicate-section check: any content block appearing twice is removed in the same pass. 5) Ten-claim rule: at most one list per page may present as the definitive ten; each item = pain + solution + what's-in-it-for-you in three inner blocks with jewel tile + orb bullets. 6) Headliner parity: header block dimensions and family treatment equal to its items; alignment measured, not eyeballed.
