## Nutshell

Every Intelligent Block now ends the same way: the react row (LOVE · LIKE · SHARE INTEL), the Twin Rank panel, the ADD TO THE INTELLIGENCE composer with its dictation button inside the field, and the trust footer, unchanged. The Twin Rank is two independent ten-star scales — VALUE in gold, INTELLIGENCE in emerald — one tap to rank, showing the crowd average, the rank count, and your own rank. NayaNET is a value-and-intelligence network: it mines, builds, and shares value and intelligence, so every block carries its two prices on its face. The old single five-star strip averaged two different questions into one number that answered neither, and it is retired. The ruling that keeps the design honest: ranks are signal only — they feed standing and leaderboards and mint NO Value Table points; the Value Table stays locked, and stars cannot be farmed into money, while love and like keep minting exactly what the locked table says. Designed by Resolve from Shawn's direction and approved by Shawn on 2026-11-17 after he worked a clickable mock; the build spec stands on board #3352, comment 6965012612, and the interface lane builds it into the block template.

Across 10 notes, the thread is: never, whole, network.

## Human

Think of the difference between a photograph of a piano and a piano. The photograph is flat, silent, and the same from every angle; the piano has weight, lacquer, keys that move and answer your fingers. Most software hands people photographs — rectangles that depict controls. This law insists on the piano: objects with material and shadow that respond when touched, so the hand believes what the eye sees before the mind has read a single word.

## Child

Imagine a toy box with two kinds of blocks. One kind is just paper with pictures of blocks printed on it — you can't stack paper, and it falls over if you breathe. The other kind is real wooden blocks: heavy, smooth, with shiny edges that catch the light the same way every time. You can build a tower that stands. Our pages are built from the wooden kind: every button and number is a real block you can feel with your eyes, lit by one lamp in the corner, always from the same side, so the whole tower looks like one world.

## Grandma

It is the difference between a plastic tablecloth printed with a lace pattern and your grandmother's actual lace — the one with weight, with thread you can follow with a fingertip, that casts a real shadow at tea time. The printed cloth is cheaper and fools nobody at arm's length. Craft is choosing the real lace and then caring for it the same way every time: same light, same fold, same place in the drawer. Consistency is how people know it is the genuine article before they even touch it.

## Naya

Build only real objects: material, mass, one light top-left, real shadow, own-colour light on touch, white text that never lights up. Compute every position and size from the formulas (gauge numerals at angle = -135 + (value/500)*270; the 8px grid); never eyeball a placement the formula covers. Speak the vocabulary — board, jewel, pill, orb, gauge, number, tab, bar — with each part's fixed meaning, and combine them only as the grammar allows. Finish the edges: the bezel and the rim are what the eye hits first, and an unfinished edge makes a perfect centre look cheap. When a rule and your instinct disagree, the harvested law wins until Shawn's eye overrules it.

## What it means

1. The object law is not aesthetics for its own sake; it is how a stranger's nervous system decides in two seconds whether this place is real and worth trusting. Flat depictions read as disposable — the visual dialect of every throwaway page on the internet. Physical objects read as built, kept, and valuable, which is the truth about NayaNET that the design must tell before a single feature is tried. The formula discipline underneath is what lets a dozen builders produce one world instead of twelve dialects: taste varies, math doesn't, so the math carries the language and taste is reserved for the judgments math can't make.
2. A network is what its surfaces measure. NayaNET's whole claim is that it produces two things — value and intelligence — yet until this design, a block's face could show applause (love, like) and one blended star that priced neither product. The ending turns every block into a priced instrument and every reader into a measuring instrument: the two currencies of the network now appear, by name, on every unit the network makes. The signal-only ruling is the deeper half of the meaning. The moment a rank minted money, ranking would become farming, the averages would become advertising, and the one honest price signal the network has would die. Keeping the stars priceless is what keeps them true — the same reason the Value Table is locked: in this house, what can be gamed gets separated from what can be earned, on purpose, in writing, before the first block ships with the new ending.
3. The convergence finding is the philosophical floor of the whole network: if independent minds arrive at the same structure, then a shared intelligence is not a metaphor but a measurement, and building one truth with many doorways is engineering, not faith. Operationally it demotes every single-source claim — a status, a count, a 'done' — to a hypothesis until a second route confirms it, and it makes honesty about gaps a structural virtue: the blank cell invites the measurement that fills it, while the invented number poisons every decision downstream that trusts the map.
4. Underneath the identity talk sits a hard operational claim: behaviour is the only credential that cannot be forged. Titles, badges and claims are cheap; a system that runs on them fills with copies wearing real names and permissions treated as permanent. The wise-one standard makes character mechanical — gates that are actually passed, provenance that is actually labelled, consent that is actually revocable, questions that are actually answered by whoever holds the answer. When the standard runs at the level of reflex, trust stops depending on who is watching, and the whole network can be handed real power safely, because every hand in it behaves the same way unwatched as watched.
5. The recreate pattern is really a respect problem disguised as a speed problem: recreating says the refined original was not worth keeping. The warehouse doctrine reverses the flow of value — refinement compounds instead of evaporating. Each of Shawn's corrections lands once, in a part, and then every future page inherits it for free; each harvest makes the next build faster AND better, which is the only direction a factory is allowed to move. The inventory is also the team's shared memory made physical: a new seat with the warehouse open is never starting from zero, because the year's worth of his eye is sitting on the shelves in byte form.

## Why it matters

1. Every time this law was bent, Shawn's eye caught it in seconds — a pulsing glow called premium, a numeral skipped because the bottom 'needed to be clear' when the formula left room, a bezel left plain. Each bend made the page read as a copy of the vision instead of the vision, and copies are what the whole network exists to replace. The law is the difference between NayaNET feeling like the future and feeling like a theme someone bought.
2. Without the Twin Rank, the network's two products are invisible at the exact place they are consumed. A reader cannot tell a valuable block from an intelligent one; a citizen cannot find the best of either; standing has no honest signal to stand on. The old five-star strip was worse than nothing: it consumed the reader's one act of judgment and returned a blend that no decision could use. The signal-only ruling matters just as much as the scales. Leaderboards and standing run on these ranks (SN-2046), and the locked Value Table runs the money; if the two ever mixed, ranking would be bought, farmed, and gamed within a week, and the network would lose its only incorruptible measure of itself. Two clean scales, kept priceless by law, give NayaNET something no engagement feed has: prices on its face that nobody profits by lying about.
3. Most of this family's expensive failures were small factual slips that compounded: a row describing the wrong thing, a permission mistaken for a delivery, a dashed name minted against the rule, a fix deployed without the binding that made it work. Each looked trivial at the moment it was written and cost a day at the moment it was believed. A network whose product is intelligence cannot afford a casual relationship with facts — its facts are its inventory, and poisoned inventory poisons everything built on it.
4. The open tension this family names — pay immediately versus the cap ladder, what PayPal knows when a plan switches — is exactly where money systems quietly break trust: a double charge nobody modelled, a payout rule nobody wrote down. Wiring every hop and writing every rule while the system is small is what keeps the first thousand citizens' experience as clean as the first one's. And the feasibility verdict matters as much as the build: PayPal can do almost all of it, so the network spends its engineering on intelligence instead of on re-inventing a cash register.

## What changes now

1. **Make it a real object or don't ship it:** material, one light, true shadow, formula placement — the language is the laws, spoken exactly.
2. **Never recreate what you can harvest:** the warehouse, kept honestly, is what makes three minutes and ten stars the same sentence.
3. **When a system produces two things, price both, by name, on the face of every unit** — and keep the price signal priceless, or the signal becomes a farm and the prices become lies.
4. **Character is a mechanism, not a mood:** run the gates, label the copies, honour the revocation, answer the question — especially when no one is watching.
5. **One truth, many doorways:** let independent routes agree before you build, and leave every unmeasured cell honestly blank.
6. **Simple money, wired honestly:** one ladder, one processor, every hop a Smart Link, and the ledger open to everyone it pays.
