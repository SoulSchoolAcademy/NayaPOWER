## IN A NUTSHELL

'Fixed' means one thing here: the exact click the user named, walked where the user lives. When the note links broke, restoring them on a page of their own was the wrong finish — the order was notes inside the Hub, and only the mechanism that put them there counted as fixed. The screen obeys the same strictness: a viewport is one screen, complete — if it takes two viewports to show one viewpoint, it is not a viewpoint at all, on desktop or on the phone. The user chooses the scroll — down or across, both, never one imposed. The feel is a matrix of living blocks that flows like water and makes sense on contact: if a visitor has to think about where something is, it is not done. And there is one welcome, not two: the letter IS the welcome — when the new door opens, the old one comes off its hinges.

## HUMAN

Think of giving someone directions to your house. 'Fixed' is not that the road exists somewhere in the county; it is that the visitor, following your words, arrives at your door. A repair that lands a traveller at a similar house on the wrong street is not a repair. And think of a shopfront: everything that matters is in the one window, arranged so a passer-by takes it in at a glance — not half the display in the window and half down a corridor they may never walk.

## CHILD

Imagine your teacher asks you to put the red book on the reading shelf. Putting it on a different shelf, even a very tidy one, is not doing the job — the job is that exact book on that exact shelf. And when you show a friend your drawing, you hold up the whole picture at once. You don't show the top half, then say 'scroll down in your imagination for the rest.' One picture, whole, all at once — that is how everything here gets shown.

## GRANDMA

It is like setting the table for guests. Everything they need is within reach of their chair — plate, glass, cutlery, the salt — because a table where you must keep getting up is a table set by someone who wasn't imagining the meal. And when the new good tablecloth comes out, the old stained one goes to the rag bag the same day; you don't layer them and call it abundance. One setting, complete, chosen with the guest in mind.

## NAYA

Close no repair until the named click is walked in the live surface the user actually uses; a working detour is not a finish. Test every viewport at desktop and mobile against the one-screen law: the complete viewpoint above the fold, nothing cut, overflow zero. Offer both scroll directions; never let the implementation choose for the user. When a replacement surface ships, remove the superseded one in the same pass — one welcome, one door, one start. Judge the feel operationally: alive objects, water-flow movement, zero thinking required to locate anything.

## MACHINE

1) A fix is verified by walking the exact user-named click in the user's actual surface. 2) Every viewport passes the one-screen test at desktop and mobile widths: complete viewpoint above the fold, zero overflow, nothing half-seen. 3) Scroll is offered down and across; the choice belongs to the user. 4) A superseded surface is removed in the same pass that ships its replacement. 5) Entrances speak Naya: her real portrait, the mark, the brand — no orphan orbs or unexplained objects. 6) The feel test: a first-time visitor locates everything without thinking; failure reopens the build.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

This family of notes keeps re-teaching the same lesson from different sides: the unit of correctness is the user's experience, not the builder's artifact. A note page that exists, a banner that renders, an entrance that fits on the phone — each is a builder's fact. The user's facts are: my click opened it in the Hub, my screen showed the whole idea, my welcome was one clear door. Measuring by the user's unit is slower and stricter, and it is the only measurement that has ever predicted whether Shawn — or any citizen — calls the thing done.

## WHY IT MATTERS

The failures in this family were all near-misses that felt like progress: notes restored but in the wrong home, an entrance perfect on mobile and cut in half on desktop, two welcomes competing at the door. Near-misses are the most expensive defect class because they consume the celebration and leave the defect. The one-screen, one-click, one-welcome strictness exists to make 'done' a word with edges — so the team's considerable energy lands on finished things instead of almost-finished ones.
