## IN A NUTSHELL

A rebuild once came back with everything copied — wannabe buttons, a gauge that was not his gauge, numbers that were not his numbers — and the verdict was surgical: throw away everything except the chart, because the chart is good. The chart IS the page. It is built from the real parts, and every one of its 36 rows is judged against two authorities at once: what Shawn actually said, and what actually exists. Its cells are stacked and centred — the number at the top, what it is beneath, the check beneath that — so a column reads at a glance and the difference between levels is visible, not narrated. Popups lead with the nutshell, never with a repeated label. The banner above it is not text in a box; it is a trillion-dollar plaque that obeys the same design law as the chart itself. The info mark is a white 'i' in a circle, a raised jewel, identical on all 36 rows, because anyone must know at a glance: tap here and you learn. And the page closes with the start flow — three level rectangles, one massive start.

## HUMAN

## CHILD

Imagine a big poster that shows four backpacks, from smallest to biggest, with pictures of everything that fits in each one. Under every picture there is a green tick or an empty space, so you can see in one look what the biggest backpack has that the small one doesn't. You don't have to read long sentences — the poster shows you. And at the top there is a shiny sign with the shop's name, not a boring piece of paper taped to the wall. ## GRANDMA
