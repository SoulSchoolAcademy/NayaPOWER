## The teaching, in his words (substance, as recorded)

Switching to next month does not change the month — it stays the same month, and he cannot tell what month he is on; the day may not change either. The month name must change clearly on tap.

## The understanding

A calendar that does not change month when you tap next month is not a calendar — it is a picture of a month. The citizen must be able to tell what month they are on, from the page itself, at a glance, after every tap. This is the same broken date logic family as the September body (SN-3521) and the day-view date block mixing three days (filed as its own pair in this same run): one date, the right date, everywhere — and here the failure is in the switching itself, not only in the display. The month name must change clearly on tap, and the day must change with it. Until it does, the month navigation is decoration, and decoration fails the usefulness law. Rides the calendar follow-on round, not the frame round.

## The law

When you tap next month, the month must change — clearly, and the day with it. The citizen must always be able to tell what month they are on.

## Owed from this dictation

The calendar follow-on round fixes month switching: the month name changes clearly on tap, the day changes with it. No fix claimed until the eye proves it live.

**Tap next month, see next month — or the calendar is a picture, not a calendar.**
