## HUMAN

Think of describing a house to someone who will buy it from your words alone. You do not list the lumber. You walk them through the door: here is where the morning light comes in, here is the room your kids will do homework in, here is the lock on the gate and who holds the keys, and here — pace it off yourself — is exactly how big the garden is. If any measurement you give them turns out wrong, the whole house becomes suspect. Vision is not the opposite of precision; vision is precision about the things that matter, in the order a person lives them.

## GRANDMA

When someone comes to your door with a grand story, you learn to ask three small questions: what is it, exactly — in plain words? What does it cost me, exactly? And who else is it for — people like me, or people in a brochure? The stories that survive those questions at a kitchen table are the ones worth repeating to the neighbours. This picture was built to survive the kitchen table: plain names for its parts, its money shown in figures you can check, and a named first customer it serves before it serves the world.
