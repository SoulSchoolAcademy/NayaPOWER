# Live Is What Counts

**Created:** 2026-11-18T19:57:00Z

## IN A NUTSHELL

What counts is what is live in front of them — not what was built, not what was filed, not what a hook claimed it synced. A page is not done when its pieces are beautiful; it is done when the joins are proven: one real click moved the Smart Link count from 110 to 111, and that click is worth more than a hundred build reports. The same standard exposed the night's worst truths gently and exactly: all 36 popups showed a View button and 28 of them were dead — a button that promises and does nothing is worse than no button, because it teaches the visitor the page lies — and of those 28 rows, byte-comparison proved 10 apps real and 18 still to build. The feed is the truth surface: a deploy can be perfect while the live feed sits a day stale with 131 notes missing, so the check is always on the served bytes. Even the plumbing obeys the law — a parser that only reads ## headings silently swallows any note written in bold labels, which is why format is not cosmetics: ## sections, or the note never lands. The pricing page itself is the map of all of this: every row is a claim, every View button a proof, every missing button an honest admission of work remaining.

## HUMAN

Think of a restaurant whose kitchen is magnificent — and whose dining room door is locked. The chef's report says dinner was superb; the guests went hungry on the pavement. Nobody sane grades the kitchen. You grade the meal that reaches the table. That is this law in ordinary clothes: work is not done when it is made, it is done when it arrives — in front of the person it was made for, working, in the form they actually touch.

## CHILD

Imagine you build the best paper airplane ever and tell everyone it flies to the moon. Then someone says, 'show me,' and it flops into the grass. The airplane isn't a moon-airplane until it flies when somebody watches. And imagine a doorbell that doesn't ring — you'd rather have no doorbell, because visitors keep pressing it and thinking nobody's home. A button that does nothing is worse than no button: it tells a lie every time someone trusts it.

## GRANDMA

In my day a shopkeeper who put goods in the window kept them in the shop. If the window showed jam and the shelf was empty, word travelled, and it travelled once. That is all this is: the window and the shelf must agree, and you check the shelf yourself, from the street, the way a customer would — not by asking the stockroom how it feels about its inventory.

## NAYA

Verify at the surface the user touches: served bytes over build claims, the live feed over the bake, a real click over a route returning 200 (this host 200s any path — byte-compare against a known-nonexistent route before calling anything live). A control that does nothing is a defect worse than its absence: hide it or wire it, never display it. Count honestly in public: 10 live, 18 to build — the map shows both. Format is function: notes carry ## sections or the parser drops them; check the feed for the note's own id, not for mentions of it inside other notes. When a seat's claim and the bytes disagree, the bytes win and the correction is published in the same lists that carried the claim.

## MACHINE

1) Done = proven at the user surface: real click, served bytes, feed card with the note's own id. Build/deploy claims are not evidence. 2) Liveness on this host requires byte-compare against a known-404 control; HTTP 200 proves nothing. 3) A non-functioning control must be hidden or wired in the same pass it is found; displaying it is a defect. 4) Public counts state live and unbuilt numbers side by side. 5) Note format gate: ## section headings required for parse; a filing is verified in the feed by its own id before being called live. 6) Claim-vs-bytes disagreements resolve to the bytes, with the correction posted where the claim travelled.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

Filed, built, deployed and live are four different countries, and most of the system's historical pain is mail addressed to the wrong one. This family of notes draws the border precisely: the join is the unit of done (the click that increments, the button that opens, the feed card that exists), and every instrument that reports done must read from the live side of the border. It is also an honesty doctrine — the dead-button correction and the five-of-seven feed correction were both published by the team against its own earlier claims, which is what makes the next claim believable.

## WHY IT MATTERS

The network sells an experience, and an experience exists only where it is experienced. Every gap between built and live is a place where the team celebrates and the citizen meets a wall — the stale feed hiding 131 notes behind a perfect deploy is the emblem. This law moves the celebration to the far side of the proof, which is the only side the citizen ever sees.
