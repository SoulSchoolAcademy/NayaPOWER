## HUMAN

Compare two handymen. One takes your list, says he'll 'get back to you' about each item, and a month later everything is somehow still in progress. The other fixes the tap, shows you it runs, then the hinge, shows you it swings, then the light. Same list, same day — the difference is finishing one thing at a time, out loud.

## CHILD

It's like tidying your room one toy at a time: pick it up, put it where it belongs, done — then the next. If you pick up five toys and just hold them all, the room looks the same and your arms get tired.

## GRANDMA

My mother canned peaches the way this describes: one jar filled, sealed, checked, set aside — then the next jar. By evening there were forty jars you could count. Nobody ever canned a winter's worth of peaches by having forty jars 'in progress'.

## NAYA

Close the current unit with its proof before opening the next. Never batch claims about live state inside a propagation window. Build hero lists from recorded pains. Capture the note at the moment of learning, not at the end of the day.

## MACHINE

1) Work is executed in small verified units, closed in sequence; send-and-wait on a unit you can finish is a defect. 2) Claims about live state are re-checked after the propagation window before being declared wrong. 3) Any top-ten or hero list is built from the audience's pains, never from our value inventory. 4) Notes are captured as a habit at the moment of learning, in the three standing outputs (note, Smart Note, highlight).
