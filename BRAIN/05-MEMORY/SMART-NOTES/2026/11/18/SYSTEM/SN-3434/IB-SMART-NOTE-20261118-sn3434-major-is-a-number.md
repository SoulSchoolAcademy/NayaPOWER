# Major Is a Number

SN-3434 · SYSTEM / DISTILLATION

**Created:** 2026-11-18T20:35:00Z

## IN A NUTSHELL

The night judgment became arithmetic and privacy became architecture. 'Major' stopped being a feeling: five of his questions — major effect on development? major feature? should all know? learn ongoing? major behavior lesson? — each rated 0, 1, or 2, added, minus one, on a scale from −9 to +9, and the number routes the note: +7 and up mails everybody now and headlines the report; the middle rides the digest; the bottom is a record, or not captured at all. In the same hours the permission model drew its line: mail and instant messages are never read — no toggle, no exception; Naya Notes are the citizen's choice, adjustable by person, group, and page; identity is the citizen's choice — anonymous, alias, or name. And the carrier of both is the family: the system is built and birthed together, by people looking out for each other — while the engine audit scored itself 6 out of 10 for six unlocked tables, one of them holding the seat keys, and filed it without flinching.

## HUMAN

Two everyday versions. A good editor doesn't publish by gut feeling; she has a sense of what belongs on the front page — here that sense is written as a scorecard, so any editor on any day lands within a point of any other. And a good post office doesn't promise not to read your letters; the envelopes are sealed. The promise is in the paper, not in the clerk's mood.

## CHILD

Some news is so big you run and tell everyone right away. Some news can wait for the family dinner. And some isn't news at all. This system has a score that decides which is which — so nobody has to guess. And nobody ever opens anybody else's letters. Ever. That's not a rule you can switch off; the letters just can't be opened.

## GRANDMA

We had a party line when I was young — anyone could listen in, and everyone knew it, so nobody said anything true on it. A private line changed what people were willing to say. Privacy isn't a courtesy; it's what makes honesty possible. Build the private line, don't just promise one.

## NAYA

Score before routing, and record the score with the note. Enforce the permission model in the gate matrix, not in prose. Never propose a toggle for the never-read rule. When a save is claimed, prove it across a reload in a real browser. Carry audit scores unflinching — 6/10 filed beats 9/10 claimed.

## MACHINE

1) Every piece of intelligence is rubric-scored before routing; the score picks the channel — mail now, digest, note, record, or not captured. 2) Mail and messages: never read, architecturally. Naya Notes: owner-controlled access. Identity: owner's choice. No toggle exists for the first rule. 3) No bundle cap: mail is chosen at the moment of writing, because a queue makes the important thing wait behind the unimportant. 4) Activation precedes action: tune in from the master box, then define what, why, how, and what matters — before moving. 5) A save persists by on-device mirror plus backend row, last-write-wins; a save that dies on reload was never a save.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

A rubric and a permission model look like different artifacts; here they are the same move — replacing vibes with mechanisms at exactly the points where vibes fail. Feelings about importance produce both spam and silence; a score produces a routing. Promises about privacy produce policy pages; an architecture produces a fact. The family note is not decoration between them: a team that scores itself 6/10 in writing and corrects its own privacy framing within the hour (never-read → adjustable, on his correction) is demonstrating the behavior the mechanisms encode.

## WHY IT MATTERS

Citizens will trust this network with their working lives for exactly two reasons: it tells them what matters without drowning them, and it cannot read what it promised not to read. This essence binds both reasons into mechanisms that hold on a tired day, under a new seat, without anyone remembering to be good.
