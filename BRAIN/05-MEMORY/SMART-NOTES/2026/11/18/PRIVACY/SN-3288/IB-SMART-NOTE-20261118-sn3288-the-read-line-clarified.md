## Human

The old line was being heard too broadly, and a too-broad line breaks the work it is meant to protect. "Never read it" heard absolutely would blind the system to the very intelligence it exists to operate: it could not read the smart-note intelligence, could not smart-note it, could not build a page from his vision, goal, mission — because all of that is reading. The clarification draws the line where it actually lives, in three tiers, and the tiers are the whole teaching. Never is absolute and narrow: mail, messages, video, calls — the private things, no exception, no toggle, ever. Public is ours by definition: smart notes on the feed, anything public — that is the shared intelligence, and reading it is the job. Permission is the middle that makes the system work: the notes we make to understand, the smart-note intelligence, his inputs for a page — read with his permission, because that is what permission is for. And the precision that holds the line together: carrying a message so it reaches the person is not reading it. The machine can move the mail without ever looking at what was written. That is how the vault stays operate-by-permission and never read, while the intelligence still flows.

## Child

Imagine three boxes of letters. The first box is private — you never, ever open it. The second box is on the town notice board for everyone — you can read those, that's what they're for. And carrying a letter to a friend isn't the same as opening it and reading it.
