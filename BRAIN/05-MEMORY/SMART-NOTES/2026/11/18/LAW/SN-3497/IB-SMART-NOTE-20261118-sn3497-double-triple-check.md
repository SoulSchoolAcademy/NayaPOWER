## The teaching, in his words (verbatim)

I look at it first and figure out what's wrong, fix it, then send it to him to check, then I check it AGAIN after his check — so it always gets double checked, triple checked. Never straight from a round to his eyes without my eye first, and never finished after his check without my eye again.

## The understanding

Three checks, in order, no shortcuts. First the captain's eye — she looks, figures out what's wrong, fixes it — because a round's own verdict is never the last word on its own work. Then his eye — he checks what she sent, because his eye is ground truth and nothing is landed until he sees it. Then her eye AGAIN, after his check — because his check finds things, and a fix made after his check is a new version that has never been looked at. A round that goes straight to his eyes skips the first check and wastes his attention on what she would have caught; a round that is called finished after his check skips the last check and ships an unverified fix. Double checked, triple checked — the sequence is the process, and the process is standing from now on. It governs every round, every page, every time.

## The law

The standing check process, from now on: the captain looks at it first and figures out what's wrong, fixes it, then sends it to him to check, then checks it AGAIN after his check — double checked, triple checked. Never straight from a round to his eyes without her eye first, and never finished after his check without her eye again.

## Owed from this dictation

Every round carries the three checks in order: captain's eye → his eye → captain's eye again. A handoff to him without the captain's first eye is incomplete; a round closed after his check without her eye again is unfinished. The check process is standing — it does not need re-ordering per round.

**My eye first, his eye, then my eye again — double checked, triple checked, every time.**
