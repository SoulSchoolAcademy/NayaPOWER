## The understanding

Two checks, both necessary, neither a substitute for the other. The captain's browser check sees the page from the user's perspective — she walks it, measures it, catches what a round missed about its own work. His direct click gives him the full view — he opens it himself, on his own screen, and gives full feedback, because a link sent is how he checks. So the order has two halves, and both bind: never ask him to check a page without its link — a check without a link leaves him with nothing to open; and never send a link for a page my eye hasn't passed yet — a link without her eye first sends him unverified work. The ~22:45Z ask is the same rule in its first form: he asked for the DIRECT link when a page is being shown on the captain's screen, so he can open the full view himself and give full feedback. The ~22:50Z order makes it standing, for every page, every time. It stands beside the double/triple check (SN-3497): her eye first, his eye, then her eye again — and his eye always arrives with the direct link in hand.

## The law

Both checks, every page: the direct link goes with every page check, and my eye passes the page before any link goes to him. Never a check without its link; never a link my eye hasn't passed.

Every handoff to him carries the direct link to the exact staged version, named by commit or blob, and only after the captain's eye has passed those bytes. A screenshot of the captain's screen is never the check on its own — the link goes with it, so he can open the full view himself.

**My eye first, then his click — both checks, every page, every time.**
