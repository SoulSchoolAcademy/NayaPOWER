## The broken cycle, named

Edits in the morning. Half the day giving edits. Waiting all day, a few pages seen. Sleep. Wake to mostly-done-but-not-right, with mess-ups. Edits all day again. Sleep. Nothing completed in an efficient, effective timeframe. That cycle is over — he named it, and naming it is what ends it.

## The fix — the law from now

1. **LIVE TIME.** He tells me where things sit, lane by lane, whenever he asks — pricing page, the unfolds (Smart Flow), the Hub, the Personal smart app, the calendar system and the inner calendar. The whole team works in parallel: one on one thing, another on another, a team on the bigger parts — and actually COMPLETES it.
2. **NON-STOP SEQUENCE.** Updated pages come back continuously: fix this, fix this, next one, fix this, fix this, next one. No sending it out and then somehow nothing comes back.
3. **ONE HOUR MAX.** An edit round runs 20 minutes, sometimes 5 or 10, an hour at the most. No round runs more than an hour of doing edits.
4. **ONLY VERIFIED WORK REACHES HIM.** I check every page myself before it goes to him — the captain looks first (15:15Z). If it is not aligned and not good, it goes back, never to him. Sending him unverified work is the first problem.
5. **MANAGE IT PROPERLY.** Be on top of what is ready to be viewed. Send it, next one, next one. In between, check alignment — that is the work between sends.

## Why it matters

This is the Continuous Cycle Law with a number on it. The law already said: no finished work sits more than fifteen minutes without its next step; an interface in front of him every hour or something is wrong; done means in front of his eyes. The live sequence is that law lived in real time: the hour is now a ceiling on the round itself, not just on the wait between rounds. A round that runs past an hour without a page in front of him has already broken the sequence — the fix is not to work faster in the dark, it is to send the page, get the edit, and move to the next one.

The diagnosis underneath is management, not speed: "you're just not sending it back to me, you're not checking on the work, you're not finishing it, you're not managing things properly." The machine's job is the sending, the checking, and the finishing — in that order, non-stop.
