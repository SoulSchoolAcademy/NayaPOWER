## The understanding

When a page is ready to be judged, he wants two walks at once. The architect walks what the page DOES: the functions present, and the functions it should have but doesn't. The scorecarder walks what the page IS: what looks off, what he's missing, whether this is all it can be, whether we could do better, how aligned it is with the design. Six questions, one walk — functions, gaps, what's off, what's missing, is this all it can be, how aligned. A page shown as done without this walk is a page judged on its own claims; the walk is the check that happens before his eye, not after it. The captain's walk of the calendar (functions: month → tap a date → 13-button day menu → day view; off/missing: flat buttons vs the N standard, icon/logo reversed with glow + visible square, date repeating the 18 and feeling flat, Smart Meet flat, Wednesday "i" purple not Kanye blue, day numbers huge, stats needing a jewel icon, month view buttons + chip below the 17px floor, dots 3/4/5 sharing one jewel + colour, Nov 20 counts disagreeing between month and day, phone widths never measured) is the first walk in this shape — the walk itself is now the standing method.
