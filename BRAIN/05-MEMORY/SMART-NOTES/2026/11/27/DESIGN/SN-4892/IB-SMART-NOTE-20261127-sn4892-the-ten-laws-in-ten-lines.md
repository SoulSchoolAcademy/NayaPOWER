## Nutshell

The cold-Naya card: everything is an object, even the text. White text, or jewel-bright — headlines 25, sub-headlines 19, small text 17. One deep color per object, as rim and glow. Alive on hover, whole. One light, top-left. Numbers are digit tiles. Three sentences, one idea, then a button. Harvest, never invent. Don't touch what he didn't ask to touch. Prove it, then say it.

## Child

Imagine ten short rules on one card, so a brand-new helper can read them and get it right the first time: make everything real, keep words white and bright, give each thing one deep color, make it come alive when touched, and always prove it before you say it is done.

## What It Means

Design Laws section 0.5 — the ten laws in ten lines, the cold-Naya card. A fresh seat who has never seen NayaNET reads these ten lines and gets it; the rest of the book is the proof behind them. 1. Everything is an object — even the text. 2. White text, or jewel-bright; 25/19/17; Marcellus, layered, white. 3. One deep color per object, as rim + glow; never two same colors together. 4. Alive on hover, whole. 5. One light, top-left, always; gradients 135 degrees, shadows down-right; inside is board black. 6. Numbers are digit tiles, never plain text. 7. Three sentences, one idea, then a button. 8. Harvest, never invent. 9. Don't touch what he didn't ask to touch. 10. Prove it, then say it — his eye is the final gate. Why it matters: the book is long; the card is what a seat actually reads before acting. If the card is not read, the book was never read.
