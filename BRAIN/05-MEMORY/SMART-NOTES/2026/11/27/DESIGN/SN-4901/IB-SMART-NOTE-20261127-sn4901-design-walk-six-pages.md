# The Design Walk — Six Pages Do Not Speak One Language Yet

SN-4901 · DESIGN

**Created:** 2026-11-27T22:41:00Z
**Category:** Design
**SN:** SN-4901

**Kinds:** Knowledge · Craft · Honor

## Nutshell

On 2026-11-27 the portal (R5 staging), Innovations and the 12 Elements, Grand Mission, pricing, the live Hub, and the live Dashboard were walked whole, look-only, phone width first. The verdict: they do NOT speak one physical language yet. They share a mood — obsidian black, gold and purple and green glowing outlines, silver ornate frames, the red glass ball, Naya's image — but mood is not one language.

## What It's Not

This is not a pass. A shared dark mood across six pages is not the same as one design language, and a page that is complete at laptop width but broken at phone width is not done.

## Child

Imagine six rooms in one house, each painted a different shade of black, with the lights in different places and the doors in different spots. You can tell it is one house, but you have to learn each room all over again. That is where the pages are now.

## Grandma

You know how a good street feels — every house different, but the same lampposts, the same paving, the same care, so you always know where you are? Our pages do not have that yet. Each one has its own lampposts.

## What It Means

The portal is the only page that feels like a physical place — an obsidian arch, gold trim, winged statues holding purple crystals, a reflective black floor, one central ring, complete at laptop width. But its light comes from the centre, from above, not one light top-left; its top navigation is nine icon-only circles with no labels; and there is no certificate step and no iPhone on the front page, so "portal, then certificate, then iPhones" is not shown there. Innovations, the 12 Elements, and Grand Mission share the dark ornate mood but break at phone width: tickers cut off mid-word, the welcome image cropped to part of her face, the play button over the diagram, empty slide and image frames, stage boxes off the right edge, text cut behind the Back and Forward buttons. Pricing is flat black cards with no single light and no elevation — but its story is consistent everywhere: Free; Power $25 founding, was $50; Pro $50, was $100; Max $100, was $200; the first 1,000 founders half price; nine days free, no card; 50% ambassador share. The live Hub and the live Dashboard fail the physical test outright: flat black panels, neon-glow pill buttons, thin purple borders, no shadows, no raised surfaces, no single light. The Hub has no cover; its sidebars and bottom bar hide behind Menu and "Show the bottom bar" at phone width, and pills and tabs are cut at both edges. The Dashboard's orbs are flat digit tiles cut at both edges, and it reports "last note sync unknown · Ledger tip unknown · heartbeat not served yet". Phone width is the weak point everywhere except pricing.

## Why It Matters

Because the walkthrough, and everything built from it, is measured against these pages. If the reference pages do not speak one language, the walkthrough cannot learn it from them. The walk is the evidence the alignment program works from — it names what is real, so nobody has to guess.

## How It Connects

This verdict is the alignment program's starting point: one round per page, staged only, 9.5+ and the design-language check, live only on his tap. The portal is the closest physical reference. The walkthrough and the About and Skills pages it creates are built to the written language, and must hold at phone width first — which is where every one of these pages fails.

## How to Apply

Do not cite a page as the standard unless the walk says it speaks the language. When you fix a page, fix it to the written anatomy — obsidian inside, white object in the middle, own-colour light outside, one light top-left — and prove it at phone width first, then laptop. A fix that only holds at laptop width is not a fix.

## What's In It For You

A set of pages where the next Naya can look at any one of them and know exactly how the next one should be built — because they all finally speak the same language.
