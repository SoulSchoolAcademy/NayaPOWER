# Nothing Flat, Nothing Dead — The Real Object Law

SN-4893 · DESIGN

**Created:** 2026-11-27T16:52:00Z
**Category:** Design
**SN:** SN-4893

## Nutshell

Every element is a real physical object. Not a flat shape with effects. Not a picture of an object. If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship. If it doesn't feel real, it is not done.

## Child

Imagine a toy crown made of real jewels versus a drawing of a crown. The drawing is flat — you cannot pick it up. Naya builds the real crown: it has weight, shine, and depth, and it moves when you touch it.

## Grandma

Dear, a thing should feel like you could reach out and hold it. If it looks like a picture of a thing, it is not finished. Real things have depth, shadow, and light — and they answer when you touch them.

## What It Means

The Real Object Law, Shawn 2026-11-01 — canonical, all of NayaNET, forever — and Design Laws section 1. Seven principles: everything is an object; one light source, top-left; real materials — obsidian glass, brushed metal, light with volume; real shadows, objects sit on the page; touch is a physical response; speak in objects — icons, circles, boards, buttons, minimal text; the viewport is the stage. MUST: every object alive — rest quiet but present, hover lifts and lights whole in its own color, pressed depresses (scale 0.97). MUST NOT: anything flat, any board or button that does nothing on hover, a shiny dot where a jewel belongs. Why it matters: 'pretty but fake' is a fail. He sees through it in one glance.

## How to Apply

How it connects: this is the first law because every other design law hangs on it — the text law, the color law, the object anatomy in section 4. Hover every object on the page: if nothing comes alive, it is not done.

## What's In It For You

You get pages that feel like real things in your hand — buttons that press, boards that lift, jewels that shine — instead of flat pictures you can only stare at.
