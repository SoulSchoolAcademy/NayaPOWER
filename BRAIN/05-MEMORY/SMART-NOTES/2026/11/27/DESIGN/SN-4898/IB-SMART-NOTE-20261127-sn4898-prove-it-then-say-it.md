# Prove It, Then Say It — His Eye Is the Final Gate

SN-4898 · DESIGN

**Created:** 2026-11-27T16:57:00Z
**Category:** Design
**SN:** SN-4898

## Nutshell

Receipt before report. His eye is the final gate — no checker pass, no count, no score overrides what his eyes see. A page that reads terrible is terrible, whatever the counts say. A checker PASS is a milestone inside the machine, never the verdict.

## Child

Imagine you say you cleaned your room, but nobody looked inside. Saying it is not the same as showing it. In Naya, you show the clean room first — a picture, a look with your own eyes — and only then do you say it is done.

## Grandma

Dear, show me before you tell me. A claim is only words until someone has seen it with their own eyes. His eyes are the ones that decide — not a score, not a checklist, not a machine saying pass.

## What It Means

Design Laws section 0.5 law 10 and section 11, and the Eleven Laws 1-2 (Shawn, 2026-11-21). If you break a law, you say so — a round that ships a known violation without naming it is a fail. Mechanical checks never prove the experience: read it as a reader, top to bottom, the way he reads it. The bytes said the stars were #8e24aa and #ffffff; his eye said pink. The checker proves the build contains the fix; only his eye proves the fix is visible. Live is the only surface his eye can trust — finished builds go live on the official URL. Why it matters: checker passes were reported as verdicts while his eye scored the pages 1 out of 10. That law was violated all night — never again.

## How to Apply

How it connects: prove-it is the Protocol's sixth step, and the Live Loop (section 15) is its home — a page is not done on staging; done means live on the official URL, on his tap, tested by him using it. Nothing reaches him until it has been verified, walked whole, phone first.

## What's In It For You

You only ever see work that someone has actually looked at, on the real page, the way you will see it — never a claim dressed up as a delivery.
