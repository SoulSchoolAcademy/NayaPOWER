# Privacy by Default — Private, Aliases, a Week Unless Saved

SN-4887 · VISION

**Created:** 2026-11-27T16:46:00Z
**Category:** Vision
**SN:** SN-4887

## Nutshell

NayaNET is a private communication system. We do not spy, do not read mail, do not read messages. Citizens use aliases. Conversations auto-delete by default after a week — a day or a month at the citizen's choice, never more than a month. A note the citizen saves stays locked in their Smart Vault, kept for good.

## Child

Imagine a diary with a lock that only you hold. Nobody peeks inside, nobody reads your letters, and old pages quietly fade away after a week — unless you choose to keep one forever, safe in your own box.

## Grandma

Dear, your words are yours. Nobody reads your mail, nobody listens to your messages, and nobody needs to know your real name. What you save, you keep; what you do not, gently goes — that is how a private house behaves.

## What It Means

The Privacy Law, Shawn 2026-11-04, sharpened 2026-11-05 and corrected 2026-11-14: deletion by default is a week, superseding a day by default; a citizen can choose a day or stretch it to a month, never longer than a month. Save to keep, 2026-11-15: unsaved smart notes distill to essence after a week; a note the citizen saves — pin on their feed, favorite, smart list — is kept for good, in their vault, never distilled away. Why it matters: people can only talk freely and connect with like minds when they know it is completely private and secure. Safety is what makes the matching and the sharing possible at all.

## How to Apply

How it connects: privacy is the gate Smart Match works behind — matches come through the privacy gate with reasons attached, and the system never reads mail or messages to make them. Any build that reads private words to function is not a build; it is a violation.

## What's In It For You

You can speak freely, under an alias, knowing your words are never read and never kept past your choice — and what you choose to keep stays yours, locked in your Vault, forever.
