# The Input Law — Dictation First, Confirm Always
SN-2054 · SYSTEM-INTELLIGENCE / INPUT
**Created:** 2026-11-04T20:30:00Z

## In A Nutshell
Every input in NayaNET is dictation first — voice, then typing, then paste from your own AI. Nothing enters unconfirmed: Naya plays your words back, beautifully formatted, and you confirm or edit before anything is saved or fired.

## The Law (Shawn's dictation, 2026-11-04)
**1. Dictation first, on ALL inputs, no exceptions.** The order of every input is: voice first, typing second, pasting from your own AI third. This is not a feature of some fields — it is the input layer of the whole system.

**2. The confirm loop.** Dictate → Naya plays it back, beautifully formatted → you confirm or edit → next. Nothing enters the system unconfirmed — not a note, not a message, not an onboarding field. The mission, the goal, the vision you speak while creating a page pass through the same loop.

**3. Commands are actions, confirmed before they fire.** A dictated command (make a note, send a mail, post this) is played back as the action it is — and only fires on confirmation.

**4. Three things your words can become.** Plain input that fills a page. A **Naya Note** — private, summarized into your Smart Box, pinnable and shareable by you alone. Or, only by your deliberate choice, a **Smart Note** — an Intelligent Block the whole network learns from, shared anonymously, with your own copy kept in your feed.

**5. Playback comes next; conversation does not.** Voice playback of everything is wanted as soon as possible, sequenced after dictation + confirmation. Live conversation mode is explicitly NOT the design.

## The Seam With The DNA
The design DNA says no voice buttons — that law governs display surfaces, where nothing may beg for attention. The Input Law puts the mic INSIDE the input itself. Different layers, no conflict: the surface stays silent; the input listens.

## How It Applies
The Welcome walk is the first build site: dictation-first with the confirm loop where citizens state their offers, needs and wants — the fields the matching engine reads. Every app inherits the layer; nobody designs their own input.
