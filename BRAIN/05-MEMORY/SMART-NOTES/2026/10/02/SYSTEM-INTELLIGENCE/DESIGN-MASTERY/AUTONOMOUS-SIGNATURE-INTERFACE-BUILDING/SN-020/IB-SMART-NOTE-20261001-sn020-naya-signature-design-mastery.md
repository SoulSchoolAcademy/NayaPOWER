## 🧭 KEY DECISIONS / PRINCIPLES

- Design is a first-class intelligence capability, not a final polish phase.
- Signature does not mean every page looks identical; signature means invariant judgment, craft and interaction principles produce family resemblance across different compositions.
- Every page must be individually designed around its purpose; shared tokens/primitives create coherence without template sameness.
- Automatic self-score is mandatory but never sufficient; independent challenge closes quality gates.
- Rendered observation is mandatory because source review cannot detect many visual and interaction failures.
- Every accepted page becomes a reference example and regression baseline.
- Every rejected or weak page becomes an anti-example with the reason it failed.
- Design learning must be scoped, evidenced, provenance-preserving and supersedable.
- The system should generate alternatives before committing when composition or experience architecture is underdetermined.
- Repair loops target the weakest weighted dimension first rather than adding arbitrary polish.
- The design system must distinguish invariants from optional patterns and project-specific expression.
- A complete application is the unit of success; exceptional isolated screenshots are insufficient.
- Design automation must never override truth, privacy, authority, accessibility, performance or governance.
- The Human Director's aesthetic direction is high-value evidence, but the system should translate it into reusable principles rather than requiring repeated restatement.
- The long-term objective is NayaNET that can continuously build and improve smart applications with extraordinary usefulness, beauty, coherence and experience quality.
