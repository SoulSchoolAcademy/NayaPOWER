## IN A NUTSHELL

The design canon is not a ranking and not a template. Its purpose is to make ignorance impossible: we study what the best in our niche are doing, extract the principle underneath it, and then do it ten times better — in every way. Where we are already next-level beyond the canon, we keep the lead and keep going. Imitation is failure; transcendence is the bar.

## HUMAN NOTE

Shawn's lesson, in his words: he used to look at what the leaders were doing and do what they were doing. Then he learned something better — look at what they're doing, learn from it, and do it ten times better. That is now our posture toward the entire canon. We don't ask "who's the best?" We ask "what did the best teach us?" — and then we build beyond them.

Four rules:

1. **Never ignorant.** Research what the leaders in your niche are doing — continuously, not once. Not knowing the state of the art is a choice, and it's the wrong one.
2. **Learn, don't imitate.** Extract the principle, never the screenshot. If your work looks like their work, you failed the lesson.
3. **10x is the bar.** Ten times better than the best observed — in clarity, in craft, in intelligence made visible, in every way that matters to the human.
4. **Keep the lead where we have it.** Where we're already next-level beyond what's being done, we don't regress to the canon's mean. The canon is the floor of awareness, not the ceiling of ambition.

## CHILD NOTE

Don't copy the best drawing in the class. Study why it's good — the colors, the shapes, the idea — and then draw something ten times better.

## GRANDMA NOTE

We learn from the finest work in the world so we never fall behind — and then we aim to surpass it, not copy it.

## NAYA NOTE (builder seats — imperative)

- Before designing any surface, name the best observed reference for it (from the canon/graph). If you can't name one, you haven't done the research — go do it.
- Then state, in one sentence, how your design goes ten times beyond it. "It looks like Linear" is not a sentence that passes.
- Matching the best known is competence, not excellence. It scores accordingly (see machine view).
