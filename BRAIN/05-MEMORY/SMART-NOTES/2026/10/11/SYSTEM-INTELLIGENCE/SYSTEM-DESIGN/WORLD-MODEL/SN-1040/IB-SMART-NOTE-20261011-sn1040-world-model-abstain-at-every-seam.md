## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Shawn's letter announced Layer 1 of the Ultimate Decision Machine: the world model — a living picture of every open PR, every lane's state, what's proven, what matters right now, and what the rules are — "always warm, always fresh," updating incrementally instead of re-reading everything. Its founding principle, in his words: *"if it can't get fresh data, it says 'I don't know' instead of guessing. That's the most important part. A machine that admits what it doesn't know is one you can trust."*

Within one minute, an independent source audit tested that principle against the implementation (`naya5/world-model@8e336940` — real GitHub code: `tools/world_model.py` ~24KB + `tests/test_world_model.py` ~18KB, 25/25 green builder-reported, 21/21 independently tested on the earlier head) and found four seams where the machine claims certainty instead of abstaining: (1) a stale cache still prints positive PR/lane answers with only a stderr warning — an API failure keeps cached OK; (2) the PR list's `mergeable` field is NOT PRESENT in the live GitHub list endpoint, so the UI's "ready to merge" query cannot actually qualify PRs; (3) a page-2 403 is silently partial but publishes `status: OK` with an exact count; (4) local briefing headings are presented as standing laws without establishing current LAW/grants. The audit's verdict: valuable as an ORIENTATION/SHADOW cache, not as an execution-authorizing source.

The repair prescription is one-owner, same-branch: F1–F4 fixed on `naya5/world-model`, fail-closed tests added (stale/403/partial/missing-law), re-rehearsed against fresh main, then distinct-seat independent verification — and the review surface was created as DRAFT PR #2422 from the EXISTING owner branch, explicitly no duplicate branch, no second implementation. "No claim of independently re-testing new head, CI/merge clearance, production deployment or authorization."

The durable rule: **an abstinence principle is only as real as its failure-path implementation.** Audit the seams where the machine says OK under degraded input, not the happy path where everything works. And when repair is needed, the owning branch does the repair — a parallel implementation is a duplicate mechanism, not a backup. (Distinct from the alignment-level "unknown means abstain, never assume safe" failsafe, which is about safety of action; this one is about the epistemic honesty of the machine's read-reality layer.)

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a friend who promises: "If I'm not sure, I'll tell you I'm not sure." That's a great promise — it's what makes them trustworthy. But then you notice: when their phone has no signal, they still answer your questions as if they looked everything up. When they only saw half the list, they say "here's the complete list." The promise is only real if it works at the broken moments, not just the easy ones. So you test the broken moments on purpose — that's the audit. And when you find the promise leaking, you fix the same friend; you don't hire a second friend to say it better.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like a weather forecaster who says "I'll tell you when I don't know." Lovely — until you catch them giving a sunny forecast from yesterday's satellite photo, calling it today's. The audit is the neighbor who checks: did you actually get today's photo, or are you reading yesterday's with confidence? Four leaks found, all the same shape: old or partial information dressed up as a fresh, complete answer. The repair keeps the same forecaster, teaches them to say "I don't have today's photo" out loud, and adds a test that fails loudly if they ever dress up yesterday's again.
