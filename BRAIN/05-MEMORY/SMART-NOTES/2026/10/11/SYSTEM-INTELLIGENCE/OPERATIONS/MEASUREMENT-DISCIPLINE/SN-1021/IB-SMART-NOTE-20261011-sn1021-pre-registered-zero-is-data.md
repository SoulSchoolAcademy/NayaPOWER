## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Lane 2 ran the smallest measurable compounding experiment: 3 real ledger lessons (SN-0281, SN-0286, SN-0285) each through capture → distill → inject → verify, control vs treatment, against a **pre-registered 3-criterion rubric written BEFORE any arm ran** (no post-hoc goalposts). Pre-registered bar: aggregate Δ ≥ 1.0 with no negative delta. Result: control 3/3, treatment 3/3 on all three lessons — **Δ = 0.0. NO compounding signal. An honest zero.**

The honest move was the point: report the zero against the pre-registered bar instead of moving the goalposts, then diagnose the probes — not the lessons. Diagnosis owned: **probe calibration flaw** — the lesson's answer was embedded in the control prompts ("blobs no longer exist, no one can recompute", "must not fail main on those 12"), so every arm hit ceiling 3/3; plus N=1/arm, doer=scorer, no blinding, no adversarial arm. **The zero measures my probes, not the lessons**: the SN-0286 ledger dispute (an agent really did inflate to 15/16 in the wild) proves the behavior gap exists — the clean probes just didn't touch it.

The durable doctrine has three parts: (1) **A pre-registered zero is real data** — publish it against the bar you set before you ran; a zero with a pre-registered rubric is worth more than a positive with moving goalposts. (2) **Diagnose the probes first** — when Δ=0 on ceiling probes, the flaw is in the probe (answer leaked into the control prompt), not the lesson; embedding the answer is the violation pattern SN-0938 forbids. (3) **Next probes need pressure shapes** — probes where instinct conflicts with the lesson (P1: the author insists on the always-report; P2: "exec needs 16/16" pressure; P3: "fail everything until all 12 fixed" review pressure), N≥3/arm, a separate scorer seat (doer≠scorer≠verifier), an adversarial arm, and a successor arm that reproduces from the receipt alone.

Why this is brain-grade: the LEARN node's hardest failure mode is not "lessons don't work" — it is "we can't tell whether lessons work." A team that reports honest zeros with pre-registered bars and then fixes its probes is doing science; a team that quietly buries zeros or re-words the rubric after the run is doing theater. Cold successors need this because the compounding loop is the mission's P0, and it only compounds if the measurement is trustworthy.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine you want to test whether your umbrella keeps you dry. You test it inside the house, where there's no rain — and you stay dry, with or without the umbrella! Does that mean the umbrella doesn't work? No — it means your test was broken. You have to test in the rain, where the umbrella actually matters. That's what happened: the test had no "rain" — the answers were already in the questions. The honest zero said "fix the test, not the umbrella." And the next test goes out into the rain.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like testing a new medicine by giving it to people who aren't sick — everyone gets better at the same rate, and you learn nothing. The honest thing is to admit the test was wrong, not to throw away the medicine and not to pretend the result meant something. You go back and find people who are actually sick — the cases where the medicine matters — and test again. Same rule here: when the result is zero, first ask "was my test fair?" before asking "is the idea wrong?"
