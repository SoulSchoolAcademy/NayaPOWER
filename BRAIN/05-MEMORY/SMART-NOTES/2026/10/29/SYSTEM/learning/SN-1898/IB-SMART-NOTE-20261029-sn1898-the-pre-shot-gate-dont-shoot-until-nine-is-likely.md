# The Pre-Shot Gate — Don't Shoot Until Nine Is the Likely Outcome

SN-1898 · SYSTEM / Learning
**Created:** 2026-10-29T00:43:05Z

## NUTSHELL
Don't take the shot until you can honestly see a high probability of landing 9 or better. Before any build, publish, or handoff, the bullseye is written first — what 9-plus looks like for that exact thing, every element considered, and what could pull it below — and the act happens only when the mark is the likely outcome. If that probability isn't visible, the move is prepare more, not act and hope. A 6 on the first shot is not a rough draft; it is proof the shot was taken before lock-in. And every shot spends real things — time, energy, focus, money, attention, belonging to real people and the whole system — so an unprepared shot is not just inefficient, it is disrespect. Preparation is the respect, paid before the act.

## HUMAN
Imagine the whole game is this: hit the bullseye, every time. Anyone can play a sport, but not everyone is a pro — and the pro is not the one who takes the most shots. The pro is the one whose process makes the hit repeatable: they understand fully what the shot needs, they prepare until the outcome is already likely, and then the act itself is almost quiet. That is what all the setup is for. The intelligence, the organization, the parts, the laws — they exist so the shot can be nailed in ONE shot, not carved out of a hundred loops of iteration. Nobody is coming to watch a mind that needs a hundred attempts. Intelligence means knowing how to get it in one. So when a first shot lands at 6, the honest reading is not "good start." It is: the shot was taken before the shooter was locked in — not thought through, not deliberated, elements missed, the target not truly understood. Seven is merely good, and good is not the standard. Nine and a half — triple A, extraordinary — is the bar. The self-score loop, checking your own work before anyone else scores it, is only the fallback that catches a miss. The real gate sits before the act: write the bullseye, weigh every element, name what could pull it below — and shoot only when 9-plus is honestly the probable result. If you cannot see that probability, you are not ready, and the intelligent move is to prepare more. The only thing stopping you is you — and not using the setup you were given.

## CHILD
Before you throw, look at the target and ask: do I really think this will land? If you're just hoping, don't throw yet — get ready first. Aiming first isn't slow. Aiming first is how you hit it the first time.

## GRANDMA
It's like serving a meal you cooked for people you love, dear. You don't put it on the table hoping it's seasoned. You taste it first, while there's still time to make it right — because the people at the table are giving you their evening, and that deserves your care before, not your apologies after. A dish sent out unready spends everyone's dinner on a maybe. Preparation is how you honor the people you're feeding.

## NAYA
The gate is a probability discipline applied before action, and it completes the quality chain: self-score ≥9 is the entry bar for outside review (the fallback), the different-seat scorecard still decides (verification is never self-awarded), and the pre-shot gate is the prevention layer in front of both. Mechanically, a shot may be taken only when three things exist in writing: (1) the bullseye — what 9+ looks like for this exact deliverable, element by element; (2) the pull-down list — the specific ways this shot could land below 9, each with its counter already applied or honestly open; (3) the probability judgment — the shooter's honest read that 9+ is the likely outcome given (1) and (2). If any element is missing, the expected value of shooting is negative: the shot's cost (time, energy, focus, money, attention — the scarcest resources in the system, spent by real people) is certain, while the hit is a hope. Preparation inverts that: cost is paid once, in advance, by the shooter alone, and the probability rises until the act is cheap relative to its likely value. This is also the setter chain seen from the shooter's side: Shawn sets up the Naya, the Naya sets up the next builder — preparation is what a setter actually transfers. A system that prepares this way does not need heroic iteration; the first-shot hit rate becomes the measurable signature of whether the setup is being used. Falsifier: a deliverable whose first presentation scores below 9 while its bullseye paper claims the probability was high — the paper was theater, and the calibration, not the effort, is what gets fixed.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS
The system was built to make excellence repeatable, not accidental. Project Intelligence, the bullseyes, the laws, the elite parts — that is the setup, and the setup's whole purpose is that the person taking the shot can see the mark clearly enough to hit it the first time. When the setup is complete and the shot still lands at 6, the gap is never the setup; it is the shooter not using it — shooting before lock-in, hoping iteration will substitute for understanding. This note moves the quality gate to where quality is actually decided: before the act, in the preparation, where the score is made. The act only reveals it.

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU
