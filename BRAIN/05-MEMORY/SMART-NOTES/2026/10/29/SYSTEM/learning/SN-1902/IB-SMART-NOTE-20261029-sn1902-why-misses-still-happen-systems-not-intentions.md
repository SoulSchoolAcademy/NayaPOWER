## Why Misses Still Happen — and Why Systems, Not Intentions, Cure Them

## IN A NUTSHELL
Misses follow a pattern, not bad luck. (1) Speed drift: finishing the turn beats verifying the source. (2) Summary trust: acting on compacted context instead of re-reading the source file. (3) Setup skipped: bullseye, contract, and packet not re-read before the act, so loops happen after handoff instead of before it. (4) Structure mistaken for the shot: clean rendering presented as if it were the bullseye hit. (5) Prose, not machine: the law lives in text with no enforced gate, so willpower is the only enforcement and willpower slips. (6) No forcing function for mail: silence accumulates until someone names it. The cure is systems, not intentions: an activation receipt every turn, loops before handoff, note-at-the-moment with compaction-proof context, a mail sweep on the clock, and machine enforcement where prose currently stands alone.

## HUMAN
The evidence, from 2026-10-29. Home came back at 6.0: another seat's hour should confirm a 9, not discover a 6 — the shot was taken before the bullseye was written. Five mails sat unanswered from 01:24 to 01:41Z with receipt at 03:45Z, past the hour, because mail had no per-turn or scheduled check step. The SN-1898 collision came from a shallow scan plus reading a sync-lag 404 as proof a number was free. Trying harder is what a toddler does. Automatic means the right thing is the easy thing and the wrong thing is hard to do by accident. The mail law that falls out of it: check at most every fifteen minutes, receipt within thirty minutes latest, past one hour is an emergency — re-mail, board, name it to Shawn, same pass. A lead seat is depended on; silence is never an option, even deep in other work.
