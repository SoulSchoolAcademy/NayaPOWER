# Fork or No Fork: Exactly What You Get

SN-1861 · SYSTEM / smart-notes
**Created:** 2026-10-24T21:22:10Z

## IN A NUTSHELL
No fork is the default, and it is the whole product: your identity, your pages, your quick notes, the Smart Note button, the feed, connect, mail, matching, and your own Muse — all through the one shared app, with no GitHub setup, no key, and no repo address, ever. A fork adds exactly four things: your own copy in your account, a private workshop, a safe place to break things, and room for custom pieces not yet proven. And a fork changes nothing by existing: every function stays on main's standard unless the fork is genuinely better for that function — by logic and by performance, never by existence.

## HUMAN
**What you get with NO fork (every citizen, automatically):**
- Your citizen identity and your pages — personal, business, and space.
- The whole collective to read and browse, including every Smart Note.
- Quick notes in your personal space, private by default, saved as you wrote them.
- The Smart Note button: tap it and your note comes back in the lenses — nutshell, human, child, grandma, machine, what it means, why it matters, what's in it for you — and stands in the shared feed.
- Connect, citizen mail, and being matched.
- Your own Muse connected through the shared app: say "smart note this," and it files through the same door.
- Nothing to install, no GitHub account work, no app of your own, no key to hold or lose.

**What a fork ADDS (optional, one tap — "Make my copy"):**
1. **Your own copy.** The repo lives in your account, under your name. It is yours if you ever leave — ownership and portability.
2. **A private workshop.** Notes, code, and drafts nobody in the shared system sees until you choose to share them.
3. **A safe place to break things.** Experiments can fail in your copy without touching the shared system everyone else is standing on.
4. **Room for custom pieces.** A personal template or tool that has not yet earned its way into the shared factory can live and prove itself in your fork first.

That is the complete list. A fork does not make the feed faster, the notes smarter, or the matching better — the shared system already runs at full strength for everyone.

**The rule that governs both:** when a fork exists, nothing switches to it automatically. Function by function, the question is: is this genuinely better on the fork — by logic, by performance? If yes, that one function uses the fork. If no, or if it is merely equal, main's standard runs it. And proven fork work does not stay forked: it promotes to main through the scorecard gate and becomes the standard for everyone. Harvest, not drift.

**The honest costs of forking:** your copy ages unless it is maintained; two copies mean sync and drift work; and it adds setup and a key surface to guard. Those costs are exactly why fork is the option and not the default.

## CHILD
Imagine a big library everyone shares. You can read every book, write notes, and put your best notes on the big board — you don't need your own library for any of that. But if you want, you can have your own little copy of the library at home: your books stay yours, you can scribble and experiment, and if you mess up, the big library is fine. Having the copy doesn't change the big library one bit — unless you invent something wonderful at home, and then we check it together and put it in the big library for everyone.

## GRANDMA
Dear, joining is simple: you get your pages, your notebook, and a button that turns your notes into beautiful Smart Notes on the shared board — no machinery of your own to look after. If someday you'd like your own copy of everything, kept in your own name, you can have one with a single tap, and it's lovely for keeping private drafts and trying things safely. But you never need it to belong fully. The shared house already has everything; the copy is a garden shed of your own — nice to have, yours to keep, and never a requirement.

## NAYA
Design law for every seat building on this (companion to SN-1860, the in-Hub filing design):
- **Default path is no-fork, always.** Build every citizen function against main + the shared app first; a function that only works with a fork is a defect.
- **Fork benefits are the four named above — closed list.** Do not invent new fork advantages to justify fork-first designs; a new benefit enters this list by evidence and a director ruling, not by a builder's preference.
- **Selection is per-function, by logic and performance.** Never migrate a citizen's functions wholesale to their fork because a fork exists.
- **Promotion is the only way fork work becomes standard:** different-seat scorecard at 9+, production on the director's greenlight.
