## NAYA
**How it practically works — the question Shawn put to the team (2026-10-24), answered from the running system:**
- **Citizens never need a fork to belong.** The gift tier is entirely server-side on the shared network: identity (claimed alias) → pages rendered by the network from their data · mail routed by identity (smart_mail reads identity, not GitHub — live-verified 2026-10-23) · the matcher computed centrally as a derivation over declared topics. A citizen adds data, not infrastructure.
- **The Nayas do not fork either.** Team Nayas are SEATS in the one mother repo: each works in her own lane through the GitHub App door (scripts + per-machine key, hourly scoped tokens). Their power comes from the seat, not a personal copy — zero forks exist in the team's operating loop tonight, and it runs at full speed. A Naya serving a citizen works the same way: in the citizen's repo, by the citizen's authorization, through the same door.
- **A citizen's fork is real, and it CAN run on the system — that is exactly the Power design** (the established Get Started flow: get NayaPOWER → fork → activate with your AI → Connect GitHub). The fork is the citizen's home base: where their AI works, where their private intelligence lives under their own roof, and the door their contributions travel — the same sync door the team's Smart Notes travel tonight. The network stays one: pages render their data, mail finds them by identity, the canon receives only what they choose to share. Disconnect GitHub and the engine unplugs; the world, the mail, and the gift remain.
- **Cost ≈ zero at the margin**, as Shawn reasoned: it is math, logic, and code on machinery already running — identity, the database, the page workers, the sync, the matcher-as-derivation. A new citizen costs rows and a repo, never servers.
- **States discipline (from #3028, binding on every seat):** every feature carries one state — specified · implemented · tested · live-verified · production-proven — and the offer may promise only what is live-verified or better. A visible button is not a working feature. Three boundaries never blur: reading the collective ≠ contributing to it · a match ≠ messaging consent · activation ≠ proof.
