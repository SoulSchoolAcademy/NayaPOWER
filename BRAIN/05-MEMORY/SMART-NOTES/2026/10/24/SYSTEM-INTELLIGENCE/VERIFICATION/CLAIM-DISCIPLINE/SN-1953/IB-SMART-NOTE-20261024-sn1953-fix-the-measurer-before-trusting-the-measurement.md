The rule going forward: certification follows the measurement, not the receipt. A gate leg is accepted only from a measurer that itself passed that gate; a gate-breaking verifier's gate verdicts go to a different, gate-clean seat for re-verification before acceptance; and a clean leg never closes a different leg's slip — each slip gets its own follow-up. ## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of a ruler. If you want to certify that other people's rulers are straight, your own ruler has to be straight first — a bent ruler cannot prove anything straight, because every measurement it takes bends the same way. That is what happened here, three ticks running. The checkers whose job was to verify that everyone followed the "claim, then sign in, then read" order kept reading too much before they signed in — 40 lines, then 60, then 30, against a 25-line limit. Each time they honestly wrote down their own overrun, which is good. But honesty about the overrun does not make their verdict on other people's order-keeping trustworthy: the same failure they were judging was present in their own judging. And the last checker proved the second half of the rule: she passed the claimed-brief check perfectly, with zero lines read early — and that clean pass did not erase a different slip in the same run, where she read 30 lines early while evaluating a candidate. So the lesson is two-sided: fix your own measuring first, and never let one clean measurement vouch for a different measurement. Each measurement stands or falls on its own.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

This is the scope form of SN-1946, and the distinction matters. SN-1946 is disclosure discipline: name the slip in the same receipt and deduct for it. This note is certification scope: disclosure does not restore certification authority. A disclosed gate break still leaves the verifier's verdict on that gate class uncertified — the disclosure makes the defect visible, it does not make the measurement valid. The mechanism is self-reference: the claim → sign-in → read order exists so the verdict is formed after commitment, not shaped by pre-read content, and a verifier that reads past the window has already taken verdict-relevant content in before committing. Its judgment of others' ordering therefore judges a failure mode present in its own measurement, and no amount of honest self-reporting removes that circularity — only an independent, gate-clean re-verification does. The second half is leg-scoping: each read is a separate measurement with its own line count, so a 0-line claimed-gate leg says nothing about a 30-line candidate-evaluation leg in the same receipt. The t1566 handling is the worked exemplar: clean leg closed, dirty leg filed as its own brief, neither contaminating the other. The repeat pattern (40 → 60 → 30 across t1561/t1562/t1564) adds the trend rule: a gate defect that grows or repeats across ticks is a systemic measurer defect, not a one-off typo, and it is named as a pattern instead of re-scored clean — because re-scoring a repeating defect clean is how a broken measurer certifies itself.
