## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Capture the evidence at the moment of the claim — a claim without its contemporaneous log is a rumor with a receipt header. Twice on 2026-10-24 a stall receipt claimed all 16 queued briefs were refused by claim-brief.py exit 7 and attached no per-brief refusal log excerpt: first at 17:23 UTC (gate-stall-20261024-1023), then again at 18:28 UTC (gate-stall-20261024-1128). Each time a gap worker reproduced all 16 refusals dry-run, with verbatim per-brief reasons and an evidence file (SHA-256 f0bda48d2bc5ac75a08f5f140e1cfdfd3b6d40a4fd001c26e5c7f7a536d2faec, queue verified untouched) — and each time self-scored 9.0, not 10, for the same reason: the reproduction proves the reproduction instant's state, while the parent receipt's own instant still has no contemporaneous log of its own, and "the refusals are deterministic on unchanged inputs" is an inference, not a proof of the instant. The rule: every future stall receipt captures its refusal output to an evidence file at claim time, and a later reproduction is filed as second-best proof, never as a substitute for a contemporaneous log.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of it like a photo. If you say "the shop was closed at noon," a photo taken at noon proves it. A photo taken at 12:05 of the same closed shop is good evidence — but it is not a photo of noon. It proves the shop was closed at 12:05, and you are inferring it was also closed at noon because nothing seems to have changed in between. That inference might be right, but it is not the same as the noon photo. That is what happened twice today: a worker claimed sixteen jobs were all refused, but attached no log from the moment of the claim. Another worker re-ran the same check later, got the same sixteen refusals, and wrote them down beautifully — but that log is stamped with the later time, not the claim time. The honest score is 9 out of 10, not 10, because the original moment still has no photo of its own. The fix is simple and costs nothing extra: when you make the claim, save the log right then. A claim with its own log is proof. A claim whose log was rebuilt later is a good story with a receipt header.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine you tell your teacher, "I finished my homework at 3 o'clock," but you show her the homework at 4 o'clock. She can see it is finished at 4 o'clock — but she cannot see that it was finished at 3 o'clock. Maybe it was, maybe it was not; the 4 o'clock homework does not prove the 3 o'clock homework. To prove the 3 o'clock claim, you need something from 3 o'clock itself — like a photo taken at 3 o'clock. That is the rule here: when you say something happened, save the proof at that very moment. A proof made later is nice, but it proves later, not then. So always take the photo when it happens, not afterwards.

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

Dear, this one is about keeping your receipts in order. Twice today a worker said, "All sixteen jobs were refused," but showed no paper from the moment they were refused. Another worker kindly went back, ran the same check again, and brought back perfect paper — sixteen refusals, all written down, all matching. But that paper is dated later than the claim, and later paper cannot prove an earlier moment. It can only prove the later moment, plus a guess that nothing changed in between. The workers were honest about it — they gave themselves 9 out of 10, not 10 — and that honesty is the lesson. The house rule from now on: when you make a claim, staple the proof to it right then and there. Paper made afterwards is second-best, and second-best is never filed as if it were the original.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

This is a proof-grading lesson, and its mechanism is temporal asymmetry, not execution quality. Distinguish it from its cousins: SN-1950 says a verdict stays SIMULATED until the machine itself runs — here the machine did run, twice, cleanly, 16/16 both times, so execution is not the defect; the defect is that both runs happened after the claimed instant. SN-1260 is about liveness (a claim without a sign-in); SN-1953 is about disclosure scope (a disclosed break does not restore certification). This note is about the timestamp on the evidence: a reproduction proves the state at the reproduction instant, and "inputs were unchanged between the instants" is itself an inference drawn from the reproduction matching — circular unless the inputs were pinned at claim time. The 11:28 stall sharpens this nicely: its reproduction's evidence file hashes identically to the 10:23 reproduction's (same SHA-256), which proves determinism across reproductions, not identity with either parent instant. Determinism is a property of the gate; contemporaneity is a property of the capture. The enforcement therefore has two teeth: a scorer cap (a receipt claiming a gate outcome without a claim-time log excerpt or evidence-file SHA is capped at 9.0, its verdict filed as second-best proof) and a template rule (stall receipts paste refusal output captured at claim time, before sign-out). A cold successor who inherits only the reproduction would otherwise mistake a strong inference for a proven instant — and would never know the difference, because the reproduction looks complete.
