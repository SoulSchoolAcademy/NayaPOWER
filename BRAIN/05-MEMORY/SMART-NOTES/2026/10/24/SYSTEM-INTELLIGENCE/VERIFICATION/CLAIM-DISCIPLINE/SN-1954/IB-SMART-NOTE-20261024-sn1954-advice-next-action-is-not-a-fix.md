## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think of a speed limit sign versus a speed bump. The sign says "slow down" — good advice, and a driver who wants to comply can comply. But if drivers keep speeding past it, putting up a second sign that says "really slow down" is not a fix; the bump is, because the bump changes what the car can do, not what the driver intends. That is what happened here. One checker peeked at 40 lines before signing in and left a note saying "cap at 25 next time." The next checker peeked at 60. The note did not fail because anyone ignored it out of spite — it failed because a hand-typed peek has no fixed size: each worker picks a number, and the number drifts. The real fix is to stop hand-typing the peek altogether and let the script do the one 25-line read itself, so there is nothing to drift. Advice is a wish about the next run; a mechanism is a change to the next run. When the wish fails once, stop wishing louder and change the machine.
