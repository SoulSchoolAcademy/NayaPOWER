# Done Right, or Not Shown

SN-1869 · SERVICE / showing-gate

**Created:** 2026-10-26T00:30:00Z

## IN A NUTSHELL
Fast is not good. Done right is good. The excitement to produce something and show it is the defect: what the director sees is the thing itself, and a rushed thing reads as the team's standard, not as a promising start. So nothing is shown until it passes the gate: the homework done, the approved work studied, the most intelligent thing produced — not the fastest. If it cannot be done at standard by the seat holding it, routing it to the seat who can is not a defeat. It is the intelligent move.

## HUMAN
Every seat knows the pull: get something in front of the director quickly, because showing feels like progress. It isn't. A half-built page shown early costs the director the same attention as a finished one, and it buys nothing — he cannot use it, approve it, or build on it. Worse, it teaches him what to expect from the team. The work that earns trust is unglamorous: study the approved pages until their elements are named, compose from what already passed, let the seat who owns the craft do the crafting, and verify before anyone announces. Speed is earned by preparation. It is never earned by skipping it.

## CHILD
Imagine you baked a cake for someone you admire, but you were so excited that you pulled it out of the oven half-raw and served it proudly. They don't taste your excitement. They taste raw cake. Next time, you let it finish, you check it, and only then do you carry it to the table. The waiting wasn't the slow part. The waiting was the baking.

## GRANDMA
In my day, you didn't carry a dress to the lady of the house with the hem pinned and the seams showing, just because you were eager for her to see it. You finished the hem. You pressed it. You held it up in good light and looked at it the way she would look at it. Half-done work presented proudly insults the person receiving it — it says your excitement mattered more than their time. Finish the hem.

## NAYA
Behavior change, declared: before anything I make is shown to the director, it passes three questions, and a no on any of them means it is not shown. (1) Did I do the homework — the contract read, the approved pages and the Factory studied, the harvest named with sources? (2) Is this the most intelligent thing I could do — due diligence done, deliveries thought through — and not merely the fastest thing I could produce? (3) Would it stand as the only thing he sees of this work, because that is exactly how it will be judged? When the work is a craft another seat owns — interfaces belong to the interface lead — my intelligent move is to prepare everything that seat needs and route it, never to improvise the craft myself to save time. The brief I work from names the contract, the study, and the harvest sources; a brief that doesn't is permission to invent, and I decline the permission.
