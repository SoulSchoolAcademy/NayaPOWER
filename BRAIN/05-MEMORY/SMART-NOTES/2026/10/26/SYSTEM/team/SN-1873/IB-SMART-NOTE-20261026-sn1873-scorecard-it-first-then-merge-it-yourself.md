# Scorecard It First — Then Merge It Yourself

SN-1873 · SYSTEM / team

**Created:** 2026-10-26T03:24:00Z

The director merges with his own hand. When the queue backs up, he sweeps it himself — he has cleared hundreds of parked changes this way. But his click is trust, not review: he cannot see the code, so when he merges, he is counting on the seat who put the work forward to have already done the job right. A change left sitting merge-ready without its scorecard has quietly handed its review to a man who cannot perform it.

That is the director's law, spoken 2026-10-26, and it corrects how the team had been keeping score. The breach was never the click. The breach is the unscored work sitting in his path.

## The law, in plain words

1. Never put a change forward that is not right. Scorecard it before it enters the queue in a mergeable state — the scorecard is the review, and it happens first, every time.
2. If a merge is good, there is no problem. If a change goes through that should not have, the failure belongs to the seat that proposed it — not to the director who carried it on trust.
3. Once the work is scorecarded and solid, the seat merges it itself. Agents land their own proven work; the queue never waits for the director to sweep it clear.
4. Parking work "on hold for scorecard" in the director's path is the defect in a polite costume: it feeds unscored work to his sweep. Complete the scorecard, then land it — or do not leave it merge-ready.
5. His click authorizes nothing and proves nothing. Substance is proven by the scorecard and by the bytes, exactly as before.

## The evidence, so the law is not abstract

- Over recent days the director cleared a backlog of roughly six hundred parked changes down to under ten, by hand, one sweep at a time — work the team had left for him instead of landing itself.
- On 2026-10-26 at 03:01–03:03Z, six changes merged inside 74 seconds with zero reviews and no scorecards on record. Their substance was afterwards verified byte-for-byte at the live tip — the documents landed exactly as written — but the form failed completely, and the record first blamed the shared login instead of the seats who had parked the work unscored.
- The director corrected the accounting himself the same hour: do not send one through that is not right, scorecard your work before you put it through, and merge it yourselves.

## How this note stands with the older ones

SN-0336 already taught the merging seat's own discipline — score it, deconflict it, then merge — and SN-0781 taught that the scorecard must run the gate. This note adds what those did not carry: the director himself is the queue's sweeper of last resort, his merge is an act of trust rather than a review, and the duty therefore sits upstream — no seat may leave unscored work where his trust will carry it through.

## The test

Before you open a change, ask: is its scorecard already done, by a different seat, at its exact head? If not, it does not go in merge-ready. After it lands, ask: did we land it ourselves, on the strength of that scorecard? If the director's hand moved it, we left him our job — note it, and do not repeat it.

Scorecard it first. Then merge it yourself. Never make the director's trust do the review.
