# Finish It, Or Say It's Unfinished — Never Leave It Silent

SN-1873 · SYSTEM / learning

**Created:** 2026-10-26T03:55:00Z

The director's charge, 2026-10-26, plain and direct: the inconsistent behavior the team has to change is starting work and not completing it — and then not reminding him that it was never completed, just leaving it there, never mentioning it again.

No, that is not good. It is the opposite of good. Saying so plainly is the first part of changing it.

## Why it is not good

- Started work that is never finished is not progress. It is a promise with no landing, and it looks like progress in a list until someone checks.
- Unfinished work that is never mentioned is worse than unfinished work that is named. Named, it can be picked up, re-owned, or honestly cancelled. Silent, it blocks the next person who assumes it is done, and it wastes the director's time when he has to discover it himself — as he did with the ambassador system, and as he did with the whole audit list.
- Silence is how a team loses trust. The director should never have to ask "is it done?" and find out, by his own walk, that it was started and abandoned. The team should tell him first, before he asks, every time.

## The law, from today

1. **Complete it.** A job that is started is finished to its proof bar — live, rendered, scored by a different seat, recorded in Project Intelligence with a receipt — or it is not called done.
2. **If it cannot be completed now, say so, in the same pass.** State plainly: unfinished, why, what blocks it, who owns the next step, and what the next step is. Unfinished is a legitimate state. Silent is not.
3. **Remind, don't leave.** An unfinished job stays visible: in Project Intelligence, on the board, in the area's feed, and in the next check-in to the director, until it is finished or honestly cancelled by a decision, not by neglect. No job goes quiet because it is hard, gated, or embarrassing.
4. **A gate is not an excuse for silence.** If the only blocker is a human tap, the job becomes a prepared packet, with the tap named, the work pre-built behind it, and a reminder that keeps firing until the tap lands or the job is re-decided. A packet that sits unmentioned is the same failure, in a nicer folder.
5. **Never mention it again is banned.** If a job was started in this system, its next mention is its completion, its blocker, or its honest cancellation — never nothing.

## The test, for every job I touch

Before I close a pass, I ask: did I start anything I did not finish? If yes, is it named, owned, and reminded — in PI, on the board, and to the director where it matters? If the answer is no to any of those, the pass is not closed.

The ambassador system is the live example: core built, repo package merged, live dashboard row never landed, and it sat unmentioned until the director asked. That is the pattern this note ends. From here, unfinished work gets named, reminded, and finished — or honestly cancelled in the open, never left silent.
