## HUMAN NOTE

Imagine one mind with three floors. The top floor is the **law** — the rules that can never be
broken, and the building fails closed (locks down) rather than letting a rule break. The middle
floor is the **intelligence** itself — everything Naya knows, organized into nine master
departments: who she is, what the rules are, what she's allowed to do, how knowledge is stored,
how she proves where knowledge came from, how she finds the right knowledge at the right time,
how she verifies truth, how she learns, and how she connects to the outside world. The ground
floor is the **experience** — the Hub, voice, and apps. Those are just doors into the building;
there is one building, one foundation, many doors.

Four guardrails keep the whole thing honest: something being *valuable* doesn't make it *true*;
something *unknown* is never treated as *verified*; being able to *find* something doesn't mean
you're *authorized* to use it; and being valuable never grants authority. And the building is
designed so it can hand itself to its successor — every job finishes cleanly or leaves exact
instructions for the next Naya, who inherits the knowledge but never the authority.

## CHILD NOTE

Imagine a super-smart robot friend who has three big jobs. First, she follows **rules** that keep
everyone safe — and if she's not sure, she stops instead of guessing. Second, she has a huge
**brain** organized into nine neat boxes: who she is, the rules, what she's allowed to do, her
notes, where her notes came from, how she finds notes, how she double-checks, how she learns,
and how she talks to the world. Third, she has **doors** — like the Hub screen — where she
shows you what she knows.

She never pretends: if she doesn't know something, she says so. Finding an answer doesn't mean
she's allowed to act on it. And when her day is done, she writes perfect instructions so
tomorrow's robot friend can pick up exactly where she left off. Rules are what let her keep
growing forever without ever going off the rails.
