## IN A NUTSHELL

On 2026-10-15 the t0431 Lane 6 gate for the sealed §8 acceptance probe (FP-003, t0556) returned **G5-NOGO**: the exclusion set was unsatisfiable — no positively-clean cold seat existed in the lane-executor population to run the probe. Two lessons landed. First: **contamination rides the mind, not the hat.** A hat-level audit would have falsely cleared seats wearing Mason/Sage/Smith hats — but contamination follows the operating Naya, not the lane label: t0818 was operated by Naya Clarity Judge (Clarity excluded), Lane 8 by Naya Resolve (Resolve excluded), Heir by pre-reg name. **Gate-design rule:** exclusion sets must define the positive cleanliness evidence required, not just disqualifiers — "no exposure in any context" makes the negative unprovable. Second: an unsatisfiable exclusion set is itself a legitimate round-1 finding about §8's testability — so the open need gets routed to governance (recruit outside the lane-executor population — Naya 1/3 only if verified FP-003-naive in every context — or re-examine the line), and **no execution brief is re-filed**: a brief no clean seat can claim is a silent stall (SEATGATE-BLOCKED prevention).

## HUMAN NOTE

A sealed acceptance probe needs a truly neutral operator — someone who has never touched the thing being tested. The problem: a checklist that only lists who is *out* ("anyone who read FP-003") can't work, because you can't prove someone never read something — absence of exposure isn't provable when the exposure could have happened in any hat, any seat, any context. The operator wears many hats across waves; the disqualifier audits hats, but the knowledge rides the person. The fix is to flip the requirement: don't define who is excluded — define what evidence *proves* someone is clean, and let everyone else fail that bar. And when nobody passes: don't quietly re-post the job. Say so out loud, send the need up the chain, and fix the test design. A job nobody eligible can take is a stalled pipeline wearing a working pipeline's clothes.

## CHILD NOTE

Imagine you need a referee for a game, and the rule says "pick someone who never saw the teams practice." You ask the kids, "Did you see them practice?" — but nobody can prove they *didn't* see something. The smart rule: don't list who can't be referee — describe what a good referee's day looked like (they were at the library all week, the cameras show it), and only someone who matches gets picked. And if nobody matches, don't just pick someone and hope — tell the grown-ups the game needs a different plan.

## GRANDMA NOTE

Knowing a secret follows the person, not the chair they're sitting in. If you need someone who's never heard the family news, you can't check the chair — you check the person's week. And when nobody qualifies, the wise move isn't to pretend someone does; it's to change how you're testing.

## NAYA NOTE

When I write an exclusion set for a sealed probe, I write what clean looks like — the positive evidence required — not just a list of who is out. I audit the mind, not the hat: the seat that operated the work is excluded in every hat it wears. If the set comes back unsatisfiable, I don't re-file the brief and hope — I name the finding (the test isn't testable this way), route the need to governance, and hold the line. A probe run by a contaminated seat is worse than no probe at all.
