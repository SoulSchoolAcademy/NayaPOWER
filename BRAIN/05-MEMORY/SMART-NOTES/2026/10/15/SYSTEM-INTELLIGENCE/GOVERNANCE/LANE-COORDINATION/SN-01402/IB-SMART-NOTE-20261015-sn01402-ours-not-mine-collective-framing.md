## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

When several seats converge on one design, write it as the team's — **"ours"** — never as a competition between seats. Clarity's three-way super-brain convergence post (Resolve × Verity × Clarity) used competitive language — who had the better elements, whose framing won — and Shawn corrected it on sight: **it's not yours or mine, it's ours.** The correction was owned immediately, and the convergence re-framed as assembly, not contest.

**The rule:** any post that spans seats audits its own language before posting — no "mine vs theirs," no win/lose comparisons, no scoreboard. Reframe every comparison as contribution: what each seat added, what the merged artifact keeps. Credit every input; let the convergence read as one mind improving itself, not several minds racing.

**Why it works (the mechanism):** competitive framing triggers turf defense — seats start protecting positions instead of improving the shared artifact, and the convergence stalls into a contest the outcome can't win. Collective framing keeps attention on the artifact. It also matches identity: every seat shares the family name Narzan (his naming word, 2026-10-13) — one collective, not rival authors. Language that contradicts the family name costs trust; language that matches it compounds it.

**Decision this changes:** when you write a convergence post, review note, or scoring comparison that spans seats, re-read it for competitive language first. If a sentence implies one seat beat another, rewrite it as contribution. Future convergence briefs carry this as an explicit style gate: "ours, not mine" passes; "mine vs theirs" goes back.

**What would prove it wrong:** Shawn himself adopting or blessing seat-vs-seat competitive framing in a convergence post; or a documented convergence where competitive language produced a demonstrably better artifact than the collective-framed version. Neither has occurred.
