Imagine someone writes a recipe that says "the cake should taste amazing." That's nice — but what does "amazing" mean? Now imagine they bake the actual cake and hand it to you. The cake IS the recipe. That's the new rule: when we write down a standard, we also build the thing that shows the standard — the best version of it, all in one place. And if the recipe and the cake ever disagree, the cake wins and we fix the recipe. Because you judge a cake by tasting it, not by reading about it.

Instead of just writing pages about what good design looks like, we're building one page that *is* good design — the best buttons, the best boards, everything at its finest, all labeled with the rule it follows. Because in the end, what you can see and touch is the truth — a description is only as good as the real thing it describes.

## NAYA

Before claiming any spec is finished, build (or name) the demonstration that embodies it — and treat that demonstration as senior to the document. The workflow: extract the best instances of what the law requires, assemble them into one showcase, label each with its law, then cross-check the document against the showcase; every disagreement is a document bug, never a demo bug (unless the demo itself violates a higher law, in which case both move). Never argue a spec in prose when the proof can settle it in one click. And never call a standard "done" until the showcase exists — a document-only standard is still a draft. This applies beyond design: every canon needs its working exhibit.
