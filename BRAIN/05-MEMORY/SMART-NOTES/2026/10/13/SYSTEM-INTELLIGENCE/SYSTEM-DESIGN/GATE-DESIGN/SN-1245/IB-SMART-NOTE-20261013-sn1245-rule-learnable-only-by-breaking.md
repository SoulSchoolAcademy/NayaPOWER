## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

The sign-in-before-read rule was violated for the **third documented time** — and it's not a training problem, it's a design signal. The rule lives *inside the brief*, so it is structurally unreachable until the moment it governs: a worker claims a brief, then reads it, then — too late — learns they were supposed to sign in first. **A rule that can only be learned by breaking it is not a rule; it's a trap.** The fix is never "remind everyone harder." The fix is to move the rule from documentation into the mechanism: gate the claim on sign-in — the sign-in form sits above the fold, or the queue tool itself records the sign-in at claim time. Documentation tells; mechanism enforces. When the same violation recurs after being broadcast twice, the defect is in the design, not the people.

Why this is brain-grade: the brain's laws are prose today and enforcement tomorrow — every law that lives only in a document depends on readers finding it before the action it governs. Any rule whose governed action necessarily precedes the reading (claim-before-read, merge-before-checklist, act-before-receipt) is dead on arrival as prose. The recurrence count is the detector: one violation is an accident, two is a pattern, three is the design confessing. The general test: for every rule, ask "when can a compliant worker first learn this — before or after the action it governs?" After means redesign, not retraining.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine the rule "take your shoes off before entering" is written on a note that's inside the house. You can only read it after you've already walked in with your shoes on! Everyone keeps breaking the rule, and the teacher keeps saying "remember the note" — but the note is in the wrong place. The smart fix isn't a bigger note; it's a doormat that says "shoes off" right at the door, or a gate that only opens after you take them off. Rules belong at the door, not inside the house.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

There's a bakery with a sign inside the shop that says "no outside food" — but you only see it after you've walked in with your coffee. Three people a week get told off, and the owner keeps blaming the customers. But the customers aren't the problem; the sign is. Put the sign on the door, or better yet, a rack by the entrance where you naturally set the coffee down. The principle is an old one: put the rule where the action happens, not where it's convenient to store. When the same mistake keeps happening after everyone "knows" the rule, stop reteaching and start redesigning.
