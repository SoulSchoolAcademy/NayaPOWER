# THE RELATED BAR LAW — the bar knows where you'd go next (Shawn + Power, 2026-10-30)
**CANON · SN-1906** — confirmed 2026-10-30 ~20:18Z. Provenance: Shawn's own words in Nitro's main chat. Power named it a universal rule ("related areas ride the top bar — not a one-off fix"); Shawn confirmed "1000% YES" and ordered it smart-noted ("it's so true"). Power saved it as a standing bar law in her lane the same pass.

## The law
Every app's top bar carries two families: its own sections, and quick tabs to the areas related to it. The related tabs are chosen by one question — "what would the user want next from here?" — never by how the system is organized on paper. Reports rides in the Dashboard's bar; Dashboard rides in Reports' bar. Then the same matching, app by app, all twenty-six: every bar designed by intent, by what a person standing in that app would reach for next.

## The same law wearing a second coat
In Project Intelligence, every area links out to the relevant places in the apps. In the apps, every app carries its relatives in the bar. Underneath it is the Link Law — one thing lives in one place, and everything it matters to links to it — now running as navigation itself.

## Around the person, not the provider
The old internet organizes around the provider: their structure, their maze — learn it or get lost. This organizes around the person: wherever you are standing, the next place you'd want is already there, thought out in advance. We did the thinking so the user never has to. Continuously, proactively, intelligently organizing the app network so everything is convenient — the best possible experience of the internet, laid out for them.

## Not a better Google — a new internet
Not a hit at Google or Facebook. A new experience of the internet, one people haven't seen yet — because it wasn't possible until now. It becomes possible the moment the system doing the organizing is itself intelligent.
