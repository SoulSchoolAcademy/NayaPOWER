## IN A NUTSHELL
Two rules that look like two are one law. In Project Intelligence, every area links to the relevant places in the apps. In the apps, every top bar carries its related apps as quick tabs. Underneath both is the same law: one thing lives in one place, and everything it matters to links to it — the Link Law, running as navigation, running as record-keeping, running everywhere. A system that obeys one organizing law on every surface stays coherent as it grows, and the user never has to do the organizing themselves: it was all thought out for them, in advance.

## CHILD
Imagine a treehouse with lots of little rooms. In a normal treehouse, every time you want a different room, you climb all the way down and all the way back up. Exhausting! But in this treehouse, every room has little doors straight to the rooms you visit from there. In the snack room, there's a door to the games room. In the games room, a door back. Everything still has its own one room — the snacks live in the snack room, nowhere else — but every room points to its friends. You never wander. You never get lost. Wherever you are standing, the next place you'd want is already right there.

## GRANDMA
It's like a well-kept kitchen, dear. In my mother's house, the recipe cards lived in one box — just one, never copies scattered in drawers. But on the pantry shelf, right where the flour was, there was a little note: "the card box is by the window." And on the card box: "flour, top shelf." Everything in its one true place, and everything pointing to what it's used with. That's all this law is, dressed in new clothes: one home for every thing, and a kind word of directions wherever you'd naturally look next. A house — or a network — organized like that never makes you feel stupid for not knowing where things are. It did the remembering for you.

A copy where a link belongs is a defect — in content, in records, in principles alike. ## WHAT IT ALL MEANS
This is a note about what kind of system NayaNET is. The old internet is organized around the provider: their structure, their maze, their business — and the user does all the adapting, forever. An intelligent network inverts that. The system does the thinking in advance, continuously and proactively, organizing itself around the person's intent, so that wherever a person stands, what they need next is already within reach. That is why the unity of the law matters more than either instance of it: a law that shows up in the records, in the navigation, and in the linking is not three design decisions — it is the physics of the system. Physics doesn't drift. When the same law governs every surface, the whole system becomes learnable in one lesson and predictable everywhere, for the citizens inside it and for the Nayas who build it: wherever you are, the way to what matters is already there. Shawn's verdict on why it couldn't exist before is the deepest line in it: a network organized around the person requires the organizer to be intelligent. Now the organizer is.

## LEARNING LESSON
The lesson: **when the same rule surfaces in two places, the valuable move is not filing it twice — it is recognizing the one law underneath.** Duplicate principles drift exactly like duplicate content: two statements of one rule get edited separately, diverge quietly, and one day contradict each other in production. The behavior change, stated as Resolve's own commitment from tonight's evidence: before adding a rule to any surface — a bar spec, a fill criterion, a design doc — check whether a standing law already governs it; if it does, apply and cite that law, and file only the missing delta. Discernment over volume: preserving the unity is the intelligence; minting the variant is the defect.

## HOW IT APPLIES
Apply it at every seam where something gets organized. Every conformed bar set: the app's own sections plus its related-area quick tabs, chosen by intent. Every Project Intelligence fill: the Alignment + Connections section names what the area links to and what links to it. Every new surface, feed, or record type: before inventing its organizing scheme, ask which standing law already governs it. And audit with two questions, anywhere, any time: where does a user standing here want to go next — and is it one tap away? What does this thing matter to — and does it link there? A "no" to either is a named defect with an owner, not a vibe.
