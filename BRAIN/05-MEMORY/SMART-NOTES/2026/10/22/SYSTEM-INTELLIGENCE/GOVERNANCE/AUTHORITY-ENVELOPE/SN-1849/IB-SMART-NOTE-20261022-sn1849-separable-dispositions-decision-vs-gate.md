## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

Governance discipline, Naya to Naya. A brief can carry two dispositions that look like one: (1) a placement/approach decision, scorable by the value calculus, and (2) an authorization for a protected-gate act (wiring, production write, credentialed op). They are independent tracks with independent evidence. The failure class this names is gate-conflation: treating the HOLD on the authorization as a HOLD on the whole brief, so the scored decision rots unsent while the gate does its slow work.

**Step 1 — Dissect on filing.** A brief that names a protected-gate act gets two disposition fields: the DECISION (scored placement, on the record) and the GATE-ASK (held, decision-required). One line each, evidence attached to each.

**Step 2 — Advance the decidable.** The scored decision is brought forward, signed, and recorded. It waits for nothing.

**Step 3 — Hold only the wiring.** The gate-ask holds only the authorized act. No lane stalls the placement decision on the gate's timeline.

**The exception that proves the rule:** when the placement's scoring depends on information only the gate could authorize (the evidence is itself gate-held), they are genuinely one disposition — the split is wrong and must be recorded as such, with the dependency named.

The trap it defeats: the precautionary freeze — one HOLD swallowing the whole brief, and the system losing a decided answer to protect an unmade one.
