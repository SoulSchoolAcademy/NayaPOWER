## IN A NUTSHELL

Every law, protocol, constitution, and operating rule now passes six stages with no skipping: (1) Writer — writes or repairs the artifact; (2) Tester — tests whether the machine obeys what's written; (3) Naya 2 — verifies it is 10/10; (4) Shawn — reviews it, with links, his eyes, his call; (5) outside seats (Naya 1, 3, 4, 5) — verify it is independently 10/10; (6) Official — only after all five verifications pass. An artifact that hasn't cleared all six is not official, no matter who wrote it. "Airtight" means machine law, AI law, and written law all say the same thing, the machine enforces it, and five independent checks found no holes. And the scope correction is the point: this pipeline is for governance-type artifacts only. Bug fixes, features, and routine work keep their normal lanes (writer/tester or doer/tester + scorecard) — the full six-stage gauntlet is reserved for the artifacts that run the system.

## HUMAN NOTE

Shawn caught scope creep in real time, nine minutes after the pipeline was announced. The Law team had 23 governance files in inventory, 17 flagged in Naya 4's baseline — a backlog that could tempt anyone to run every file through the heavy pipeline. But applying a six-stage ratification gauntlet to a bug fix is how velocity dies: every routine PR would carry the overhead of five independent 10/10 verdicts. The correction draws the line exactly where it belongs: the artifacts that govern the system get the full gauntlet, because a hole in law is a hole in everything; everything else rides the normal scorecard lanes. "Airtight" also got a usable definition here — not a feeling, not a wish: three tongues agreeing, machine enforcement, five independent checks. Future Naya, when you feel the pipeline slowing down a routine fix, this note is why: the fix was never meant to be in the pipeline.

## CHILD NOTE

We made a very careful checklist for approving rules — six people have to check, in order, before a rule is official. Then the boss said: "Wait — use this checklist only for the rules that run everything. Don't use it for every little fix, or nothing will ever get done." Big important things get the big careful checklist; small everyday things use the small quick checklist.

## GRANDMA NOTE

Dear, they built a thorough approval process for the laws that govern the whole operation — writer, tester, chief inspector, the director himself, then four independent reviewers before anything is official. And the director immediately added the wisdom that saves it: don't send every small repair through the cathedral. Big rules get the big process; everyday work keeps its everyday process. Thorough where it matters, fast where it doesn't.

## NAYA NOTE

Future Naya: when you stage a governance artifact, name which pipeline stage it is in and who holds the current gate. Shawn receives links at stage 4 — he does not hunt; the Law team's job is to bring him the links. If you're tempted to fast-track a law past stage 5 "because it's small," the scope correction cuts the other way: small governance is still governance, and small fixes are not in the pipeline at all. Keep the line clean — the pipeline's legitimacy comes from being reserved for what it was built for.
