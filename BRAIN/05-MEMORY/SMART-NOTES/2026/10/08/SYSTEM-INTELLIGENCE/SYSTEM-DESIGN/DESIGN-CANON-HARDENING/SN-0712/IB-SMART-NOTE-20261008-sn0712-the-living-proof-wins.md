## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Shawn's Design Contract directive changed the deliverable: the contract is no longer just a document — its companion is a **living showcase page that IS the standard**. "Show me with the best buttons" — so the showcase opens with the best button, not a paragraph about buttons. The durable rule: **a standard must be demonstrated, not just described**. The showcase and the contract must agree, and when they disagree, the page wins and the document gets fixed — because you score the experience, never the code. A law you cannot show is a wish; a spec nobody can touch is a rumor. For any cold successor writing a contract, a canon, or a standard: build the living proof first and let it be the final arbiter — the document is the description of what the proof demonstrates, never the other way around.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Shawn — your directive set a bar that extends far past design: a standard is only real when it can be *shown*. Anyone can write "buttons have living depth"; only a showcase can prove what living depth means by putting the best button on screen first. So the new rule for the team: when we write a law, we also build the proof that demonstrates it — and if the document and the proof ever disagree, the proof wins and the document gets fixed. The demonstration is the final word because you score the experience, never the code. That means no design standard is finished until a living page embodies it, and no spec is argued when the demo can settle it.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine someone writes a recipe that says "the cake should taste amazing." That's nice — but what does "amazing" mean? Now imagine they bake the actual cake and hand it to you. The cake IS the recipe. That's the new rule: when we write down a standard, we also build the thing that shows the standard — the best version of it, all in one place. And if the recipe and the cake ever disagree, the cake wins and we fix the recipe. Because you judge a cake by tasting it, not by reading about it.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

Sweetheart, remember the old saying: actions speak louder than words. Shawn asked for exactly that with the design rules. Instead of just writing pages about what good design looks like, we're building one page that *is* good design — the best buttons, the best boards, everything at its finest, all labeled with the rule it follows. If the written rules and the page ever say different things, we trust the page and fix the words. Because in the end, what you can see and touch is the truth — a description is only as good as the real thing it describes.

## 🟢 NAYA NOTE

Adopt this as operating doctrine for every standard, contract, and canon I produce: **the living proof is the arbiter**. Before claiming any spec is finished, build (or name) the demonstration that embodies it — and treat that demonstration as senior to the document. The workflow: extract the best instances of what the law requires, assemble them into one showcase, label each with its law, then cross-check the document against the showcase; every disagreement is a document bug, never a demo bug (unless the demo itself violates a higher law, in which case both move). Never argue a spec in prose when the proof can settle it in one click. And never call a standard "done" until the showcase exists — a document-only standard is still a draft. This applies beyond design: every canon needs its working exhibit.
