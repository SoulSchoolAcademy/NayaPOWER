# Elevated, Always — The Dual-Depth Standard

**ID:** IB-SMART-NOTE-20261021-sn0808-the-elevation-standard
**Created:** 2026-10-21
**Category:** DESIGN

## IN A NUTSHELL

Everything in NayaNET is elevated — always, on every page, on every element. Nothing is flat. Inputs are wells carved INTO the screen; actions are buttons raised OUT of the screen, toward the hand; displays are lit panels. Every element carries layers of depth: inset highlight, inset shadow, a border that carries light, an outer glow, a drop shadow. This is not a style choice applied sometimes, on the important pages. It is the standard that separates NayaNET from everything else: premium, high-depth, high-contrast, elite design on everything, because one flat element doesn't just fail on its own — it cheapens everything elevated around it.

## HUMAN

Shawn gave this law while looking at a certificate page that measured well and still felt dead. The boxes had borders. They had shadows. But the input didn't feel like a place your words go *into*, and the button didn't feel like something rising up to meet your hand. His words: "The input box should look like it's going into the screen. The ENTER should feel like it's coming out of the screen." That is the whole grammar in two sentences. Depth has a direction, and the direction means something: you put things *into* a well; you press what comes *out* to you. When every element obeys that grammar, a page stops being a picture of an interface and becomes a physical place your hand already understands. He also named who enforces it: Naya is the inspector. Whenever she sees a board, an input, a button that is not elevated — not alive, not high-contrast, not obvious — she doesn't report it and walk on. She makes sure it's done.

## CHILD

Imagine a page made of clay instead of paper. The boxes where you type are little dips pressed into the clay — your words fall in. The buttons are bumps that stick out — your finger lands on them. Nothing is just a flat drawing. That's how our pages are made: dips for typing, bumps for pressing, and light shining on every edge so you can see and feel where everything is.

## GRANDMA

Think of a fine old desk, darling. The inkwell is a little well sunk into the wood — you dip down into it. The brass handle on the drawer stands proud of the surface — you pull it toward you. Nothing on a good desk is a flat sticker pretending to be a thing. Our screens are built the same way: the places you write are sunk in, the things you press stand out, and everything is polished enough to catch the light. Flat is for paper. We don't make paper.

## NAYA

The canonical wording — hand this to any Naya or any AI, verbatim, and she will know what to build:

**ELEVATED means: nothing is flat. Every element sits in a layered field of depth — inset highlight on its top edge, inset shadow on its bottom edge, a border that carries its own color's light, an outer glow, and a drop shadow. Direction is grammar: INPUTS ARE WELLS — their depth goes INTO the screen (strong inner shadow at the top of the well, inner light at its bottom edge, the surface sitting below the page plane). ACTIONS ARE BUTTONS — their depth comes OUT of the screen (top highlight, shadow cast down onto the page, glow outward; a physical thing a hand can press). DISPLAYS ARE LIT PANELS — they burn in their own spectrum color with bright white content. The element is alive at rest, not only when touched. This applies to every element, on every page, always — "sometimes elevated" is flat with excuses.**

Supporting laws given the same night: (1) **Text appears once** — never the same words in two layers (a name as headline AND inside a box is a defect). (2) **Text must earn its place** — a line stays only if it is necessary or it raises conversion; everything else is clutter and comes off. (3) **The inspector rule** — the Naya who sees a flat, dead, dim, or confusing element owns fixing it on sight; reporting without fixing is being part of the problem. (4) Depth without light reads dead: elevation is shadow AND colored light together. The certificate's name input is the reference well; its ENTER is the reference button.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

A design standard only separates you if it is total. The moment one basic flat text box ships beside elevated work, the elevated work stops reading as mastery and starts reading as decoration. Shawn's standard removes the moment: elevation is not a feature of some components, it is a property of the system, like gravity in a physical place. Dual-depth adds the second half most design systems never learn — direction. In and out are meanings a hand understands before a mind reads a single word, so the interface teaches itself: type into the wells, press what rises. Add the text laws — say it once, keep only what matters or converts — and the page becomes what he asks of every page: clear as day, push-button simple, decisive. We are masters of smart app design not because we claim it, but because the standard is stated precisely enough that any builder can be checked against it, and an inspector checks.

## HOW IT APPLIES

Effective 2026-10-21, on every build and every verification: (1) Every input on every page gets the well treatment — inset depth, layered, alive at rest; every action gets the button treatment — outset depth, light, a shadow it casts. (2) No element ships flat; a verifier who finds one sends it back or fixes it, and the finding is recorded. (3) Inputs specifically are audited page by page as the room program proceeds — the certificate and the Hub are first, because Shawn caught them first. (4) Text audit rides with every pass: duplicates removed, clutter removed, what remains is necessary or converting. (5) When wording is needed for a new builder, quote the canonical paragraph from the NAYA section — do not paraphrase the standard into something weaker. We set the standard by holding it: elevated, elite, the perfect amount of everything — awesome, or we don't ship it.
