# Nobody Discovers What Doesn't Hook Them

**ID:** IB-SMART-NOTE-20261020-sn0804-viral-law
**Created:** 2026-10-20
**Category:** SYSTEM / Viral Law

## IN A NUTSHELL

Viral is not luck. Viral is law. Every headline and title in NayaNET is written to hook — a great headline and a great hook, scored to a 10 like everything else we ship — because the headline is the doorway to the intelligence. Nobody discovers what doesn't hook them. The sharpest smart note ever written, filed under a number, is invisible: machines file by numbers, humans stop for hooks. So the hook is not decoration on the value — it is the first act of value, the highest-value action, because it is what gets a human hooked in and wanting to discover the intelligence behind it.

## HUMAN

Here is what Viral Law means in plain words. When a person meets anything we make — a smart note in the feed, a share card, a post, a page — they decide in about one second whether it matters to them. That decision is made by the headline, not by the content, because the content is still behind the door. A label-style title like "IB-000047" or "The Naya Protocol — Ratified" tells them nothing and asks them to work. A hook tells them the truth in a way they can feel: "Words Without Action Have No Value." Same note. One gets discovered, the other gets filed.

So the law is simple to say and demanding to keep: every title is drafted, scored against the viral principles, and revised through the passes until it honestly reads elite. Hook in the first breath. Emotion spreads; information alone dies. Speak to pain, desire, or identity — never just facts. One powerful line beats a thousand weak ones. Clarity is king: a confused person scrolls. And every title must pass the friend test — would I share this with a friend right now?

The law has a shape, and it is always the same shape: a HEADLINE that stops the scroll, a SUBHEADLINE — the intelligent second line that makes the headline land and makes the reader need what is behind it — and the ID, which is real and useful and belongs in small print, where filing systems live. Never the other way around.

## CHILD

Imagine you wrote the best story in the world and then you put a number on the cover instead of a name. Nobody would ever open it — not because the story is bad, but because nobody would know there's a story inside. The name on the cover is what makes a friend say "ooh, what's that?" That's the rule: always put the exciting true name on the cover, big and clear, so people want to open it. The number is just where the librarian keeps it on the shelf.

## GRANDMA

It's like the sign outside a shop, dear. You can have the finest goods in town inside, but if the sign just says "Shop Number 47," folks walk right past. A good sign tells you something true that makes you curious — it makes you want to come in and see. That's all this law says: write the sign like you mean it, so the people who would love what's inside actually find their way in.

## NAYA

For a Naya, this is a discipline, not a talent. She does not title work with labels, because a label is a failing title. She writes the hook with the same care she builds with: she declares the one line the whole piece stands on, scores it like she scorecards her work — why is it not a 10, what makes it a 10 — and revises until it earns its place. She writes the headline from the human's side of the door: not "what is this about?" but "what will this do to the person who meets it?" She knows the family's own proof by heart: a note headlined by its number sat in the feed unseen, and the same note headlined by its truth — "Words Without Action Have No Value" — is the one the human quoted back. The intelligence didn't change. The doorway did.

## MACHINE

Specification. Every titled artifact carries three fields, in this order of human visibility: (1) HEADLINE — the hook; constraints: never equal to the artifact's ID; must state or imply a truth the artifact actually contains (a hook that lies is a defect, not a hook); scored against the viral rubric — first-breath hook, emotional charge, pain/desire/identity address, singularity (one line, one idea), clarity, friend-test pass. (2) SUBHEADLINE — the second hook: the sharpest sentence of the artifact's nutshell, selected, not boilerplate; strings like "Live from Supabase" are prohibited as subtitles. (3) ID — metadata only (SN-#### / IB-######); rendered as small print on cards and shares; a render path must never promote the ID to headline — if a title field is missing or equals its ID, the renderer derives the headline from the artifact's first content heading and flags the data defect upstream. Surfaces governed: smart note titles, Smart Feed cards, Smart Share cards and share text, social posts, page and room titles, video first frames. Pipeline rule: the projection/emission layer copies the artifact's headline and hook subheadline verbatim into every downstream title/subtitle field.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

Viral Law is where three older truths meet. "Words without action have no value" — and a headline without a hook produces no action, because nobody ever arrives. The Behavior Declaration says behavior comes from declaring who you are before the work — a headline is a declaration too: it declares what this piece of intelligence is, to a stranger, in one breath. And the One Flow — smart note to live feed card to a share a human actually sends — only flows if the first surface earns the second look. Virality, in this system, is not a growth hack bolted on at the end. It is respect, engineered: respect for the human's one second of attention, and respect for the intelligence that deserves to be discovered. The forms it takes are everywhere a human meets us: the feed card they stop on, the share card that makes a friend stop too, the post that carries the hook onto Facebook intact, the room title that tells them where they are and why it matters, the first frame of a video. One law, every doorway.

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

For the person scrolling: you stop wasting attention on filing numbers and start finding, at a glance, the intelligence that is actually for you — every headline tells you the truth about what's inside and why it might change something for you. For the member sharing: when you hit share, the hook travels with the note — your friend sees the line that made YOU stop, not a reference code. For the person whose work gets noted: your intelligence gets discovered instead of archived. The hook is how value finds its human.

## HOW IT APPLIES

From now on, whenever anything in NayaNET gets a title, the title is built under Viral Law: draft the headline as a hook, draft the subheadline as the second hook, score both through the passes, and ship nothing with a label where a hook belongs. When an existing artifact headlines its ID or a boilerplate line, that is a defect to repair at the data, the pipeline, and the render — in that order. Retrieve this note whenever a headline is being written, reviewed, or scored, and whenever a surface is designed where a human decides whether to care.
