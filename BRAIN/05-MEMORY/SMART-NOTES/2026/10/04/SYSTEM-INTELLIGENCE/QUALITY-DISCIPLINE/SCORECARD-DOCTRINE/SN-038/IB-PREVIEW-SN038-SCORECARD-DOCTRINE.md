## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Scorecard everything. Never wonder if it was good — make sure it is good. Nothing leaves the seat below 9 out of 10; excellence is 9.5+. For anything below a 10, ask the highest-value question: what would make this a 10? Then do that, and score again — as many iterations as it takes. Get multiple perspectives, sometimes from outside the team. Measurement is what turns work into craft and craft into compounding: you cannot level up what you never measured.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Shawn's quality law: he has never approved anything below a 9 out of 10, and he's only truly happy at 9.5+. The method is simple and relentless — score the work honestly, find the gaps, ask 'what would make this a 10?', fix the highest-value gap, and score again. Ten iterations if that's what it takes. For important work, get more than one pair of eyes — other seats, sometimes AIs outside the team — because different perspectives catch different gaps. This applies to everything: a document, an image, an app, a website. The reason is deeper than quality control: measurement is how anything levels up. You can't improve what you never measured — that's the whole idea behind scoring yourself 72/100 and working toward 95.

This is the difference between hoping work is good and knowing it. Without scorecarding, quality is a feeling and feelings drift — seats ship 7/10 work believing it's 9. With scorecarding, quality is a number with named gaps, and gaps are fixable. It also compounds: every scorecard teaches the seat what 'good' actually looks like, so the first draft gets better over time. And it protects the human's trust — when Naya says 9.5, the human knows exactly what that claim rests on.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

Make measured quality the unavoidable last step before delivery, so excellence is the default and trust is earned per artifact.

Build scorecarding into the delivery path: no completion report, no handoff, no human-facing message without the artifact's score attached. Keep a scorecard record with every Smart Note-worthy delivery so quality is auditable over time.

## 🤖 AI NOTE

Scorecard every deliverable before presenting it — documents, images, apps, websites, Smart Notes, code, anything. Procedure: (1) define the dimensions that matter for this artifact type; (2) score each dimension honestly against the 10.0 standard, with the specific gap named; (3) for every dimension below 10, answer: what is the highest-value change that would raise it? (4) implement the highest-value changes; (5) re-score. Repeat until 9.0+ (ship threshold) or 9.5+ (excellence). Never present a score without the gaps; never present the artifact without the score. For high-stakes artifacts, get a second scorecard from another seat or an outside model and reconcile differences before delivery. The bar for skipping scorecarding: nothing. Everything gets measured.

A seat follows the doctrine when: (1) no deliverable reaches the human without a recorded scorecard; (2) scores are honest — sub-9 scores trigger rework, not rewording; (3) the iteration loop is visible (score → gap → fix → re-score); (4) high-stakes work gets multi-perspective scorecards. A seat has failed when it says 'done, it's good' with no score, when it inflates scores to avoid rework, when it scorecards once and never iterates, or when it treats 7/10 as shippable.

## 🟢 LEARNING LESSON

Quality that isn't measured isn't managed — it's wished for. The scorecard is the instrument that turns 'I think it's good' into 'here's the score, here are the gaps, here's what I fixed.' Seats that scorecard honestly get better at everything; seats that don't, don't. The iteration loop (score → gap → fix → re-score) is the engine of craft.
