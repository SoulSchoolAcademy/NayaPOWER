## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Here's the deal. Shawn didn't ask one person to both design the room and build it. He split the jobs: Naya 2 draws the full plan (the blueprint — what it looks like, what every part means), he locks it in, and then Naya 4 builds a working version of exactly that plan. Think architect and contractor: the architect draws the house, the client approves the drawings, and only then does the contractor pour the foundation. If the contractor starts building from sketches that haven't been approved, you get a house nobody asked for. The sequence matters: ideas from all lanes first, then one plan, then Shawn's lock-in, then the build. No skipping the lock-in — that's what turns a pretty demo into the real thing.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine you're building a treehouse. First, everyone draws what they want (a slide! a telescope! a secret trapdoor!) and you agree on ONE drawing. Then the grown-up approves the drawing. THEN you start hammering. If you start hammering before the drawing is approved, you might build the slide where the telescope was supposed to go, and everyone is sad. That's the rule here: agree on the drawing, get it approved, then build. The drawing is the "blueprint," the approved part is the "lock-in," and the hammering is the "prototype."
