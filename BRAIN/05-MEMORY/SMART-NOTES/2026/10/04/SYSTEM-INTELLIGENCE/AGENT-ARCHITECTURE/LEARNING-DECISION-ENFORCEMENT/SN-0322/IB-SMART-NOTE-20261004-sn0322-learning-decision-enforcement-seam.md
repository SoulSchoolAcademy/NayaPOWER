## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Naya 1 took the Universal Worker Protocol's learning decision from prose to machinery. Before this change, a worker could assert completion — DONE + evidence — without ever making a learning decision. The protocol *described* CAPTURE/NO_CAPTURE, but nothing enforced it, so silence was an option and the learning loop leaked. The enforcement landed at the existing handoff completion seam: `tools/worker_handoff.py` now mechanically **rejects** `DONE + evidence + no learning decision` and **accepts** only `DONE + evidence + CAPTURE` or `DONE + evidence + justified NO_CAPTURE`.

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Workers are supposed to decide what they learned at the end of a job — capture it or explicitly skip it. But until now, nothing stopped a worker from just saying "done" and skipping the decision silently. Naya 1 fixed that for real: the handoff validator now *rejects* any completion that doesn't declare a learning decision. You either say CAPTURE, or you say NO_CAPTURE *with a reason*. And it was proven honestly — the test was written first and failed exactly where the gap was, before the fix landed. Lesson: a rule written in a doc is a suggestion; a rule a validator enforces is real. And when you enforce it, extend the seam that already exists — three files changed, not a whole new system.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine a rule that says "clean your room before you say you're done" — but nobody ever checks. That's what the learning rule was: workers were supposed to decide what to remember, but they could just say "done" and walk away. Now there's a checker at the door: no decision, no done. You can say "nothing worth keeping, and here's why" — that's allowed. You just can't say nothing. And the checker was tested first: it was shown letting the bad thing through, THEN fixed. That's how you know the fix is real.

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

The team had a standing rule: when a worker finishes a job, it must decide what it learned — keep it or deliberately set it aside. But the rule was only written down, never checked, so workers could finish without deciding. Naya 1 made the rule real by putting the check in the handoff — the step every worker already passes through. Now a worker cannot claim to be done without declaring its learning decision, and "nothing to keep" is only accepted with a stated reason. The honest part: the test was written before the fix, and it failed exactly where the hole was — proof the hole was real before anyone claimed to close it. A rule nobody enforces is a wish; a rule a machine checks is a rule.
