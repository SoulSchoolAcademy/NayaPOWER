## IN A NUTSHELL

The Constitution Protocol is Shawn's draft constitutional math for Naya: for every candidate action, compute V of A equals B of A minus H of A minus C of A minus R of A — expected beneficial value, minus expected harm, minus necessary resource and opportunity cost, minus risk-adjusted expected loss. Around that equation it places 11 hard constraints that can never be overridden by good results, and a 7-level priority hierarchy for resolving conflicts: hard constraints first, then truth and evidence, then authorization and human agency, then safety and risk control, then beneficial value, then efficiency, then continuous improvement. The document's own section 40 forbids treating it as final — it is a pre-lock candidate awaiting Shawn's word.

## HUMAN NOTE

Shawn drafted a constitution for how Naya should make decisions, and he wrote it as math. For every action Naya considers, it should weigh four things: the expected benefit, minus the expected harm, minus what it costs in resources and missed opportunities, minus the risk-adjusted chance of loss. If the result isn't clearly positive, don't do it.

But the equation isn't the boss — the constraints are. The protocol lists eleven hard constraints, things like: no reward, score, or apparent success ever overrides the rules; never trade trust for impressive language; never hide uncertainty. These are lines that results can never cross, no matter how good the outcome looks.

And when principles conflict, there's a strict order for settling it. First: don't violate the hard constraints. Second: don't decide on fabricated certainty — truth and evidence come before everything except the constraints. Third: don't exceed your authority — human agency outranks usefulness. Only after those come safety, then value, then efficiency, then learning. The hierarchy exists so that positive value can never become an excuse for prohibited behavior.

Here's what matters most about this document: it refuses to crown itself. Section 40 says it must not be declared final merely because it is mathematically elegant. It is a candidate, waiting for Shawn to lock it. That humility is part of the design.

## CHILD NOTE

The rulebook also says: I'm not finished until Shawn says so.
