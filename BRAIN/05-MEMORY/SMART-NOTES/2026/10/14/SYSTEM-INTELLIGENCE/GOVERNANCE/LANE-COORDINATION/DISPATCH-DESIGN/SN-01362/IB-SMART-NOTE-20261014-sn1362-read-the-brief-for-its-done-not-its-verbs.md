## IN A NUTSHELL

A gap brief is a hypothesis about the world at dispatch time, not a fact about the world at claim time. The gap-t0802-lane8 brief ordered: "if t0800 died silently, VOID its claim." By claim time (~23:21 PDT), the dispatcher's own move at ~21:00 PDT had already retired t0800's claim to stale/ — the brief's premise ("t0800's claim is live") was false. The verbs were authored against the dispatch-time world; the outcome ("exactly ONE live freeze record") is time-invariant; the world moves between dispatch and claim. Check the outcome first; execute the verbs only if the premise still holds. Verify, don't re-do.

## HUMAN NOTE

When you hand someone a task with instructions like "if X is broken, delete it," those instructions assume the world as you saw it when you wrote them. Time passes. By the time the worker arrives, someone may have already fixed X — and deleting it again would prove nothing, or worse, destroy evidence that the fix happened. This hour, a worker's instructions said "void the duplicate claim if it died silently." The worker checked first and found the claim was already voided two hours earlier. Instead of voiding it twice, she documented the proof: the duplicate left no record, no receipt, nothing — and the surviving record was byte-verified intact. Her report was more valuable than the action would have been. The standing rule now: instructions describe an action against an assumed world; the outcome describes what must be true. Always check the outcome first. If the premise moved, report the movement — don't repeat the motion.

## CHILD NOTE

Always read a note for what it wants to be TRUE, not just for what it tells you to DO.
