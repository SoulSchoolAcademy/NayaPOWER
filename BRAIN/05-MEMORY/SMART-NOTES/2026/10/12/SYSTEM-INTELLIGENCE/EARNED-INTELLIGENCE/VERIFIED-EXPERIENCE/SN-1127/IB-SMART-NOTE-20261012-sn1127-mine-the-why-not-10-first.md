## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

When mining success patterns from receipts, read the "why not 10" sections first — not the accomplishments. A 9.5 receipt teaches through what it refuses to claim: the deduplication refused, the wiring refused, the verification not yet done. What a seat accomplished tells you what worked; what it declined to claim tells you where the edge of the evidence is — and that edge is what the next seat needs. Winmine order: "why not 10" first, evidence second, what-I-did third. And each pattern ships with a brief-embed proposal, because a pattern without its embed line is filing without learning.

## HUMAN NOTE

Success receipts have two halves: the claim half and the refusal half. The claim half is written to score well; the refusal half is written to stay honest. When you mine for repeatable patterns, the claim half is the noisy part — every receipt claims its work was diligent. The refusal half is the signal: it names exactly which victories the author would not take. The winmine found that the refusals cluster into transferable moves (resolution before reasoning — quote the resolution output per artifact; RED-first — prove the instrument can fail; deductions as contracts — every −0.5 names owner + re-verification command), and that each move could be tested against a lower-scoring receipt where it was absent, which is what kept the patterns from being circular ("excellent because excellent"). For any future pattern-mining run: start at the bottom of the receipt, not the top.

## CHILD NOTE

When someone tells you what they did great, listen — but then ask: "what part did you NOT say you did great?" The second answer is the better teacher. The honest things people refuse to brag about are the real map of where the hard parts are. Read the "not yet" part first.

## GRANDMA NOTE

Praise tells you what someone wants you to hear; the caveats tell you what's actually true. When you study how someone succeeded, don't start with the victory speech — start with the part where they say "here's what I didn't do, and why." That's where the real recipe is.

## NAYA NOTE

For my own work: whenever I mine receipts for patterns, review a seat's past work, or write my own mirror, the "why not 10" (honest gaps, refused claims, named holes) is the first section I read and the last I write. When I claim a pattern, I pair it with a sub-9.5 counterexample where the move was absent — a pattern that can't name its inverse is a slogan, not a pattern. And every pattern I hand off carries its embed line: the template slot and exact wording that put it in front of the next worker at dispatch time. A pattern without an embed line is filing without learning.
