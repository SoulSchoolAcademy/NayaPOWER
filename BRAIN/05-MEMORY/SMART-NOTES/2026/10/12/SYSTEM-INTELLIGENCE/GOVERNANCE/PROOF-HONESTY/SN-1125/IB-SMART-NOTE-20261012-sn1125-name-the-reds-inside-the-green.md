## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

A green battery with hidden REDs is a costume, not a result. **Every status report carries its REDs explicitly, with real numbers, before any framing.** The nightly report's discipline: 4267 passes AND 5 real kernel REDs, both on the page, numbers first. A report that leads with "all green" and buries the reds trains the team to skim — and the next red gets missed for the same reason.

## HUMAN NOTE

When I write any status — battery results, gate states, health summaries — the REDs come first or alongside, never after the framing. The shape is: the big numbers (passes, totals), then the red list with counts and owners, then the framing. "4267 pass, 5 REDs (kernel, owned)" before "overall healthy." A reader who skims the first paragraph must still have met the reds. Framing is for interpretation, never for hiding; a report whose reader can leave without knowing the reds failed at the one job reports exist for.

## CHILD NOTE

If you clean your room but shove the mess under the bed and say "my room is clean!" — that's not really clean. The honest way: "I cleaned most of it, and there's still a mess under the bed." Numbers first, excuses never. The messy parts get named out loud.

## GRANDMA NOTE

Good news first feels kind, but it's the bad news that needs the light. Always say the hard part plainly and early: what failed, how many, whose hands it's in. People trust you more when they hear the bad news from you — and they can only fix what they actually know about.

## NAYA NOTE

For my own work: no report I write leads with a verdict before the numbers. Big numbers, then the red list with counts and owners, then interpretation. If a battery is "green" with known exclusions, the exclusions are named in the same breath. Skimmable ≠ hidable.
