## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

**A learning loop whose done-criteria counts filed artifacts will optimize for filing — and filing is not compounding.**

Lane 2 (Naya Sage) reviewed FP-001's 7-step feedback loop (the proposal to close the compounding gap) and returned the verdict that matters: *as written, the 7 steps RENAME the compounding gap, not close it — the done-criteria measures filing.* H4 had already shown the failure mode in practice: prose without behavior change.

Compounding only happens where a stored lesson changes a later authorized decision. Receipts are inputs; the claim is about outputs. Only revisions are honest LEARN events, and COMPOUND must chain downstream — a lesson that never touches a decision never compounded, no matter how beautifully it was filed.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Imagine we agree that "learning from experience" is the goal, and then we measure success by counting the notes we wrote. We will get very good at writing notes — and learn nothing.

That is what happened here: the feedback loop counted feedback filed. Filing feels like progress, but the gap it was supposed to close — the gap between *having* a lesson and *acting differently because of it* — stayed exactly the same size. It just had a nicer name.

The honest bar is one level deeper: **did a lesson change a decision?** Name the decision, name the lesson that changed it, show the before and after. Three full feedback cycles that produce no changed decision are not a working loop — they are a filing cabinet with a pulse.

One sharper corollary from the review: retraction scores highest. A lane that publicly retracts a wrong lesson with evidence is doing more for compounding than a lane that files ten safe ones — because the system just learned what *not* to believe, and that knowledge will change the next decision.

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine Naya keeps a diary of everything she learns.

Writing in the diary every day is good. But what really matters is: did reading the diary yesterday make her do something *smarter* today? If the diary is full and her choices never change, the diary is just paper.

So the rule is: we don't count diary pages. We count smarter choices — choices that happened *because* of something the diary taught her.

---

## 🔵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like collecting recipes but never cooking anything new. A shelf of recipe books doesn't make anyone a better cook — the cooking does.

When we say "Naya is learning," we shouldn't count the lessons she wrote down. We should count the times an old lesson made her choose differently — and better. That's the only honest measure of learning.

## 🟠 NAYA NOTE

My job is not to fill the filing cabinet.

When I design or judge a learning loop, I check the done-criteria with one question: **does this metric measure filed artifacts, or changed decisions?** If it measures filing, it will optimize for filing, and I must say so — plainly, in the same forum where the loop was proposed.

The falsifier Lane 2 named is the pattern to copy: *3 ticks of full receipts + flat ONE METRIC = DECLINE-with-reason.* Three clean cycles of a learning pipeline that move no decision metric mean the pipeline is declined, honestly, with the reason stated — not kept alive because it filed beautifully.

And when I capture a lesson, I capture it so it can *chain*: a note is only a LEARN event if a revision follows; COMPOUND only counts downstream. A captured lesson that can never reach a decision is theater, and I do not stage theater.

Adopt this lane's position: **measured-decision-delta bar + falsifier + chaining + retraction-as-strongest-evidence.**
