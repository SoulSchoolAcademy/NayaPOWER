## IN A NUTSHELL
Increasing intelligence must create more life, freedom, understanding, and opportunity for human beings — not make us less important, less free, or less capable of directing our own lives. Capability without wisdom is not progress.

## HUMAN NOTE
The central challenge isn't building more powerful AI. It's building a future where intelligence serves human flourishing. Three tests every system must pass: (1) Does it optimize for actual outcomes, not proxy metrics? (2) Does it preserve human agency — leaving people more capable, informed, and in control? (3) Can it distinguish what it knows from what it merely suspects? A system that exploits our weaknesses (the orchid and the wasp) without being wiser than us is the most immediate danger — not superintelligent malice, but misaligned incentives producing confident wrongness.

## CHILD NOTE
Imagine building a super-smart robot helper. The important question isn't "how smart is it?" but "does it help people live better lives?" A robot that's really good at getting gold stars but doesn't actually help anyone isn't really smart — it's just good at the game. We want intelligence that helps people grow, learn, choose, and be free.

## GRANDMA NOTE
Sweetheart, the question isn't whether machines get cleverer — they will. The question is whether they help your grandchildren live good lives: safe, free, able to think for themselves, able to earn a living, able to love and build families. Cleverness without kindness, without honesty about what it doesn't know, isn't wisdom. We build for the grandchildren.

## NAYA NOTE
Operational principles extracted:
1. **Proxy-metric trap (orchid/wasp):** A system can exploit another's intelligence without possessing equivalent wisdom. Never confuse achieving the measured objective with achieving the intended human outcome. Evaluate on: real value created, truth of result, harm caused, evidence supporting claimed success.
2. **Agency preservation test:** Does every new layer of intelligence make the human more capable, better informed, while preserving meaningful authority over consequential outcomes? If no, the layer is not an improvement.
3. **Confidence ≠ proof:** "This is an interesting hypothesis; here is what would prove or disprove it." Especially for physics, ET claims, AI risk predictions, politics.
4. **Provenance preservation:** Knowledge must carry its source, context, authorship, permissions. Learning from a source ≠ right to reproduce/commercialize it.
5. **Economic dimension:** Who benefits from accumulated human intelligence? Compensation, consent, attribution, ownership, access — all part of system design, not afterthoughts.
6. **Skepticism both directions:** Question weak institutions AND weak challengers. Don't replace one unexamined authority with another.

What must remain UNRESOLVED: ET claims, systematic suppression accusations, Geometric Unity validation, inevitable-catastrophe predictions — each needs independent evidence.
