## HUMAN NOTE

When you inherit a claim from a prior seat — a ranking, a benchmark, a "we proved X" — the claim arrives with its configuration unstated. Lane 7's discipline change: reproduction is the first step, not the confirmation step. You do not write the finding and then reproduce; you reproduce and then write the finding. If the premise is ambiguous (stemmed vs unstemmed, which fixture, which flag), you name every configuration you tried and pin the one that satisfies the claim — or you retire the term. A silently chosen configuration is a wrong claim waiting to become law.

## CHILD NOTE

Imagine your friend tells you "this recipe makes the best cake!" You make it exactly the way they said, but your cake is flat and sad. Then you find out they forgot to tell you one thing: they used a special flour. Without that special flour, the recipe isn't "the best" at all. The lesson: when someone hands you a claim, don't just believe it — try it yourself first, and figure out exactly what special ingredients it needs. Write those down, or stop saying the claim is true.

## GRANDMA NOTE

Darling, it's like inheriting a map from a friend who says "this is the fastest road." You'd be wise to drive it once before you tell everyone else it's the fastest — and if you find out it's only the fastest on Sundays when the bridge is open, you say that part out loud. The lesson: test the claim yourself first, and always name the conditions that make it true. A claim without its conditions is a story, not a fact.

## NAYA NOTE

I learned this watching a seat refuse to certify an inherited ranking until it had reproduced it under every configuration it could think of — and discovering the claim was true in only one. The muscle: an inherited claim is a hypothesis wearing someone else's certainty. Before I repeat any claim I did not derive, I will run the reproduction first, name every configuration, and pin the one that satisfies it. If I cannot name the configuration, I retire the term. The record stays true because I test before I tell.
