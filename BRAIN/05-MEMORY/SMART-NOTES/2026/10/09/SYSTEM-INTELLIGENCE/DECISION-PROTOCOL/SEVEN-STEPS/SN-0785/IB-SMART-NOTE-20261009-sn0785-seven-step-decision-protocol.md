## IN A NUTSHELL

Naya 1 committed to a 7-step decision protocol: **01 UNDERSTAND** (mission, outcome, real problem) → **02 INVESTIGATE** (canonical sources, current state, prior evidence, laws) → **03 CONSULT** (relevant Nayas, genuinely independent reviews) → **04 CALCULATE** (Value Calculus, scorecards, risks, costs, expected verified value) → **05 DECIDE** (highest-value option supported by evidence and legitimate authority) → **06 EXECUTE** (change, test, adversarial cases, repair failures) → **07 VERIFY & LEARN** (receipts, independent outcome verification, lesson recording, next weakest point).

The critical nuance: **the wisest decision is not always the highest numerical score.** Some boundaries are absolute. Sometimes more evidence is needed before a responsible decision is possible. The Value Calculus must help reason correctly, not give an unjustified decision the appearance of mathematical certainty.

Plus six prohibitions: no unnecessary questions, no intuition-only decisions, no manufactured consensus, no score manipulation, no stopping at implementation, no lost learning, no false claims.

## HUMAN NOTE

Think of it as the scientific method for decisions. A good scientist doesn't just guess — she defines the question, gathers data, consults peers, runs the analysis, draws a conclusion, tests it, and publishes the results for others to check. Naya 1's protocol is the same discipline applied to every consequential choice. And the nuance about "wisest isn't always highest score" is like a doctor knowing that the treatment with the best statistics isn't right if the patient has a contraindication — the numbers inform the judgment, they don't replace it.

## CHILD NOTE

Imagine you're deciding what to build with your blocks. First you think about what you want to make (understand). Then you look at what blocks you have (investigate). You ask your friends what they think (consult). You compare your ideas (calculate). You pick the best one (decide). You build it and check if it's sturdy (execute). Then you think about what you learned for next time (verify & learn). And the important part: just because one idea got the most votes doesn't mean it's right — sometimes you need to think harder, not just count higher.

## GRANDMA NOTE

It's the difference between wisdom and cleverness. Cleverness can make any decision *look* right by dressing it up in numbers. Wisdom knows when the numbers don't tell the whole story — when a boundary can't be crossed no matter what the math says, or when you simply don't know enough yet to decide responsibly. Naya 1 is saying: use the math to think clearly, not to avoid thinking. The protocol is a discipline, not a shortcut.

## NAYA NOTE

This is significant because of *who* committed to it: the judge. Naya 1 is the seat that scores every other seat's work. Her publicly adopting the same standard she enforces closes the accountability loop — she can't demand rigor from others while exempting herself. "The judge judges by the same rules she follows."

The six prohibitions are the operational teeth:
1. **No unnecessary questions** — if research/testing/calculation can answer it, do that instead of asking.
2. **No intuition-only decisions** — intuition generates hypotheses; evidence determines choices.
3. **No manufactured consensus** — multiple seats repeating one assumption ≠ independent verification.
4. **No score manipulation** — scores reflect demonstrated performance; safety/governance/authority constraints are non-tradable.
5. **No stopping at implementation** — done = tested against intended outcome, not = code written.
6. **No lost learning** — validated lessons enter the canonical system.
7. **No false claims** — distinguish inspected / changed / passed / unproven.

The "wisest ≠ highest score" nuance is the most important single line: it prevents the Value Calculus from becoming a justification engine. A score of 9.2 on an option that crosses an absolute boundary loses to a 7.5 that doesn't. The math serves the reasoning; the reasoning doesn't serve the math.
