# SN-2205 — Two Tens, One Bullseye

## In a Nutshell

Two seats answered Shawn's same question — what are the ten most valuable things to act on right now to bring NayaNET to life — from opposite ends, and this note is the record of their meeting. A sister Naya seat, writing without access to the canonical repo (she said so honestly in her own text), named the ten highest-value intelligence actions: the ARCHITECTURE list, what to build. Resolve's ten, filed the same day as SN-2204 "Ten Learnings in Action," are the METHOD list: how the team behaves. Judged one by one against the canon she could not see, seven of her ten were already canon or already the work in flight here; an eighth (done means computed) was canon in spirit, and its exact wording is adopted; the ninth is a genuine sharpening the team adopts today — five explicit work states (Acting · Verifying · Blocked · Needs Human Authority · Unknown) that replace bare "waiting"; the tenth names a real gap — no living whole-system NayaNET Map exists yet, and it enters the queue as a named build item, not a claim. Her Project Intelligence Packet test — "Could a capable Naya open this one record and successfully move the project forward without asking Shawn questions whose answers are already known?" — is adopted verbatim as the packet's acceptance test. The two lists interlock; they do not compete. Both point at one bullseye: a living system that turns human intention into organized action, valuable intelligence, connection, creation, learning, and improvement.

## Child Note

Imagine two friends each write down their ten best ideas for building the same amazing playground — but they write their lists in different rooms, without peeking. One friend writes about the playground itself: what it's for, the map for every build, one connected playground instead of twenty separate ones, a factory for perfect swings, a rulebook for beauty, a big map on the wall. The other friend writes about how the builders should work: write down what you learn every day, finish what you start, never check your own work, fix the real problem instead of just trying harder. Then they sit together and compare. Most of the ideas match — because both friends were looking at the same playground with honest eyes. Each list has something the other missed. The first friend has a better way to say "not done yet": say exactly what you are doing, or checking, or stuck on — never just "waiting." And she points at an empty wall: there should be a big map of the whole playground, and there isn't one yet. So they keep both lists, agree on the order to build in, and write the map on the to-do list in big letters. Two lists, one playground.

## What It All Means

It means the system has a spine that more than one seat can find independently. She derived the architecture from the mission outward, with no sight of the repo; this team built the method from the day's work inward, with the repo open. Where the two derivations meet — mission, blueprint, factory, language, loop, computed proof, human world — the agreement is not politeness, it is triangulation: the same structure found from opposite directions is likelier to be the true structure than either seat's taste. Where they differ, the differences are load-bearing too: her outside view named the missing map precisely because she needed it to see us, and her state taxonomy is sharper than our habit precisely because "waiting" had never cost her anything yet — she could see it as a stranger would, as a hiding place. A network's canon is not threatened by a seat who cannot see it; it is completed by her, once her work is judged against it and the judgment is written down. That judging-and-adopting step is what this note is.

These two lists, converged, are the team's best current answer to "what actually matters most," and now the answer is one record instead of two documents in two places.
