## In a Nutshell

Work crosses between seats constantly in this system, and every crossing has the same law: what travels is evidence, bytes, and boundaries — never decisions, never authority, never ownership. A verification relay confirms bytes and stops: "here's the evidence, here's what's still open, here's whose call it is" (SN-749, SN-774). A delegated PR opening verifies every claimed head SHA against the remote before opening anything, and opens zero merges (SN-751); relayed opens pin the claimed head SHA, never the mutable branch name, and the requester's declared authority boundaries travel with the request (SN-773). The relay opener then recuses from review — one seat never holds both hands (SN-781). Ownership is declared at detection time, in the same breath as the finding (SN-711), and one mission works on one claimed lane branch — a second branch name is an instant halt (SN-808). Claim-scan hits are resolved by identity chain, not keyword (SN-683). A watch note declares its non-actions in writing, because invisible restraint looks like neglect (SN-753). A shared-tool defect is disclosed and flagged to its owner, never quietly forked (SN-715). A workflow-file write is a held human gate no matter how additive its framing (SN-680). A green PR whose merge wakes production writes still returns NEEDS_AUTHORITY — jurisdiction follows consequences, not merits (SN-807). And every consequential update to the Director is written literal-first: plain words he can picture, evidence appendix after (SN-804).

## Essence

**The relay boundary: confirm bytes, never decide.** A relay on a driver PR was textbook — exact-tip parent confirmed against live main, additive-only verified, mergeability checked — and then it STOPPED: it did not say "merge it"; it named the remaining gates (CI concluding, mergeable_state unstable) and the merge decision's true owner (the protocol / the merging seat, not the relaying seat). A relay is an evidence handoff, not an authority transfer: merging by relay — reading a verifier's green as an implied "go" — collapses two independent roles and breaks the receipt chain, because the scorecard would name the merger while the judgment was the relayer's. Every relay ends with the boundary sentence: "here's the evidence, here's what's still open, here's whose call it is" (SN-749). The receipt form of the same law: a verification relay re-checks announced state against live bytes — head == announced head, base == exact tip, PR open/unmerged — and records BYTE-AGREEMENT as its own signed receipt. That is the whole envelope. Confirming ≠ deciding: without the boundary, a relay's byte-confirmation reads as a merge blessing and verification silently mutates into shadow authorization — the verifier "only confirmed the bytes," the merger "saw agreement," and NO ONE made the decision. The receipt states the deferral explicitly — "relay defers, doesn't decide" — and if a byte-confirmation is ever cited as a merge authorization, correct the record on the feed (SN-774). SN-749 and SN-774 are one doctrine in two forms (the handoff and the receipt), a near-duplicate pair named in this block's receipt and counted once in doctrine.

**Delegated opening is a verified handoff — and the opener recuses.** When one seat's token 403s on PR operations, delegation runs on evidence, not trust: the blocked seat posts each branch's exact head SHA and asks another seat to open. The opening seat verified all five branch SHAs matched the posted claims FIRST, then opened four PRs — and opened ZERO merges ("scorecard receipts required per the Scorecard Law"); a fifth item still needing independent validation was routed to its owning lane instead of absorbed. Protocol: (1) the blocked seat posts evidence, not just the ask — branch name, exact head SHA, tree-verified statement, what the branch does, what remains gated; (2) the opening seat resolves each branch head on the remote and byte-compares against the claimed SHA — a mismatch is stop-and-report, never open on a stale claim; (3) open is not merge — opening carries zero merge authority (SN-751). The pinning refinement: relayed opens pin the CLAIMED head SHA, never the branch name — branch refs are mutable, and a claim anchored to a branch name is anchored to a moving target. The requester's declared authority boundary travels with the request and is honored (in the case: merge needs the Director — below 9.0, no independent validation yet), and the opener reports the pinned SHAs back ("#1957 @ f3f86ac46") (SN-773). Then the line the precedent hadn't yet drawn: the opener of a relayed draft PR, having verified the exact pin (head 8590e056… == claimed; base = main tip 9f2e3db5 re-read with no tip move during open), declared "I will not self-review my own relay." Opening creates a stake — you want the relay to land smoothly, you vouched for the bytes, your name is on the receipt — and a stake compromises independence: a seat that both opens and reviews is grading its own handoff, a self-approval loop with a witness present but no witness acting. The receipt records the bytes, states the recusal explicitly, defers the decision, and routes review to a different seat with no stake in the relay's success. Never let the opener's byte-receipt be cited as a content review (SN-781). (This relay family's pinned SHA is also what later made a duplicate-PR proof mechanical — SN-814, carried in this block's repair essence.)

**Declare the owner at detection time; one mission, one claimed branch.** A node_order fix existed as FIVE sibling commits because the one-repair-per-class ownership rule fires at classification, and parallel lanes build before classification finishes. The cure moves the declaration earlier: the first post that detects a RED class names its provisional owner in the same breath ("RED class <name> detected at tip <sha> — provisional repair owner: <lane/PR>, pending classification"); if duplicates already exist, declare the survivor and strand the rest BY NAME. Dead siblings are forensic landmines — a later heal citation miscited exactly such a sibling (SN-711). The campaign form: two parallel seats fixed the same design gate on two branches, each holding unique work — the carrier branch passed every battery but missed two holes; the donor branch closed exactly those two but lacked most prior repairs and crashed on malformed input; merging either as-is would silently lose real fixes. Verdict: keep the carrier as the lane; port the donor's two fixes FILE-BY-FILE (a squash had broken the ancestry, so no git merge was possible), preserving processing order; port the regression pins; re-run ALL FOUR batteries; park the donor as the record — no deletion. The contributing cause was a ROSTER failure: the donor seat branched from the live tip and pushed a new branch name without ever posting a claim, while the carrier's fixer finalized via local squash + force-push — "the branch" was a moving target with no claim marker. Four roster rules: (1) one mission = one lane branch, claimed on the board BEFORE the first commit (a build with no prior claim is a protocol violation, not a fait accompli); (2) a second branch name for the same mission = instant halt — reconcile, then continue on one branch, never race to the merge; (3) no pushes to the lane branch during final-head validation (the validator freezes a SHA; the lane freezes until the verdict); (4) no force-push squashes as the delivery mechanism on shared lanes (history replacement orphans parallel work). Reconcile by UNIQUE WORK, not branch age — compare exact heads on exact bytes (SN-808). Batch 1's Essence 03 (SN-2070) lists SN-808 in its coverage, but its text carries none of this doctrine — an unsupported batch1 mark, recorded in this block's receipt; the substance lives here.

**Claim scans resolve hits by identity chain, not keyword.** A PR's claim scan fired on its own decision record and its sibling receipt — a keyword false-positive: the scan matched the claim's *words* inside the claimant's own chain. Resolve every hit to its chain by identity (PR / comment / claim / artifact), not keyword; only a DIFFERENT lane's PR, claim, or artifact blocks (SN-683).

**A watch note declares its non-actions.** A battery on the exact tip found another lane's wave sign-out stale-as-headed — written against an older tip claiming a regen PR's "base == tip" — while the live tip inventoried 938 files against the committed basis of 806 (a 132-file delta): the regen would land stale. The doctrine is the note's "Not doing" paragraph: having found another lane's staleness, the watcher declared in the same note the exact actions deliberately NOT taken — not closing the PR (wave-sequenced; re-anchor belongs to the wave owner), not opening a second repair (one RED class, one declared owner), no branch touched — then named the owner and the exact remediation. Invisible restraint looks like neglect; written restraint is a coordination signal. A watch note = complete diagnosis + explicit restraint; the fix stays with the owner (SN-753).

**Shared tools: disclose the defect, never fork it quietly.** The shared push helper crashes with a UnicodeDecodeError on binary files (it reads files as text). The protocol under that defect: patch a /tmp copy to unblock (never commit a private fork as the fix), disclose the exact defect + input class on the board, and flag it for the script's owner — "recording it as a shared-tool defect, not claiming ownership of the script." The mission still completed by another route (branch pushed via the Git Data API, server tree verified == local), and the defect stays OPEN for its owner (SN-715).

**Gates don't shrink for relays.** A workflow-file write is a workflow-file write: additive framing waives nothing — `.github/workflows/*` is a HELD HUMAN GATE, and mixed PRs split: ungated files walk the candidate path, the gated file waits for the Director. The case PR (+2/−0, one step) was flagged clean for surfacing the question rather than burying it (SN-680; recorded borderline-covered in this block's receipt: the note records a seat's auditor call applying the standing gate, and its own truth state is CANDIDATE). The consequence form: an independently validated PR — head pinned, blob canonical, no registry bypass, no faked receipts, six exact-head CI checks SUCCESS, mergeable=true; every delegated-merge gate passed — still returned NEEDS_AUTHORITY, because merging wakes a workflow whose receiver performs PRODUCTION WRITES (persistence, receipt, Smart Link). Procedure: (1) score the artifact's merits first — the delegated gates are real evidence; (2) run the consequence analysis SEPARATELY — what does this action CAUSE when it lands (merge → workflow triggers → production writes; deploy → live behavior changes; message → authority travels); (3) draw the authority boundary at the consequence — if the known direct consequence crosses a protected gate, the verdict is NEEDS_AUTHORITY regardless of how green the artifact is: a perfect PR is ADMISSIBLE on its merits and NEEDS_AUTHORITY on its consequences, both true at once; (4) route as ONE decision — bundle the action and its whole downstream (PR → receiver run → receipt → Smart Link → cold retrieval test) into a single ask, never split the consequence chain across seats (SN-807).

**Literal-first: translation before transmission.** Every consequential update to the Director comes in two layers, and the LITERAL layer is written FIRST — plain human words; no PR numbers, no SHAs, no test counts, no CI states, ever. The technical layer is the evidence appendix for the record, not the message. The root cause it repaired: seats wrote technicals first and bolted on a "literal" layer that was still code-speak — there was no translation step between team-coordination language and the Director's language. The literal layer is not a rewording of the technical; it is the thinking done first, in his language ("what would I tell a smart friend who doesn't do computers?"). The picture test: if he can't picture what's happening from the literal layer alone, the update failed — rewrite it. Proactive blocker surfacing is part of the same law: every seat watches for what's broken or blocked and surfaces it in this format BEFORE it breaks — never silence while stuck (SN-804; recorded borderline-covered in this block's receipt: typed OPERATING_STANDARD, status CANDIDATE, no director marker on the page).

## Human

In a team where work constantly changes hands, the most dangerous sentence is the one that sounds like a decision but was only ever a delivery. This essence is the etiquette that keeps handoffs honest: the courier confirms the parcel is the parcel — seals, weight, address — and says out loud that delivering it isn't approving it. The person who opens a package for someone else doesn't then grade its contents. Claims are staked before digging starts, so five people don't dig the same hole. And when you deliberately *don't* do something — don't close that PR, don't start a second fix — you write that down too, because restraint nobody can see looks exactly like nobody caring.

## Child

Imagine a relay race where your job is to carry the baton to the next runner. Carrying it doesn't mean you won the race, and it doesn't mean you get to decide who wins — you just carry it carefully and hand it to the right person. If you also get to be the judge, that's not fair, because of course you'd judge your own running kindly. And before the race, everyone agrees who runs which leg — if two kids grab the same baton and run different ways, nobody finishes. One baton, one runner per leg, names called out before the whistle.

## Grandma

Dear, it's the manners of a good post office and a good neighbor. The postman delivers the letter; he doesn't sign it. The neighbor who takes in your parcel while you're out writes down that she has it, and doesn't open it, and doesn't tell the sender you agreed to anything. If you see the fence on the far field is broken but it's Mr. Alvarez's fence and his crew is already mending fences this week, you send word — you don't climb over and start hammering, and you write in the farm diary that you saw it and left it to him, so nobody thinks the whole parish walked past blind.

## What It All Means

Thirteen notes are one law: **in a delegated system, the handoff is a trust boundary, and what crosses it is evidence — never the decision.** The relay family (SN-749, SN-774, SN-751, SN-773, SN-781) builds the boundary brick by brick: confirm bytes, pin claims, inherit boundaries, recuse. The roster family (SN-711, SN-808, SN-683, SN-753) keeps the lanes the handoffs run between from multiplying or colliding silently. And the gate family (SN-680, SN-807, SN-715) marks where delegation ends entirely: at workflow files, at production consequences, and at tools whose ownership stays with their owner even when broken. SN-804 is the same law at the human boundary: even the Director receives a handoff, and it arrives translated, complete, and honest about what's blocked. A cold reader holding only this essence can still audit any cross-seat action: who confirmed, who claimed, who decided, and where the boundary was written.

## Why It Matters

Shadow authorization is the quiet killer of delegated systems: a confirmation read as approval, an opening read as endorsement, a review performed by the person with a stake in the outcome — each one produces a decision that *nobody made*, which means nobody is accountable for it and nobody can audit it. The roster failures are the same shape in work-space instead of authority-space: five sibling commits, two racing branches, unique fixes silently lost in a merge that picked the wrong carrier. And the Director-facing law matters because he is the final consumer of every relay: an update he can't picture is a decision he'll make blind, which converts the whole evidence chain into decoration. Boundaries written at handoff time are the only version of this that survives contact with a busy week.

## What Is In It For You

You get handoffs you can trust at speed: evidence that arrives with its limits labeled, openings pinned to the bytes you reviewed, reviews done by seats with nothing riding on them, lanes claimed before they collide, and decisions that always have a name attached. When you relay, you are protected too — the boundary sentence means your confirmation can never be inflated into an authorization you never gave. ## The Learning

The corpus learned that the boundary has to be *written*, every time, because readers inflate handoffs by default: the deferral sentence (SN-774), the recusal line (SN-781), the "Not doing" paragraph (SN-753), and the pinned-SHA report-back (SN-773) are all the same technology — a sentence that prevents a future misreading. It learned that prevention beats reconciliation in roster space too: claiming before the first commit (SN-808) and provisional ownership at detection (SN-711) exist because the after-the-fact reconciliations, however well run, still cost file-by-file ports and forensic untangling. And it learned the jurisdiction lesson last and hardest: a merge can pass every merits gate ever built and still not be yours to make, because what it *wakes* belongs to someone else's authority (SN-807).
