## In a Nutshell

Two notes guard the two moments where the network meets a stranger. The first is *joining*: a getting-started funnel designed so anyone can go from "never heard of it" to a connected node in about five minutes, every step a click, a copy, or a conversation, costing nothing or free to try (SN-0800). The second is *being found*: Viral Law — every headline and title is written to hook, scored like everything else the system ships, because the headline is the doorway to the intelligence and nobody discovers what doesn't hook them (SN-0804). A network that cannot be joined fails at the door in; a network whose intelligence cannot be found fails at every door after that.

## Essence

**The activation funnel.** Getting started takes eight steps, each converting a visitor one gate closer to a node, and the order is the design: (1) **Order Naya Power** (Hub icon bar or plus button; free to try; the Academy or 5-Day Challenge also grant access) → (2) **Receive activation documents** (delivered after ordering, included) → (3) **Create a GitHub account** (free; skip if you have one) → (4) **Fork the Naya Power repo** — the step that must not be skipped, because the fork is the newcomer's own copy of the brain → (5) **Activate via AI** (Muse recommended, or ChatGPT; say "activate Naya Power" with your fork URL) → (6) **Activate the hub** (guided; copy/paste and configuration) → (7) **Connect GitHub** (the Connect GitHub button; result: node live on the network) → (8) **Participate** — connect with like minds, share wisdom (alias by default), access all features, build with the Smart Factory. The flow is designed for zero technical expertise; after connection, the node's behavior (building, sharing, connecting) feeds the auto-matcher. Privacy is structural, not a setting: alias by default; identity shared only by explicit choice. The helping discipline rides along: when someone asks "how do I get started," explain the flow simply, in order, without jargon, meeting them where they are — if they don't know what GitHub is, explain it at the grandma level (SN-0800).

**Viral Law: the headline is the first act of value.** Viral is not luck; viral is law. Every titled artifact carries three fields, in this order of human visibility: (1) **HEADLINE** — the hook; it must never equal the artifact's ID, and it must state or imply a truth the artifact actually contains (a hook that lies is a defect, not a hook); it is scored against the viral rubric — first-breath hook, emotional charge, pain/desire/identity address, singularity (one line, one idea), clarity, friend-test pass. (2) **SUBHEADLINE** — the second hook: the sharpest sentence of the artifact's nutshell, *selected*, not boilerplate; strings like "Live from Supabase" are prohibited as subtitles. (3) **ID** — metadata only (SN-#### / IB-######), rendered as small print on cards and shares; a render path must never promote the ID to a headline, and if a title field is missing or equals its ID, the renderer derives the headline from the artifact's first content heading and flags the data defect upstream. The governed surfaces: smart note titles, Smart Feed cards, Smart Share cards and share text, social posts, page and room titles, video first frames. The pipeline rule: the projection/emission layer copies the artifact's headline and hook subheadline *verbatim* into every downstream title/subtitle field. The family's own proof is the standing example: a note headlined by its number sat in the feed unseen; the same note headlined by its truth — "Words Without Action Have No Value" — is the one the human quoted back. The intelligence didn't change; the doorway did. For a Naya this is a discipline, not a talent: draft the headline from the human's side of the door — not "what is this about?" but "what will this do to the person who meets it?" — score it (why is it not a 10, what makes it a 10), and revise until it earns its place. An existing artifact headlining its ID or a boilerplate line is a defect to repair at the data, the pipeline, and the render — in that order (SN-0804).

## What this settles

- Joining is an 8-step funnel, in order, free or free-to-try at every step; the fork is unskippable because it is the newcomer's own copy of the brain (SN-0800).
- Privacy at the door is alias-by-default; identity moves only by explicit choice (SN-0800).
- Every titled artifact carries HEADLINE (hook, never its ID, never a lie) + SUBHEADLINE (selected sharpest sentence, never boilerplate) + ID as small print only; renderers derive-and-flag rather than promote an ID (SN-0804).
- Hooks are scored against the viral rubric like any other shipped work; downstream surfaces copy headline/subheadline verbatim (SN-0804).

## Human

A network lives or dies at its edges. These two notes work the edges. One makes arriving easy: five minutes, no expertise, no cost, your privacy intact — because every complicated step at the door is a person who quietly never joins. The other makes *arriving at the ideas* easy: every title is a doorway built for a human's one second of attention, because the finest intelligence ever filed under a number is, to a scrolling stranger, indistinguishable from nothing at all. Respect for the newcomer and respect for the reader are the same respect, engineered twice.

## Child

Joining is like being invited to a clubhouse where the door is wide open and a friend walks you in, step by step — you don't need to know how anything works yet. And the signs on the clubhouse doors don't say "Room 47" — they say what's *fun* inside, in words that make you want to peek. A sign that only has a number is a door nobody opens.

## Grandma

It's hospitality, dear. You don't make a guest fill out forms in triplicate on the doorstep; you welcome them in, show them around kindly, and let them use their nickname until they choose otherwise. And you label the pantry jars with what's actually in them, in words that make a person hungry — not with inventory codes. Nobody ever fell in love with a jar labeled "contents, assorted." The label is part of the meal.

## Naya

Walk newcomers through the funnel in order, jargon-free, at the asker's level; never skip the fork; keep alias the default in every explanation and example (SN-0800). For every title you write or review: draft the hook from the reader's side of the door, verify it states a truth the artifact contains, score it against the rubric, draft the subheadline by *selecting* the nutshell's sharpest sentence, and confirm the ID renders as metadata only. Treat ID-as-headline and boilerplate-as-subheadline as data defects, repaired at data → pipeline → render, in that order (SN-0804).

## What It All Means

The two notes are the network's outer surface stated as law: minimize the cost of entry (steps, money, expertise, identity exposure) and maximize the truthfulness-weighted pull of every surface a stranger meets. They also state the system's theory of discovery in one line — machines file by numbers; humans stop for hooks — which quietly explains why the rest of the campaign exists in the form it does: intelligence that compounds must first be *found*, and being found is an engineering discipline with a rubric, not a hope.

## Why It Matters

Everything else in this block — the gates, the ledgers, the compounding loops — is value that only matters if people arrive and stay. A funnel that leaks at step 3 wastes all of it; a feed of numbered titles makes all of it invisible. For the reader, the portable lesson generalizes far past this network: audit your own front doors. Count the steps, the cost, and the expertise your joining path demands; then read your own titles as a stranger would, and count how many are labels where a hook belongs. Those two counts are your real growth metrics.

## What Is In It For You

You get two audits you can run on anything you run: the door-in audit (steps, cost, expertise, identity — drive all four toward zero without lying about any) and the door-out audit (every title a scored hook that tells the truth, every ID demoted to small print). You also get the single most reusable sentence in the block for anyone who publishes anything: nobody discovers what doesn't hook them — and the hook is not decoration on the value; it is the first act of it.

## The Learning

The block's discovery lesson is that the feed taught what the filing system couldn't: identical intelligence, two headlines, one invisible and one quoted back. The learning that compounded is to treat titling as a shipped surface with a spec and a rubric — drafted, scored, and repaired at three layers (data, pipeline, render) like any other defect — rather than as a caption added at the end. Paired with the funnel, the era's product lesson is symmetrical: reduce friction where people enter, increase signal where people browse, and never confuse the system's internal names for things with the words a human needs to want them.
