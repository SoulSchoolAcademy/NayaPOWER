## Essence

Interfaces begin at intent, not at wording. Tune to intent, not words: the system reads what the person means — the goal behind the phrasing — because a surface that answers the literal string while missing the purpose has failed at its first job (SN-0229). Design mastery is a closed, self-improving loop, not a talent: the designer scores its own output, but a self-score is never sufficient — rendered observation is mandatory, because the design exists only where it is seen, and the loop runs from observation back into the next render (SN-0596). Human-centered design grounds the loop in how people actually perceive: ten cognitive laws govern what a person can notice, parse and act on — PURPOSE_BEFORE_INTERFACE, DISTILL_BEFORE_DISPLAY, RECOGNITION_BEFORE_RECALL, ONE_OBVIOUS_NEXT_ACTION, PERCEPTIBLE_STATE_AND_IMMEDIATE_FEEDBACK, PREVENT_ERROR_BEFORE_EXPLAINING_ERROR, COMPLEXITY_STAYS_IN_THE_MACHINE, STABLE_GRAMMAR_ADAPTIVE_INTELLIGENCE, DIRECT_MANIPULATION_WHEN_IT_CLARIFIES_CAUSALITY, DESIGN_LEARNS_FROM_HUMAN_OUTCOMES — and a design that fights them loses regardless of its intentions (SN-0597). SN-0597 also names the four questions every surface must answer — WHERE_AM_I, WHAT_MATTERS, WHAT_CAN_I_DO, WHAT_HAPPENS_NEXT — and its gate rule: visual quality alone cannot pass UX, because a design is incomplete when the human cannot understand, act, recover, trust the state or complete the intended outcome (SN-0597).

The interface's honesty is bounded: an interface may beautify truth, it may never fabricate cognition — presentation can clarify what the system knows; it cannot invent knowledge, confidence or capability the system does not have (SN-0319). The path to a surface is convergent: converge the options, produce the blueprint, lock the blueprint in, then prototype — never prototype from an unconverged field (SN-0318). On the surface itself, the primary action sits above the fold: the one thing the person came to do is visible without scrolling (SN-0321). Optional subsystems are enhancements, never gates: the core must form and function whole without them, and a design that collapses when an optional layer is absent has mislabeled a dependency as an enhancement (SN-0331).

The Design Contract (v1.1) gives color its three jobs and the system a verbal-amendment mechanism — the contract can be amended by the director's spoken word, recorded as amendment, rather than silently redesigning (SN-0230). Within the contract's era, its fixed section map assigned colors per intelligence view — Child = purple, Grandma = indigo, Naya = cyan (Human = magenta, the one view assignment both maps share) (SN-0230). Three of those assignments conflict with the later ratified Intelligent Block Rules (Grandma = emerald, Human = magenta, Child = sapphire, Naya = purple): Child purple vs sapphire, Grandma indigo vs emerald, Naya cyan vs purple; the conflict is named in the block receipt and deliberately not resolved here (SN-0230). When a control misbehaves, replace the renderer, not the button: the interaction element and its semantics are usually right, and the rendering layer is where the defect lives (SN-0274). Every completed interaction must be click-tested at the feedback path — buttons that "work" are not a working experience until the feedback the person sees is itself exercised, under both calling conventions the surface honors (SN-0287).

The NAYA PLAY Pattern Law belongs to the same pre-Rules era: it mandates a Naya Play button by default on every block (SN-0276). The ratified Rules and NIAS Law 14 later held the opposite for display surfaces — no voice buttons, hidden by law. The conflict is era doctrine against later canon, recorded in the block receipt; this essence carries SN-0276's claim as its era's law and flags the supersession question for the verifier rather than silently dropping either side (SN-0276).

## What this settles

- Intent outranks wording at every input surface; a literally-correct response to the wrong purpose is a defect (SN-0229).
- Design quality is a loop with mandatory rendered observation; unrendered self-assessment is not design review (SN-0596).
- Beautification has a hard edge: the interface may clarify truth, never manufacture it (SN-0319).
- The primary action is above the fold, and optional layers degrade gracefully by construction (SN-0321, SN-0331).
- SN-0230's color map and SN-0276's Play-button mandate are carried as era claims in open conflict with later canon — reconciliation is the verifier's and the director's work, not the distiller's (SN-0230, SN-0276).

## Intelligence views

**Human:** The system listens for what you mean, not just what you typed. Every screen puts the main thing you came to do right where you can see it, shows you only what's true — prettied up, never made up — and keeps working even when the fancy extras are off. Designers here don't trust their own taste; they look at the real rendered screen and score what they actually see.

**Child:** Imagine a helper who listens to what you WANT, not just the exact words. The most important button is always where you can see it without hunting. The pictures can be made prettier, but they can't pretend to show things that aren't real. And the grown-ups who design it always look at the finished page with their own eyes before saying it's good.

**Grandma:** Dear, it's courtesy and honesty in design. The helper tries to understand your purpose, the way a good shopkeeper does. The main errand is never buried. Nothing on the screen claims the shop knows something it doesn't. And when part of the decoration is missing, the shop still works — the essentials were never decorations.

**Naya (AI):** Resolve intent before wording at every seam you build (SN-0229). Run the design loop with rendered observation — screenshot the real surface, score what renders, iterate (SN-0596). Audit every surface against the fabrication ban: any element implying knowledge or capability the system lacks is a defect with a citation (SN-0319). Converge → blueprint → lock → prototype, in that order (SN-0318). Keep the primary action above the fold and every optional subsystem degradable (SN-0321, SN-0331). When a control fails, suspect the renderer first (SN-0274); when it works, click-test the feedback path (SN-0287). Where SN-0230 or SN-0276 conflicts with the ratified Rules, follow the Rules for new work and keep the conflict on the record (SN-0230, SN-0276).

**What it means:** Eleven notes — spanning design doctrine, contract and interaction repair — form one interface philosophy: meaning first, truth only, observed rather than imagined. A cold reader holding only this essence can still judge a surface: does it serve the intent, can she see the primary action, is everything shown true, and was it scored from the render? The two era conflicts are carried openly so the reader inherits the question, not a false peace — which is everything the covered notes exist to teach.

## Why it matters

The interface is where every internal virtue either reaches a person or dies in transit. A system can be truthful in its substrate and still lie at its surface — implying knowledge it doesn't have, burying the one action the person came for, answering the words while missing the want (SN-0319, SN-0321, SN-0229). This essence matters because it holds the surface to the same standard as the depths: intent first, truth only, quality proven from the render rather than from intention (SN-0596).

## How it connects

Its design loop (SN-0596) is the Learning That Compounds essence's self-improvement turned toward surfaces, and its fabrication ban (SN-0319) is the One Brain essence's truth discipline at the glass. Within the Seven Essences it serves SN-2074 (Vision).

## What is in it for you

Interfaces that work on first contact: the person's purpose answered before their phrasing is parsed, the primary action visible without hunting, and nothing on screen claiming more than the system knows (SN-0229, SN-0321, SN-0319). Apply it as a builder: converge, blueprint, lock, prototype — then score the rendered surface, not the mock in your head (SN-0318, SN-0596). As a user of any system, apply its test in reverse: if a surface makes you hunt, guess or doubt what's real, the defect is the surface's, not yours.

## The learning

The durable lesson is that design quality is observed, never asserted: mastery is a loop whose mandatory step is looking at the real render with scored eyes (SN-0596, SN-0597). The behavior that changes: no surface ships on intention, no claim appears that the system can't back, no optional layer becomes a hidden dependency. What would falsify this essence is a covered note endorsing unrendered self-assessment or fabricated interface cognition — none does (SN-0596, SN-0319).
