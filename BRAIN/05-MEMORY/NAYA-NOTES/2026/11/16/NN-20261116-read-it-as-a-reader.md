## Why it matters

Simple is the most dangerous kind of wrong. The letter job sounded simple — just add the images — so simple that nobody checked it, not even him: he was so confident it was done right that he didn't look until today, and when he did, it was all messed up. And the checks made it worse, not better — they PASSED it. 86 beats present, in order, scroll works: every part ticked, and the reading experience was still broken. A big stack of text and pictures with no unfold, off to the left, uncentered — the parts were all there and the whole was unreadable. Counts, scroll, and buttons are mechanical checks, and mechanical checks cannot prove an experience. The only check that proves a reading experience is reading: the eye must READ it as a reader, top to bottom, the way he reads it — scrolling down, reading as it comes — and call it broken when it doesn't present well, even if every part ticks. The checker in the letter round now carries that as the gate: read the letter top to bottom as a reader before it's called done, not just count the beats. Filed as law in AGENTS.md the same pass.

## How to use it

Verify anything a human reads by reading it as a reader, top to bottom, the way he reads it — not by counts alone. Simple jobs get the reader's eye precisely because they seem too simple to need it. A page that doesn't present well is called broken even if every part ticks. The letter this corrects is SN-2980; the sibling law is SN-2981 (use what works, never make it new).
