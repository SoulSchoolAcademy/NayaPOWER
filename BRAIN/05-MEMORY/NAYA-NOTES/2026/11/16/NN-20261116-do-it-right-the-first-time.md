## Why it matters

A summary is not a step toward done — it is a redo he has to do himself. Eight or nine summaries mean he has to go put the right stuff in eight or nine times: the machine did the easy shape of the work and handed him the hard part — the actual words, the actual decisions — eight or nine times over. A prototype is the same debt in a different shape: something that looks like progress and isn't, because it was never meant to be the thing. We are professionals, and professionalism is not a tone, it is a standard: go all in, do things right, ready to go, completed and done. The legal pages are the proof of the cost — round 1 built summaries (58 boards) and called it the pages, and now the writer has to draft all eight properly (74 sections, ~6,600 words), he has to give the text on the four rulings only he can make, and the builder has to create the pages extraordinary — the whole job, again, because the first pass was a summary instead of the work. Filed as law in AGENTS.md the same pass.

## How to use it

No summaries, no prototypes: work arrives complete, ready to go, done. A draft that needs his words says so, and the parts only he can rule on are named, not hidden. Every round is judged by the redo test: if he has to put the right stuff in again, the round didn't do it right the first time.
