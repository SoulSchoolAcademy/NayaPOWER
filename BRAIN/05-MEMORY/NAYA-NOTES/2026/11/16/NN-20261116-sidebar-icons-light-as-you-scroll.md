## What it is

Shawn, 2026-11-16 18:56Z — SIDEBAR ICONS LIGHT AS YOU SCROLL (instruction, live walk, filed in MEMORY.md the same pass): every sidebar item gets its own icon, and as you scroll down through each area its icon lights up in its own different color. The sidebar is the map of the page: each area has its own icon, and the icon tells you where you are — lit, in that area's own color, while you are in it. The nine area icons that came off the Hub's bottom bar go in the sidebars in logical order, each carrying its area's count.

## Why it matters

A sidebar without icons is a list of words; a sidebar with icons that never light is a map that never says where you are. Icons that light in their own colors let the eye know in one glance — the lit icon is recognizable without reading. The instruction settles where the nine area icons belong — not on the bottom bar, where they crowded the five, but in the sidebars in logical order, each with its count, lighting as you scroll through its area. A static list of words is decoration; a lighting map is orientation.

## How to use it

Every sidebar item gets its own distinct icon. As you scroll through an area, its icon lights up in that area's own color. The nine area icons live in the sidebars in logical order, each carrying its area's count; the bottom bar keeps the five. A sidebar is verified by scrolling: the lit icon must track the area you are in, at every width. Sibling rule, same sitting: One Icon That Lights Up.
