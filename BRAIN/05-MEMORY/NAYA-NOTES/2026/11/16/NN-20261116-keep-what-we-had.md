## What it is

I wanted you to keep what we had — what we had was beautiful. I just wanted you to add images. His diagnosis of every redo: "whenever you redo something everything's all flat and crappy and shitty and unelevated and boring and terrible text — you had the text and the buttons and everything nailed and then you go and put the images with the terrible text and the flat buttons… instead of putting the images with the good buttons and the good text." STOP-BUILD ORDER, same pass: "We're not going to have you build anything." His direction for any future work, when and if he re-authorizes it: "Go back to what we were doing and just add the images in and make it look extraordinary. Like they were meant to be there." The 21:40Z build order ("Hello, I want you to build it. I just want you to build it right.") supersedes the stop-build FOR THE DECK PRESENTATION ONLY. Proof the rule works: the add-images-only round PASSED 21:46Z — 8 hunks, 0 deletions, 0 text/button/layout changes, 7 pictures in, nothing else moved.

## Why it matters

A rebuild is a bet that the new thing will be better than the old thing. When the old thing is already nailed, the bet is irrational: the upside is marginal and the downside is losing the thing he loved. A coordinator PASS (66/66 beats, 20 pictures byte-identical) that Shawn rejected outright proves it — a pass on the wrong thing is not a pass. When trust is spent, the only way to earn it back is a round that proves, by diff, that nothing else moved.

## How to use it

No round rebuilds a page he called beautiful. The brief says ADD, and the diff must prove only additions — 0 deletions, 0 text or button changes. A stop-build order is obeyed literally until he re-authorizes it, for that presentation, in his words. The 21:46Z add-images round is the template.
