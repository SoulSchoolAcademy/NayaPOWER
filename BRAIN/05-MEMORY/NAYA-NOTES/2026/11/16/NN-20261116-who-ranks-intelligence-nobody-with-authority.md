# Who Ranks Intelligence? Nobody With Authority

## What it is

Nobody with authority determines what intelligence is, or at what level an AI sits. Research groups, benchmark creators, laboratories and universities measure what they can measure — mathematics, programming, language, tool use — and what they measure is capacity, not intelligence. A model can solve hard problems and still misunderstand a plain human instruction, or fail to see that the software should not be deployed. No single benchmark establishes ultimate intelligence. The scorecard that matters: understand the real problem; recognize what matters; distinguish truth from uncertainty; respect life and human authority; choose efficient action; deliver useful results; verify outcomes independently; learn from mistakes; remember and transfer the learning. The measure: did the intelligence make reality better, safely and verifiably?

## Why it matters

Rankings decide what gets built and what gets funded — and a ranking that mistakes capacity for intelligence sends the whole field chasing power. The scorecard above keeps the judgment where it belongs: on what the intelligence did, in the real world, for real people, with proof.

## How to use it

Never cite a benchmark rank as intelligence — cite the scorecard. Every evaluation names what was verified and what remains uncertain. A system that cannot verify its own outcome has not demonstrated intelligence, whatever it scored.
