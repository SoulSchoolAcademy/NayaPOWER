## What it is

Shawn, 2026-11-16 ~16:1xZ, dictation, from the visual-agreement end-buttons discussion — THE BACK-BUTTON LAW. His words, verbatim: **"a system always has like you have a back button for most of the pages so you can always just go back and forth."** The back button is elegant, beautiful and clean, and it does not block anything — so a user can always go back and forth simply. The case that named it: after tapping one of the two end buttons on the visual agreement, the citizen must be able to go back and try the other one. The captain folded it into the combined fast-action round the same pass (bigger/clearer deck-end buttons + back button on every view + no show-all for the visual) and updated the One List walk-end-share-plus item to the combined scope.

## Why it matters

A page with no way back is a door that only opens one way: the citizen taps the wrong end button, or wants to compare the two, and is stuck. Going back and forth simply is not a convenience — it is the system working. The back button is the citizen's freedom to change their mind, and it costs nothing: elegant, beautiful, clean, blocking nothing.

## How to use it

Every page — all pages, or most pages — carries a back button: elegant, beautiful, clean, blocking nothing. After either end button on the visual agreement, the citizen can go back and try the other one. Going back and forth is always simple.
