## Why it matters

The mistake is not a one-off, it is a pattern — "you make this mistake all the time" — and the pattern has a shape: instead of using what works, I try to make it new. The new thing is always bigger than the job: a big long thing that's not really useful, a big mess that doesn't function, a massive amount of work that makes a mess of it. The letter is the proof: the unfolding already existed in the deck, beautiful and working, and all the job needed was the pictures put in where they make sense — done. Instead the format was rebuilt from scratch, made to look like the other one, and what came out was a big page of unformatted text and images, not great presented, not even an unfold anymore. The law ends the pattern mechanically: before any seat builds anything, it names the existing working thing it is reusing — file, blob, bytes — and takes it verbatim. If there is a working version, there is no new version. A seat that rebuilds what already works has failed before it starts, and the captain's sense check catches it: "did I use what works, or did I make it new?" Filed as law in AGENTS.md the same pass.
