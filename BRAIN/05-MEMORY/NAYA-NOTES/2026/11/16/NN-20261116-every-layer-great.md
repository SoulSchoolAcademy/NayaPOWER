## Why it matters

Some jobs have layers, and one layer good while the others aren't never works. That is the whole law, and the legal pages are the job that proved it: the content layer was summaries, the presentation layer was left-hung plain text — one layer bad and the other bad too, and the whole thing read as a scam. But the deeper truth is that a single good layer can't carry the rest: a beautiful presentation over one-sentence summaries is still not a legal agreement, and airtight drafting in plain unformatted text still reads as not legit to a stranger's eye. The project is one ultimate project, not a stack of separate passes that each get to be "good enough" on their own: the WHOLE thing has to be good — not only good but GREAT — the most elite, ten-star, professional, pro, premium, top-of-the-line, all-star presentation AND outputs AND design AND intelligence put into it. Every layer, or the project isn't done. That is the verdict a round is judged by: not "did my layer pass" but "is the whole thing great in every layer." Filed as law in AGENTS.md the same pass.

## How to use it

Judge every round by the whole: a round is done when the WHOLE thing is great in every layer — presentation, outputs, design, intelligence — not when one layer passes. The legal pages are the proof case: content drafted properly AND presentation professional — both layers, or the job isn't whole. The letter is the same standard (SN-2980): centered, unfolded, distilled — every layer of the reading experience.
