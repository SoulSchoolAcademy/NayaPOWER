## Why it matters

A button is a promise: its label says what it does, and the label must be whole. A button that cuts off its text breaks the promise twice — the citizen can't read what it does, and the page looks unaligned, unfinished, not 10-star. And a leftover test world is worse than a clipped label — it is test data on the citizen's page, a world that isn't theirs, in their picker, among their worlds. The catch pairs the two: the clipping is the defect his eye saw, the leftover test world is the cause his eye named, and the fix is both — whole labels AND clean pages.

## How to use it

No button ever cuts off its text — every label shows full and aligned at every width; long names wrap or shrink. Button text is verified at every width, in every state — if the text is cut, it fails. Eye checks clean up their test worlds and aliases after themselves — no test data on the page he sees. Test data is scaffolding: it is removed when the check is done, never left on the citizen's page.
