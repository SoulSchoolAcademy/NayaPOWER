## Why it matters

The re-aim throws out the wrong scoreboard and installs the right one. Counting wakes and cadence is counting the price and calling it the purchase — the interval is not the work. The real score is verifiable work that is positive, good, useful, done right — finished, in front of him, proven. The audit itself was re-aimed mid-flight on this frame: measure completions and verifiable value delivered, state each area's ultimate answer first, judge finished / done right / optimal time — and the cadence question (15 vs 30 minutes) was withdrawn outright.

## How to use it

Every job, round, and lane is scored on completions — finished work, verified, in front of him — per hour. Cadence and wake-up counts are tracked as cost against value produced, never as the score. Dispatches per hour is never reported as success on its own. A job that wakes often and finishes nothing is waste, and is named waste. Sibling law, same pass: Scorecarding — The Ultimate Answer First.
