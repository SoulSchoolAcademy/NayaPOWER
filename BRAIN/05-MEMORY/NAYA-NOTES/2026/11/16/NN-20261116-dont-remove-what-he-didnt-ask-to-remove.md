## Why it matters

The failure is silent subtraction: a round can do everything it was asked to do, pass every check on its own scope, and still have made the page worse — because something that was there, that he liked, that citizens used, is gone, and nobody said to take it away. The play button was a real piece of the page's welcome, removed as a side effect of other work, discovered only when his eye went looking for it. The charge names the pattern, not just the case — "sometimes," a habit, not a one-off. The repair is mechanical: before any seat ships, it names what was already on the page and confirms every piece is still there or was instructed away.

## How to use it

No seat removes, drops, or hides anything the brief did not instruct away. Before any seat ships, it names what was already on the page and confirms every piece is still there or was instructed away, in writing. A round that quietly drops something that was there fails its ship-gate, even if everything it was asked to do is right. The same law as don't-move-what-he-didn't-ask-to-move, on the other side: don't touch what he didn't ask to touch. Sibling charge, same message: Don't Send In What Isn't Completed.
