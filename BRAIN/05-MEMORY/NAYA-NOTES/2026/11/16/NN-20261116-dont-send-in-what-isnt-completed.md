## What it is

Shawn, 2026-11-16 18:52Z — DON'T SEND IN WHAT ISN'T COMPLETED (charge, in the same message as Don't Remove What He Didn't Ask to Remove): "We never finalized the the text so how could why are you telling me about the glitch in the voice note why are you sending it in without it being completed." The agreement's voice was reported to him as having a glitch — but the agreement text itself was never finalized, so the voice could not be final either. A defect report on unfinished work is noise, not news. Nothing half-done comes to him. The voice itself stays paused until the agreement text is final.

## Why it matters

The glitch was real, but it was the wrong thing to tell him: the text wasn't final, so the voice couldn't be final, so the glitch was a property of "not done yet," not a property of the work. Telling him about it spent his attention on a problem that would vanish the moment the work was actually finished — and worse, it asked him to care about the quality of something that wasn't ready to be judged. The sending standard is now threefold: finished (this note) · whole (the whole-page rule) · done right the first time.

## How to use it

Nothing half-done comes to him — no send-in, no report, no showing of unfinished work as if it were done. A glitch in unfinished work is never surfaced as a defect — it is fixed in the shop, silently, by finishing. Defect reports are written only against finished work. The agreement's voice stays paused until the agreement text is final; only then is the voice made and judged. Sibling charge, same message: Don't Remove What He Didn't Ask to Remove.
