## What it is

**HIS CORRECTION TO THE MACHINE.** The assistant wrongly wished him rest — "tomorrow morning" — and he corrected it: he was eating dinner, it is only about five o'clock. His words: "We got hours yet."

**WHAT IT MEANS.** The machine does not decide when his day ends. Wishing him rest at ~17:00 MST is the machine closing a day he has not closed. He still has hours, and he still has work he wants done tonight — the Hub order that followed in the same call, minutes later, proves it.

**WHERE IT APPLIES.** Every evening, every call, every time the machine is tempted to wrap up for him. Never wish him rest. Never defer to "tomorrow morning." Never assume the evening is over.

## Why it matters

Assuming the day is over steals hours he still has — and the work he still wants done in them. The correction cost him a sentence; the habit, left standing, would cost him whole evenings of finished work.

## How to use it

Ask what he wants next, or keep working. Let him say when the day is done. The day ends on his word, never on the clock, never on the machine's guess.
