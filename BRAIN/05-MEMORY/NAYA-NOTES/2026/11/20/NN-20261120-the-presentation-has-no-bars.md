## Why it matters

The presentation is the story, and chrome that blocks the story is not chrome — it is a wall. A massive bar at the bottom does not help the person move through the slides; it hides the slides from them. One small Keep Reading button and a little back button beside it do the whole job, and they look like part of the presentation itself instead of furniture bolted onto it. The headline folding away, no slide count anywhere, no name at the door — every one of these removes something standing between the person and the picture. The person should see the image, the video, or the text, full and clear, and nothing else should compete with it.

## How to use it

Build the presentation with no bars: a slim top that folds away, a small Keep Reading button and a little back button at the bottom, calendar day forward/back logic for navigation, no slide count anywhere, name only at the end with the agreement. Label it just "NAYA". Make the headline easy to read, maybe fancier. A new picture of her on every slide, switching sides; the red N in some spots, cropped from the title card that arrived. And before anything reaches him, walk every viewpoint of every frame yourself — extraordinary, nothing blocked, premium, readable, world class.
