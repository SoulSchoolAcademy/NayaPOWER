## What it is

**SAY THE PLAN, NOT THE WORD (his question):** "And what are you going to do with the stars?" The first reply was: "The stars." He had to ask again — "And the rate doesn't intelligent." — before a real answer came: "For the stars, they'll be purple and white with that same DM flow inside, no gold or blue, and they'll sit cleanly without colliding. And the orb at the top of the intelligent block will get its flow back around it, so it feels like one continuous, elegant system, just like you described."

## Why it matters

He asks "what are you going to do" because he wants the plan stated plainly enough to judge — the colours, the flow, the collision, the orb — so he can say yes, no, or different before the work is done. An echoed noun gives him nothing to judge and costs him another turn to get what he asked for the first time.

## How to use it

When he asks what you will do, say what you will do: the concrete change, in plain words, in the same turn. Never answer a question with the word it asked about. If the plan is not settled yet, say what is not settled and what you will check — that is still an answer; the word alone is not.
