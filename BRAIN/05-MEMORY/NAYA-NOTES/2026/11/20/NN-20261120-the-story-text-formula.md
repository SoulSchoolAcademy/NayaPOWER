## What it is

**The formula, replacing image-description (11:40–11:50 MST).** Do NOT describe the image you're seeing. For every slide, over and over: **this is what it is · this is why it matters · this is what's in it for you.** Clear, direct, warm and inviting, not long. This supersedes the Queen's Story approach of writing the queen's words FROM what each image shows — the words still come from the image, but they never describe it.

## Why it matters

The words are the story now, not the pictures. A picture with a description beside it is a normal webpage; a picture whose own short story is told in her voice — what it is, why it matters, what's in it for you — is the kingdom, and a child can follow it to the end. The formula is the same on every slide so the person never has to learn a new shape; the passion and realness are the same so the voice never goes flat; and the captain writing it intentionally is what keeps the agents from filling the frames with whatever. When the text needs more room, it gets its own slide instead of being squeezed — the story stays beautiful, one image at a time.

## How to use it

Write every slide of the agreement in the formula, in the captain's hand: what it is · why it matters · what's in it for you — clear, direct, warm and inviting, not long, never a description of the image. Slide 1 opens with his approved hook, in the epic voice he approved. If the text won't fit beautifully with its image, let it run onto the next slide on Keep Reading; text-only slides are proper, two in a row are okay. Read it aloud to him slide by slide as it's written, and keep the voice a child can understand all the way to the end.
