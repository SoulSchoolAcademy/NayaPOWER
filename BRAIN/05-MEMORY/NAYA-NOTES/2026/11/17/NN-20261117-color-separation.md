## Why it matters

The spectrum flow is judged by the eye, not by the hex table. Two hexes that differ on paper and read as one color on the screen are the same color, as far as the person looking is concerned — and the person looking is the only judge that counts. A flow with two purples together is not a flow at all; it is a stutter. The same fault sits in the Leaderboards boards (Overall and Top area both read purple/magenta — the eye flagged it 19:41–19:46Z, fix round dispatched 19:46Z), so this is not one list's problem. Wherever neighbors in the spectrum read as the same color, the step is too narrow and the colors are wrong until the eye can tell them apart.

## How to use it

When you lay out any row, list, or set of steps that flows through the spectrum, hold it up to the eye, not the hex table: can you tell the top one from the bottom one at a glance? If not, widen the step until you can — change the color, not the label. The test is his words: "I just can't tell" means the colors are wrong, whatever the hexes say. The Hub eye-notes fix round carries this law; the Leaderboards fix round carries the same law for its first two boards.
