## Why it matters

The day menu is the front door to a day — the first thing he taps — and right now it is a mess that is hard for his brain to put in order. The notification bar fixes the order: the action comes first, because the action is what he came to see — how many messages, how many people liked, commented, loved, took an action. And the categorization fixes the rest: doors grouped by what they are, separated, color making sense, labels short — so a person can find their worlds at a glance instead of reading a wall of long labels stuck together. "We want them to easily find their worlds and have them logically put together" — that is the whole law of the day menu in one line, and it is the same law as the text laws: less text, more sense, everything in its right place, nothing that makes the brain work to find what it wants.

## How to use it

The day menu is built in this order: notification bar first (live counts, personal + business + space, all dynamic), then the doors grouped by category and color (news together; business/personal/thing together; journal/horoscope/weather together), appointments and timeline at the bottom, labels short ("Smart Notes", "Posts", "Notes" — never "for that day"). The check is his own: can he find his worlds easily, at a glance, without his brain having to put the mess in order? If not, it goes back.
