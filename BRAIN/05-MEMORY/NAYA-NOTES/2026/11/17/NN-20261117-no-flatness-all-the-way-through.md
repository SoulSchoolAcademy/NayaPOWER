## Why it matters

This is the Real Object Law sharpened into a rule, and the test he gives for it is the zoom: look close, and if you are disappointed, it is not done. A button that reads as real at arm's length and flat when you zoom in was never a real object — it was a picture of one. The inner icon is the cherry on top: it is the smallest thing on the page and the most-touched, and grey, flat, illegible inner icons are what make the whole page feel unfinished even when the big boards are right. White, multi-layered, and representative — you can see what the icon is, and it means what it sits on. Flatness is a defect, wherever it is, and the rule has no exceptions: all the way through.

## How to use it

When you build or judge any icon, button, tile, or control, zoom in. If it goes flat, it goes back — add the layers, the elevation, the depth, until it reads as a real object up close. The inner icon is white, never grey; it has layers you can see; you can tell what it is at a glance; and it represents what it sits on — the icon is the thing, not a decoration on the thing. A round that ships a flat icon anywhere has failed the rule, even if everything else on the page is right. The Hub eye-notes fix round carries this law.
