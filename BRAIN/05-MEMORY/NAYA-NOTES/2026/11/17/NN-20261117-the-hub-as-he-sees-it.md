## Why it matters

His eye is the final gate, and this is his eye reading the Hub as a reader: the page is better — the buttons look great, the thing looks good — and these notes are what make it right. Each one is small and each one is law-shaped: a control that sits on the wrong side of the thing it opens; a glow that floods the middle instead of lighting the edge; a caption line that explains what the tabs already say; a greeting that forgets whose morning it is; two items sharing one icon, two sharing one color, two sharing a color side by side so the eye reads disorganization. None of these is a feature. All of them are the difference between a page that looks good and a page that reads right. The rule underneath them all is the one he states plainly: doubled-up colors close together throw his perspective off, and a page that throws his perspective off is not 10-star service, however good its buttons look.
