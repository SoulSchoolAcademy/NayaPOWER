## Why it matters

A double-up is a clone sitting just behind the thing, offset by a hair, so the eye reads depth — one object sitting on another. Move it a little too far and the trick breaks: now you SEE the spacing, you see two things with a gap between them, and the eye reads it as a mistake, not as depth. It looks weird. It doesn't look pro. The calibration is not a number you measure — it's a feeling you check with the eye: can you feel the depth? Can you see the gap? If you can see the gap, you went over much — pull it back until the gap disappears and only the depth is left. Just a tad. That is the whole law: the offset is a hair, never a step. And it generalizes: every depth effect in the system is judged the same way — depth you feel is pro, spacing you see is a mistake.
