## Why it matters

A Skills page built as ten more questions would bury the one thing it exists to say. The résumé anatomy puts the message first: who you are, then the statement of what you offer and what you are looking to do for them — and everything else sits under that headline. A stranger landing cold sees the offer before they see the detail. And because the page does not have to sell, it can welcome: a dancer, an athlete, a kid good at AI — all of them get to say "this is what I'm good at" and "connect with me," without having to turn themselves into a store.

## How to use it

Build the Skills page as a résumé: top — who you are (name, phone, and the rest); then the headline statement — "this is what I have to offer and this is what I'm looking to do for you"; then the experiences, if they want to list them. A business: "here's my services." A person: "this is what I'm good at" — sports, technology, AI, dance, whatever it is, in their own words. End in connection, not necessarily a sale: "connect with me, I'm good at XYZ." Do not force ten questions onto the Skills page — its test is whether the message gets across.
