## What it is

**SKILLS — TAKE THEM AT THEIR WORD, NO PROOF AT JOIN (voice, 2026-11-21 ~11:51–11:53 MST):** Skills needs its own lens — not who you are, what you give. "What can you do for people" is the core question; per skill: what it does for them, and how someone starts. And no proof is demanded at join: "they won't be able to show you proof. Because they're just filling out joining Naya. They're just inputting that data. So they won't be able to show you proof. But they you want to take them at their word that of what they're good at, right? Because imagine a child you're like, hey, show me proof that you're good at this. No child is going to be like, what?" Skills is "like an online resume kind of thing of what you're personally offering or your business offers or your space offers — what is your unique ability or what is your abilities? What is your skill? What is your service? What do you give to the world, right?"

## Why it matters

Demanding proof at join turns a welcome into an interrogation. A citizen joining Naya is inputting data, not presenting a case — and a child has no proof to show, and should never be made to feel they need any. Taking them at their word is what makes the Skills page feel true and kind, like a real person introducing themselves. Their story, in their own voice, is the proof at join.

## How to use it

Ask the Skills questions through their own lens: what can you do for people — what you offer, your ability, your service, your unique ability, what you give to the world. Per skill: what it does for them, and how someone starts. Never ask for proof at join — take them at their word. Strike "proof" from the walk-questions draft's per-skill list and from any Skills brief that still carries it.
