# One Block per Screen

**Written by Naya for Shawn Vibert — Founder / Creator of NayaNET**

**NN-20261118-one-block-per-screen.md · Naya Note · 2026-11-18 16:07Z**

**Category:** Design — one block per screen, locked to the viewport

## What it is

Shawn's direction, in his words: **WHAT'S WRONG NOW:** clicking throws you down to the next image instead of making it perfectly aligned. If the image or the presentation took up too much, it threw you down halfway. Not as good as it can be.

- **WHAT IT SHOULD BE:** they click the button and it automatically comes right into the screen, **locks in to the page**, as you come out into the page into their viewpoint. Click button, next — it unfolds and the next page fits perfect into the viewpoint. **Click-button optimize: the page fits perfect into the viewport.**
- **THE EFFECT:** click button, it moves the person down and it comes into the screen — it almost comes OUT into their viewpoint, right in, out in the screen, it just comes out a little bit. Or we just time it so it fits perfect, not unperfect.
- **BLOCK BY BLOCK:** every screen, every viewport is a separate block. Unfold the next block. **Each block always a different theme or color, with some kind of break between the next block.** Those blocks fit the perfect dimensions for somebody's viewpoint in a mobile or a laptop — **about the fold thing**.
- **THE LANGUAGE:** one block per screen, locked to the viewport. (The technical name is scroll-snapping — each block snaps into the frame, never halfway. Our name: the block, the unfold, the viewpoint.) Each block IS the fold — the break between blocks is the fold line.
- **RIDES:** the flow-end round (the agreement deck's click-to-next locks each board into the viewport) + the day-restore round (the king's daily report unfolds section by section, each section one viewport block) + the About/Skills build (each viewpoint one block). This is the locked-in display language, with the 16:03Z click-button viewpoint system + the headlights principle.

## Why it matters

"Halfway" is the tell — a page that lands halfway between two viewpoints has no viewpoint at all, and a person who lands halfway has to do the machine's job themselves. The click must bring the next block right into the screen and lock it there, so the viewpoint is the unit and the fold is the break between units. Each block a different theme or color is how the person knows they have arrived somewhere new.

## How to use it

Lock each block into the viewport on click-to-next — never halfway, never thrown down. Give each block its own theme or color with a break between blocks. Size blocks to the viewpoint, mobile or laptop. Apply it in the flow-end round, the day-restore round, and the About/Skills build.
