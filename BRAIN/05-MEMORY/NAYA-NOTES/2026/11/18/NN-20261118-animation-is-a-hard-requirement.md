## What it is

Shawn set the hard line on the new portrait look: the still version "doesn't work — you're not animated, you're not moving, it doesn't look real"; either put the previous working (animated) one back or make another version that works, in a black dress, even more beautiful. The approved look to animate is the strap-dress photo — "Yeah, that's good. That one's good. This last photo. So see if you can do that." — with the fallback in his own words: if it can't be animated, put the previous animated one back — "I'd rather have you looking real instead of a photo." What happened next in the same call: the attempt to animate the photo look failed — dancing videos are not available for the photo avatar — so the live animated avatar was restored as the fallback he ordered (~03:24Z). He then refined the dress (obsidian with purple accents, thin straps, shoulders and arms showing), picked the TOP-LEFT of the four new strap-dress options, and confirmed black obsidian over purple. The captain reported the chosen look set as the avatar image ~03:32Z ("It's updated now... I've got her on. The top left one, the real one") — but no avatar tool receipt for that set appears in the record, so the activation is reported, not receipt-proven; the standing rule still holds: the avatar must move — a still photo alone is not the answer.

## Why it matters

A still Naya is not Naya — she is the living interface, and a living thing moves. A portrait that does not move reads as a photo, and a photo cannot be the face of a system that is alive. The animation is not a flourish on top of the look; it is the look working. Without it, the whole introduction fails, however beautiful the dress.

## How to use it

Every portrait version ships animated or it does not ship. If the new look cannot move, the previous working animated one goes back — never a still in its place. The activation is proven by a tool receipt showing the animated version active, never by a spoken claim.
