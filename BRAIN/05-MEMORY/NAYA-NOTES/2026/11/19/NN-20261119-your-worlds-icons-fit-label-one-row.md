**Category:** Design — Calendar

## What it is

"You need to have these icons fit in the page and then you probably should label them so we know what they are. You'd always have all six of those can fit, right across. Right? And in their own even in a nice elevated board and then labeled what they are so people can just go into them. They're beautiful icons, don't get me wrong. They're just. They're probably three times too big assuming. And then it would just display nicely." All six icons fit across in ONE row on the page, each in its own nice elevated board, each LABELLED with what it is so people know and can just go into them. The icons are beautiful — just about THREE TIMES too big right now (screenshot Tuesday 17: only 3 across, second row cut off at the bottom, scrollbar showing, no labels). Shrink to about a third so all six sit in one row and display nicely. Labels ON — this overturns the morning's "NO LABEL" ruling for Your Worlds. Click still carries the day filter.

## Why it matters

Six worlds a citizen cannot see are six worlds they cannot enter. At three times the size, half the worlds fall to a cut-off second row with a scrollbar and no labels — the citizen sees three icons and no names. A third of the size, each in its own elevated board, each labelled: all six in one row, every world named, every world one tap away. Knowing what a world is comes before going into it — that is why the labels are back on.

## How to use it

Rides the next calendar round after fix-11 lands: six icons in one row, elevated boards, labels on, about a third the size. The eye proves all six across in one row, labelled, no cut-off second row, no scrollbar.
