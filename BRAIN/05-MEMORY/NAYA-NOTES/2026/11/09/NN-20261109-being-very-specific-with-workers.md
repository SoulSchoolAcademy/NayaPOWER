## Why it matters

Specificity is not a guarantee — it is a protection. A seat given vague instructions guesses; a seat given exact instructions can still miss the picture, as pass-2 proved: the brief said the orbs must mount, the verifier measured them mounted 10/10, and his eye saw a mess. The lesson is not "specificity fails" — it is that specificity is necessary and not sufficient. What closes the gap is the live check: his eye on the rendered viewpoint, at real widths. The brief must be as specific as it can be, AND the work must be judged by eye — never by the seat's own numbers alone.

## How to use it

- Write every brief super-specific, very detailed, clear, defined — exact element, exact change, exact measurement, what must not be there.
- Never take a seat's numbers as proof — a seat can follow the words and still miss the picture.
- The backstop is always his eye on the live viewpoint, at real widths — the brief's specificity is the protection; the live check is the proof.
- When a seat's work comes back messed up despite a specific brief, the answer is more specificity AND a live check — never less of either.
