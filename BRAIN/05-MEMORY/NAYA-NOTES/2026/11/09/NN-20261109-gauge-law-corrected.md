# Gauge Law, Corrected: Suit the Gauge to the Situation

**NN-20261109-gauge-law-corrected · Naya Note · 2026-11-09 19:17Z**

**Category:** Law — Design

## What it is

His correction, 19:17Z Nov 9 — SUPERSEDES the 15:39Z "one gauge, reused everywhere" law — recorded from his words as given, on the drill view:

- "you changed all the gauges so they're all the same... I do like that one the best, but the other one that you had in there was not bad too, because then it was clean and crisp when you go into the stats... it has to suit the situation, right? Just because one is better, like bigger, one is smaller — maybe another style would be better, but you suit it to its function in that moment, right? So I'm conflicted... I think that you need to have the other one come back, like the one that was mostly black, because you need to be able to see it, right, when it's smaller. I thought maybe if it was smaller you'd be able to see it, but no, unless you can fix being able to see the numbers."
- **The ruling:** TWO gauges, each where it suits. BIG = the gear gauge (his favorite, for the hero / large display). SMALL = the mostly-black gauge (clean and crisp, numbers readable at small sizes — the one the stats used before the one-gauge change). Shrinking the big gauge does not make it readable; if the numbers can't be read, use the other gauge.
- **The drill view's presentation is off** ("something's definitely off" — scattered orbs at the corners with tiny unreadable numbers, the X floating, the view bar at the bottom): the drill-in must be clean and aligned — number + gauge + real stats, aligned — not scattered.
- Pass-3 brief amended (§1c, §2, §3, §9) and the correction sent to the pass-3 coordinator the same pass.

## Why it matters

One gauge everywhere was a simplification that broke readability: the big gear gauge, shrunk small, hides its own numbers. His correction is the law the other way — suit the gauge to the situation: the gear gauge where it is big and loved, the mostly-black gauge where it is small and must stay readable. If the numbers can't be read, the gauge is wrong for that size, and shrinking it further never fixes it. The drill view gets the same rule: clean and aligned, number + gauge + real stats — not scattered orbs at the corners with tiny numbers. The 15:39Z one-gauge law is superseded, not deleted: it was the step that taught us the gauges must suit their function.

## How to use it

- TWO gauges, each where it suits: BIG = the gear gauge (hero / large display). SMALL = the mostly-black gauge (small sizes, where numbers must stay readable).
- Shrinking the big gauge does not make it readable; if the numbers can't be read at that size, use the other gauge.
- The drill-in is clean and aligned — number + gauge + real stats, aligned — never scattered orbs at the corners with tiny unreadable numbers, never a floating X, never a view bar at the bottom.
- Pass-3 carries the amended brief (§1c, §2, §3, §9); judged by eye on the rendered viewpoint, at real widths.
