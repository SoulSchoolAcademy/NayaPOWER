## What it is

Shawn's 15:15Z dictation, his words verbatim: "it's not a 10 because you say it is, not a 10 because I say it is — it is a 10 because it's a 10, because we've checked to make sure that it's a 10 all the way through." The standard: a score is a claim until the checking makes it true — checked all the way through, not declared. The same dictation gave the method for when stuck: scorecard it — 10 loves / 10 don't-loves / how to make it 10x better / solutions → a plan. And the distillation rule: when notes reach hundreds/thousands, distill to the core — the ultimate intelligence, the key takeaways — ~100–150 core pages, not 1,000; raw notes deleted after distilling (the distill machine: fix built, NOT merged, NOT live — One List `distill-machine-merge`, queued). The walk-end scorecard the same hour was the proof of the method in action: captain's 6, 10 loves / 10 don't-loves / 10 fixes, order agreement → world → hired → package — and Shawn reacted 👍 with no correction, the plan standing unchanged.

## Why it matters

A machine that grades its own homework will always pass — and a machine that always passes stops improving, because nothing is ever found wanting. The checked-10 is the only standard that keeps the machine honest: it forces the walk-through, the second eye, the named bullseye, and it makes a 6 with a fix list more valuable than a 10 claimed. The scorecard method turns "stuck" from a dead end into a plan, and the distillation core keeps the intelligence usable — a hundred pages that matter instead of a thousand that bury each other.

## How to use it

No score ships without the checking behind it named — what was walked, at what width, by whose eye, against what bullseye. A 6 with 10 loves / 10 don't-loves / 10 fixes outranks a 10 claimed. When stuck, scorecard before asking: 10 loves, 10 don't-loves, 10x better, solutions → plan. Distill to the core — ~100–150 pages.
