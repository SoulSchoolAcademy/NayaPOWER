Showing one finished item of a ten-item project does not save him time — it spends his time. He then has to do the work the machine should have done: hold the other nine in his head, and tell us about them, one by one, in the next message. A project that arrives whole is a gift; a project that arrives in pieces is a chore he did not ask for.

Nothing is shown to him from a multi-item project until every item is proven on the page — one message, one link. A one-item round on a page with open items is the wrong shape; the round's bullseye is the whole list. Sameness is completed whole together. What is not in the round (membership) is named openly when the round is described, never discovered by him later.
