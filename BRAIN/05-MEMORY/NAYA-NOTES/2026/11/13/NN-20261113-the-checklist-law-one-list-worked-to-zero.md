## Why it matters

A list is a promise with ten parts, and delivering one part is not delivering the promise. The failure was structural: one bullseye per round means a ten-item list needs ten rounds, and nine of them never got briefed, so nine items sat silent while the machine reported the one round done. The Checklist Law fixes the structure: the list becomes one checklist on the floor, in his words, and the round's bullseye becomes the whole page as he sees it. And "done" is redefined at the only place it can be honestly defined: on the page he sees, proven, not at round-close.

## How to use it
