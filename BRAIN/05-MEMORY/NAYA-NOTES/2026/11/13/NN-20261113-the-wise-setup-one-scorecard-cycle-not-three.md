## Why it matters

Three scorecard cycles felt like thoroughness, but the evidence said otherwise: makers were landing 8–9/10 on the first pass all day, and the checkers agreed every time — the extra cycles were polishing work that was already good, not transforming work that was not. The wise move is to cut the cycles that add nothing and keep the stage that adds everything: the checker stays, because the checker's real job is the audit that keeps the captain's check at 5 minutes instead of 25, and that 5 minutes is the whole machine's bottleneck. The possibility proof is the other half of the wisdom: a brief must prove its own criteria are possible before a maker ever sees it.

## How to use it

Maker: ONE scorecard cycle, then hand it over. Checker: stays, as auditor — PASS/FAIL + one line. Captain: 5-minute decision, never a re-verification. Every brief carries a possibility proof before dispatch. Queues never empty: ≥2 briefs per seat, always.
