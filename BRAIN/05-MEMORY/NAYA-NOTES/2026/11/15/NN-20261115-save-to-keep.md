## What it is

Shawn, reading the Living Calendar definition (02:55Z), answered the retention tension himself — SAVE TO KEEP: "The only thing I could think of is like because we don't keep everything right. for smart notes, um, I guess if they save them, they keep them, they pin them on their feed, so that's why like if you want um are the smart notes if you see them in the public feed and you love them you want to save them that we recommend you do because we don't keep them forever You just steal all our notes, into their essence, so like, that's why we encourage people to like if there's a conversation you had, you might want to smart note it, and save it, as on your favorites or in your smart list, or when you save it it's there for good for you like where we don't get rid of it right so we encourage you save your the smart notes." Filed as law in MEMORY.md. The rule: unsaved smart notes distill to essence after a week (deletion by default); a note the citizen SAVES — pin on their feed, favorite, smart list — is kept for good, in their vault, never distilled away. That is why we encourage saving: the saved note is how a day stays alive forever, and the backdating calendar can only reach back as far as what was saved. This information is NOT to sell data — it is to optimize the system to give 10-star service.

## Why it matters

A system that kept everything would be a surveillance system; a system that kept nothing would be a forgetful one. Save-to-keep is the middle that honors both laws: privacy by default, permanence by choice. It puts the citizen in charge of their own memory — the system does not decide what mattered, they do, one tap at a time — and it makes the Living Calendar honest: the calendar does not promise a past it cannot deliver, it promises the past you kept.

## How to use it

Saving (pin / favorite / smart list) keeps a note for good, in the vault — never distilled, never deleted. Unsaved notes distill to essence after a week, per deletion by default. The calendar's backdate is described honestly: it reaches back as far as what was saved. The system encourages saving, in plain words, at the moment a citizen sees something they love.
