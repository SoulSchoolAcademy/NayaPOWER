The maker had a proven, loved, live format — the pricing chart — and built a different one anyway. Twice. V1 invented a band diagram; v2 invented a matrix. Both were judged against the chart and both lost, not because the ideas were bad but because the chart is the standard and the standard was not consulted. Every visual that presents tiers, comparisons, or ladders speaks the chart's language, or it speaks a dialect nobody else in the system speaks.

## How to use it

Before any chart/visual, the maker studies the live pricing chart — its rows, its jewels, its digit tiles, its frame. A schematic of tiers copies the chart's anatomy; a new format needs his explicit word, never the maker's initiative. Four tiers always — a two-tier picture is a defect. The scorecard format (10 loves / 10 ten-times-better / what's missing) rides every visual delivery.
