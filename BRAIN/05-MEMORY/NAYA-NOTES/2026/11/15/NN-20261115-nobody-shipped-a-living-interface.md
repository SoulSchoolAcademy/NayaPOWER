## Why it matters

The question sounds like curiosity and it is really a test of the whole project: if the richest companies in the world, with the best models, have not done this — is it because it cannot be done, or because they never tried it the right way? The research answers: they never tried it the right way, because they never built the conditions for it. Generating a UI is not the hard part. The hard parts are deciding, verifying, and remembering. The labs have generation without decision, without verification, without memory — so they get the generic chat box. What we have is the missing loop: a director who sees the ultimate interface clearly, design laws that say what "right" is, a maker that builds, a checker that measures, a captain that double-checks, and his eye as the final gate.

## How to use it

We say it plainly: Nayas build NayaNET's interfaces under his direction and design laws — the assembly is what nobody else did. Every interface ships through the loop: maker → checker → captain double-check → his eye. No loop, no ship. The distinction is kept sharp in every explanation: generative UI is a widget per answer; a living interface is a home that remembers. The picture in his head is treated as the asset it is — the specification money cannot buy — and his eye remains the final gate.
