# No Line May Parrot a Backstage Instruction

## What it is

Shawn, reading the staged agreement (00:31Z), called out one line in the deed: "Between NayaNET and you — written out, so it feels official." His correction: "so it feels official" is his backstage instruction to the captain echoed back to the citizen — "you're telling them what I'm saying to you... that makes no sense." The agreement does not say it FEELS official; it IS official. The line was rewritten in her voice, stating the fact plainly. Standing rule from this: no line may parrot a backstage instruction. If a line reads like something said to the maker, it does not ship. The captain owned the miss plainly to him, and the correction went into the running v4 build as addendum #4.

## Why it matters

A backstage instruction is a note from the director to the maker. When that direction leaks into the text itself, the citizen reads the scaffolding instead of the building: they are being told what the maker was told, and the spell breaks. The test is simple and mechanical: read a line and ask who it sounds like it was said to. If it sounds like it was said to the maker, cut it and state the fact instead.

## How to use it

Every citizen-facing line is read with the backstage test before it ships. A line that echoes a brief, a correction, or a design note is rewritten to state the fact, or cut. The deed carries the plain fact, never "so it feels official."
