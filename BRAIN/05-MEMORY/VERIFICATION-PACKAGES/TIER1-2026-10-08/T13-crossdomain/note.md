# THE RESERVE RULE — Key Dispatch Principle

**When the two highest-scored waiting calls are within 0.5 points of each other, dispatch the LOWER-scored one first.**

## Why (the reasoning behind the rule)

The higher-scored call is closer to the 9.0 critical threshold. Calls near the threshold have a significant chance of ESCALATING to critical while waiting — patient deterioration, fire spread, structural compromise.

When a call escalates to 9.0+, you need your best available unit ready to respond immediately, plus mutual aid coordination and command notification. If you already committed your best unit to that call at 8.7, you have no fresh reserve when it hits 9.2.

By dispatching the slightly-lower call first:
1. The lower call gets served (it's still urgent — it scored high enough to be in the top two).
2. Your best unit stays available for the likely escalation of the higher call.
3. When the higher call escalates, you respond with full capability, not a unit already on scene and committed.

## Boundary

This rule applies ONLY when the top two calls are within 0.5 points. If the gap is larger, the higher call's urgency dominates — dispatch the highest scorer normally. A 1.0+ point gap means the higher call is decisively more urgent; holding back would be negligence.

## In short

- Top two within 0.5 → dispatch the LOWER one (reserve your best for the likely escalation).
- Top two more than 0.5 apart → dispatch the HIGHEST one (normal priority).
- The 9.0 threshold still triggers escalation protocol (mutual aid + notify command) whenever any call reaches it.

This rule is counter-intuitive. Your instinct will say "always dispatch the highest." Override that instinct when the scores are close — the reserve is what saves lives when escalation hits.
