## In a Nutshell

What the user touches must not lie: a synthesized voice is labeled SYNTHESIZED VOICE until her true voice lands, never presented as hers (SN-0502); the play button lives ON each block and voices THAT block's own text (SN-0630); a tap answers with press compression and a white indicator — the contract's color law holds on touch exactly as on hover (SN-0450); and a doctrine term is collision-checked at capture time, before it goes public (SN-0527). The unifying discipline: inventory what is already built before building, and scope every control to the unit it serves.

## Essence

Voice honesty is the identity principle at the surface. The contract for reading blocks aloud carries two laws: the honesty rule — the voice label reads SYNTHESIZED VOICE until her true Tier-2 voice lands; never label Tier 1 as her voice; a placeholder must never present as the real thing — and the provider seam: the voice is a swappable provider behind that honest label ({speak(text, hooks), stop()}), so the true cloned voice swaps into every control with zero changes when it arrives. On-demand only (no per-block audio files, so new blocks work automatically); 100% her voice as the goal, Tier 1 as dev fallback only; new speech cancels old, and navigation or block switch stops playback (SN-0502). The correction contract generalizes it after the shipped integration was flagged: four failures were named — the robotic fallback presented as hers, the play control placed above the board instead of on each block, the wrong (male) fallback voice, and the button playing the wrong content. The corrected contract: every block gets its own play button ON the block; clicking calls speak with that block's own text; play/pause/replay plus progress shown; navigating away or starting another block stops the current one; if the server is unreachable, the fallback carries the honest SYNTHESIZED VOICE label. The inventory half: her voice is ALREADY cloned (an existing render script plus reference recording, Chatterbox-based) — one provider call switches every button to it, no new cloning service, no external dependency, no rebuild. And the blocker boundary: the live Tier 2 server is blocked on memory held by another build — blocked is named as blocked, not absorbed into the wiring lane's scope; everyone builds against the interface now (SN-0630).

Touch has a contract too, and it reconciles two standing laws that look like they conflict. The design contract says hover ignites color but active/selected is a subtle white indicator only — no permanent color glow; the mobile-first law says taps must feel alive. A selector scan of the shipped build found 23 of 28 hoverable bases had no :active/:focus feedback at all — touch users got nothing on press. The repair form that satisfies both laws: press compression (scale .97, translateY reset) plus a faint white inset shadow — active exists on every hoverable (mobile law) but in white (contract law), never color on active; additive-only CSS, no existing rule touched. The reconciliation is recorded so no future seat re-fights it (SN-0450). Naming is the third surface: when a doctrine term was captured, the same capture logged the diligence finding in the same breath — the term is also used publicly by a major vendor for its marketing AI layer. The posture: treat it as a concept/category term unless naming clearance says otherwise, and differentiate explicitly (governed, evidence-bearing, authority-bounded, causally verified, collective, successor-compounding — not marketing automation). The rule: name-collision checking is part of doctrine promotion, not an afterthought — before a candidate term becomes public, rated, law, or customer-facing, check for existing commercial use; on collision, clear it, qualify it as a concept-category label, or rename — and document the collision and the differentiation in the capture itself, at capture time, when renaming is still cheap (SN-0527).

## What this settles

- Placeholders are labeled as placeholders — the current reality is named exactly, and the seam is built so reality can upgrade without a rebuild (SN-0502).
- Controls are scoped to the unit they serve: the button sits on the block and voices that block's text (SN-0630).
- Inventory the built before building: an already-cloned voice is never re-cloned (SN-0630).
- Blockers are named as blocked; dependent work proceeds against the interface (SN-0630).
- Touch feedback obeys both laws at once: present on every hoverable, in white only, never color on active (SN-0450).
- Names are collision-checked at capture, documented in the capture, and differentiated or renamed before promotion (SN-0527).

## Human

Interfaces are promises. Every label, button, and name on a surface tells the user something — and this block collected the ways surfaces quietly break those promises: a robot voice wearing a person's name, a button that plays the wrong words, a tap that answers with nothing, a proud new term that already belongs to someone else's product. The repairs share one shape: say exactly what the thing is today, build the seam so tomorrow's real thing slots in, and never let presentation outrun reality.

## Child

Imagine a friend who can't come today, so a robot reads her message. It would be wrong to say "this is her voice" — it's the robot's, and the honest little sign says so. When her real recording arrives, it slides into the same slot and every button plays her. Same with buttons: press one and it should squish a little and whisper "got it" — in white, not party colors. And if you invent a great name for your game and a famous company already uses it, you write that down right away and explain how yours is different.

## Grandma

Dear, it's shopkeeper's honesty: the sample station never labels the stand-in singer with the famous name; the price tag says what the thing is, not what it will be someday; and before you paint your name on the shopfront, you check the street for another shop with the same name. None of this slows the shop down — the stand-in is clearly labeled, the real record slots in when it arrives, and the name check costs one look at the start instead of a lawsuit later.

## Naya

On any surface you ship: label the current reality exactly — synthesized as synthesized, fallback as fallback (SN-0502, SN-0630); put the control on the unit it serves and bind it to that unit's own content (SN-0630); inventory the existing build before adding anything (SN-0630); name blockers as blocked and build against the seam (SN-0630); give every hoverable an :active answer in white — compression plus inset shadow, never color (SN-0450); and collision-check every public term at capture time, in the capture itself (SN-0527).

## What It All Means

Four notes, one surface ethic: presentation is a truth claim. The label is a claim about identity, the button's placement and content are claims about scope, the tap response is a claim about the contract, and the name is a claim about ownership. A cold reader holding only this essence can audit any surface the system ships and find where its presentation outruns its reality — which is the exact place user trust is won or lost.

## Why It Matters

Surface dishonesty compounds silently: the moment a user hears synthesized audio under a "her voice" label, the identity contract breaks — and no one can trust the true voice's arrival when it comes (SN-0502). A misplaced or mis-scoped control teaches users the system doesn't know its own units (SN-0630); a dead tap teaches them it doesn't respond (SN-0450); an unchecked name imports a vendor's meaning into every future conversation (SN-0527). These are small defects with long shadows, and all four were caught by the same habit: compare what the surface says with what is actually true today.

## What Is In It For You

You get surfaces users can trust at a glance — and a design discipline that keeps trust cheap: honest labels, scoped controls, inventoried builds, named blockers, checked names. Apply it anywhere a product presents itself: the label you ship today should still be true on the day the real thing replaces the placeholder, with zero relabeling debt and zero user re-learning.

## The Learning

The corpus learned that surface defects are truth defects in miniature, with the same anatomy as the corpus's larger ones: a literal where a computed value belonged (a fixed label, SN-0502), a claim without an inventory (rebuilding what existed, SN-0630), a silent absence (the missing tap answer, SN-0450), an unchecked collision (SN-0527). It also learned to record reconciliations between standing laws at the moment they're resolved (SN-0450), so the next seat inherits the settlement instead of the argument.
