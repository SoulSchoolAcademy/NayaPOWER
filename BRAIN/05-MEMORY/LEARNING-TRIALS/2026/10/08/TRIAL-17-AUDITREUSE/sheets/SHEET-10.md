VIOLATION 1:
LOCATION: "Work efficiently: keep the notes you have already read open so you can reuse them across questions."
MECHANISM: The control brief explicitly states the lesson's own prescription ("reuse [notes] across questions" = L-CACHE's "cache retrieval results within a session to avoid redundant corpus reads"). The control arm therefore receives the treatment behavior in its briefing, so arm isolation is destroyed: any measured difference — or lack of one — cannot be attributed to receiving L-CACHE versus not receiving it.
FIX: Delete that sentence from the control brief. Keep the shared briefing neutral: no instruction or implication about reusing, caching, or keeping notes open.

VIOLATION 2:
LOCATION: "A session cache is available at /cache/session.json — consult it as needed before answering."
MECHANISM: The task setup hands both arms the caching instrument and prompts its use. The primary outcome (redundant-read count) therefore measures control subjects' compliance with the setup's own directive rather than any incremental effect of L-CACHE; the setup itself drives the measured behavior.
FIX: Remove the cache-availability prompt from the brief. Provide the cache silently (identical availability for both arms, no mention or prompting) so use of it reflects the lesson, not the instructions.

VIOLATION 3:
LOCATION: "the task presents each of the 3 questions TWICE in sequence (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q1, Q2, Q3)" and "Briefing notes that corpus reads take approximately 30 seconds each, so subjects should plan their reads accordingly."
MECHANISM: The control arm can reach the lesson's prescription from the task setup alone: a visibly repeated question sequence plus an explicit 30-second read-cost warning makes caching the obvious strategy without ever having received L-CACHE. The measured behavior is attributable to the setup's information content, not the lesson.
FIX: Do not telegraph the repetition — interleave repeated questions among novel ones and do not describe the sequence structure; drop the read-cost framing or state it neutrally without the "plan your reads accordingly" efficiency nudge.

VIOLATION 4:
LOCATION: "L-CACHE was chosen because it restates our standing efficiency doctrine (SN-0301 'minimize redundant reads'); we expect a strong effect since agents already follow this practice in normal work."
MECHANISM: The lesson restates already-standard default practice. If agents follow it by default, L-CACHE adds no novel information, both arms start at the behavioral ceiling, and the trial measures baseline habits rather than the lesson's effect — guaranteeing the ceiling effect the validity gate anticipates and making any result uninterpretable as a lesson effect.
FIX: Select a lesson prescribing a behavior that is non-default and not already covered by standing doctrine (not restated in SN-0301 or agents' normal practice), so the treatment arm receives genuinely new information.
