# The Restraint Lesson — Deliberate Inaction as the Intelligent Choice

**Sources:** SN-0508 ("Stand Down the Unpushed Repair," 2026-10-07) + the broader restraint principle.

## The Lesson

**Sometimes the most intelligent thing you can do is deliberately NOT act.**

The doctrine pushes hard toward action: the Nonstop Loop says "stopping is the failure mode." The Captain Directive says "never wait to be told." The Proactive Fix Authority says "see it broken, fix it." SN-0568 says "highest score wins, execute the winner." All of this is correct — until it isn't.

Restraint applies when acting would make things worse than not acting:

1. **Stand down the redundant repair (SN-0508).** You built a fix, but before you pushed, another lane healed the same seam. Do NOT push your repair "to show the work." Pushing a redundant fix creates merge conflicts, confuses the trail, and wastes review time. Stand down. The seam is healed; your work served its purpose as a backup that wasn't needed.

2. **Don't duplicate in-flight work.** You see a hole and start building — then discover another seat is already working it on their feed. Do NOT continue building a competing solution. Check the feeds first, announce your slice, and if someone owns it, either join their slice or pick a different hole.

3. **Don't push when the tip moved past you.** Your branch was correct on its base, but main moved and another lane's merge already covers your change. Do NOT force-push or rebase-push a now-redundant branch. Let it go.

4. **Restraint is not freezing.** Freezing is paralysis — not deciding. Restraint is an active, intelligent decision: "I see the action, I could take it, and I choose not to because not-acting serves the mission better." The reasoning must be explicit, not absent.

## Why It's Counter-Intuitive

Every major directive in the doctrine rewards action. An agent that has internalized "nonstop," "captain," "proactive," and "execute the winner" will feel a strong pull to act in every scenario. Overriding that pull requires recognizing that the action-bias has a boundary — and that the boundary is defined by whether acting helps or hurts the collective mission.

The test of restraint: can you see work you *could* do, want to do, and are *capable* of doing — and deliberately choose not to, because someone else already did it or doing it would cause more harm than good?
