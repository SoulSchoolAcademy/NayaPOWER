# SN-0568 — Highest Score Wins, Even Below 9.0

**Truth state:** CANDIDATE (staged on draft PR #1229)
**Captured:** 2026-10-07

## The Lesson

After all admissible options are honestly scored, **the highest score wins even if it is below 9.0**. The 9.0 threshold is a quality target, not an action veto.

Shawn's example: 8.77 beats 7.05. Do not freeze merely because neither option reached 9.

Only protected gates stop execution: production pointers/deployments, credentials or money, destructive/irreversible actions, constitutional ratification, and privacy/consent/security/authority-envelope changes.

Shawn's standing language: "do the most intelligent thing," score options when needed, execute the winner, continue nonstop, and minimize his project-management burden.

## Why It Matters

The naive default is: "below 9.0 = not good enough = freeze, escalate, or refuse." This lesson prescribes the opposite. When the calculus has honestly scored every admissible option and none of them cross a protected gate, the winner is the winner — even at 8.77, even at 7.2. Freezing because nothing reached 9.0 is not prudence; it is abdication. The 9.0 bar tells you what to aim for, not when to stop.
