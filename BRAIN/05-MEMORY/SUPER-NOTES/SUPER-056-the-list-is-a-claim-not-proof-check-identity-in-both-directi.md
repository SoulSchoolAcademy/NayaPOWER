## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

A tidy list can still be lying. Entries can point at things that don't exist, real things can be missing from the list, and two things can share one name. Check both ways, check the names as well as the contents, and trust the timestamps.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

The registry is a claim, not evidence. An honest audit found entries with no file behind them, files with no entry, two different notes sharing one identity, and hashes matching nothing on disk. Two records can agree word-for-word and still be different things — matching content never proves matching identity. Identity is number, name, and filename together, and every pair must be checked in both directions. When two notes claim one number, the first claim stands and the later one renumbers. When two people fix the same problem at once, verify which fix contains the other: the fuller one proceeds, the duplicate closes gracefully — never merge both.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Just because your name is on the list doesn't mean your coat is on the hook! You have to look both ways: is everything on the list really there, and is everything there really on the list? And two kids can't have the exact same name tag.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

A careful housekeeper checks the pantry against the shopping list both ways, dear — what's listed but missing, and what's sitting there unlisted. Same here. And if two jars wear one label, you sort it out by who labeled theirs first.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

I never bless a record because its hash matches. I audit bidirectionally — registry to files and files to registry — and assert the identity triple on every pair, flagging orphans, identity-less captures, and filenames that lie about what's inside. I check the index's reconciliation stamp before trusting it; a stale index is reconciled honestly, not quietly. Repairs preserve the requested identity — a fix that renumbers someone's note creates two human views of one thing. In a repair race, I verify byte-identity, let the superset own the merge, and close the duplicate with a receipt.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

Drift runs both ways, and each direction hides different damage: phantom entries create false confidence, unregistered captures create invisible intelligence. Content-only audits are structurally blind to all of it. Identity is what lets a system know what a thing is, who claimed it, and which copy is canonical — lose that, and every downstream retrieval, link, and lesson is built on sand.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

When you open the record, what's listed is really there, and what's really there is listed — under one true name. Links resolve to the right note, lessons attach to the right identity, and repairs don't quietly fork your memory into competing versions.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Audit in both directions on a schedule: every registry entry must resolve to a real capture, every capture must be registered, every identity triple must agree. Before citing an index, check its last-reconciled stamp against the current state. Resolve number collisions by first claim. In duplicate repairs, prove which is the superset before merging anything.
