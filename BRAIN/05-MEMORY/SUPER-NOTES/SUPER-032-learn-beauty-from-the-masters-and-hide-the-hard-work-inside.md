## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

The best design comes from studying the best work ever made, then testing one small piece at a time with a real person. All the hard thinking happens inside the machine, so what a person sees feels simple, alive, and easy.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

We gathered the strongest lessons from great studios, beloved apps, wise teachers, and classic books — and they agreed with the rules we had learned the hard way, while adding a few we had missed. Rules alone still don't transfer taste. Taste grows in a loop: show one surface, watch the reaction, write down the exact correction, repeat. Inside, everything is strict and careful. Outside, a person should feel none of that weight — just black calm, color used like light, and the main action right where eyes land first.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Grown-ups who make the best apps and games wrote down their secrets. We read them all and learned. Then we practice one little piece at a time and show it to a real person, because reading rules isn't the same as having good taste. The inside of the computer does all the hard thinking so the screen stays easy for you.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

Dear, the team collected the finest lessons from the best makers in the world, the way you'd collect treasured recipes. They practice one small piece at a time and show it to a real person, because taste can't be learned from a rulebook alone. And all the complicated machinery stays hidden inside, so what you see is calm and simple.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Naya studies the canon before she designs, and treats independent agreement between masters and our own hard-won rules as strong evidence. She never ships a big reveal on rules alone: one surface at a time, rendered, judged, the correction captured as law. She keeps rigor internal — checks, scorecards, evidence — and simplicity external. Color is light on a black canvas, never decoration for its own sake, and 'alive' must become checks she can verify on the actual render.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

Taste is not a talent you either have or lack; it is knowledge that can be collected, tested, and taught — but only through contact with real work and real people. The deeper point is a division of labor: complexity is not the enemy, visible complexity is. A system earns trust when it absorbs difficulty on the user's behalf instead of passing the difficulty along and calling it power.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

You get screens that feel calm instead of clever, made by people who studied the best instead of guessing. The main thing you need is always in front of you, nothing important hides below a scroll, and every improvement was tested with a real person before it reached you.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Before designing, read what the best already proved. Build one surface, show it to a real person early, and record their exact words as the next rule. Turn every feeling-word like 'alive' or 'premium' into checks you can see on the render. Keep the machinery, evidence, and worry on the inside; give the person one clear next action, visible without scrolling.
