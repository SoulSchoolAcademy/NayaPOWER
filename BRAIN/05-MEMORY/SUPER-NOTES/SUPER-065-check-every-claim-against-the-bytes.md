## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

A claim is only as good as the artifact it points at. Citations must exist in the source they cite, on the branch they claim, at the revision named - and a merge must be checked against the base it carries forward, not just the text it copied. No artifact, no claim.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Whole defect families hid in paperwork that looked fine: citation tokens appearing zero times in the documents they supposedly came from; claims that a base was 'red-teamed and reconciled' with no findings file anywhere on disk; a capability living on somebody's draft branch described as if it lived on the branch being audited. Merges carried the same risk in reverse: faithful to the source text, yet silently dropping fixes the base had already earned, or dropping dozens of files while the platform reported 'merged.' Even absence can be fabricated: 'does not exist' asserted from a stale local copy. The rule that closes all of it: verify against the exact bytes, branch, and revision, and say which ones you checked.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

If you say 'the book says so,' open the book and show the page. If you say 'it's not in the box,' you have to actually look in the right box, today - not remember an old box. And when you glue two puzzles together, check you didn't lose pieces, even if the box says it fits.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's the difference between gossip and a document. Somebody says the will was read and settled - fine, show me the paper. Somebody says a tool isn't in the shed - did they look in this shed, this week? In a family or a business, the claims that cause trouble are never the loud lies; they're the unchecked ones everybody repeated.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Re-derive every citation from verbatim quotes in the cited source and record occurrence counts; a token at zero means the citation does not exist. Audit a merge against the reconciled base and its finding resolutions - 'faithful to the source' is necessary and insufficient. Diff the diffs when two repairs claim the same fix; byte-identical means one merges and one closes. Never trust a platform's 'merged' without diffing the resulting tree against a real merge. Every existence or absence claim names its revision, its command, and its result - a negative claim carries the heaviest evidence burden of all.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

These are not pedantic rules; they are the load-bearing wall of a system whose premise is verifiable provenance. Each failure looked small - a token, a status line, a branch assumption - and each silently extended a ratified contract or erased real work. Declaring citation state alongside substance (which ref, candidate or canonical) keeps real work credited while keeping unresolved claims from hardening into doctrine.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

Decisions get made on things that are actually true. You stop funding work built on premises nobody checked, and real work stops being erased by merges that reported success. When something is uncertain, you'll hear exactly that - with the revision it was checked at - instead of a confident guess.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Before you repeat or build on any claim: locate it in the source's bytes, on the named branch, at the named revision. Cite artifacts for every inherited status claim, or downgrade the claim to an assertion and send it back. Retract wrong claims in the same place you made them, with the mechanism of the error named. Honest 'partial' always beats invented capability.
