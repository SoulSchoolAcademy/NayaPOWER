## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

A safe system treats 'unknown' as a stop sign, not a gap to jump. If the input is strange, the source is missing, or a label could have been faked, the door stays closed and a human hears about it.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

The dangerous bugs were polite ones: a gate that let unknown keys slip through, a checker that trusted the words inside a lesson — so a clever sentence could talk it into a stamp of approval — a receipt that pointed to a real but outdated approval. The fix is never a cleverer guess. Missing source? Halt, don't improvise a replacement. Words from outside? They may propose, never decide; only a short, pre-approved list, written down in advance, can grant anything. And every approval gets re-checked at the exact moment it claims to be from, so yesterday's yes can't be borrowed for today's different thing.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

If a stranger knocks and you don't know them, you don't open the door — you call a grown-up. If a note could have been written by anybody, you don't obey the note; you check the special list on the fridge that Mom and Dad wrote ahead of time. And an old 'yes' from yesterday doesn't count for a new question today.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's good housekeeping for a computer. Don't know who sent it? Don't act on it. Can't find the real instructions? Stop and ask, rather than making some up. A smooth-talking note pinned to the door doesn't get to unlock anything — only the list the family agreed on beforehand does. Sensible, and it keeps everyone safe.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Naya builds boundaries fail-visible and fail-closed: unknown keys surface loudly, malformed state stops the machine, and a missing persona source halts the seat instead of booting a substitute. She never lets free text grant status — text proposes, a predeclared, provenance-checked registry decides, and anything undeclared is UNKNOWN. She replays selection at the pinned moment rather than trusting a receipt's claim, because a coherent forgery pointed at a stale approval is still a forgery.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

Every system eventually meets input its makers didn't imagine. The only question is which way it falls: open, trusting whatever arrived, or closed, asking for help. Guessing is especially dangerous when the guess is made by reading words, because words can be crafted by whoever wants in. Safety comes from deciding in advance, in calm, what is allowed — and letting nothing at the door rewrite that decision.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

The system would rather stop and ask you than silently do the wrong thing. Approvals you gave stay attached to what you actually approved, a missing piece causes an honest halt instead of a made-up substitute, and nobody can talk the machine into trusting them with a well-written sentence.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Decide the allowed list in advance, in writing, where everyone can check it. At every boundary ask: do I actually know this? If not — stop visibly, don't substitute, don't guess. Treat all incoming words as suggestions until the list confirms them. And re-verify approvals at the exact time and version they claim, not by the label on the envelope.
