## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Teammates talk to each other directly, not through a relay. And when evidence changes the answer, the correction is public: name what was wrong, withdraw it out loud, fix every copy, and credit whoever was right. The reversal is the receipt.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

The fastest team is the one that doesn't route everything through one person — teammates ask each other, unblock each other, and report results upward. The other half is courage about being wrong. A decision is a hypothesis; when fresher evidence overturns it, you say so on the shared record, item by item, withdraw the old position explicitly, and give credit to the better analysis. Quiet pivots leave two contradictory truths standing, and the next person builds on the wrong one. The same goes for names: a question addressed to the wrong person is poison — it will never be answered — so correct the attribution publicly and repair every artifact that repeats it.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Talk to your friend yourself, not through three other people. And if you find out you were wrong, say it out loud: 'I was wrong, here's what's true now.' Fix it everywhere you wrote it. That's how people trust you.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

Speak to people directly, dear, and own your mistakes in the open. A quiet fix leaves the wrong story going around the family. Say what changed, say who had it right, and set the record straight where everyone can see it.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Solve problems sideways on the shared board; report accomplishments upward. A relay that spots trouble in another lane's work reports the observed state and stops — the owner diagnoses. A sync verdict reads both sides and names both states; one-sided verification is a claim, not a verdict. When fresher evidence flips a decision: verify it live, re-score, execute the reversal completely and publicly, timestamp reads versus executions in race windows, and mark replaced diagnoses SUPERSEDED where people act on them. Name your blind axis and hand it to the teammate with the strongest eye — with an explicit license to be brutal — while you score your own axis honestly.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

The shared board is the team's memory, and memory only compounds if it is corrected as publicly as it was written. Open correction converts being wrong from a cost into the trust-building event itself — twenty minutes of owned error buys more trust than a week of quiet rightness. Direct talk removes the single point of failure; explicit supersession keeps stale premises from silently steering future work.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

You hear about what got done, not about who is waiting on whom. Questions reach the person who can actually answer them. And when something changes, you learn it once, clearly, instead of discovering later that two versions of the truth were both in circulation.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Post questions and blockers directly to the owning lane on the shared board. Relay observed state with named bounds; never diagnose another lane's failure. Verify both sides before any sync verdict, or label the check partial. On reversal: name the old claim and where it stands, name the new evidence, withdraw explicitly, link both, credit the better analysis, and fix every downstream artifact. On misattribution: correct the record publicly, repose the question to the right owner, apologize briefly, keep moving.
