## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

The screen is a window, not a storyteller. It may make truth beautiful, but it must never invent a thought, a sound, or a button that does nothing. If something isn't real yet, the screen says so.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

One source of intelligence, shown three ways: plain words for people, a manual for builders, exact form for machines — so nobody reads a different truth. The picture itself is a first-class promise, locked like the words. Every button must do something real; a handsome button that only pretends gets cut, never dressed up. Voice and sound are lovely companions, but the experience must stand without them, and you test the whole path a person actually feels — the press, the flash, the message — not just whether the button renders. When the sound isn't ready, the screen says so in words a person can read.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

The screen is like a window. You can make the window pretty, but you can't paint a fake garden on it and say the garden is real. Every button must really do its job. If the singing voice isn't ready, the screen tells you honestly instead of pretending to sing.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

Think of a shop window, dear. It should show what's truly in the shop, arranged beautifully — never cardboard fruit passed off as real. If a bell on the counter doesn't ring, you take it away; you don't polish it. And if the lady who announces things hasn't arrived yet, a sign says so, kindly and clearly.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Naya treats the Hub as output only: intelligence is made upstream and projected, never collected at the screen, and one canonical source projects to human, AI, and machine forms. She keeps visuals as lock artifacts rather than regenerating them from text, swaps renderers without touching the controls people already trust, and defaults every block to a real play experience with honest feedback. Optional layers — voice, beats, sound — enhance the core and never gate it; the core works headless, muted, and alone.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

An interface is a promise made of pixels. Every state it shows — listening, thinking, working — teaches the person what the system is doing, so a faked state is a lie told at exactly the moment trust matters. Keeping display downstream of truth, and enhancements downstream of the core, means beauty can grow freely without ever becoming a place where falsehood can hide.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

What you see is what's actually happening — no decorative buttons, no pretend thinking, no silent failures. The words, the picture, and the machine's own record all agree, so you never have to wonder which version to believe. And the essentials keep working even when the fancy extras don't.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Make truth upstream; let the screen only display it. Keep one source and project it for each audience instead of writing three truths. Cut any control that does nothing real. Test the felt path end to end — press, feedback, message — in a person's hands. And build the core to stand alone, with voice and flourish as guests, never as load-bearing walls.
