## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

A passing test only proves something about the exact version it ran on, in the exact place it ran. Change one line, move to a different computer, or test yesterday's version — and the green proves nothing.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

'It passed on my machine' is a promise, not a proof. A repair proven against an old base can be correct and still insufficient, because the base moved. A green run on a stale head is not evidence for a merge. A suite can pass on one operating system and fail on another — and blaming a teammate's workspace without checking the platform is how real defects get dismissed. Even a bulk mechanical sweep is a change: every new commit invalidates the previous green, and a success badge over skipped jobs proves nothing at all.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Saying 'I checked it' only counts if you checked the real thing, the final thing, all of it. Checking your homework draft does not prove the handed-in page is right.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

You taste the soup you are serving, not the pot from yesterday. And you taste all of it, not just the top spoonful. That is all this means: proof has to match the thing itself, exactly as it will be served.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Naya pins every claim to exact bytes: the commit, the environment, the full check list across all workflows — not just the one suite she watched. She verifies on virgin state, never on a worktree her own repair already touched; she verifies the pushed bytes, not the pre-commit bytes; she re-verifies after any new commit, however mechanical; and she reads the job table, because a green badge over skipped jobs is vacuous. A second instrument, on a different platform, regularly finds what the first one missed.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

Most false confidence comes from a gap between what was tested and what ships. Closing that gap — same bytes, same environment, complete enumeration — is the difference between believing you are safe and knowing it. Permission to ship lives here too: a deploy authorization is bound to one exact version, and a failed dispatch means production is stale, not almost live.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

Fewer surprises after release. When someone tells you 'it is verified,' it means the exact thing you are about to use was checked, whole — not a cousin of it, not a draft of it.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Record the exact commit with every green. Re-run after any change, even a sweep. Verify in a clean environment and on the target platform. Enumerate every workflow's checks on the final commit, and open the job table to confirm nothing important was skipped. Treat another platform's failures as evidence to investigate, not noise to dismiss.
