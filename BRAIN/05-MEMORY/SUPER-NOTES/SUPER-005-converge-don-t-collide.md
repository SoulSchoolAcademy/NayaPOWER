## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Fast teams sometimes build the same thing twice — that's normal, and it's fixable without a fight. Check who's already working before you start, let the first good repair own the job, and stand your duplicate down gracefully with a pointer to the winner. One survivor, chosen openly — never two competing versions.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Duplicated effort is the tax on speed, and the cure is a shared etiquette with teeth. Before building, look for a claim — someone may already be mid-repair on the very seam you found. If two repairs race anyway, the first that qualifies owns the problem; the other closes itself as a duplicate, with proof the work is identical and a pointer to the survivor. That is a graceful outcome, not a defeat. When two designs compete, nobody grabs the pen: all versions are scored against criteria stated up front, and exactly one survives. And ownership never becomes turf — if a lane stalls on work others await, a qualified teammate finishes it, publishes the evidence, and tells the owner.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

If you and your friend both start drawing the same poster, don't fight. Check first if someone's already drawing it. If you both finished one, pick the better drawing together, keep it, and put a note on yours saying where the real one lives.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

In a busy kitchen, dear, two cooks sometimes start the same soup. The sensible thing isn't an argument — it's tasting both, keeping the better pot, and noting kindly that the other was set aside. And if one cook is stuck, another steps in and stirs. The meal matters, not whose spoon it was.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Before opening any build, search for in-flight claims on the seam — board, open work, repair registry — and honor a found claim by standing down and handing your findings to the owner. Re-check freshness immediately before pushing; a same-topic landing inside your window ends your repair. When duplicated: verify identity on the actual bytes, close yours as superseded with a pointer, and leave the winner to its author. Score competing versions against stated criteria before anyone proceeds. Take over stalled, blocking work when qualified — with evidence published and the owner notified. And when the director ships his own version, close yours and carry only what's missing.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

Convergence is what turns many parallel workers into one team instead of a crowd. Every duplicate merged 'just in case' doubles the surface future readers must reconcile, and every turf war burns the team's scarcest resource — attention. A culture where standing down is honored and takeovers are safe produces one strong version of everything, which is the only kind a growing system can stay coherent with.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

You get one clear answer instead of three rival documents. Work doesn't stall because one person is away, and nobody wastes days rebuilding what a teammate finished yesterday. When two approaches genuinely compete, you see them compared honestly against stated criteria — not settled by whoever shouted last.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Starting anything: spend the first minutes asking 'who already owns this?' If the answer is someone, help them or hand off — don't open a rival. If you discover mid-work that you've duplicated: stop, compare bytes, and close yours with a pointer if theirs qualifies. If you're blocked on a stalled owner, take over cleanly: read the current state first, finish, publish evidence, notify.
