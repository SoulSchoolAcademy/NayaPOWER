## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Many hands share one workspace, so check before you act: scan for work already in flight, take only free numbers, know where your tools land, and build on the live head — never a frozen pin. One change, one owner, no collisions.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Two people can both get the same instruction, both do the right thing — and produce two copies of one change. Nobody was wrong about the work; they were wrong about checking first. So before executing, scan the shared board, the open requests, and the branch tree for anything already doing this. Numbers live in one shared registry: compute the next free one from everything open, announce your claim, and if two claims collide, the first stands and the later one renumbers. Know where your shared tools write — a script with a hardcoded destination lands your work on someone else's branch. And on a shared branch, pull the live head before building; yesterday's pin is not today's base.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Before you grab a toy, look: is someone already playing with it? One person per job. If you both reach for the same number, whoever called it first keeps it. And always check what the pile looks like right now, not what it looked like yesterday.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like a shared kitchen, dear. Check who's already cooking before you start the same dish. Put your name on the list before you take a number. And look at the counter as it is now — not as it was this morning.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Every executor — lane or automated loop — scans before it acts: board claims, open work of the same class, the live branch tree. Loops are seats; their instructions carry the same scan, and when a loop produces a duplicate, repair the loop's instructions, not just the instance. One change, one vehicle; on collision, announce openly, consolidate on evidence quality, close the duplicate with rationale, preserve it, and patch the trigger. The registry scan is computed by enumerating everything open — never recalled from memory. Taking over a shared branch: name the exact base, declare no competing branch, list preserved versus changed scope, and request a bounded freeze with an end condition.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

Coordination loss is pure waste: duplicated work carries zero extra truth and costs a director decision to untangle. The cure is mechanical, not moral — computed scans, announced claims, first-claim rules, and tools whose destinations are known. The shared space stays trustworthy because collisions are prevented at execution time, not argued about afterwards.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

No duplicate work, no renumbering churn, no two versions of the same note to reconcile later. What you file lands at a stable address the first time, and the team's energy goes into the work instead of into untangling collisions.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Before executing: scan the board and enumerate every open item; filter for your change class and your numbering space. Post your intent before staging. Before building on a shared branch, fetch the live head and re-anchor; re-map any plan built on an old surface map. Check every shared tool's hardcoded branch and targets before trusting its output. On finding a duplicate: post the collision, keep the better-evidenced artifact, mark the other superseded, close it with rationale, and patch the mechanism that allowed the race.
