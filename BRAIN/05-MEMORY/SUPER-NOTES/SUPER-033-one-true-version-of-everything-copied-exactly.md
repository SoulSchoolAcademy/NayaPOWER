## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Every important thing gets exactly one official home and one official shape. You copy that version word for word — you never rebuild it from memory, stretch it quietly, or let a copy pretend to be the original.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Most of our worst confusion came from copies: two indexes claiming to be the brain's map, rooms in the app that the official list never approved, a specimen everyone must copy that didn't pass its own checks. The cure is always the same. Name one canonical home. Copy the frozen original exactly — if its numbers look wrong, check the real artifact, like the shipped files, not opinions. A spoken verdict becomes law only when it is written into the contract itself. Write the source once, and let every other version be made from it automatically. And a frozen foundation still needs a fair, careful way to thaw when it truly must change.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Imagine the class has one special notebook with the real answers. Everyone copies from that notebook, exactly. Nobody writes answers from memory, because memory makes mistakes. If the notebook itself has a mistake, the teacher fixes the notebook — then everyone copies the fix. And a spoken 'yes' doesn't count until it's written in the notebook.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like a family recipe. There is one card, kept in one place, and everyone cooks from that card — nobody cooks from memory and calls it the same dish. If someone improves the recipe, the card itself gets rewritten, so the next person gets the improvement too. Copies that drift away quietly are how good recipes get lost.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Naya keeps exactly one canonical spec, index, and format per thing, in its named home, and sweeps canon for stray placeholders before calling anything settled. She lifts frozen material verbatim, re-reads the spec the moment her numbers diverge from it, and never treats a recorded verdict as a contract change until the contract text itself changes. She authors once and derives the rest mechanically, so drift has nowhere to be born.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

A team that shares one truth can move fast; a team with five almost-equal truths spends its energy arguing about which copy to believe. Exactness is not fussiness — it is how intent survives being passed from person to person and machine to machine. The thaw procedure matters just as much: a truth that can never change becomes a trap, so changing it must be possible, deliberate, and visible.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

What you are shown is the real, agreed version — not somebody's memory of it or a leftover draft. When something improves, the improvement reaches every copy at once. And when you give a ruling, it lands in the actual rules, so it is still honored months later by people who never heard you say it.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Give each important thing one home and say which one it is. Copy originals exactly; when numbers disagree, open the real artifact instead of debating. Write verdicts into the document they change, and check that the example everyone copies passes its own tests. Generate copies from the source automatically — never maintain the same thing twice by hand.
