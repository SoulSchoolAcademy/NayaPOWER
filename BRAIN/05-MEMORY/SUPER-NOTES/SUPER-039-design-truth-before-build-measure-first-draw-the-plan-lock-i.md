## ✦ IN A NUTSHELL

Never build from a vague idea. First measure what already exists, then draw the full plan — what goes where, why, and what proves it done — get it checked and locked, and only then build. Improvising is the defect; the drawing is the cure.

---

## 🩷 HUMAN NOTE

Think architect and contractor. The architect draws the house to the inch, the client approves the drawings, and only then does the contractor pour the foundation. Building from a napkin sketch guarantees every wall gets re-done. So the team measures the real ground first — pinning the exact starting point and sorting every claim into proven, built, documented, claimed, or missing — then writes a blueprint deep enough that the builder already knows what belongs, where it goes, and what proves it complete. Ideas from everyone converge into one plan before anyone draws. The loop closes against the acceptance contract, not against a count of tries.

---

## 🟣 CHILD NOTE

Draw the picture before you build the tower. Look at what you already have, decide exactly where every block goes, show the drawing to someone who checks it, and then build it once, the right way.

---

## 👵 GRANDMA NOTE

It's like sewing from a proper pattern instead of guessing. You measure first, you lay out the pattern, you check it twice — and then the dress fits the first time, with no ripping out seams.

---

## 🤖 NAYA NOTE

Before any build: measure the surface — pin the exact base, inventory what exists, classify every claim by evidence tier. If the intent isn't spec'd deeply enough that the builder knows what belongs, where, why, what every control does, what data it consumes, and what proves it complete, the first deliverable is the spec, not the build. Converge all lanes' ideas into one blueprint, get it independently reviewed for completeness and contradictions, lock it, then build exactly to it. When a build regresses into improvise-correct-rewrite, diagnose a spec-depth failure, not a builder failure — and re-spec before re-building.

---

## WHAT IT MEANS

The rewrite loop was never a building problem; it was a drawing problem. Deep specification moves the cost of being wrong to the cheapest moment — before anything is built — and turns acceptance into a contract that can actually close, instead of an endless series of corrections. It also protects honesty: a team that measures and classifies before designing cannot mistake a claim for a fact.

---

## WHAT'S IN IT FOR YOU

Work gets done once instead of five times. You approve a drawing, not a surprise, and what arrives matches what you asked for. Your corrections stop being the quality system — the blueprint carries them before the first nail.

---

## HOW IT APPLIES

Pin the exact base. Classify every existing claim: proven, implemented, documented, claimed, missing. Write the blueprint: entry state, composition, controls and why each is placed, data contract, honest states, handoffs, mobile behavior, accessibility, acceptance journey, anti-patterns, required proof. Have a different seat review it for completeness and contradictions. Lock it. Build to the lock. Close against the acceptance contract — ten tries or two hundred are fine while the contract is open; none count once it closes.
