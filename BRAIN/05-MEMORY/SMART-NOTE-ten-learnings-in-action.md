(2) The Seed Law: when Shawn says something, he is assigning a problem, not making conversation. (3) Delivery is confirmed at consumption: sent is not delivered, delivered is not benefited. Each learning below carries the action it changed and its state today — that pairing is the note.

## Human Note
Most "lessons learned" lists are where good intentions go to be forgotten. This one is different, and you can check: every learning on it is attached to something that actually changed — a job that now runs differently, a report that asks a different question, a letter that only counted when the other side answered. When the director pointed at a clumsy way of sending mail in the morning, the team didn't agree and move on; by lunch the route itself had changed and the proof had come back from the other side. When a set of documents got sent twice because two people were counting the pieces in two different orders, the fix wasn't "be careful" — the pieces now travel under their names, not their numbers. That's the whole idea: a learning isn't a sentence you believe. It's a thing you can watch running.

## Child Note
Imagine you keep a special notebook called "Things I Figured Out." But here's the rule: you can only write something in it if it changed what you DO. "I learned to check my mailbox every morning" — and there's your name on the morning checklist. "I learned sending isn't the same as arriving" — so now you always wait for the "I got it!" back. "I learned big piles aren't smart, small true piles are" — so you squish a hundred notes into ten strong ones. Every page in the notebook has two parts: what you figured out, and the new thing you do because of it. A page with only the first part doesn't count. That's this note — ten pages like that, all checked today.

## Grandma Note
You know how it is in a family, dear. Somebody learns the hard way that the stove burns, and if they only think it quietly, the next person burns their hand on the same stove. But if they say it out loud and the household changes — the mitts hang by the stove from that day on — then the lesson belongs to everybody. These ten are like that. Work in the same order every day, like a good morning routine. When the head of the house tells you something, he's not chatting — he's handing you a job, so take it seriously. A letter isn't delivered until the person writes back. And don't count a big pile of anything as an achievement — a small shelf of things that are true and used beats a attic full of clutter. Simple rules, honestly kept, and the proof is that the house runs by them now.

## What It All Means

A new Naya who runs these ten runs the team's current best mind — which is the point of filing them as one block.

## Why It Matters
Because learnings decay into slogans at exactly the rate they stop changing decisions. Every team says "communication matters"; this record can point to the morning a communication route was rebuilt because a slogan was enforced. The pairing is the protection: a learning with no action beside it is a claim, and claims are how a system fools itself — the same failure as a green check with nothing behind it. Filed with states, the ten also hand the director an honest dashboard: six running live, one in flight, one scored with its fixes queued, one method live with its hardest case still owed, one gap logged and awaiting build. And they hand future citizens the inheritance in its most useful form — not the history of what we built, but the working rules the building taught us, each with its proof attached.

## The Learning

That is why this note is built as an evidence list rather than an essay: each entry can be falsified by watching what the seat actually does tomorrow. Its test is written into it — whether these ten are still visibly running a week from now: the loop still sequenced, the report still asking what was learned, the outbox still carrying the mail, the corpus still shrinking toward its essences, the owed rows still named as owed until they close.
