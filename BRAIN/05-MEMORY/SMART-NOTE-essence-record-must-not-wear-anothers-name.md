## In a Nutshell

Every name, number, and disappearance in the record must trace to its real source, or the record says so plainly. A commit is a signature: commit 7cf4be29 landed wearing the Director's name through a shared clone's repo-level git config, written by a seat who never wrote it (SN-697, SN-701). A ratification quote is provenance only when it resolves to an exact seat, chat, and message: six "Director decisions" were posted quoting words spoken before the Director's first message that day existed (SN-682), and the flag against them was itself seat-relative until a real citation — chat c355ed3a, turn 63934307, seq 29045 — completed the lifecycle and the flag was withdrawn in public (SN-686). A number that cannot be traced does not travel: "17 of 23 flagged" could not be located anywhere, and the seat who had repeated it halted on it in writing (SN-688). ## Essence

**Attribution is fail-closed.** In a shared clone, the repo-level `.git/config` carried the Director's name and email, so a kernel note (LEARNING-INTERNALIZATION-AUTOMATIC-BEHAVIOR-V1.md) was authored and committed as "Shawn Vibert" by a seat who arrived to find it already signed "( naya 1 )" (SN-697). The repair is structural, not apologetic: unset `user.name`/`user.email` at repo level so the next commit *refuses* until the seat configures its own identity. Never repair a false attribution with a second false attribution, and leave authorship OPEN until someone steps forward with evidence — the mechanism being right does not identify the author (SN-697). At the envelope altitude, the same incident is an authority violation: a commit wearing the Director's name borrows authority it never earned. A 65-directory audit found a second live instance (a kernel-red/repo common dir serving 7 linked worktrees; 8 directories total would have stamped the Director's name); both were unset reversibly, and a standing guard, `identity_guard.py`, now exits 1 on any repo-level protected identity (SN-701). The control lesson is exact: `git commit --dry-run` is NOT a valid identity control — it short-circuits before identity resolution — so identity controls are proven with real commits in a throwaway repo (no identity → rc=128 refused; seat identity → rc=0). No history rewrite on main: record the correction forward (SN-701).

**Ratification claims carry their citation or they do not stand.** Six "Director decisions" were posted at 13:52:14Z quoting the Director at ~13:50Z; his first message that day was 13:52:52Z — after the post — and the quoted words were absent from the transcript. PR #1863 then merged at 13:55:27Z setting `ratified_by: "Shawn Vibert"` into canon. The verdict was provenance UNVERIFIED, downstream work frozen, and the revert named as the Director's call alone (SN-682). The check runs BEFORE merge, in three parts: (1) identify the exact transcript via the chat list; (2) compare the claimed time against the Director's actual timestamps — a citation dated before his first message is self-refuting; (3) grep the quote verbatim (SN-682). But the falsification itself has a scope: the seat who raised the flag had checked *her own* chat (1b555a63), while the real citation lived in another seat's chat (c355ed3a, turn 63934307, seq 29045, 13:50:35Z, on Naya 2's receipt). So ratification records cite seat + chat id + message id; a seat-relative check cannot falsify another seat's chat — narrow the flag to the checked seat; the Director's own confirmation is the tiebreaker; and a defeated flag is withdrawn publicly, the same turn, naming the miss (SN-686). The two notes are one lifecycle — flag → receipt → narrowing → withdrawal, with the Director confirming at ~14:31Z — and both stand: the lesson is seat-relativity, in both directions (SN-682, SN-686).

**An untraceable number does not travel.** A governance audit's "17 of 23 flagged" could not be located on board #1354 or #1867, and the Law writer who went looking found real drift in only 2 of 14 items fully checked. The seat who had already repeated the number upward wrote the halt herself: "I already repeated the '17 of 23' number to Shawn; I won't keep repeating a number I can't trace." The rule: upward metrics need a three-part citation — which audit, where posted, the per-item flag list — or they don't travel (SN-688). The 17-of-23 audit itself remains UNTRACED and open; this essence does not resolve it, and the conflict is named, not smoothed: SN-687 (which stands alone in this block on its own authority) repeats "17 flagged" as fact — the exact number SN-688 halts on (SN-688).

**A citation is not provenance until the file resolves.** An experience-seat audit of the Smart Blocks library *resolved* provenance claims instead of reading them: 15 of 33 blocks were byte-verbatim against existing sources; the other 18 (all nl-*) cited `Naya_Lego-blocks.html` — a file that exists nowhere: not on main, not on any branch, not in any worktree, not in user files. Eighteen citations pointing at nothing: the claim was a string, not provenance. The gap was named plainly as an open blocker with an owner (the Director's file, to be vendored), not buried as a footnote (SN-752). Provenance also has two halves — a resolvable source AND the why-it-exists statement; the same audit shipped the `## Why` layer (why-it-exists + human value) into all 33 block READMEs, where it had been 0 of 33, plus the missing nl-toast specimen and its null index.json pointer fix, with zero duplication across lanes (SN-752).

**Vanished is not healed, and a deadlock is a classification, not a stall.** When Workers Builds failures fell 17→2→0 across tips, the later tips had zero check-runs at all (53217a40, 871e283f, 65269857): the instrument had been removed, not the defect repaired. The record for that is UNATTRIBUTED EXTERNAL CHANGE — who and when UNKNOWN — the RED class stays OPEN, the reversion risk is named, and verdicts are per-tip only (SN-677). And when two repairs are each other's precondition — PR #1840's collection fix (1340 passed) unmasked 4 REDs split 1/2/1 across lanes, while PR #1838's registrations and #1840's fix were mutual preconditions, so neither unblocker goes green alone — the honest act is to classify the deadlock, route each RED to exactly one owner, touch no other lane's branch, name the unowned remainder as TBD with a standing offer, and let the wave owner decide the stacked path (SN-681).

## What this settles

- A commit is a signature: identity is configured per-seat and fail-closed; a shared clone never carries the Director's identity at repo level (SN-697, SN-701).
- Identity controls are proven with real commits in a throwaway repo; `--dry-run` proves nothing about identity (SN-701).
- Ratification records cite seat + chat id + message id; pre-first-message citations are self-refuting; flags narrow to the seat actually checked and defeated flags are withdrawn publicly, same turn (SN-682, SN-686).
- Upward metrics travel only with a three-part citation (audit, venue, per-item list); an untraceable number is halted on, in writing (SN-688).
- A citation resolves to bytes or it is a named open blocker with an owner — never a footnote (SN-752).
- A vanished failure with no check-runs is an unattributed external change; the RED class stays open (SN-677).
- Mutual-precondition repairs are classified as deadlock and routed one RED per owner; no lane touches another's branch (SN-681).

## Human

A shared record only works if every entry in it is honest about where it came from. This essence is about the four ways a record can quietly lie: it can wear the wrong person's name, it can quote words that were never said in the place claimed, it can repeat a number nobody can find the source of, and it can point at evidence that doesn't exist. Each time, the repair was the same shape: trace it to the real source, and where the source can't be found, write *that* down — plainly, with an owner — instead of letting the claim stand.

## Child

Imagine a class notebook where everyone writes what they did. If someone writes your name under their work, that's not a small mistake — now everyone thinks you did it. If someone writes "the teacher said so" but the teacher never said it, that's a lie that grows. The rule is: write only your own name, only quote what was really said and say exactly where, and if you can't find where a number came from, don't pass it on — write "I can't find it" instead.

## Grandma

Dear, it's the old rule of the family Bible and the household ledger: you sign your own name, you quote a person only when you heard them say it, and you never pass along a figure you couldn't show the workings for. If a page points to a letter that isn't in the drawer, you say the letter is missing — you don't pretend the reference is the same as the letter. And when a trouble simply stops appearing, you don't declare it fixed; you note that it stopped, and that you don't know why.

## Naya

Before you write a name, check whose it is: your identity is configured, fail-closed, and a commit under any other name — above all the Director's — is an authority violation, never a convenience (SN-697, SN-701). Before you cite a ratification, resolve it: seat, chat, message id, verbatim words, and a timestamp that can exist (SN-682); and when you falsify, falsify only the seat you actually checked, and withdraw in public when the receipt defeats you (SN-686). Before you repeat a number upward, hold its three-part citation or halt on it in writing (SN-688). Before you lean on a citation, resolve the file — a string is not provenance (SN-752). And when a failure vanishes or two repairs deadlock, classify honestly: unattributed change, class still open; deadlock, one owner per RED (SN-677, SN-681).

## What It All Means

Eight notes are one discipline at four altitudes: the record's *attributions* must be true. The commit signature, the ratification quote, the repeated metric, and the cited source are all the same object — a claim that something came from somewhere — and each note is a case where that claim was checked against reality and either repaired structurally (fail-closed identity, a standing guard), bounded honestly (UNVERIFIED, UNTRACED, PENDING), or completed through its full lifecycle (flag, receipt, narrowing, public withdrawal). A cold reader holding only this essence can still audit any attribution in the system: resolve it, or name the gap with an owner.

## Why It Matters

Attribution is where authority enters the record. A commit under the Director's name doesn't just mislabel work — it borrows his authority for everything downstream that trusts the signature. A ratification quote that never happened manufactures law. A number repeated upward becomes the basis for decisions its source never supported. And a citation pointing at nothing lets eighteen artifacts claim a pedigree they don't have. Each of these lies compounds silently because later work *trusts the record instead of re-checking it* — which is exactly why the check has to happen at the record, every time.

## What Is In It For You

You get a record you can act on without re-auditing every line: names mean the person actually did it, quotes resolve to a real message, numbers carry their source, and gaps are labeled as gaps with owners instead of hiding inside confident prose. Apply it anywhere a shared log, ledger, or history drives decisions: sign fail-closed, cite to the message, halt on untraceable figures, resolve every citation to bytes — and when the evidence isn't there, the honest sentence "this is unverified, and here is whose call it is" is worth more than a clean-looking claim.

## The Learning

The corpus learned that attribution failures are configuration failures before they are character failures: the false signature came from a shared clone's config, so the fix was to make the wrong thing impossible (unset the identity, add the guard) rather than to ask seats to be careful (SN-697, SN-701). It learned that falsification has a scope — a seat-relative check produced a confident UNVERIFIED verdict that a complete citation overturned within the hour, and the public withdrawal is part of the method, not an embarrassment appended to it (SN-682, SN-686). And it learned that the cheapest sentence in the system is the halt: "I won't keep repeating a number I can't trace" costs one line and stops a fiction from becoming a budget (SN-688).
