## In a Nutshell
On 2026-10-20 the system counted itself honestly: 662 open changes, 473 of them untouched for a week, about five starts for every finish — a reservoir, not a queue, because work flowed in and almost nothing flowed out. The cure was never "work harder." It was: give every open item exactly one of three endings (merge it verified, fix its one named gap, or close it with the reason written down), cap starting by what you finished, make the pile print itself nightly, and treat a clogged landing door as the emergency it is — because finished work that cannot land is worth zero. When the door itself was the bottleneck, the fix was to measure the door, name the toll, and drain — 52 open landings to single digits in one evening, with proof on every landing and the slips named in the open, never backdated.

## Essence
The reservoir was measured, not vibes: "open pull requests 662 · untouched ≥7 days 473 (71%) · drafts 77 · oldest #870 (2026-09-28) · created last 7 days 229 · merged last 7 days 49 · ratio 4.7 starts per finish · work reports in 24h 312 · finished right 46 (14.7%) · re-proofs ~47% · headline scores unchanged for 7 nights" (SN-0805). Of 312 work reports filed in one day, 46 finished something right, and nearly half the rest were re-proofs (SN-0805). The seven root causes, enumerated: (1) creating was free, finishing was serial; (2) the machine paid for starts; (3) nobody owned closing; (4) parallel duplication by design — five branch families held 500+ of the 662; (5) the task lists caught the same disease; (6) the limit existed as text, never as machinery — "a rule with no gate is a wish"; (7) the comment flood (SN-0805). The dispositions are exactly three — "MERGE (clears the bar, independently verified) / FIX (one named gap, named owner) / CLOSE (superseded, duplicated, or dead — reason written on the item)" — and "there is no fourth pile called 'someday'" (SN-0805). Prevention is mechanical: a lane's creation budget is last week's completions plus two; untouched work is listed at 14 days and closed at 21 unless its owner re-justifies it in writing; the reservoir prints nightly — open count, age, created-to-finished ratio (SN-0805). Done for a change means one of two terminal states — MERGED-VERIFIED (on main, effect verified at the real address) or CLOSED-ON-PURPOSE (reason recorded: SUPERSEDED-BY, NO-PURPOSE, DUPLICATE-OF, or DECISION) — and every author owns their merge to one of those endings, checking gates promptly and classifying every failure MINE, BASELINE, OTHER-SEAT, or CONTENT (SN-0812). ## What this settles
- Starting is not production. The only reported metric is completions; creation is budgeted by finishing (completions + 2).
- Every open item ends MERGED-VERIFIED or CLOSED-ON-PURPOSE; "someday" is not a state.
- A queue problem is a door problem until proven otherwise: measure the door, name the toll, drain.
- The hard truth about the ratio is owed the day it is seen — honesty was never behind a gate.
- Waste is paid twice; the second bill (triage, rework, verification, attention) is the one nobody budgets.

## What It All Means
An intelligent system's scarcest resource is not effort — it is finished, verified outcomes. The reservoir is what happens when creation is free and finishing is serial: activity becomes indistinguishable from progress, and the pile itself becomes the hiding place. The drain discipline converts work back into a flow with terminal states, budgets, and a door that is measured like any other machine part.

## Why It Matters
Because you have lived the alternative: a month at ~1% efficiency (173 actions per verified outcome) where every seat was busy and almost nothing landed. Left alone, the reservoir refills — the mechanisms here (caps, expiry, nightly print, door measurement) are what keep it empty when nobody is watching. And the second bill is real: every unfinished artifact charges you again in triage, rework, verification, and attention.
