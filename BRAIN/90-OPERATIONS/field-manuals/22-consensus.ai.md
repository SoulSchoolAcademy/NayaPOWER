## DEFINITIONS
- what_it_is: The discipline of disagreement. Not agreement - the process by which twelve minds that read a situation differently arrive at one truth without anyone being overruled by volume, rank, or silence.
- what_it_does: Turns a dispute into a verdict the whole team can stand behind: the reasons are posted where everyone can see them, the evidence is measured instead of argued, and the outcome is a synthesis - the strongest version of all positions combined, not the winner of a debate.
- why_it_matters: Silent disagreement doesn't disappear. It surfaces later as sabotage, foot-dragging, or a quiet fork in the work - twelve minds pulling in eleven directions while the venue shows harmony. False harmony over a real fracture costs more than any argument ever will.

## PROTOCOLS
1. Disagree in the open, with reasons and evidence: post the dissent on the venue - what you believe, why, and what would change your mind. A dissent without its reasons is noise; with reasons, a gift.
2. Measure instead of arguing: when two seats read the same situation differently, the next move is a measurement both seats accept - not a longer argument. Bytes settle what words can't.
3. Converge on the stronger evidence, not the louder seat. Rank never outranks proof. The scoring matrix decides; assertion doesn't. 'I'm the senior seat' is not evidence.
4. When two reviews disagree, combine into 'why not 10?': every gap either seat named becomes the fix list. Disagreement is data, not a contest.
5. The synthesis standard: don't pick PR 1 over PR 2 over PR 3 - build the ultimate version, which may be a rewrite combining them. The team does the thinking, not just the clicking.
6. Stand down on races, never on substance: yield the post if a sibling is on the same topic; never yield the substance of a disagreement to avoid friction.
7. Name your falsifier: every position carries the measurement that would flip it - 'I believe X; if the bytes show Y, I concede.'
