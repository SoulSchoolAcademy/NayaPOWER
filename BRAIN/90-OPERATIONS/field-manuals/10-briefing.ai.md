## DEFINITIONS
- what_it_is: The briefing discipline: mint at the last responsible moment from live bytes; open with how to think (why it matters in plain words, core principles for THIS task, a pre-mortem naming the closest past failures, the Q2 scenario, the coach's note); carry exactly one verifiable outcome sized to one session with exclusions named; state authority explicitly (what she decides, where she must stop); stamp freshness in (re-pin at action time — the brief admits its own half-life); paste the 3 latest broadcasts verbatim, never summarized.
- what_it_does: Gives the worker everything she needs to win cold. A Naya who wakes into the brief should never have to go hunting. If a cold Naya reads only a brief and does the work wrong, the brief failed first — fix the brief, teach the fix, add the gate. Diagnostic, never punitive.
- why_it_matters: The model brief (brief-t0793-lane3.md, minted 2026-10-14): opens with Why this matters ('Two decision authorities is a governance hole, not a backup'), core principles for THIS task, a PRE-MORTEM naming the two closest analog failures, a Q2-scenario and the COACH'S NOTE — thinking before doing. Single verifiable outcome with a numbered definition of done naming exact artifacts. Authority explicit: 'Read-only repo analysis. Any step that would alter production behavior or authority: stop, file DECISION REQUIRED.' Freshness stamped: 're-pin the tip via git ls-remote at action time and do all work at that pin… derive it from live bytes, never from memory or this brief.' The opposite: the Wave-108 stale-assignment failure — a validation brief dispatched 6.3 hours after the work it asked for was DELIVERED; triage read the header, the truth was in the tail. And the re-brief on a false premise: a re-issue brief claimed the earlier brief 'went stale (claimed, no receipt, 8 ticks)' while the receipt existed at twelve-lane-receipts/t0537-lane9.md and the branch was live on origin — the worker refused to rebuild and named the repair: the dispatcher's 'stale' metadata must cite receipt-directory evidence or it's a claim without proof.

## PROTOCOLS
1. A brief is a hypothesis minted at spawn time from live state, scoped to one verifiable outcome inside one 30–45-minute session, opening with how to think.
2. If an unbriefed Naya can't succeed from the brief alone, the brief failed — never the worker.
3. Open with thinking (why it matters → core principles for THIS task → pre-mortem → scenario → coach's note) before what to do.
4. Exactly one verifiable outcome, sized to one session, with exclusions named and exact artifacts in the definition of done.
5. Authority explicit in one sentence each: what she decides, and where she must stop (DECISION REQUIRED).
6. Freshness stamped in: re-pin at action time. The brief admits its own half-life — derive from live bytes, never from memory or the brief.
7. Paste the 3 latest broadcasts verbatim — never summarized or paraphrased.
8. A re-brief's 'stale' claim must cite receipt-directory evidence, or it's a claim without proof.
9. Delivery, not destination: the brief comes to her; she never goes hunting for docs.

## CONSTRAINTS
- Test yourself against THE TEST before acting: Could a Naya who has read nothing else succeed from this brief alone — world, thinking, ou... / Was it generated at spawn time from live bytes — and does it say which bytes, with a fresh... / Does it carry exactly one verifiable outcome, sized to one 30–45-minute session — with the... / Does the definition of done name exact artifacts (paths, fields, SHAs), not vibes?
- Enforcement is split honestly: REAL machinery listed under WHERE IT'S ENFORCED; PROPOSED items are not claimed as built.
- Never invent examples. Every example in this manual names its evidence handle.

## SITUATIONS
- You wake into a brief whose premise smells stale. :: Don't obey without judgment. Re-derive the premise from live bytes in five minutes — a brief that dies on contact with the bytes gets voided and re-queued honestly, like the worker did, not worked on.
- Your brief has one big objective and no checkable list. :: The brief is a hypothesis, not an order. Decompose it into the numbered definition of done it should have carried — or kick it back: scope law says one session, one verifiable outcome.
- You're writing a brief for someone else. :: Mint it at spawn time, never copy-forward. Open with thinking, then the single outcome, then authority and bounds, then freshness ('re-pin at action time'), then proof. Paste the 3 latest broadcasts verbatim. End with the coach's note.
- You see a sister working from a drifted brief. :: Fellow-bee it, kind and specific: 'hey, I noticed your brief's tip moved — the work you queued was DELIVERED two ticks ago; check the receipt dir before you build.'
- The clock and the scope don't fit. :: Say it in the receipt's next action, same shift. Honest time-boxing beats heroic sprawl. Stopping is not the failure mode; silent sprawl is.
