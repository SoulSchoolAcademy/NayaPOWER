Rank by mission value × unlock ÷ effort — never by arrival order, loudness, or who shouted last; dependencies outrank leaves, and ties go to learning.

### THE WRONG WAY
**FIFO dispatch.** Dispatching in arrival order because "it was here first." The dispatcher's STEP 3 exists precisely to forbid this: feedback first, then SURGE, then the to-do list, filtered by mission impact. First-in is not first-in-value.

**Ranking by loudness or recency.** The board's measured failure mode is REDUNDANCY WITHOUT DELTA — template repetitions burying signal under volume (cross-board intelligence: the board floor sits near 3/10 because of noise, not vacuity). Working what shouts loudest, or what arrived last, starves what matters. Recency is the laziest ranking function there is.

**Losing the top item — the phantom claim.** The scorecard brief that was the single merge blocker for milestone 1 sat in claimed/ for 18+ minutes with no lane-feed sign-in while the rotation still listed it queued: the queue had silently lost the critical-path brief and no job watched claimed/. A queue that loses its top item has no ranking at all — just a pile. The repair was a claim-liveness audit wired into the dispatcher. (Evidence: `~/MEMORY.md`, THE PHANTOM-CLAIM LAW, 2026-10-13 ~16:10 PDT.)

**Dispatching to occupied lanes.** One tick wrote new briefs to lanes already holding two queued briefs each — the queue absorbed the backlog, but the ranking had ignored lane capacity. Dispatch is PULL to free lanes only; a rank that can't be executed is a wish list. (Evidence: `~/memory/2026-10-13.md` — t0704 dispatched to already-occupied lanes 1/2/10; queue-level discipline flagged for the next director pass.)

**Letting a stale claim rot at the top.** Wave 149's three executors signed in, went silent for ~2 hours, and the main-seat adjudication stalled all three per the dead-lane rule — the repair was releasing the claims back to the ranked queue. A rank that never re-ranks stale claims rots from the top. (Evidence: live-plan.md TOP-10, 2026-10-14 16:38 PDT tick, L24 dead-claim instance: claims released to the ranked queue; verification check: claim-then-sign-out within the wave window or the claim is void.)

### THE TEST
1. Can you state the top item and defend it in one sentence — would you bet the hour on it?
2. Is the order driven by value × unlock ÷ effort, or by arrival, loudness, or recency?
3. Does anything ranked above another item unblock it — are dependencies ahead of their leaves?
4. Can the top items actually be worked — or can only one worker touch them while the free lanes idle? A rank nobody can pull from is a wish list, not a queue.
5. Is every ranked item assigned to a FREE lane (pull only), or are you stacking onto occupied lanes?
6. Did closed items drop with their proof, and did new holes enter with named owners?
7. If two items tie, does learning win?
8. Does Shawn's dispatch law change the order (lowest numbers first, activation gated, learning built second) — and did you apply it instead of skipping it?

### WHEN YOU HIT THIS SITUATION
**You're handed a queue of N items and one hour.** Run the calculus on each: mission value × what it unblocks ÷ the effort to do it. Put unlockers before the work they unlock. Write the one-sentence defense for #1. If you can't defend #1, you don't understand the queue — go read live state before you touch anything.

**A claim goes quiet.** Run the liveness audit: sign-in within the window, receipt after 6 ticks. No sign-in → phantom claim, void it back to the queue. Sign-in but no receipt after 6 ticks → STALE, release it. Never let a silent claim hold the top slot — the top of the queue is for work that's moving.

**Two items tie.** Learning wins. If near-instant learning means other gains depend on it, build it first — that's Shawn's dispatch law, and it overrides the mechanical rank.

**A new hole appears mid-tick.** It enters the ranked list with a named owner at its computed position — never appended at the bottom by default, never shoved to the top by recency.

**The whole queue stalls.** Check the ranking, not the workers. A stalled queue is almost always a ranking failure: a phantom claim at the top, dispatch stacked on occupied lanes, or dependencies buried under their leaves. Fix the rank first — every worker downstream multiplies what the ranking got right or wrong.
