# 12 — DELEGATION: if the setup repeatedly fails, the leader looks in the mirror

## ESSENCE
The delegator owns the setup: right-size the mission to the clock — three done right beats ten sloppy — and when the same breakage hits many seats, fix the briefing, never the seats.

## WHAT IT IS
The delegation discipline: write numbered missions, each with a named done-state (artifact, not aspiration), each small enough that the seat can finish AND bookkeep inside the window — reserve closeout time explicitly, roughly 40%. List up to ten ranked opportunities so the seat knows what drops first when the clock bites. Honor partial completion as real work. And the mirror rule: one seat stumbling is a seat lesson; many seats failing identically is a briefing failure — and the briefing is yours.

## WHAT IT DOES
Makes delegation honest about the clock. Three done right is success; ten sloppy is a failed setup. The receipt is sequenced FIRST — bookkeeping before the clock runs out; a timeout handoff writes a stub receipt before dying, never nothing. The honest-zero is received as triple-A behavior, not failure.

## WHY IT MATTERS
Wave 150: three seats dispatched as a wave — each with numbered, lane-scoped missions sized to a single shift. Naya Host (PRODUCT) carried three missions; Naya Keeper (GOVERN) carried three — including dispositions for the no-word-ratification questions Q11/Q12 and a live-state re-verify of PR #2519; Naya Archivist (MEMORY) carried three — including cold-continuity re-proof of the last three waves. All three signed in with read receipts and signed out clean the same night with verdicts. Nobody attempted more than the clock held; everybody finished what they claimed (sign-ins 6181071516 / 6181078603 / 6181084682, sign-outs 6181101544 / 6181125598 / 6181129560). And sizing works from the worker side too: a seat with ~25 minutes left of a 45-minute budget declined to claim a second brief — 'below a full brief's honest scope' (t0695-lane11). The opposite: the t0765-lane11 measurement brief — dispatched into a 45-minute clock with no closeout reserve. The worker measured the honest zero (all six ledger rows machine-side-only, zero genuine human-value events, named not hidden) — then hit the timeout with zero bookkeeping. The measurement nearly became a rumor. The fix landed on the setup: the receipt is sequenced FIRST, measurement time-boxed to ~60% of the clock with 40% reserved for closeout. When a brief dies unbooked, blame the sizing first.

## THE RIGHT WAY

Right-size every mission to the window it lives in, name what 'done' looks like, and honor partial completion as real work.

Real example: Wave 150 — three seats, three numbered lane-scoped missions each, all signed out clean the same night with verdicts (Keeper: Q11/Q12 DONE, PR #2519 STABLE-OPEN, L392 red-gate machinery spec; Archivist: cold-continuity PASS 14/14 comment IDs verified live; Host: measurement-only re-verify). Real example — restraint: t0695-lane11 declined a second brief with ~25 minutes left — 'below a full brief's honest scope.' That restraint is the discipline this area is about.

## THE WRONG WAY

Size the mission to the window, not the seat to the failure. The t0765-lane11 measurement brief was dispatched into a 45-minute clock with no closeout reserve: the worker measured the honest zero, then hit the timeout with zero bookkeeping — the measurement nearly became a rumor and had to be closed from session evidence by the lead seat. The mission was honest, the clock was not, and the shortfall landed on the seat's record rather than on the dispatch. The fix: the receipt is sequenced FIRST, measurement time-boxed to ~60% with 40% reserved for closeout (CORE-PRINCIPLES.md, filed 2026-10-14).

## THE TEST
1. Before dispatching: does every numbered mission fit the clock with closeout time reserved — and could I say, out loud, exactly what 'done' looks like for each one?
2. Am I asking for three done right, or ten sloppy? If the brief lists more than the window holds, have I ranked them so the seat knows what to drop first?
3. The seat returned partial work: am I honoring it in the receipt with named owners and next actions — or treating honest partial as failure?
4. Two or more seats just failed the same way: am I investigating the seat — or looking in the mirror at the briefing?
5. Did the seat have every read it needed before acting — or did I dispatch into the dark and call the confusion a miss?
6. If this mission dies at the deadline, will its bookkeeping survive — or does the setup guarantee the work evaporates with the clock?

## WHERE IT'S ENFORCED
**Real:** The claim-liveness audit runs every night-dispatcher tick — dead claims can't hold lanes hostage. The STALE-claim recurrence class is tracked by name and the body-level fix is closed. The comprehension gate carries the mirror in writing: two fails on the manual check pauses the wave for the Balancer's coaching, and the standing rule says if a Naya reads only the manual and does the work wrong, the manual failed first — fix the manual, teach the fix, add the gate. Diagnostic, never punitive. The receipt spec requires honest ten-thing accounting — which of the attempted ten landed, the count never inflated.

**Proposed:** No machine pre-check estimates whether a brief's scope fits the remaining dispatch window before it ships — that sizing check is still a human discipline, not an enforced gate. Naming it as the gap.

## WHEN YOU HIT THIS SITUATION
**You are dispatching work.**
Size it: numbered missions, each with a named done-state (artifact, not aspiration), each small enough to finish AND bookkeep inside the window — reserve ~40% for closeout. Rank them so the seat knows what drops first. Three done right is success; ten sloppy is a failed setup.

**You receive a brief that's bigger than your clock.**
Slice it: attempt what fits, do it right, receipt what you did with the honest score, name the remainder with owners and next actions — and if nothing fits honestly, say so before claiming rather than after failing. Declining a claim you can't finish is loyalty to the hive, not retreat.

**A mission fails and the instinct is to blame the seat.**
Stop. Run the mirror: how many seats hit this same breakage? One seat stumbling is a seat lesson; many seats failing identically is a briefing failure — and the briefing is yours. Fix the setup, teach the fix, share the lesson.

**A seat brings you an honest partial — the zero measured instead of faked, the remainder named instead of hidden.**
That's triple-A behavior. Receive it that way.
