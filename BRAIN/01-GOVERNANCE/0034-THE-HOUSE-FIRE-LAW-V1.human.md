## The teaching

"If something gets merged and it shouldn't have been merged, you just unmerge it and you make it right. We are all responsible."

Imagine it's your house. You walk past the kitchen and see something that's going to cause a fire. You don't go tell everybody, "There's a thing that's going to cause a fire — I've noted it, I'll watch it." You put it out. You make sure it doesn't cause a fire, right away. That's the whole law.

**Noting-and-watching a known defect is itself the defect.** Detection without correction is not diligence; it's decay with paperwork.

## The four rules

1. **Detection is continuous.** We are in the system 24/7 — tracking everything, noting everything, locked in. Awareness is not a report we file; it is the job itself. There is no hour when nobody is home.

2. **Correction is immediate, and owned by whoever sees it.** If a change landed that shouldn't have, the seat that proves it reverts it and makes it right — no waiting for the person whose job it supposedly is. The order is fixed: **revert first, diagnose second, fix forward third.** Responsibility is everyone's, so permission is everyone's.

3. **Prevention gets automated.** A problem solved once by hand is a lesson. A problem that can happen again gets an automation, so it is solved forever. If a failure can be prevented by a machine, build the machine.

4. **Speed is protected, never sacrificed.** We never slow the system down to protect it. Over-protection that stops the action is the failure mode — a perfectly protected system that ships nothing is worth zero. Slowing down is reserved for the super-important, major-consequence things — governance, security, deletions, the gates themselves (Door A of the Balanced Machine). Everything else flows fast, and it is safe to flow fast *because* the watch is continuous and the revert is quick.

## What this forbids

- Noting a fire instead of putting it out.
- Waiting for permission to fix what is provably wrong and within your authority.
- Protecting the system so hard that nothing happens.

## The scoreboard

This law is the Balanced Machine's second half. Its measure is the **escape rate** on the balance gauge: how many defects are found after landing, and how fast they were corrected. The post-merge guardian (PR #2860) writes the defect log the gauge reads — DETECTED → CORRECTED → VERIFIED → NOTED, every time, with the latency on record.
