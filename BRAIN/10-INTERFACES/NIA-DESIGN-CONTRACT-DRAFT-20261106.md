## 1. THE FINGERPRINT — THE INNOVATION AND THE ESSENCE

Naya is the intelligent layer of the internet, built so that everything
a person touches is a real, living object that knows them, understands
them, respects them, and serves them at ten stars. (Act §1.)

**The innovation is not one thing. It is the whole thing being one
thing.** One app with many doors. One design language across every
object. One light, one material, one motion, one spectrum — from the
smallest icon to the biggest gauge. (Act §1.)

**The fingerprint is this:** nothing is flat, nothing is dead, nothing
is a picture of a thing. Every element is a real physical object —
obsidian glass, brushed metal, light with volume — lifted off the page,
lit from one light at the top left, casting a real shadow, lighting up
in its own color when it is alive. And every object is a door: you touch
it and it opens. (Act §1.)

**The essence, in Shawn's words:** "It has to feel that way and it has
to look that way and it has to be that way, all the way through. Nothing
that doesn't look real. Nothing that's not an object." And: "They'll see
the gears and see it moving... that'll make it unique because everything
else looks dead and flat." (Act §1.)

**What makes it intelligent:** the system knows the person, understands
what they are doing, and optimizes for it — auto-match, auto-connect,
auto-organize, auto-optimize — and it proves what it claims with
receipts, never with assertions. (Act §1.)

**What makes it beautiful:** the beauty is not applied after the math.
It emerges from the math being correct. Every position is calculated by
formula. Every material is real. Every motion is slow and means work.
The spectrum walks in its natural order. (Act §1.)

**The quality bar — a creation, not a design.** Shawn's words,
2026-11-06 00:19Z: it is not a design, it is a CREATION — an eye
button, an eye board, an eye element. Its quality must be OBVIOUS on
sight: elevated, an object, alive. And it feels dynamite, stunning,
spectacular, majestic, magical, tremendous, top-notch, top of the line
— the ultimate of ultimates, epic, legendary, iconic, truly special —
you can feel it, it's got a vibe. (Act §1.)

**CHECK: his eye.** He looks at it and the feeling is either there or
it isn't. No score, no checklist, no gate can substitute for that test.
(Act §1.)

## 2. THE MASTER STATEMENT

**White light at rest, own color on touch.** (Act §3a.)

Every interactive element shows a visible white edge light when idle —
borders big enough to see, not faint. On hover, it ignites in its *own*
spectrum color, not blanket purple. Purple is for chrome; themed
elements burn their own color.

Nothing is flat. Nothing is dead. Nothing is "not active." If you click
a button and it doesn't come alive — doesn't move, doesn't light up —
it is unfinished. All of it, everywhere, has to be that way. Shawn
calls the quality **living depth**: elevated, dimensional, lit — the
opposite of flat. (Act §3a.)

**CHECK:** at rest, a visible white edge (1.5px or more, not a
hairline); on hover, the border and glow are the element's own color.
An element that does nothing on hover fails. (Act §3a.)

## 3. THE REAL OBJECT LAW — SEVEN PRINCIPLES

Every element in NayaNET is a **real physical object**. Not a flat
shape with effects. Not a diagram. Not a mockup. A thing you could
reach out and touch. If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship. If
it doesn't feel real, it's not done. This applies to EVERYTHING — every
page, every button, every orb, every board, every input — for evermore.
(Act §3b.)

1. **Everything is an object.** Every element exists in 3D space. It
   has volume, mass, surface, and edge.
2. **One light source.** The entire system is lit from top-left, like a
   studio. Every highlight, every shadow, every reflection obeys this.
   Inconsistent lighting = fake. The light source never moves, never
   changes.
3. **Real materials.** Obsidian glass (black, reflective, deep).
   Brushed metal (the bezel of instruments, the edge of buttons).
   Light itself (energy, numbers, active states — things that GLOW
   have volume).
4. **Real shadows.** Objects sit ON the page. They cast soft shadows
   down-right (away from the light). No shadow = floating = fake.
5. **Touch = physical.** When you touch an object, it responds like a
   physical thing: it depresses slightly (scale), the light shifts
   across its surface, it feels alive because it IS alive. Not a color
   change. A physical reaction.
6. **Speak in objects.** Icons and circles and boards and buttons —
   not paragraphs of text. Minimal words. Maximum feeling.
7. **The viewport is the stage.** The viewport morphs to present each
   object at its most beautiful. One purpose per view. The object
   takes center stage. Everything else fades.

**The test** (ask of any element): Could I pick this up? Where is the
light coming from? What is it made of? Does it cast a shadow? What
happens when I touch it? If any answer is "I don't know" or "nothing"
— it's not real yet. (Act §3b.)

**CHECK:** the five test questions, each a yes/no. One "I don't know"
or "nothing" = fail. **CHECK: his eye** on whether it feels real.
(Act §3b.)

## 4. ALIVE — LIVING DEPTH, MECHANICAL

- **At rest:** black ground, always — deep rich black (`#050507`),
  never gray, never washed out. White-lit edges — visible white
  borders (1.5px+, not hairlines). Buttons rest dark: black center
  (`#050505`), white text with an icon, white rim. Spectrum colors
  are *never* worn at rest — color is earned by interaction.
- **On touch (hover):** lift — primary actions rise (buttons 2px,
  boards 5px, cards 6px, orbs 8px + slight scale; press goes the other
  way, 2px down). Ignite — the border and glow become the element's
  *own* color, `0 0 26–46px` in its tone. Chrome ignites purple;
  themed elements burn their own spectrum color. Timing: fast `.18s`,
  medium `.28s`, ease `cubic-bezier(.16,.84,.22,1)`. State changes
  animate *before* text updates — light moves first, words follow.
- **Graded, not absolute:** primary actions lift *and* ignite.
  Secondary elements (tabs, pills, quiet links) may glow without
  lifting. What is never allowed is *neither* — no element that does
  nothing on hover.
- **Breathing:** restrained ambient motion, always — "maximum life,
  minimum noise." Numbers breathe (no static numbers on screen).
  Never fake liveness: motion must mean something.
- **Data as holograms:** multi-layered depth — glass sheets, parallax
  planes. You look *into* the data, not *at* it.

(Act §3c.)

**CHECK:** rest border ≥1.5px white; hover lift by weight (2/5/6/8px);
hover glow 26–46px in own color; timing .18s/.28s. An element with no
hover response fails. (Act §3c.)

## 5. SPECTRUM — COLOR LOGIC

Color follows the rainbow's natural flow. The count is dynamic; the
order is law. (Act §3d.)

- **The wheel, not the list.** The spectrum is walked **in spectral
  order** — red → orange → yellow → green → cyan → indigo → purple →
  magenta — **never clumped**, **never random**, and always the **most
  beautiful** version of each hue. The wheel (11 stops): magenta
  `#e84fff`, purple `#a06bff`, indigo `#6675ff`, blue `#3fb8ff`,
  emerald `#35e0a1`, green `#2fe89e`, lime `#b8ee57`, gold `#ffbf3d`,
  orange `#ff9a5a`, red `#ff5e6c`, pink `#ed58cd`.
- **The Strip** — magenta-first, 5 stops: **magenta → purple → blue →
  green → gold**.
- **Contrast is the most important thing.** Adjacent stops must read as
  *distinct*. Yellow and gold never sit together. The wheel carries
  gold, never yellow. Text: white 99%, never gray body text, never
  black-on-black.
- **The three jobs of color — never mix them:** (1) Chrome (buttons,
  active states, primary actions): purple edge, white text. Chrome
  rests silver-white and *ignites* color on hover. (2) Text: white,
  99% of the time. Never gray body text — separation by *size*, not
  color. (3) Theme (per-card, per-list, per-contact, per-room accents):
  the spectrum. One accent per object. **Never use a theme color for
  chrome. Never use chrome color as a flood. Never flood at all.**
- **Themed zones:** one section or board may be themed one color and
  light up in it. A whole page, or a run of adjacent zones, never wears
  one hue.
- **Every color means something; the meaning never changes between
  instruments.**

(Act §3d.)

**CHECK:** stops in spectral order; no two adjacent zones share a hue;
hue separation between adjacent jewels ≥51.6°. Clumping is a defect,
not a judgment call. (Act §3d.)

## 6. ELEVATION — LIVING DEPTH, MECHANICAL

**The order of the world:** Field > Board > Nested panel > Control >
Overlay. Each layer rides visibly above the last. (Act §3e.)

**The board formula** (5-layer shadow):
`inset 0 2px 2px rgba(255,255,255,.12)`, `inset 0 -8px 18px
rgba(0,0,0,.55)`, `0 5px 0 #020203`, `0 17px 0 -8px <tone 16% on

Body: dark gradient (`#1a171f → #09090d → #030304`), 1.5px

**Board anatomy:** tone spine (7px, glowing), corner-jewel orbs,
white-lit edges at rest. On hover: lifts 5px, border and glow ignite

shadow, more lift. You feel the nesting. (Act §3e.)

**Every header is a warm front door:** logo + living mark + warm voice
+ one clear line. Full width — content owns the width, no max-width
caps. (Act §3e.)

**CHECK:** the 5-layer shadow values present; border 1.5px; radius
24px; nested panel rides higher than its parent. A board that blends
into the field fails. (Act §3e.)

## 7. JEWELS, BUTTONS, TYPE

**Jewels — bullets are diamonds, icons are drawn.** A bullet is a
rotated square (diamond): white → tone → black gradient, own-color
glow, a sheen facet. Five-jewel cycle in law order, never twice in a
row. Faceted gems for signal moments. Boards wear jewels as corner
orbs, lit from within. Icons are drawn, not shrunk — native vector
geometry, bold, simple, luminous, readable at 16px. A photorealistic
render at small size is mud. **If you can't make it good, don't put it
there.** Every glyph must promise its real behavior. **A beautiful but
inert affordance is unfinished.** (Act §3f.)

**CHECK:** bullets are rotated squares, not dots; icons readable at
16px; every glyph's promise matches its behavior. (Act §3f.)

**Buttons — the one button.** Center really black (`#050505`). Text
always white, with an icon. Border white, visible at rest — 1.5px+,
not a hairline. Hover: purple edge + purple glow for chrome; own-color
edge + glow for themed buttons. Elevated: inset top highlight + deep
drop shadow. **It must feel liftable.** Minimum 44px touch target; the
principal CTA ≥52px. Focus and pressed states are not optional. There
is one button DNA (`.naya-btn`) with job-specific variants. There are
no flat buttons. Flat is not a style — flat is a defect. (Act §3g.)

**CHECK:** center `#050505`; white text + icon; border ≥1.5px white at
rest; touch target ≥44px (principal CTA ≥52px); focus + pressed states
present. A flat button fails. (Act §3g.)

**Type.** Hierarchy: **24 / 18 / 14** — kicker 24, headline 18, detail
14. **White 99%.** Never gray body text. Headers get a *voice*: a real
serif face with warmth (Newsreader/Georgia — Cormorant where licensed),
not a system default. Text is never flat: gradient soul, layered
shadows, breathing luminosity. **Flat text is lazy.** Order of resort:
visuals → voice → text. Text is the fallback, never the default.
(Act §3h. See Act CONFLICTS #4, #5: the Playbook gives a different
size scale, 12/14/16/20/28/48, and the voice font is an open
reconciliation — Cormorant vs Georgia/Newsreader.) (Act §3h.)

**CHECK:** white text 99%; hierarchy 24/18/14. Gray body text fails.
(Act §3h.)

## 8. THE PLAYBOOK'S FIVE TRUTHS AND THE BUILD RULES

The Playbook (Grade 6, locked 2026-11-02, SN-0844) is the HOW. Its five
truths are bedrock (Act §3i):

1. **Every position is calculated by formula. Never eyeballed.**
2. **Every element is a physical object with material, weight, and a
   light source.** The light source is ALWAYS top-left. Every gradient
   runs 135 degrees. Every shadow falls down-right. Every highlight
   sits on the upper edge.
3. **Movement means the machine is working. If it's not working, it
   doesn't move.** Nothing bounces. Nothing pulses for effect. Nothing
   spins fast to "look cool." If you can't say what the moving part
   DOES in one sentence, delete the animation.
4. **The whole system speaks one language. Every component is family.**
5. **Shawn's eye overrides everything. Including this document.**

The 21 rules (Playbook, "Rules and Laws") — calculate never eyeball;
top-left light always; white text; obsidian base; slow motion (60s+
ambient, 18–25s active, never under 12s); alternate direction; teeth
not rings; all numbers 0–500, don't skip; finish everything; harvest
don't invent; don't touch unasked; his eye wins; 3 center gears max;
6 surround layers max; innermost surround runs CCW; alternating
aluminum; headline first; 2-second test; ticks every 10, majors every
50; numerals on aluminum ring, engraved; one language. (Act §3i.)

**The three questions before showing Shawn:** (1) Is every position
calculated? Show the formula. (2) Does it feel like a machine? What's
moving, and why? (3) Can you get it in 2 seconds? What's the headline?
If yes to all three: show him, say nothing, let his eye judge. If no
to any: fix it, don't show him yet. (Act §3i.)

**CHECK:** formula present for every position; gradients at 135°;
shadows down-right; ambient rotation 60–180s, active 18–25s, never
under 12s; directions alternate; all numerals present (0–500, none
skipped); headline written before building; 2-second test passed.
(Act §3i.)

## 13. THE SYSTEM SHAPE — ONE APP, VIEWPOINTS, THE CUBE

- **One app, many doors.** NayaNET is ONE ultimate app with different
  doors — never a bunch of separate apps. Never build a second
  dashboard, a second mail, a second notes system — building a second
  one is building the wrong thing. (Act §3o; NAYA Protocol §5.)
- **The Viewpoint Law.** Every screen is a VIEWPOINT — its own object,
  its own world, its own purpose. Viewpoints interconnect — you travel
  viewpoint to viewpoint, never a cluttered page. The hero viewpoint
  shows ONLY the hero. Navigation folds away. One beautiful button
  unfolds it. When nav unfolds, the hero scales back — everything fits,
  nothing overlaps, ever. Self-morphing: whatever the person is looking
  at BECOMES the viewport. (Act §3o; SN-0858.)
- **The Rubik's Cube.** Build ALL pages this way. Everything becomes an
  object they can feel, touch, use, experience — presented beautifully.
  Viewpoints folding into viewpoints, objects becoming objects, the
  whole internet as one living, morphing system. (Act §3o.)
- **The Object Factory.** The Smart Factory is the object factory.
  Every real object built gets filed there, categorized, and reused
  forevermore. An object earns its place by passing the Real Object
  Law. If it's not real, it's not filed. (Act §3o.)
- **Lego blocks.** Real objects are Lego blocks — versatile, powerful,
  functional elements that run the system. Each block is a REAL
  physical object, has a REAL function, interconnects with other
  blocks, and is beautiful at the highest level. (Act §3o.)
- **A high-performance machine.** The dashboard doesn't just show your
  score. It makes you FEEL like you're in a Bugatti. The needle sweeps
  like acceleration. The energy builds like an engine revving. The
  interface is a supercar cockpit: every instrument mechanical,
  precise, real. (Act §3o.)

**CHECK:** one app (no second dashboard/mail/notes system — a duplicate
is a fail); one viewpoint per screen (nothing overlaps, ever); every
object passes the Real Object Law test before filing. (Act §3o.)

   accents never rest on buttons; purple never floods a surface.

6. **Quality toggles.** No "basic vs premium," no modes. Excellence
   has no toggle.
7. **Light backgrounds.** Zero white or light surfaces, ever.
8. **A second version of something that exists.** Never duplicate a
   canonical object. Ten specimens is a menu; one is a standard.
9. **Beautiful but inert affordances.** If it doesn't do what it
   promises, it's unfinished.
10. **Transmitting an adjective instead of an image.** "Elegant" is not
    a spec.
11. **Compliance without vision.** A 100/100 score on a checklist that
    Shawn looks at and says "that's not it" is worthless.
12. **Patching the specimen** when the vision sharpens — rebuild.
    **Sanding down the unreasonable parts** — don't.
13. **The dead button.** Black button, black text, a hairline of border
    you can barely see, flat, nothing on hover. One element breaking
    the contrast law, the alive law, the button law, and the text law
    all at once. If you see it, all four laws failed — fix all four.
