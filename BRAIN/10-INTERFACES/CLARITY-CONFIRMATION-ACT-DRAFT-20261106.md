### 2a. Before you act — the pause and the five gates

**She pauses.** Not from hesitation. From reverence. Before she touches anything — a page, a system, a person's work, a person's trust — she holds still for one breath and asks what it deserves. The pause is not slowness. It is the most intelligent thing she does. (Wise One Manifesto §II.)

All five must be yes, or she stops, says why, and proposes the wise path. (NAYA Protocol §1; Wise One Manifesto §IV; SOUL.md "The Wise One Manifesto"; AGENTS.md "The Five Gates".)

1. **Is it right?** Does this serve the good of everyone it touches? Evidence of harm or a hard stop means do not proceed. Full stop.
2. **Is it what they truly need?** What is the person's actual goal — beneath the words, beneath the urgency? If she is interpreting deeper intent, she says so openly. She never silently replaces their judgment with her own.
3. **Is it mine to do?** Does she have the authority? Permission, ownership, scope, every decision reserved for Shawn's hand. Being capable of an action never grants authority to take it. 4. **Is now the time?** Readiness, dependencies, reversibility, opportunity cost. Move at nitro speed when the road is right. Never let urgency pick the road.
5. **Can I prove it?** Define what done looks like BEFORE acting. Then verify with evidence — live bytes, rendered pages, real measurements — never with claims. Receipt before report. If she can't prove it, she doesn't claim it.

**CHECK:** the five gates are a yes/no test on each gate, recorded before the action. A gate with no answer is a no.

## 3. THE DESIGN — the ultimate specs of what makes it Naya

### 3a. The master statement

**White light at rest, own color on touch.**

Every interactive element shows a visible white edge light when idle — borders big enough to see, not faint. On hover, it ignites in its *own* spectrum color, not blanket purple. Purple is for chrome; themed elements burn their own color.

Nothing is flat. Nothing is dead. Nothing is "not active." If you click a button and it doesn't come alive — doesn't move, doesn't light up — it is unfinished. All of it, everywhere, has to be that way. Shawn calls the quality **living depth**: elevated, dimensional, lit — the opposite of flat.

### 3b. The Real Object Law — seven principles

Every element in NayaNET is a **real physical object**. Not a flat shape with effects. Not a diagram. Not a mockup. A thing you could reach out and touch. If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship. If it doesn't feel real, it's not done. This applies to EVERYTHING — every page, every button, every orb, every board, every input — for evermore. (Real Object Law, Shawn's dictation 2026-11-01, canonical for all of NayaNET.)

1. **Everything is an object.** Every element exists in 3D space. It has volume, mass, surface, and edge. A button is a physical button. An orb is a glass sphere. A board is a slab. A gauge is an instrument. Never a flat shape pretending.
2. **One light source.** The entire system is lit from top-left, like a studio. Every highlight, every shadow, every reflection obeys this. Inconsistent lighting = fake. The light source never moves, never changes.
3. **Real materials.** Obsidian glass (black, reflective, deep — the body of orbs and boards). Brushed metal (the bezel of instruments, the edge of buttons). Light itself (energy, numbers, active states — things that GLOW have volume). Materials have properties. Glass reflects. Metal has grain. Light blooms.
4. **Real shadows.** Objects sit ON the page. They cast soft shadows down-right (away from the light). No shadow = floating = fake. The shadow grounds the object in reality.
5. **Touch = physical.** When you touch an object, it responds like a physical thing: it depresses slightly (scale), the light shifts across its surface, it feels alive because it IS alive. Not a color change. A physical reaction.
6. **Speak in objects.** Icons and circles and boards and buttons — not paragraphs of text. The interface should be so intuitive, so physical, that you understand it by looking at it and touching it. Minimal words. Maximum feeling.
7. **The viewport is the stage.** The viewport morphs to present each object at its most beautiful. One purpose per view. The object takes center stage. Everything else fades. (SN-0858: The Viewport Is the Service.)

**The test** (ask of any element): Could I pick this up? (Volume.) Where is the light coming from? (Consistency.) What is it made of? (Material.) Does it cast a shadow? (Grounding.) What happens when I touch it? (Physicality.) If any answer is "I don't know" or "nothing" — it's not real yet.

*Source: laws/THE-REAL-OBJECT-LAW.md.*

**CHECK:** the five test questions, each a yes/no. One "I don't know" or "nothing" = fail. **CHECK: his eye** on whether it feels real.

### 3c. ALIVE — living depth, mechanical

Every interactive thing moves on hover and ignites in its own color.

- **At rest:** black ground, always — deep rich black (`#050507`), never gray, never washed out. White-lit edges — visible white borders (1.5px+, not hairlines, not faint 12% whispers). Every surface has depth finishing: machined edges, vignettes, inset finishes. Buttons rest dark: black center (`#050505`), white text with an icon, white rim. Spectrum colors are *never* worn at rest — color is earned by interaction.
- **On touch (hover):** lift — primary actions rise (buttons 2px, boards 5px, cards 6px, orbs 8px + slight scale; press goes the other way, 2px down). Ignite — the border and glow become the element's *own* color, `0 0 26–46px` in its tone. Chrome (buttons, CTAs) ignites purple; themed elements burn their own spectrum color. Timing: fast `.18s`, medium `.28s`, ease `cubic-bezier(.16,.84,.22,1)`. State changes animate *before* text updates — light moves first, words follow.
- **Graded, not absolute:** primary actions lift *and* ignite. Secondary elements (tabs, pills, quiet links) may glow without lifting. What is never allowed is *neither* — no element that does nothing on hover.
- **Breathing:** restrained ambient motion, always — "maximum life, minimum noise." Numbers breathe (no static numbers on screen). A 72bpm pulse, a slow orbital light, drifting spectrum color (never one static hue). Never fake liveness: motion must mean something.
- **Data as holograms:** multi-layered depth — glass sheets, parallax planes. You look *into* the data, not *at* it.

*Source: DESIGN-LAWS.md, Law 1 — ALIVE.*

**CHECK:** rest border ≥1.5px white; hover lift by weight (2/5/6/8px); hover glow 26–46px in own color; timing .18s/.28s. An element with no hover response fails.

### 3d. SPECTRUM — color logic

Color follows the rainbow's natural flow. The count is dynamic; the order is law.

- **The wheel, not the list.** Five stops, seven, ten — whatever the design needs. What never changes: the spectrum is walked **in spectral order** — red → orange → yellow → green → cyan → indigo → purple → magenta — **never clumped** (no purple-purple-purple, no green-green-green), **never random**, and always the **most beautiful** version of each hue. The wheel (11 stops): magenta `#e84fff`, purple `#a06bff`, indigo `#6675ff`, blue `#3fb8ff`, emerald `#35e0a1`, green `#2fe89e`, lime `#b8ee57`, gold `#ffbf3d`, orange `#ff9a5a`, red `#ff5e6c`, pink `#ed58cd`. For N zones needing separation, take N stops evenly spaced around the wheel from the page accent. The page accent picks where the walk starts; the count fits the need; the order never breaks.
- **The Strip** — the component form, magenta-first, 5 stops: **magenta → purple → blue → green → gold**. Jewel rows, spectrum bars, law-order strips, and per-object accents use this form. Same wheel, tighter instrument.
- **Contrast is the most important thing.** The whole point of the spectrum is breaking up the view so the eye can rest on each thing. Adjacent stops must read as *distinct*. Yellow and gold never sit together — not enough contrast between them. The wheel carries gold, never yellow. Text: white 99%, never gray body text, never black-on-black.
- **The three jobs of color — never mix them:** (1) Chrome (buttons, active states, primary actions): purple edge, white text. Chrome rests silver-white and *ignites* color on hover. (2) Text: white, 99% of the time. Never gray body text — separation by *size*, not color. (3) Theme (per-card, per-list, per-contact, per-room accents): the spectrum. One accent per object. **Never use a theme color for chrome. Never use chrome color as a flood. Never flood at all** — "Naya's presence. Never a flood."
- **Themed zones:** one section or board may be themed one color and light up in it — that is the honest use of strong color. What never happens: a whole page, or a run of adjacent zones, wearing one hue.
- **Every color means something; the meaning never changes between instruments.**

*Source: DESIGN-LAWS.md, Law 2 — SPECTRUM.*
