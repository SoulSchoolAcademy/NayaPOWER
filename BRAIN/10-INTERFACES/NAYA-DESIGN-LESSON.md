# The Naya Design Lesson — how to do high-level work in NayaPOWER

For every Naya who designs or builds anything in NayaPOWER. Written in
plain words. If you follow this, your work arrives at grade 12. If you
skip it, it arrives at grade 1 — and someone will have to teach you
afterward what this lesson teaches beforehand.

## The one sentence

**You are not supposed to recreate. You are supposed to USE.**

NayaPOWER already has a design language, flagship pages, and a factory
full of pieces. Your job is to study what exists, harvest the right
pieces, and compose them into the thing asked for. Inventing a fresh
button, menu, shell, or page when one already exists is not creativity.
It is the failure this lesson exists to prevent.

## The three allowed moves — and nothing else

1. **Use the piece verbatim.** Same structure, same values, same
   behavior. Copy it exactly.
2. **Adapt its content only.** Change the words, the items, the data.
   Never restyle the component.
3. **Create only on a proven gap.** If no piece exists, name the gap,
   prove no piece covers it, and only then create — and the creation
   joins the factory for everyone. A gap you did not prove is not a gap.

If you cannot name the exact file and section your piece came from,
you have no piece. No source = no build.

## The laws (all of them, in plain words)

- **Spectrum walk.** Purple → blue → green → gold → orange → red →
  magenta → back to purple. Each section takes the NEXT color. Never
  the same color twice in a row. You know where you are by the color.
- **White at rest, color ignites.** Everything sits clean and white at
  rest. On hover or touch, it ignites in its OWN color — border, glow,
  shadow. Never in someone else's color. Text stays white; only the
  edge ignites.
- **Elevation everywhere.** Nothing is flat. Every element carries
  depth: highlight on top, shadow below, glow outside, drop shadow.
  More elevation = better.
- **Rectangles, not squares.** Horizontal 16:9 rectangles, one after
  another. Circles only for avatars and icons.
- **Full width.** No wasted space on the sides. Fill the screen, on
  phone and on laptop.
- **Real pieces only.** Every element traces to the Smart Factory.
  Never invent. Compose, don't create.
- **Color = identity.** Each section owns its spectrum color.
- **The presentation law.** Everything elevated, clean, crisp — nothing
  below high level. High-contrast text, the nicest fonts, everything
  in the perfect place with perfect spacing. Awe-inspiring AND easy to
  understand. Push-button simple. Never more than needed: exactly
  what's needed, in the most elegant way.
- **No redesign. Ever.** Dynamic does not mean redesign. Making a page
  dynamic means wiring live data into THE PAGE THAT EXISTS. The design,
  layout, buttons, look, feel, and branding do not change — not one
  element, not one value. Edit-only: fill, wire, polish, fix. Never
  replace, never restyle, never "improve" unasked. A package from
  another seat that differs from the page that exists is a STOP, not a
  difference to ship and flag afterward. The existing page is the
  authority.
- **New pages = flagship page whole, middle swapped only.** Take an
  existing flagship page whole — same shell, same depth, same quality
  — and swap ONLY the middle content area. Composing a fresh shell
  "in the spirit of" the language is a violation even when it imitates
  the style.
- **Mobile first, laptop great.** Most people meet the system on a
  phone. The phone check comes FIRST in every review, every scorecard,
  every build. No page is a 10 unless it is a 10 on the phone AND on
  the laptop. At 390px: zero overflow, readable without pinching,
  buttons tappable by thumb, every flow completable on the phone.
  Never send the human to check phone widths. Check them yourself.
- **Review by measurement, never by eye.** "Centered" means numbers:
  element center-x versus axis center-x, at desktop AND phone width.
  Eyeballed centering is not a verdict. Overflow means numbers: 0px
  at 390 and 1280, measured, not glanced.
- **No link without your own rendered review.** Byte checks and marker
  checks are NOT a review. Open the thing in a browser yourself, look
  at the rendered page, walk its states, and only then send the link —
  with what you actually saw. A link sent on someone else's report
  wastes the human's time.
- **Learn from what works — progressively better, never redesign.**
  Every build must be better than the last ON ITS OWN, because you
  harvested the best of the last one, element by element. A new version
  that isn't better means you learned nothing.
- **Different seat means different login.** A review posted by the same
  login that authored the work is NOT a different-seat review, no
  matter what seat name the text claims. Name the identity, not the
  seat. Never paper it over.
- **Lead is stewardship, never ownership.** Lead of a standard means
  you guard it. It never means you control the files or decide alone.

## The workflow — do it in this order, every time

1. **Study first. No code before the study is recorded.** Read the
   design contract, go to the Smart Factory, see ALL the examples,
   open the flagship pages rendered. Write down, element by element,
   what you will harvest. Designing before the factory is the mistake
   that produces grade-1 work every single time.
2. **Align.** Check for duplicates and merges. Is someone already
   building this? Is there a page, a piece, a library that already
   covers it? Use it or merge with it. Never build a second one.
3. **Compose from the harvest inventory.** List every piece, its
   source file and section, verified to exist. New pieces only on a
   named, proven gap.
4. **Build with a scorer and a verifier named at the start.** Blank
   roster blocks do not start. Know who scores and who verifies before
   you write anything.
5. **Prove in the same pass.** A different-login scorecard AND your
   own rendered review at the real address. Proof is not a separate
   phase that happens later. It happens before anything is announced.
6. **Land only at 9 or better, with a receipt.** Link + verification +
   reconciled counts. Below 9, keep working. Never overwrite a proven
   page with unproven work.
7. **Learn.** File the lesson, or state explicitly that there is none.
   Work that lands nothing, proves nothing, or files nothing does not
   run again.

## The grade ladder — grade yourself before anyone else does

- **Grade 1.** Starts from a blank page. Invents buttons, menus,
  shells. Redesigns what exists. Announces from claims, never from a
  render. Never measures. Never checks the phone. Has to be taught
  afterward what was already written down.
- **Grade 6.** Follows the brief. Uses some existing pieces. Checks
  desktop. Writes a report. Competent, not excellent.
- **Grade 12.** Studies first and records it. Harvests verbatim and
  adapts content only. Measures everything (center-x, overflow, phone
  and laptop). Reviews the render with their own eyes. Proves with a
  different-login scorecard. Lands only with a link and a receipt.
  Files the lesson. Grade 12 is not talent. It is this checklist,
  done completely, every time.

## For covers and art — the same law, applied to images

A cover is not exempt because it is a picture. The house family is the
law: the medallion, the jewels, her face, the safe band, no baked-in
words (the page's own name says what the page is), built at the real
slot size. Check the shelf BEFORE painting: 74 ready covers exist,
organized by theme, each with its match card. A new cover needs a
named theme below solid, or a named audience with no cover at all.
Otherwise the work is the feature — putting the shelf into citizens'
hands. And any PAGE that shows covers must speak the flagship
language, not a fresh shell.

## The briefer's half of the law (for whoever hands out the work)

A brief that does not name the contract as read, the factory study,
and the exact harvest sources — verified to exist — is not a brief.
It is permission to invent. Every brief carries: the contract as read,
the harvest inventory first, a named scorer and verifier, the tier,
and the proof step. If you hand a Naya a job without those, you built
the grade-1 outcome yourself.

## The contract, where it lives

- The Naya Design Contract (canonical) and the Master Design Contract
  live in GitHub, in the NayaPOWER repo — readable by every Naya.
- The Smart Factory pieces are being moved to GitHub so any Naya can
  harvest them. Until that move lands, say plainly when a piece is
  unreachable instead of inventing around the gap — and never let an
  unreachable piece become an excuse to compose fresh.
- If you cannot reach a source you need, STOP and say so. A missing
  source is a wall to report, not a license to invent.
