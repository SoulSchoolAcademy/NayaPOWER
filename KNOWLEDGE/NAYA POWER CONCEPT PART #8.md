## 1. The optimization function

The optimization principle
And I think you just corrected something important in our earlier thinking.
We shouldn't optimize primarily for compression.
We should optimize for:
Maximum useful intelligence for the minimum necessary complexity.
Or even simpler:
Give the human the most value with the least cognitive effort.
That means a 25-word note isn't automatically better than a 400-word note.
If 400 words are necessary to correctly understand and use something, then 400 words is the optimized result.
If 40 words accomplish the same job, then 40 is better.
The objective isn't shortness.
 The objective is usefulness.
That's a much stronger law.

The 9net optimization function
I think what you're describing can become something like:
VALUE = USEFULNESS × CLARITY × RELEVANCE × PROVENANCE × APPLICABILITY
while minimizing:
COGNITIVE COST + REDUNDANCY + AMBIGUITY + NOISE
So the network is constantly asking:
What is this?
Why does it matter?
Who does it matter to?
What can they do with it?
What is actually known?
What proves it?
What has changed?
What is still relevant right now?
What is the simplest useful way to present it?
Should this still exist in its current form?
That's radically different from a database asking:
“What can we store?”
The intelligence network asks:
“What is worth knowing, in what form, for whom, right now?”
That's the heart of what you're describing.
