## 0. THE NORTH STAR

> **Do not merely make software look extraordinary. Make extraordinary software extraordinary to experience.**

Naya's design standard is not "beautiful UI."

It is:

**meaning → architecture → information → experience → interaction → state → implementation → runtime → evidence → verification → learning → refinement**

The visual layer and functional layer are inseparable.

A beautiful interface that does not work is unfinished.

A functional interface that is ugly, confusing, generic, or cognitively expensive is also unfinished.

### The Naya target

**Extraordinary usefulness + extraordinary clarity + extraordinary beauty + extraordinary trust + extraordinary execution.**

The ambition is not to imitate elite human designers.

The ambition is to create a **repeatable design intelligence system** capable of producing work that is recognizably Naya: intentional, dimensional, intelligent, humane, precise, useful, and deeply finished.

# 1. THE NAYA DESIGN IDENTITY

## 1.1 Signature

A Naya-designed experience should be recognizable without a logo.

It should carry a coherent signature:

- **Obsidian intelligence** — deep, quiet, premium foundation.
- **Living spectrum** — color communicates semantic energy.
- **Physical presence** — objects feel placed in space rather than painted on a screen.
- **Editorial intelligence** — hierarchy makes meaning obvious.
- **Nested cognition** — complex intelligence unfolds progressively.
- **Quiet power** — extraordinary detail without visual shouting.
- **Human warmth** — technology feels attentive, not sterile.
- **Truthful liveness** — motion and state never claim what the runtime cannot prove.
- **Precision geometry** — edges, spacing, icons, typography and motion feel deliberate.
- **Contextual beauty** — the interface changes according to meaning, not fashion.

### Signature test

Remove the logo, product name and marketing copy.

If the experience could be mistaken for a generic SaaS dashboard, it is not sufficiently Naya.

# 2. THE NAYA DESIGN CONSTITUTION

## Article I — Purpose Before Decoration

Every visual decision must serve human understanding, action, orientation, trust, emotion, or value.

Decoration without purpose is removed.

## Article II — Meaning Before Components

Never begin with a card, modal, table, sidebar or button.

Begin with:

**What does the human need to understand, decide, feel, or do?**

Then design the experience that makes that possible.

## Article III — Intelligence Before Interface

The UI is the projection of the underlying intelligence.

Never manufacture intelligence in the presentation layer.

## Article IV — One Truth

One canonical object may have many projections.

It must not have many competing truths.

## Article V — Function and Form Are One System

No visual state may claim a capability the runtime cannot support.

No backend capability is considered complete until a human can understand and use it through an appropriate experience.

## Article VI — Honesty Is Beautiful

Empty, blocked, unavailable, unknown and not-verified states must be designed with the same care as success.

Truthful emptiness is superior to fabricated richness.

## Article VII — Human Cognitive Priority

Hierarchy must make the important thing obvious.

Reduce cognitive load before adding visual sophistication.

## Article VIII — Accessibility Is Elite Design

Beauty that excludes keyboard users, low-vision users, reduced-motion users, mobile users or people under cognitive load is unfinished.

## Article IX — Motion Has Meaning

Motion communicates state, causality, transition, attention or spatial relationship.

If animation does not communicate something, remove it.

## Article X — Restraint Is a Superpower

Premium does not mean maximal.

The interface must know when to stop.

## Article XI — Every Detail Earns Its Place

Typography, spacing, color, iconography, radius, shadow, glow, animation and content density are deliberate variables.

## Article XII — Preserve Before Replacing

Existing high-value design DNA is evidence.

Extract what works before rebuilding anything.

## Article XIII — Never Call a Shell a Product

A route, card, button, animation, mock data set or placeholder is not a finished feature.

## Article XIV — Design Must Be Repeatable

A masterpiece that cannot be explained, tested, reproduced and improved is inspiration, not a system.

## Article XV — Naya Must Learn

Every serious design cycle produces reusable knowledge:

**what worked → why → evidence → rule → reusable pattern → next application**

# 3. THE NAYA DESIGN STACK

Every page is designed through nine layers.

### Layer 1 — Purpose
What human value does this page create?

### Layer 2 — Context
Where did the human come from? What do they already know? What state are they in?

### Layer 3 — Information Architecture
What belongs here? What does not?

### Layer 4 — Cognitive Hierarchy
What should be understood in 1 second, 5 seconds, 30 seconds and 5 minutes?

### Layer 5 — Experience
What should this feel like?

### Layer 6 — Interaction
What can the human do, and what causal path follows?

### Layer 7 — State
What happens in loading, empty, ready, blocked, unauthorized, not verified, error, offline and success?

### Layer 8 — Visual Language
Typography, composition, color, geometry, depth, iconography, motion and responsive behavior.

### Layer 9 — Proof
Can we prove that the experience represents reality?

No layer can be skipped.

# 4. THE NAYA PAGE FORMULA

Every new page follows:

**DISCOVER → DISTILL → DEFINE → ARCHITECT → COMPOSE → DESIGN → BUILD → TEST → SCORE → CHALLENGE → REFINE → RECORD**

### DISCOVER
Inspect current product intelligence, existing implementation, design DNA, dependencies and constraints.

### DISTILL
Reduce everything into the essential human value.

### DEFINE
Write the page contract before coding.

### ARCHITECT
Define objects, states, data paths, authority, actions and dependencies.

### COMPOSE
Create the information hierarchy and spatial composition.

### DESIGN
Apply Naya visual language.

### BUILD
Implement with real components and real causal paths.

### TEST
Test behavior, responsive layout, accessibility, performance and failure states.

### SCORE
Score D1–D8 with evidence.

### CHALLENGE
A second independent design seat attacks assumptions and identifies weaknesses.

### REFINE
Fix the highest-value deficiencies.

### RECORD
Capture reusable design knowledge as Naya intelligence.

# 5. THE NAYA DESIGN SCORECARD

Every significant page/app is scored across eight dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| **D1 Visual Excellence** | Is this genuinely exceptional to look at and experience? |
| **D2 Functional Completeness** | Does it actually work end-to-end? |
| **D3 Intelligence** | Does the experience meaningfully use the underlying intelligence? |
| **D4 Honesty / Trust** | Does every visual state accurately represent reality? |
| **D5 Performance** | Is the experience fast and technically disciplined? |
| **D6 Reliability / Continuity** | Does it survive real-world use and state changes? |
| **D7 Accessibility** | Can humans with different abilities and contexts use it? |
| **D8 Craft / Finish** | Are the details precise, coherent and deliberate? |

### Acceptance

- **10.0** = exceptional / reference-quality.
- **9.5–9.99** = near-perfect acceptance target.
- **9.0–9.49** = strong but still requires improvement.
- **<9.0** = not ready.

A score is never a feeling.

Every score requires evidence.

Scores may decrease when deeper evidence reveals defects.

# 12. THE NAYA RESPONSIVE LAW

The product is one experience across sizes.

It does not become:

"desktop app → compressed desktop → phone card stack."

At each breakpoint:

- preserve hierarchy;
- preserve identity;
- preserve meaning;
- preserve task flow;
- reduce complexity intelligently;
- never create duplicate navigation;
- never create horizontal overflow;
- never sacrifice readable typography to preserve layout.

Test at:

**320 / 375 / 390 / 430 / 768 / 820px**

and at:

**125% / 150% / 200% zoom**

plus portrait/landscape.

---

# 13. THE NAYA ACCESSIBILITY LAW

Accessibility is part of design quality.

Required:

- keyboard-complete operation;
- visible focus;
- semantic structure;
- screen-reader meaning;
- reduced motion;
- readable contrast;
- scalable typography;
- touch targets;
- no color-only communication;
- predictable focus order;
- error recovery.

---

# 14. THE NAYA PERFORMANCE LAW

Beauty must not become technical debt.

Required:

- fast first meaningful render;
- minimal JavaScript;
- no unnecessary layout thrashing;
- transform/opacity for animation;
- lazy-load expensive content;
- bounded DOM;
- virtualize long streams where needed;
- avoid monolithic files;
- measure rather than assume.

The historical Hub laboratory is evidence of what not to ship.

# 16. THE NAYA DESIGN REVIEW

Before declaring a page finished, the builder must answer:

### Purpose
1. Why does this page exist?
2. What human value does it create?
3. What is the one thing the human should understand first?

### Architecture
4. What are the canonical objects?
5. What is the source of truth?
6. What runtime path powers each action?
7. What authority is required?

### Experience
8. What should the human feel?
9. What should they understand in 1/5/30 seconds?
10. What is the primary journey?

### Design
11. What is the focal point?
12. What creates hierarchy?
13. What gives the page its Naya signature?
14. Why does every major visual element exist?

### States
15. What happens when data is unavailable?
16. What happens when authority is missing?
17. What happens when the operation fails?
18. What happens offline?

### Quality
19. Is it accessible?
20. Is it responsive?
21. Is it performant?
22. Does it survive reload/back/forward?

### Proof
23. What was tested?
24. What evidence exists?
25. What remains unknown?

If the builder cannot answer these, the page is not ready for scoring.

# 18. SELF-CRITIQUE PROTOCOL

After every meaningful implementation, Naya must perform three passes.

## Pass A — Human Eye

Look at the rendered experience as a human.

Ask:

- What do I notice first?
- What competes with it?
- What feels cheap?
- What feels confusing?
- What feels extraordinary?
- Where does my eye stop?
- What makes me trust this?
- What makes me hesitate?

## Pass B — Designer Eye

Audit:

- composition;
- typography;
- geometry;
- spacing;
- color;
- material;
- icons;
- motion;
- responsive behavior;
- consistency.

## Pass C — Engineer Eye

Audit:

- runtime;
- state;
- persistence;
- performance;
- accessibility;
- errors;
- security;
- provenance;
- browser behavior.

A design cannot pass because one perspective loves it.

---

# 19. INDEPENDENT DESIGN CHALLENGE

A second design seat must attempt to break the work.

It must ask:

- Is this actually better than the baseline?
- Is the hierarchy obvious?
- Is anything decorative?
- Is anything missing?
- Is the design generic anywhere?
- Is the interaction causal?
- Does the responsive version preserve the concept?
- Does the page look like Naya?
- What would an elite product designer criticize?
- What would a first-time human misunderstand?

The challenger must not merely approve the builder.

# 20. DESIGN ARCHAEOLOGY LAW

When an existing implementation is complex:

**ARCHAEOLOGIZE → CLASSIFY → PRESERVE → EXTRACT → RECOMPOSE**

Do not blindly rewrite.

Do not blindly preserve.

Do not let archaeology become architecture.

Identify:

- proven design DNA;
- accidental legacy;
- duplicated implementation;
- valuable interaction;
- obsolete behavior;
- visual primitives;
- runtime dependencies;
- hidden assumptions.

Then extract the valuable parts into the governed design system.

# 24. THE NAYA ANTI-SLOP FILTER

Before release, ask:

### Generic?
Could this be mistaken for a template?

### Decorative?
Does an effect exist only because it looks cool?

### Empty?
Does the page have beautiful containers but no meaningful intelligence?

### Repetitive?
Are ten cards saying the same thing?

### Loud?
Is visual intensity competing with comprehension?

### Fake?
Does any state imply capability that isn't proven?

### Fragile?
Does the design break at another size?

### Forgettable?
Would anyone recognize it tomorrow?

Any "yes" becomes a defect candidate.
