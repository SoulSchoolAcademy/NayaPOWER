# 🧭 Layout, Navigation & Composition Law V1

## 1. Prime law

The human should never have to reconstruct where they are.

Layout should continually answer:
- Where am I?
- What is this?
- What matters now?
- What can I do?
- Where do I go next?

## 2. Desktop shell

Default shell:
- persistent left rail;
- primary center canvas;
- contextual secondary region only where useful.

Do not create a permanent overloaded three-column dashboard.

## 3. Rail law

The rail is the stable spatial anchor.

It should:
- remain visually quieter than active content;
- show unmistakable selected state;
- preserve the canonical room order unless evidence supports change;
- use one icon family;
- preserve theme identity per room.

Do not add permanent rail items merely because a feature exists.

## 4. Search law

Intelligence search is a major door into the system.

Keep it prominent.

Search should:
- describe intelligence-oriented scope;
- support keyboard focus;
- surface results with provenance/state;
- not be reduced to a tiny magnifier icon in the top-right.

## 5. Top bar

Top bar remains quiet.

It may contain:
- current context/title;
- truthful system/connection state;
- compact high-value utility.

Do not repeat the same title three times across rail, top bar, and hero.

## 6. Hero/greeting

Greeting establishes human context, not marketing.

Keep it:
- concise;
- dynamic;
- useful;
- subordinate to actual intelligence.

Do not turn "Good morning, Shawn" into a giant landing-page headline.

## 7. Composition grid

Use consistent:
- max widths;
- column ratios;
- horizontal rhythm;
- spacing scale;
- alignment lines.

Elements that visually align should share actual grid logic.

## 8. Density law

Different rooms may have different density:
- Today's Intelligence: moderate;
- Reports: reading-oriented;
- Ledger/Evidence: data-dense but legible;
- Welcome: sparse;
- Identity: focused.

Do not force one card-grid template onto every room.

## 9. Progressive disclosure

Show:
- what matters first;
- details when requested;
- machine/evidence depth when useful.

Do not hide critical truth.

Do not expose every technical field by default.

## 10. Navigation behavior

Use real app routing:
- URL reflects room where appropriate;
- back/forward works;
- reload preserves meaningful context;
- deep links resolve;
- route changes do not wipe durable user state.

## 11. Mobile

Mobile may transform rail into:
- bottom navigation for top destinations;
- drawer;
- gesture-supported navigation.

Preserve:
- semantic room identity;
- visual depth;
- readable controls;
- current-location clarity.

Do not simply stack the desktop rail above the content.

## 12. Acceptance

Composition passes when:
- eye movement feels intentional;
- active room is obvious;
- search is easy to find;
- primary action is clear;
- spacing feels generous;
- no element is "floating for no reason";
- mobile feels designed, not collapsed.
