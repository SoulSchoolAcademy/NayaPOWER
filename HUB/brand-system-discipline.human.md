## What the symbols mean

- **Center crystal** — the essence: soul, divine spark, the awakened self. The hidden thing, now revealed.
- **Circular structure** — protection, order, cosmic intelligence. "The system behind the system."
- **Wings / side figures** — guardian energy. Protectors of wisdom; balance.
- **Crown** — sovereignty, rightful inheritance, mastery. "Remember who you are."
- **Typography** — elevated, ceremonial. "A title carved into history."

And the color semantics: purple = mystery, royalty, spiritual depth. Metallic light = divinity, precision, purity, worth. Black background = power, contrast, elegance.

## The core principle

Your words from the material:

> "A weak logo says 'here is a product.' A strong logo says 'here is a *world*.'"

The logo works because it creates a world — healing + truth + royalty + divine identity + luxury + transformation held together without tipping into fluffy, tacky, or aggressive. The proposal: this is the standard NayaNET's own brand is held to. Not "does it look nice" — "does it say *here is a world*?"

## The discipline: one logo is four logos

Treat the master logo as ONE of a system, not a single asset:

- **Full logo** — headers, hero, books, posters
- **Emblem-only** — profile icons, stickers, favicon, patches
- **Simplified icon** — embroidery, tiny placements
- **One-color** — print, foil stamp, merch

The key insight: the full logo being too detailed for tiny placements is *not a weakness* — it's system thinking. You don't shrink the masterpiece; you use the right member of the family. Same discipline applies to NayaNET's crystal "N": master emblem for hero and print, simplified mark for favicons and small UI.
