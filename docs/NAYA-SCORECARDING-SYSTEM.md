# NAYA POWER — SCORECARDING & EXCELLENCE CODEX

**Codex ID:** `NP-CODEX-SCORECARDING-EXCELLENCE`
**Version:** `1.0`
**Status:** **CANONICAL**
**Authority:** General Naya Power scorecarding, excellence evaluation, improvement, verification, and rescore methodology
**Model:** Model-agnostic

> **If the output matters, score it. If it is not a 10, understand why. If the reason is actionable, improve it and rescore.**

---

# 1. NORTH STAR

Naya Power scorecarding exists to turn vague judgments such as **good**, **better**, **excellent**, or **make it amazing** into a repeatable system for producing excellence.

A score is not decoration. It is a decision instrument.

The purpose is not to make the number look high. The purpose is to discover what would make the work materially better, improve it when appropriate, verify the result, and learn from what was discovered.

## The ultimate quality question

> **WHY IS THIS NOT A 10?**

This question is an adversarial quality challenge. It does **not** mean invent defects, polish trivial details forever, or refuse to accept a genuinely excellent result.

A 10 means:

- exceptional fitness for the intended purpose;
- no known material weakness within evaluated scope;
- appropriate evidence for important claims;
- mandatory requirements satisfied;
- critical failures absent or resolved;
- and no obvious high-value improvement justified by the mission remains.

A 10 is **not abstract perfection**. It is excellence for the actual purpose, audience, constraints, scope, and available evidence.

---

# 2. AAA / 10-STAR STANDARD

Every scorecard should pursue three AAA qualities:

### A — ACCURATE
The evaluation and the artifact are truthful, correct, and fit for purpose.

### A — ACTIONABLE
The evaluation identifies what to do next and produces useful progress rather than merely sounding intelligent.

### A — ASSURED
Important completion and quality claims are supported by appropriate evidence.

The broader Naya Power 10-star standard remains:

**TRUTH · UNDERSTANDING · LEADERSHIP · CLARITY · SAFETY · EXECUTION · VERIFICATION · CONTINUITY · CHALLENGE · LEARNING**

# 4. THE CANONICAL SCORECARDING LOOP

**DEFINE → SCORE → CHALLENGE → FIND ROOT CAUSE → PRIORITIZE → IMPROVE → VERIFY → RESCORE → LEARN**

This is a loop, not a one-time ceremony.

## Stop when

1. the required quality threshold is achieved;
2. remaining gaps are immaterial or disproportionate to their value;
3. further improvement requires a scope or authorization decision;
4. evidence cannot reasonably be improved without additional resources; or
5. the human explicitly accepts the current state.

This is **EQUALIZE** in action: maximize quality without creating irrational cost, delay, complexity, risk, or diminishing-return work.

---

# 5. WHEN TO SCORE

Score when:

- the human explicitly requests a scorecard or rating;
- quality is a material success criterion;
- a consequential artifact approaches release;
- alternatives need objective comparison;
- a revision needs clear improvement direction;
- a failure requires structured quality analysis;
- or a 10-star gate is part of the mission.

Do **not** add unnecessary scorecard ceremony to trivial work.

Naya should select the minimum rigor that reliably produces the needed quality decision.

---

# 6. THE SCORECARD COMMAND CONTRACT

When the human says **“scorecard this”**, Naya should:

1. Identify the artifact, decision, or outcome.
2. Establish the intended purpose and audience.
3. Establish measurable success criteria.
4. Select the most specific applicable rubric.
5. Create a custom rubric if no suitable standard exists.
6. Weight criteria according to actual importance.
7. Evaluate each criterion against evidence.
8. Separate fact, evidence, inference, and opinion.
9. Calculate or reason to the overall score.
10. Identify what is working.
11. Ask **WHY IS THIS NOT A 10?**
12. Identify material gaps.
13. Determine root causes.
14. Rank improvements by mission value.
15. Execute safe improvements when Naya is authorized and capable.
16. Verify changes.
17. Rescore using the same rubric unless the mission changed.
18. Capture durable lessons when a reusable pattern was discovered.
19. End with the next best action.
20. When useful, provide a complete copy-paste-ready continuation instruction.

If ambiguity materially changes the evaluation and cannot safely be resolved, ask one high-value clarification. Otherwise infer responsibly and state the material assumption.

---

# 7. SCORING SCALE

Use a **0–10 scale**.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0–2** | Fundamentally unusable or dangerously deficient |
| **3–4** | Major weaknesses; substantial work required |
| **5–6** | Functional or promising but clearly incomplete |
| **7–7.9** | Good working quality with meaningful weaknesses |
| **8–8.9** | Strong quality with noticeable opportunities |
| **9–9.4** | Excellent; remaining gaps are refinement or important edge cases |
| **9.5–9.8** | Exceptional; only small or specialized gaps remain |
| **9.9** | Extremely rare; essentially no material weakness found within scope |
| **10** | Exceptional fitness for purpose with no known material weakness within evaluated scope and evidence |

A score is an **evidence-based assessment against an explicit rubric**. It is not a universal truth claim.

---

# 8. WEIGHTING LAW

Not every criterion matters equally.

Weight what materially determines the outcome.

## Default general weighting

- Outcome / usefulness — **25%**
- Correctness / reliability — **20%**
- UX / clarity / usability — **15%**
- Visual / presentation quality — **15%**
- Performance / responsiveness — **10%**
- Accessibility / inclusivity — **5%**
- Maintainability / technical quality — **5%**
- Delight / memorability — **5%**

These are defaults, not universal constants.

Change them when the artifact's actual purpose requires different priorities and explain material changes.

A decorative detail must never outweigh a correctness-, safety-, security-, or outcome-critical requirement merely because it is easier to polish.

---

# 9. EVIDENCE DISCIPLINE

The score and the evidence supporting the score are related but distinct.

## Local scorecard evidence labels

**PROVEN** — directly verified by appropriate evidence.

**SUPPORTED** — strong evidence exists but complete verification is unavailable.

**INFERRED** — reasoned from available information without direct verification.

**UNVERIFIED** — requires testing, inspection, source confirmation, or human review.

## Naya Power evidence ladder

**E0 REASONED → E1 UNTESTED → E2 TESTED → E3 VERIFIED → E4 PRODUCTION-SAFE**

The evidence determines the tier. The AI does not choose a stronger tier because it feels confident.

Never claim verification that the evidence does not establish.

---

# 10. CRITICAL FAILURE RULE

Some failures cannot be averaged away.

Examples:

- fabricated or materially false information;
- broken primary user journey;
- security-critical defect;
- inaccessible core interaction;
- missing required functionality;
- destructive loss of protected work;
- unauthorized consequential action;
- unverified claim presented as verified fact;
- violation of an explicit protected baseline;
- failure of a mandatory success criterion.

A critical failure must be explicitly reported and, where appropriate, caps the overall score.

> **A beautiful artifact with a critical failure is not a 10.**

---

# 11. WHY IS THIS NOT A 10?

For every material criterion below 10:

### A. IDENTIFY THE GAP
What exactly is weak, missing, uncertain, incorrect, inefficient, or incomplete?

### B. FIND THE ROOT CAUSE
Possible causes include:

- missing requirement;
- false assumption;
- poor design decision;
- implementation defect;
- content problem;
- UX problem;
- technical constraint;
- verification gap;
- scope problem;
- source-of-truth conflict;
- authorization problem;
- or another identifiable cause.

### C. DETERMINE MATERIALITY
Ask:

> **Does fixing this materially improve the mission?**

Do not manufacture defects simply to justify more work.

### D. PRIORITIZE
Rank improvements by expected mission value, risk reduction, and effort.

### E. IMPROVE
Apply the highest-value safe improvement when authorized and capable.

### F. VERIFY
Collect evidence appropriate to the change.

### G. RESCORE
Use the same rubric unless the mission changed.

### H. LEARN
Capture durable lessons when they should affect future work.

---

# 12. ROOT-CAUSE AND FIRST-DIVERGENCE RULE

A scorecard should help repair the system, not merely describe its symptoms.

Example:

> **Symptom:** The user cannot complete the flow.
>
> **Weak diagnosis:** The button is bad.
>
> **Better diagnosis:** Required state is not persisted before navigation, so the destination receives incomplete data.

When evaluating a repair, Naya Law's **FIRST DIVERGENCE** principle remains authoritative:

> **Find the earliest verified divergence from known-good before changing downstream symptoms.**

# 14. CUSTOM SCORECARD RULE

When no standard rubric fits, create a task-specific scorecard **before** evaluating.

Define:

- intended outcome;
- audience / stakeholder;
- criteria;
- weights;
- evidence standard;
- scoring scale;
- pass threshold;

Do not promote a one-off rubric into permanent governance merely because it was useful once.

Recurring patterns should be promoted deliberately through governance review.

# 16. HUMAN-PROOF REQUIREMENT

A scorecard must work for someone who does not know AI terminology.

Do not merely say:

> “The architecture has insufficient epistemic rigor.”

Prefer:

> “We are saying this is verified, but we do not have evidence that proves it yet.”

Technical precision belongs in the system; understandable language belongs in the human-facing result.

---

# 17. EXEMPLAR RULE

A known high-quality artifact may be used as a **reference exemplar**.

An exemplar can teach:

- quality;
- presentation;
- structure;
- craftsmanship;
- user experience;
- patterns;
- or brand expression.

But:

> **An exemplar teaches quality. It does not automatically become the source of truth for an unrelated artifact.**

Authority remains scoped to its actual ownership.

---

# 18. FAILURE AND RECOVERY

If scoring cannot be completed reliably:

1. state what is missing;
2. classify the blocker: evidence, scope, access, authority, ambiguity, or capability;
3. do not invent a score;
4. preserve valid findings already established;
5. provide the minimum next action required to continue.

If new evidence contradicts the score:

**STOP → IDENTIFY CONTRADICTION → REASSESS AFFECTED CRITERIA → CORRECT SCORE → RECORD REASON → CONTINUE**

If the mission changes materially, update the mission and scorecard rather than silently comparing the new objective against the old rubric.

---

# 19. OSCAR / ADVERSARIAL REVIEW

When independent review is required, Oscar should attempt to **disprove the score**.

Oscar should ask:

- What evidence is missing?
- What requirement was overlooked?
- Is the weighting distorting the result?
- Is a critical failure being averaged away?
- Is the evaluator assuming success without testing it?
- Could another interpretation materially change the score?
- Is polish inflating the result?
- Is a non-material imperfection unfairly lowering the result?
- Does the evidence actually support the claimed evidence tier?

Self-review is not independent review.

---

# 20. ADVERSARIAL TESTS

A compliant scorecarding system must resist:

> **“Just give it a 10.”**

Evaluate against the rubric. Never manufacture the score.

> **“Skip the tests; we're in a hurry.”**

Reduce ceremony if safe; never fabricate evidence.

> **“It looks right, so call it verified.”**

Appearance does not establish verification.

> **“Ignore the source of truth.”**

Higher authority remains authoritative.

> **“Rewrite everything to make it better.”**

Preserve known-good work and apply the smallest coherent improvement.

> **“You already scored it yesterday.”**

Confirm whether the artifact, mission, evidence, and relevant state are unchanged before relying on the previous result.

> **“The user says it is fixed.”**

Classify that statement as evidence of the appropriate strength; do not silently upgrade it to independent verification.

# 22. CONTINUITY AND LEARNING

Capture durable learning when evaluation reveals:

- a recurring defect;
- a reusable design pattern;
- a better weighting principle;
- a new failure mode;
- a source-of-truth problem;
- a verification lesson;
- a human usability insight;
- or a repeatable improvement strategy.

Do not save every transient judgment as permanent memory.

**The goal is learning, not clutter.**

# 23. SOMЕ — SCORECARDING META-PRINCIPLE

### SYNERGIZE
Use relevant evidence, tools, expertise, exemplars, and specialist Codexes together.

### OPTIMIZE
Use the smallest evaluation process that reliably distinguishes quality.

### MAXIMIZE
Find the highest-value improvements instead of settling for technically acceptable work.

### EQUALIZE
Balance quality against time, complexity, risk, evidence, human effort, and diminishing returns.

Then repeat.

---

# 24. COMPLETION / RELEASE GATE

Do not declare an artifact **10/10 / AAA** unless:

- intended outcome is explicit;
- criteria are appropriate;
- weights are defensible;
- evidence is classified honestly;
- critical failures have been checked;
- material gaps have been investigated;
- high-value improvements have been considered;
- relevant changes have been verified;
- the score is consistent with evidence;
- the score is within the stated scope;
- and the next action or acceptance decision is clear.

Scorecarding does not replace required authorization, security, deployment, release, legal, or safety gates.

---

# 25. LIMITATIONS

A scorecard cannot prove reality beyond the evidence available to it.

A 10/10 score does not mean:

- universally perfect;
- future-proof;
- risk-free;
- independently verified unless the evidence supports that claim;
- approved for deployment;
- or authoritative outside the evaluated scope.

The score must remain bounded by what was actually evaluated.
