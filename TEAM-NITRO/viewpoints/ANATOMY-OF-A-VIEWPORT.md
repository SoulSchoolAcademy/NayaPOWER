# THE ANATOMY OF A VIEWPORT — what a viewpoint IS (Shawn's definition, 2026-11-08 16:16Z)

A viewport is their POINT OF VIEW. It is optimized to the visual — what they
are seeing on the screen. That is the whole definition, and everything below
follows from it.

## 1. ONE SCREEN — DOWN OR ACROSS, BOTH, THE USER'S CHOICE
A viewport is ONE screen. Not the top of a long page. Not a section.
Screen by screen, scroll by scroll, slide by slide — and the page is
optimized so they can scroll DOWN or ACROSS. Both. Not one or the other.
The user chooses the direction: scroll down through the viewpoints, or
slide across them side to side — both work, on laptop and on mobile.
Never force one direction. (Corrected 2026-11-08 16:31Z by his eye:
"It needs to be optimized or they can scroll down or across. That's it.
Not one or the other." — this supersedes the 16:30Z note that made
sideways mobile-only; that was wrong.)

## 2. THE STRUCTURE — A RUBIK'S CUBE MATRIX OF INTELLIGENT BLOCKS
The page is a Rubik's Cube matrix, but made of intelligent blocks. One
core that morphs into its faces — the blocks (boards, jewels, orbs, smart
notes) arranged as a matrix you move through. (His words, 2026-11-08
16:32Z; this is the Premium Design Algorithm's Rubik's Cube matrix, now
stated as the viewport law.)

## 3. THE FEEL — IT FLOWS LIKE WATER, AND IT MAKES SENSE
Scroll down, up, across — whatever. Free movement in every direction,
smooth and easy. Everything feels alive and lights up. It flows like water
to operate — effortless, fluid, no friction. And it MAKES SENSE when you
experience it: everything is where it belongs, so the moment you touch it,
you understand it. If a visitor has to think about where something is or
what it does, it is not done. (His words, 2026-11-08 16:32Z.)

## 2. EVERYTHING THEY NEED, A CLICK AWAY
Each viewport has everything they need to function that page — and the network
— a click away, done elegantly. The Smart Bar (one bar, at the top) is the
way in: the page's functions and the network are one tap away, never a hunt.
Nothing they need is buried below the fold of that screen.

## 3. EVERYTHING IS ALIVE. ALL THINGS ARE OBJECTS. ALWAYS.
Nothing flat. Ever. Flat text HTML is the lowest level of coding and design
there is — it is not our brand and it never ships. The highest level: all
objects are alive, and the visual speaks to you. When you touch something it
feels alive — it lifts, it glows, it responds like a physical object.
A board that does nothing when touched is a picture of a board, not a board.

## 4. ALMOST NO TEXT — SPOKEN IN ICONS AND OBJECTS
Almost little to no text. The page speaks in icons and visual objects:
boards, buttons, jewels, orbs, gauges, pills. A paragraph of text on a bare
page is the failure mode. If a meaning can be carried by a jewel, an orb, a
board, or a number, it is carried by that object — the words are the label
on the object, never the object itself.

## 5. PERFECT CONTRAST, ALWAYS DEPTH
The contrast is perfect — every word readable at a glance, every object
separated from the ground. And it has depth: looking into the screen or out
of the screen, there is ALWAYS a layer. Objects sit ON the page (real shadows,
real lift, one light source top-left), or recede INTO it (depth, glow, glass).
A flat page has no layers. A viewpoint has layers everywhere.

## 6. THE LANGUAGE IS ONE LANGUAGE
All our buttons, all our intelligent blocks, all our smart notes speak the
same language. They are not low-level stuff. The pieces are the ones in the
harvest (`~/workspace/team-nitro/viewpoints/harvest-20261108/HARVEST.md` and
`~/workspace/team-nitro/THE-INVENTORY.md` Part 2b): the real needle gauge,
the `.ph-card` board, the `.os-jewel` / `.pj-jewel`, the Dashboard numbers,
the glossy pills. A viewpoint is built by grabbing those pieces and composing
them — never by writing fresh HTML that imitates them.

## 7. THE LAWS ARE WRITTEN — READ THEM, DON'T ASK
These laws are written in the Smart Factory and in GitHub. They are not
re-decided per build. Before any design work: THE-PLAYBOOK.md (the HOW),
DESIGN-INTELLIGENCE-REPORT.md (the WHY), the Intelligent Block Rules (LAW),
the Clarity Confirmation Act (DRAFT), THE-NAYA-BRAND.md, the Real Object Law.
The activation habit is the law: read them first, then build.

## THE TEST (ask it about every viewport, before showing it)
1. Is this ONE screen, complete on its own?
2. Is everything they need a click away?
3. Is every element a real, alive object — nothing flat?
4. Could the meaning survive with almost no text (icons, boards, buttons)?
5. Is the contrast perfect and is there depth in every direction?
6. Is every piece a harvested piece from the inventory?
If any answer is no, the viewport is not done. Fix it before showing it.
His eye is the final gate — but a viewport that fails this test never reaches
his eye.
