## Part 2 — THE WORDS THEMSELVES TRANSLATE (second inside the round)

**What the maker builds:** a Translate tap on every note and message on
the page. One tap translates that note into the picked language through
the Workers AI translation model, on the staging worker. The translation
is labelled with its source and target language ("Translated from
Spanish"), and the original is one tap away — always. The citizen's own
words are never replaced and never stored as the translation. The tap
translates INTO the picked language without asking again (the pick from
Part 1 is the target). The source language is the note author's picked
language where the record knows it, else the viewer picks from a short
list on the tap — never silently assumed English. Multi-sentence notes
are segmented before sending (the model's known quirk). Long notes are
translated in order, never truncated silently — if a segment fails, the
tap says so plainly.

**Why on tap, not all at once:** the machine translates one note at a
time, on the citizen's tap. Auto-translating the whole feed on load
would burn the budget, risk the sentence-drop quirk across hundreds of
notes at once, and silently replace citizens' words with machine words
before anyone asked. The auto preference governs the chrome (Part 1,
free, instant) and is the default the tap inherits. That is the honest
shape of "auto translate the pages into any language for them."

**Step 0 (before any edit):** the maker proves the Workers AI binding
exists on the staging worker (read the bindings first-hand). If it is
not there, the maker adds the binding declaration to the staging worker
(a binding, not a purchase, not a secret) and proves one test
translation. If the account itself refuses Workers AI, the maker stops
the whole round and reports with the evidence — the content leg cannot
be built without it, and a half-round is not the shape.
