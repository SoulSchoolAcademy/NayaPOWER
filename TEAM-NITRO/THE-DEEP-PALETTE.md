# THE DEEP PALETTE — one color per color, deep not neon (Shawn's word, 01:30Z 2026-11-17)

## The law (his words)
Elegant and beautiful and clean and sharp — NOT a neon center, NOT rainbow-bright. The deeper versions of the colors are better: mid to darker, not super dark, not super light. Deep emerald, deep sapphire blue, deep gold, royal purple — the real nice ones. And the SAME color everywhere: the same yellow, the same green, the same blue, the same purple — not a whole bunch of different blues, purples, yellows. One unified system. Choose the nicest, the most elegant, the most beautiful, the most royal — and make everything pristine premium.

## Rules
1. **One hex per hue, everywhere.** A blue is `#0f52ba` on every page, every button, every tile, every digit. Never a second blue. Never a "slightly different" purple. If a value exists in this table, it is the only value for that hue.
2. **Deep, not neon.** Nothing rainbow-bright. Nothing that glows like a neon sign. If a color reads neon, it is the wrong value.
3. **Same lightness family.** All hues sit mid-to-darker together, so the system reads as one family — no one hue shouting while the others whisper.
4. **White at rest, color ignites** (DNA §3, unchanged). The deep color ignites on hover/touch in its own color; at rest the object is white on board black.
5. **The deck's nine colors stand.** The agreement deck's nine colors are the deck's identity, harvested verbatim — they are content, not system. The deep palette governs the system (buttons, rims, tiles, digits, chips, orbs, gauges). If his eye later wants the deck deepened, that is his call, not a round's.
