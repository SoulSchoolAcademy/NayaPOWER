# The Founding Letter — Scorecard and 10x Rewrite
Shawn's draft scored and rewritten by Naya Resolve, 2026-11-08. His raw words are the source; his canon binds the claims (honesty boundary: no invented numbers; stacked close: nine days to try her, and you're hired on top of it).

## The score
- As raw founder material: **9/10** — the voice, the belief, and the offers are all real.
- As a finished page-closer (conversion + understanding): **6/10** — a stranger has to swim too far to reach the ask, and two claims wobble.
- The rewrite below targets 9+.

## Ten things I love
1. The opener. Bugatti or golf cart — a laugh and a choice in one line. The two-second hook is already there.
2. "You're hired too." The reader stops being a customer and becomes a partner in four words.
3. The 50/50 split, stated plain. No fine-print energy. Trust you can feel.
4. "We don't advertise. We pay you to advertise." A whole business model in eight words.
5. "It's not about money — but it is about money." The honest tension disarms the skeptic.
6. Power = connected. A child gets it instantly, and it is literally the architecture.
7. Privacy as a plain promise — "not reading your mail" is the whole trust section in miniature.
8. The offers are real: 9 days of full power, a free Academy. Zero-risk stacking, not hype.
9. A founder's voice the whole way through. Founding letters convert on belief, and this believes.
10. "Be careful — you will fall in love." Confidence with a smile; it dares the reader to test it.

## Ten things I don't love
1. One long river of text. A phone reader tires before reaching anything they can tap.
2. There is no close. The letter trails off mid-thought ("just not with Naya Power") — no button, no single ask.
3. "10-star" repeats until it stops meaning anything.
4. "$5,000 a month for the rest of your life" — that needs top tier plus everybody staying forever. It reads as a promise, it breaks our honesty law, and a smart reader discounts everything after it.
5. The gold is buried. Privacy and the 50/50 arrive after long stretches; a skimmer never reaches them.
6. A few lines need a second read — "supercharger," "collective chain technology" unexplained, the trailing Naya Power line.
7. The question-stack in the middle ("What would that be like? How would you build it?…") is poetry that stalls the sale.
8. The trial's strongest fact is missing: the 9 days run at full top-level power, no card. "Zero risk" is claimed but never proven.
9. The free Academy floats mid-stream as an aside instead of landing as a stacked gift in the close.
10. What the reader actually gets day to day — the matching, the organizing, the daily reports — is scattered, never landing as one clear picture.

## Ten fixes (all applied in the rewrite)
1. Cut ~40%. One idea per beat, short paragraphs, white space as punctuation.
2. Hook first, ONE button last — the only ask on the page.
3. Stack the close in the canon order: 9 days full power → you're hired, 50% → Academy free → stay free forever.
4. Show the money math with real numbers (below) — believable beats big.
5. Move privacy up, right after "what she is," where trust is decided.
6. Keep his gold lines verbatim; cut everything that is not gold or glue.
7. Turn the question-stack into statements a grandma nods at.
8. Say "no card" out loud — it proves zero risk instead of claiming it.
9. Use "10-star" once, where it defines the promise.
10. Sign it like a founder, and echo the first line at the end — composed, not pasted.

The money math, checked: Max is $100/month; your half is $50 per member per month. Three a day for 30 days = 90 members → 90 × $50 = **$4,500 a month while they stay**. The draft's $5,000 needs every sale at the top plus perfect retention forever; $4,500 with the math shown is the honest number, and it converts better because a skeptic can verify it on a napkin.
