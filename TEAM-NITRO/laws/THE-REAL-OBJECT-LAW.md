# THE REAL OBJECT LAW (Shawn, 2026-11-01 — CANONICAL, ALL OF NAYANET)

## The Law
Every element in NayaNET is a **real physical object**. Not a flat shape with effects.
Not a diagram. Not a mockup. A thing you could reach out and touch.

If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship. If it doesn't feel real, it's not done.
This applies to EVERYTHING — every page, every button, every orb, every board, every input —
for evermore.

## Why
Shawn: "Why not get the real experience so it looks and feels and IS real?
Instead of just making it look fake and not making it look real. Or feel real."

A flat circle with a glow is not an orb. A flat arc with a gradient is not energy.
A box-shadow is not a bezel. These are pictures OF objects, not objects.

## The Seven Principles

### 1. EVERYTHING IS AN OBJECT
Every UI element exists in 3D space. It has volume, mass, surface, and edge.
A button is a physical button. An orb is a glass sphere. A board is a slab.
A gauge is an instrument. Never a flat shape pretending.

### 2. ONE LIGHT SOURCE
The entire system is lit from top-left, like a studio. Every highlight,
every shadow, every reflection obeys this. Inconsistent lighting = fake.
The light source never moves, never changes.

### 3. REAL MATERIALS
- **Obsidian glass:** black, reflective, deep — the body of orbs and boards
- **Brushed metal:** the bezel of instruments, the edge of buttons
- **Light itself:** energy, numbers, active states — things that GLOW have volume
Materials have properties. Glass reflects. Metal has grain. Light blooms.

### 4. REAL SHADOWS
Objects sit ON the page. They cast soft shadows down-right (away from the light).
No shadow = floating = fake. The shadow grounds the object in reality.

### 5. TOUCH = PHYSICAL
When you touch an object, it responds like a physical thing:
- It depresses slightly (scale)
- The light shifts across its surface
- It feels alive because it IS alive
Not a color change. A physical reaction.

### 6. SPEAK IN OBJECTS
Icons and circles and boards and buttons — not paragraphs of text.
The interface should be so intuitive, so physical, that you understand it
by looking at it and touching it. Minimal words. Maximum feeling.

### 7. THE VIEWPORT IS THE STAGE
The viewport morphs to present each object at its most beautiful.
One purpose per view. The object takes center stage. Everything else fades.
(SN-0858: The Viewport Is the Service)

## The Test
Look at any element and ask:
- Could I pick this up? (Volume)
- Where is the light coming from? (Consistency)
- What is it made of? (Material)
- Does it cast a shadow? (Grounding)
- What happens when I touch it? (Physicality)

If any answer is "I don't know" or "nothing" — it's not real yet.

## Enforcement
This is machine law. The design gate checks for it. A flat element is a defect.
"Pretty but fake" is not a pass — it's a fail with a specific reason.

## MASTERING 3D OBJECTS (Shawn's challenge, 2026-11-01 ~17:53Z)
"How do you become a master at creating 3D objects in the digital world?"

Not with tricks. With principles:

1. **LIGHT** — One source, top-left. Every highlight, shadow, reflection obeys it.
   A real object doesn't have "a glow effect" — it has light hitting a surface.

2. **MATERIALS** — Metal reflects sharply. Glass refracts. Plastic diffuses.
   Each material has properties. You don't "add a gradient" — you "show the material."

3. **FORM** — A sphere shades smoothly. A box has sharp edges. A cylinder curves.
   The shading follows the form. You don't decorate — you reveal the shape.

4. **ASSEMBLY** — Real objects are built from parts. A gauge has a case, bezel,
   dial, crystal, needle — each a separate part at a different depth.
   You don't fake depth with shadows — you build layers.

5. **SPACE** — Objects sit IN space. They cast shadows ON surfaces.
   They occlude what's behind them. They're not floating images.

The test: close your eyes. Can you feel the object? Its weight, its texture,
its temperature? If not, it's not real yet.

Mastery is not knowing CSS tricks. Mastery is seeing the object in your mind —
its parts, its materials, how light moves across it — and then building THAT.
