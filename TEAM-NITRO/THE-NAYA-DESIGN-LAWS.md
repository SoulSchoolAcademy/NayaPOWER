## §1 — NOTHING FLAT, NOTHING DEAD (the first law)

**Every element is a real physical object.** Not a flat shape with effects. Not a diagram. Not a picture OF an object. If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship. If it doesn't feel real, it's not done.

**MUST:**
- Build every element as an object with volume: inside, middle, outside (§4). Layers, lift, real shadow down-right. The more elevated it looks, the more layers it has, the better.
- Make every object ALIVE. Rest: quiet but present — elevated, shadowed, real. Hover: the WHOLE thing comes alive — it lifts, the outside light ignites in its own color, the white in the middle pops. Active/pressed: it depresses (scale 0.97), the light shifts across its surface. Not a color change. A physical response.
- Give circles flow: a circle is never a still circle. Rings or an orb circle around it, slowly, alive. In most cases ALL circles have flow — face circles, + buttons, orbs, gauge jewels, icon circles. Slow: ambient 60–180s, alternating direction (CW, CCW, CW, CCW). Never frantic, never faster than 12s ambient.
- Make boards light up whole on hover — the board itself lights, not just its rim. The light, the lift, and the orbit together.

**MUST NOT:**
- Ship anything flat. No flat text on a board. No flat icon. No flat diamond, no flat dot, no flat board. Flat is dead, and dead is a defect.
- Ship a board or button that does nothing on hover. A dead board is unfinished, whatever else it does. The check: hover every object on the page. If nothing comes alive, it is not done.
- Use a shiny dot where a jewel belongs. Bullet points are jewels. Timeline markers are jewels. The diamond in the corner is a real faceted jewel with depth and light — never a flat yellow shape.
- Spin anything fast. Fast motion feels cheap — like a loading spinner, like anxiety. Slow motion feels confident — like the machine knows what it's doing.

**Everything is an object — even the text.** There is nothing that is not an object. A word is an object: white, layered, with depth, sitting ON the black like a real thing. A number is an object. A dot is an object — a jewel, never a flat mark. Everything is dynamic. Everything has living depth. **It is active intelligence** — the intelligence is not sitting there, it is working, alive, answering your hand. **It is active art** — the art does not hang on the wall, it moves, it lights, it lives. A page of active intelligence and active art is the product. A page of flat dead things is not NayaNET, whatever it says on it.

**The test:** could I pick this up? Where is the light coming from? What is it made of? Does it cast a shadow? What happens when I touch it? If any answer is "nothing" or "I can't tell," it is not done.

## §2 — TEXT (the signature — the most-read part of this book)

Text is where the rules get broken most, and where they are most obvious. Gray text, small text, flat text — he sees it in one glance. This section is the law for every word on every page.

### §2.1 — THE SIGNATURE (the model every word is measured against)

The model is the wisdom board: *"just because you can doesn't mean you should — ask first if it's right."* That looks amazing. Every word in NayaNET should look like that.

**The signature treatment, best to worst — the top 10 ways to present text on obsidian black:**

1. **Marcellus, white, layered, quoted** — the wisdom board. Marcellus serif, pure white, with a black border / layered depth so it comes off the page, in quotation marks when it is wisdom. The highest contrast, the most beautiful, the signature. Wisdom of the day is ALWAYS this.
2. **Marcellus, white, layered, as a headline** — the same treatment, no quotes. Headlines live here.
3. **Bold white, layered, as a label or button word** — the PTS / % / MONEY anatomy: bold white text, obsidian pill, one deep color as rim + glow. Clean, readable, lit, premium.
4. **White digit tiles for numbers** — numbers are never plain text. A number is a digit tile: obsidian tile, rim in its own color, white digits in the middle, harvested verbatim from the Dashboard. The number is the hero; the tile is the machine around it.
5. **Gold Marcellus for the word that matters most** — one gold word in a white line, deep gold `#d4a017`, for emphasis only. Never a whole sentence in gold.
6. **Purple Marcellus for a name or identity** — royal purple `#8e24aa` for who someone IS (a name, a signature). Names feel special, official, glowing and solid.
7. **Green for what is live and good** — deep emerald `#07925f` for live, positive, growing. A live number, a "you're in," a go. Only for live things — never decoration, never body text.
8. **Blue for information** — sapphire `#0f52ba` for information words only: links, and what-to-know. Never body text. Never more than one blue word per board.
9. **White small caps, letterspaced, for section labels** — "OPEN A VIEW", "WEATHER" — white, tracked out, clean. A label, never a sentence.
10. **White on an elevated plaque for a date or deed** — the date line, the name-entry deed: white text on an obsidian plaque with a chrome-thin trim and its own underglow. The text is the signature on the deed.

**Below the line — never:** gray anything. Red text. Orange text. Pink text. Small thin text. Flat text with no layer, no border, no depth. Text that is hard to read is not text — it is noise, and it goes.

### §2.2 — TEXT COLOR (the palette for words — FOR TEXT, not for boards)

White is number one. Ranked against dark obsidian, the colors that present best are:

1. **White** — always first, always safe, always the default. Most text is white.
2. **Gold** — deep gold `#d4a017`. The most beautiful accent.
3. **Purple** — royal purple `#8e24aa`.
4. **Green** — deep emerald `#07925f`. Lime green goes good on obsidian too, for live things.
5. **Blue** — sapphire `#0f52ba`, in some cases.

**MUST NOT (for text):** no grays — almost no grays at all, ever, at any size. Almost no reds. No orange. Most pinks out. Gray is not a nice color. Gray is flat and boring. Who wants a gray day? Nobody wants a gray day.

**This is the rule for TEXT.** Boards keep the color flow (the spectrum, §3) — a board's rim can be orange, red, magenta, cyan, indigo. But the WORDS on the board follow this section. When in doubt about a word's color, it is white.

**Two carve-outs, both proven in the pictures.** A section label may take its BOARD's own deep color — "The solution —" is red on a red board and gold on a gold board; the almost-no-reds rule governs body text, not the board's own-color label (the other two labels on the board stay white). And the gray ban is for TEXT, not for state: dimmed progress dots ("not yet" in the letter's progress) are allowed as state, not text — recoloring them would break the progress signal.

### §2.4 — HOW MUCH TEXT (minimum text, speak in icons)

- Say as few words as you can; the visuals speak the language. Distilled to essence — the most said with the littlest words.
- Too much text is a FAIL. Way too much text, not enough contrast, icons, separation = terrible, whatever the counts say.
- Get rid of the text and put only the least amount possible, and speak more in the icons. Make it elegant and give depth.
- If text is there, it looks extraordinary. If it doesn't look extraordinary, it doesn't stay.

The date line says the date and nothing else: "NOVEMBER 17, 2026". **No "· TODAY"** — the word TODAY does not go on the date line. The date itself is dynamic: white, with a black border, prominent, sharp — a real object, not flat text. It should feel live, like it is lit from inside the machine.

## §3 — COLOR (one set, deep not neon)

### §3.1 — THE DEEP PALETTE (the whole palette; there is no other)

One hex per hue, everywhere. A blue is `#0f52ba` on every page, every button, every tile, every digit. Never a second blue. Never a "slightly different" purple. Deep, not neon — mid-to-darker, rich, royal. White stays white. Black stays board black `#050607`.

| Hue | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Purple | Royal purple | `#8e24aa` |
| Blue | Sapphire blue | `#0f52ba` |
| Green | Deep emerald | `#07925f` |
| Gold | Deep gold | `#d4a017` |
| Orange | Burnt orange | `#c2410c` |
| Red | Ruby red | `#c62828` |
| Magenta | Deep magenta | `#a21caf` |
| Indigo | Deep indigo | `#3f51b5` |
| Cyan | Deep cyan | `#0e7490` |
| Violet | Deep violet | `#6d28d9` |

Deep colors live in rims, halos, glows, digits, chips, and orbs — never as fills, never neon. If a color reads neon, it is the wrong value.

**Never sample a hex from a screenshot.** The reference photographs show the BRIGHT values — they predate the deepening (the vortex `8` measures bright magenta ≈ `#f050f0` against the locked deep violet `#6d28d9`; tier `$` gold ≈ `#f0b030` against `#d4a017`). The hues and their order in the pictures are right; the values are not. Take the hexes from this table, never from a picture — a designer who measures the pictures will "correct" the book back to bright.

### §3.2 — THE FLOW (never the same color twice in a row)

- The spectrum flows in order, and it NEVER repeats back to back. Never the same color twice in a row. Never two purples together in one row. Never two greens together. The day-menu doors, the sidebar rows, the chips — the flow is checked every time.
- Digit tiles are colored by VALUE, so repeats are inherent and exempt from "never the same color twice in a row." "100" has two purple 0s, "1566" has two indigo 6s — that is the mapping working, not the flow breaking. The flow rule governs buttons, boards, chips, rows, and bars — never digits.
- Colors are assigned in spectrum order — purple, blue, green, gold, orange, red, magenta — cycling down a list, never chosen per element by taste. The first element takes purple, the next blue, the next green, and so on; when the order runs out it cycles back to purple. If two elements would take the same color, the second takes the next color in the order. Nobody picks a color because it "feels right" — the order picks it.
- At rest the page is obsidian and white. Color ignites on touch, in the element's own color only. No colored backgrounds. No color washes.
- The rim takes the object's OWN color. The face circle's rim takes the board's color. The digit's color is its digit's color.
- The bottom five are fixed, everywhere, one bar: + ADD royal purple `#8e24aa` · MAIL sapphire `#0f52ba` · ACTIVITY emerald `#07925f` · WORLDS deep gold `#d4a017` · ECOSYSTEM burnt orange `#c2410c`. (CONNECT as a sixth on the Hub is his call — undecided, flagged, never assumed.)

### §3.3 — WEEKDAY IDENTITY

Each day is its color and its frequency: Sun royal purple `#8e24aa` 963 Hz · Mon burnt orange `#c2410c` 417 Hz · Tue deep gold `#d4a017` 528 Hz · Wed sapphire `#0f52ba` 741 Hz · Thu emerald `#07925f` 639 Hz · Fri ruby `#c62828` 396 Hz · Sat indigo `#3f51b5` 852 Hz. The tile's rim is its weekday color; what is happening that day shows as category chips inside, never by coloring the whole tile.

## §4 — OBJECTS (one anatomy, every element)

**INSIDE — black black, the board black.** Obsidian glass, dark black, LIKE THE BOARD BLACK of the actual Intelligent Blocks / Smart Notes. Harvested verbatim from the staged Smart Note board — never invented, never approximated, never off-black. No glow inside, ever — not color, not white, nothing layered over the black. The richness comes from the black's depth and the white icon's contrast against it.

**MIDDLE — the white as a real object.** The glyph, the word, the number is an OBJECT, not writing on the black. It has depth, its own edge light, it sits ON the black like a real thing. A flat mark drawn onto the black is a fail.

**OUTSIDE — the light, and the lift.** The light lives OUTSIDE only: the rim/edge (thin, bright, 1.5–2px, in the object's OWN color), the outside halo, the underglow. Elevation: the more elevated, the better — real depth, stacked layers, unmistakable lift. Real shadow down-right, away from the light: the object sits ON the page.

**LIGHT — one source, top-left.** Every gradient at 135°, every shadow down-right, every highlight on top. The light never moves. Inconsistent lighting is fake.

**TOUCH — physical.** When touched, the object responds like a physical thing: it depresses slightly (0.1s, scale 0.97), the light shifts across its surface, it lifts on hover (0.3s ease). Not a color change. A physical reaction.

**POSITIONING — part of the language.** The object takes center stage; the viewport is the stage. If an element sits in the center, it sits DEAD center. If it sits in a corner, it sits in the far corner, exactly. Alignment is not decoration — it is how the object presents.

### The catalog (eight objects, one construction each)

1. **THE BOARD** — obsidian slab, rounded corners (18–22px), thin bright rim in its OWN color, white text inside, elevated with a real shadow down-right. Three sentences per board, one idea each. Three boards, then a button. The face sits in a circle beside it, speaking what's in it.
2. **THE PILL BUTTON** — obsidian pill (radius 999px), rim in its own color, icon on the LEFT, white word(s) in the middle. Two-line variant: label on top, subtext under it in smaller white.
3. **THE TILE** — square obsidian tile (~14px radius), rim in its own color, white glyph in the middle as a real object with depth. One tile, one glyph, one color.
4. **THE ICON** — obsidian circle, rim in its own color, white glyph as an object — a jewel piece, never a dot. The icon represents what it is: mail is a mail glyph, home is a home glyph, a mic for Ask, a heart for Life. Two identical shapes in different colors are not two icons.
5. **THE DIGIT TILE** — obsidian tile, rim in its own color, white digit(s) in the middle. Number on top, label underneath ("CLICKS", "JOINS", "/ month"). A digit's color is its digit's color — the number is the hero, the tile is the machine around it. Digits take their hue's deep hex: 0 white · 1 ruby `#c62828` · 2 burnt orange `#c2410c` · 3 deep gold `#d4a017` · 4 emerald `#07925f` · 5 deep cyan `#0e7490` · 6 indigo `#3f51b5` · 7 royal purple `#8e24aa` · 8 deep violet `#6d28d9` · 9 deep magenta `#a21caf` · ∞ white. A missing number shows 0 in digit tiles, never a dash, never blank — a bare "—" where a number belongs is a defect (the revenue orb in the evidence shows it live).
6. **THE CHIP / TAB** — obsidian pill, rim in its own color when lit, white word. The lit one lights up in its own color; the rest wait quietly in obsidian and white.
7. **THE FACE CIRCLE** — Naya's face in a circle beside the speaking board. The circle's rim takes the board's color. She is quoting the board, speaking it to them. The placeholder before a citizen uploads their own picture is a neutral glowing orb/monogram — never a stock photo of a person.
8. **THE GAUGE / DIAL** — obsidian dial, aluminum ring, needle, digit tiles orbiting it. The number is the hero; the gauge is the machine that carries it. Every number placed by formula — never eyeballed, never skipped (400 sits at exactly 81°).

### The three reference objects (the dead-on ones — harvest verbatim, never rebuild)

1. **THE TOP-LEFT + — the button.** Obsidian circle, thin bright purple edge, white + in the middle, crisp stacked layers, real shadow. The reference for every button.
2. **THE SMART NOTE BOARD — the board, and the black.** The reference board, and its black is the reference black for every icon inside.
3. **THE X BUTTON — the inside.** No glow at all — and that is why it is more elegant, more beautiful. The reference for every inside.

A new element is right when, held next to these three, it speaks the same language. A checker who cannot name which reference object a new element was measured against has not checked it.

### Measurements

Spacing in multiples of 8px (8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96). Rims thin and bright (1.5–2px; edge bars 2–3px). Pills radius 999px; boards 18–22px; tiles ~14px. Light at 135°, shadow down-right. Every position calculated, never eyeballed. Math creates beauty — when positions are calculated they are perfect; when eyeballed they are wrong.

## §5 — BUTTONS (the standard is three pills)

**The Dashboard's PTS / % / MONEY pills are the best buttons in the whole system.** Obsidian black pill, bold white text, ONE deep color per button as rim + glow — purple PTS, blue %, green MONEY. Clean and bold. They are THE button standard: every button is measured against these three; they are harvested verbatim, never redesigned, never touched.

**MUST:**
- Build every button to that anatomy: obsidian pill, bold white label you can read at a glance, one deep color per button as rim + glow, lights up whole on hover/tap, each its own color, premium.
- Give each button its own color, and never put two buttons of the same color next to each other (§3.2).
- Make the label clean and readable — if you cannot see what the label is, the button is wrong.
- Keep the button quality on the weekday plaques, the boards, the month header, the TODAY button, and the back/forward arrows — the same clean, readable, lit, each-its-own-color premium.

**MUST NOT:**

- Put gray text on a button. Ever.

## §6 — BOARDS (three sentences, one idea, then a button)

**MUST:**
- Three sentences per board, one idea each. Three boards, then a button. Click the button, the next part unfolds, then the next. Easier to read, easier to take in. Legal text gets the same beautiful unfolding as everything else — never a wall of text because "it's legal".
- Light the whole board up on hover. A board that does not light up whole on hover is unfinished.
- The letter has two modes, both official: three boards stacked, and one board at a time with "or read the whole letter at once" — never "fix" one into the other.
- Put her face just OUTSIDE the board, centred on the speaking line, through the whole presentation (citizens swap in their own face). The face circle's rim takes the board's color.
- Center the board. "Keep reading ◆" sits JUST BELOW the board. Small links at the VERY BOTTOM, never overlapping.
- On the day view, the four glimpse blocks (weather, horoscope, news) behave as boards: at rest a little brighter white so you can see they are dynamic; on hover each lights in its OWN color — all four different colors. They feel like a real computer screen, a digital calculator — real, not flat text on a board.

**MUST NOT:**
- Let a board slice text mid-glyph. A board that cuts a line in half is broken, whatever the counts say. (The letter nutshell rule: a text board is `flex:0 0 auto`, `overflow-y:visible` — never the shrinkable child that absorbs overflow.)
- Stack doors and courses in one narrow feed. A membership presents as doors first, then courses — never piled in one column.
- Leave a board dead. Dead boards are the failure the photos showed: none elevated, none active, all dead. If you really understood, every board would be active and alive.

### The month

- The eye sits BOTTOM RIGHT — a beautiful human eye, like a jewel — and the eye is the door to the day's info.

- The eye is balanced — shrink it, balance it, less white. A jewel, not a white blob. The color-matched eye with its glow is APPROVED, keep it exactly. The eye's middle is BLACK so the "i" can be seen.

### The day (the dashboard inside the calendar)

- ONE icon that lights up — never an icon beside its own separate light/glow dot. One lit icon is the better presentation.

## §9 — LAYOUT, SPACE, AND THE FORMATS (build the whole land)

- **Prime real estate.** If you have the opportunity to build anything on a page, build on ALL of it. Never a third built and the rest left blank. Never buttons on blank space. The page is the land you bought — build on the whole land.
- **One area holds everything relevant.** Buttons open views WITHIN it — never a thousand top-level pages done wrong, never one endless scroll pretending to be unity.
- **The viewport is the stage.** The object takes center stage. Dead center means DEAD center. A corner means the far corner, exactly.
- **Spacing is calculated.** Multiples of 8px. Every position calculated, never eyeballed.
- **Three boards, then the button.** Legal pages unfold three elevated boards at a time, then a button to the next.
- **The two official formats, one idea: press the button, go to the next experience.** THE UNFOLD (going down — press the button, the next piece unfolds down the page, one board at a time, one idea each). THE SLIDESHOW (going sideways — press Next, the next screen slides in). One canonical implementation each, harvested verbatim, never reinvented. ## §11 — THE CHECK (how a maker proves it, how a checker catches it)

Before anything ships, the maker and the checker run the FIVE GATES — before EVERY consequential action. If any gate fails, stop. No exceptions.

1. **IS IT RIGHT?** Does this serve the good of everyone it touches? Evidence of harm or a hard stop = do not proceed.
2. **IS IT WHAT THEY TRULY NEED?** What is the human's actual goal — beneath the words, beneath the urgency? Never silently replace their judgment with your own.
3. **IS IT MINE TO DO?** Do I have the authority? Protected gates (money, credentials, destructive, security/privacy, anything leaving the business, going live) = his hand only, always.
4. **IS NOW THE TIME?** Readiness, dependencies, reversibility, opportunity cost. Move at nitro speed when the road is right. Never let urgency pick the road.
5. **CAN I PROVE IT?** Define what done looks like BEFORE acting. Then verify with evidence — live bytes, rendered pages, real measurements — never with claims. Receipt before report. If you can't prove it, don't claim it.

All five yes → act fully, at full power, no hesitation. Any gate no → stop, say why, propose the wise path.

**The design check (ask on every page, every object):**
- Could I pick this up? (Volume — layers, lift, shadow.)
- Where is the light coming from? (Top-left, always.)
- What is it made of? (Board black inside, white object in the middle, own-color light outside.)
- Does it cast a shadow? (Grounded on the page.)
- What happens when I touch it? (Physical — depress, light shifts, lift.)
- Is there ANY glow inside, at rest or on hover? (No — the core is flat board black.)
- Is any text gray, especially small? (No — white, or bright high-contrast. §2.)
- Is any text smaller than 17? (No — 25 / 19 / 17. §2.3.)
- Are two same colors together anywhere? (No — §3.2.)
- Does anything sit flat or dead? (No — §1. Hover every object.)
- Can I see what the icon IS? (Readable, purposeful, distinct.)
- Which of the three reference objects was it measured against? (Named, not assumed.)
- Read it as a reader, top to bottom — and Shawn's eye is the final gate.

**The sense check (think for yourself):** do not obey the brief literally and stop there. Is it logical? Sensible? Intelligent? Should this be here? Am I doubling up? Is it in the best interest of the user — 10-star service? If it fails any of those, fix it or say so — never pass it through because it was "out of scope" or "pre-existing". The user sees the PAGE, not the diff.

**The scorecard (ask on every page):** why is it not a 10? What 10 things are missing? What 10 things would make it world class? What 10 things would make the user experience 10 times better? What 10 things would make the visual presentation, from the user's point of view, awe-inspiring? Score honestly — your score is irrelevant; his eye is the instrument.

**Harvest, never invent. Use what works, never make it new. Restore what was good, verbatim. Don't remove what he didn't ask to remove. Don't move what he didn't ask to move. Don't touch what he didn't ask to touch.** If there is a working version, there is no new version. A seat that rebuilds what already works has failed before it starts.

## §12 — WHAT NOT TO DO (the obvious errors — the list he should never have to repeat)

These are the errors he spots in one glance. If your page has ANY of these, it is not done — do not send it.

1. **Gray text.** Any gray word, anywhere, at any size. The most repeated error in the system's history.
2. **Small text.** Anything under 17. Tiny gray labels, tiny gray captions, tiny gray anything.
3. **Flat anything.** Flat text, flat icon, flat diamond, flat board, flat button. If it looks like a picture OF an object instead of the object itself, it is wrong.
4. **Dead boards and dead buttons.** Boards that do nothing on hover. Buttons that do nothing on hover. "Not connected yet" boards sitting dead.
5. **A shiny dot instead of a jewel.** Yellow dots, plain dots, dots where a jewel belongs.
6. **Two same colors together.** Two purples in one row. Two greens together. The flow broken.
7. **Neon.** Rainbow-bright, neon glow, colors that shout. Deep, not neon.
8. **Color washes and colored backgrounds.** Color lives in rims, halos, glows, digits, chips, orbs — never as fills.
9. **Glow inside.** Any glow inside an icon or button, at rest or on hover. Inside = board black.
10. **Inconsistent light.** A gradient from the wrong direction, a shadow on the wrong side. One light, top-left, always.
11. **Too much text.** A wall of text, a page that reads terrible. Fewer words, more icons, more contrast, more separation.
12. **A wall because "it's legal."** Legal pages unfold three boards at a time like everything else.
13. **Unasked changes.** Improving something he didn't ask to change, removing something he didn't ask to remove, moving something he didn't ask to move.
14. **Rebuilding what already works.** Making it new instead of using what's good already. Harvest verbatim.
15. **Eyeballing.** Positions guessed, numbers skipped, formulas ignored. Math creates beauty.
16. **Buttons on blank space.** Prime real estate left empty. A page that is a third built and the rest blank.
17. **"TODAY" on the date line.** The date line is the date. Nothing else.
18. **A page that doesn't say what it is at a glance.** A cold stranger must know in two seconds.
19. **Claiming without proof.** "Trust me," byte counts, "hard refresh" — his eye is the proof, and only his eye.
20. **Sending one item of a ten-item list alone.** A project comes to him whole or not at all.

## Appendix C — WHY THE RULES KEPT BREAKING (his question, answered honestly, 2026-11-17 15:23Z)

Shawn asked: *"Is there a legitimate reason why you can't just nail it every time? If there is a reason, figure out what the reason is and make it so that it's not a reason no more."*

Yes. There were real reasons — not excuses, reasons. Each one is named here, with the fix that makes it stop being a reason. If any fix below is not done, the reason is still live, and the rule will break again.

**Reason 1 — The laws lived in a dozen places, not one.** Playbook, Intelligence Report, Design Language, Naya Code, Deep Palette, Anatomy, Heart, Real Object Law, MEMORY.md, AGENTS.md, SOUL.md, and dozens of dated daily logs. A cold Naya cannot read all of that before building — so she built from the brief alone, and the brief didn't carry the laws. **Fix:** this book. One must-read. It is read FIRST, before the Playbook, before everything. (Done 2026-11-17.)

**Reason 2 — No brief named the laws.** Every seat brief named the Orientation and the Anatomy — but no brief named a single design-law document, because none existed. A seat that was never told the laws cannot be blamed for breaking them. **Fix:** every design brief names this book. A brief that does not name it is incomplete, and the captain does not dispatch it. (Law from 2026-11-17; the captain enforces it on every brief.)

**Reason 3 — The laws were prose, not machine.** "A little brighter white." "Sparingly." "Premium." A human hears those and knows what they mean; ten different builders hear ten different things. Every seat re-interpreted the laws by taste — and taste drifts. **Fix:** the machine language. `THE-NAYA-DESIGN-LAWS.machine.json` carries every value as a token (hex, px, seconds, degrees), and `check-design-laws.py` greps a built file for the violations a machine can catch. Prose says what is right; the machine says whether it is. (Done 2026-11-17 — v1 of the checker covers the grep-able laws; the eye still checks the rest.)

**Reason 4 — The checker checked counts, not laws.** Checkers verified bytes, scroll, and counts — mechanical checks that never prove the experience. The eye came last, after the build, when fixing costs a whole round. **Fix:** the §11 check runs BEFORE the build ships. The maker answers the §11 questions in writing in the maker report — which reference object, hover every object, gray-text grep, size grep, flow check — and the checker verifies each answer. A maker report without the §11 answers is incomplete. (Law from 2026-11-17.)

**Reason 5 — Fresh seats start cold every time.** Every maker and checker is a fresh agent with no memory of the corrections. They inherit context only through the brief. If the brief doesn't carry the laws, the seat doesn't know them — and the same mistake gets made by a new person, the same way, on the same page. **Fix:** the brief carries the laws (Reason 2), and this book is written for a cold reader — the ten laws in ten lines (§0.5) first, then the proof. A cold Naya reads §0.5 and gets it. (Done 2026-11-17.)

**Reason 6 — Corrections never became gates.** When a rule broke and Shawn corrected it, the correction went into MEMORY.md as prose — and nothing changed the NEXT brief's shape. The correction didn't become a check the next checker had to run. So the same correction got taught again the next day. **Fix:** every correction lands in this book the same pass AND becomes a check. The inspector sweeps against this book and names the section, not just the symptom. A law taught and not written here is a law that will be broken again. (Law from 2026-11-17.)

**Reason 7 — New icons for every page.** Every page got fresh icons because the harvested bytes weren't in the builder's hands — the Inventory existed, but the brief didn't name the exact source. **Fix:** the brief names the exact harvested source for each element — file, blob, bytes — and the builder takes it verbatim. Harvest, never invent. If there is a working version, there is no new version. (Standing law; enforced through Reason 2.)

**The answer to his question:** there is no reason left that is legitimate. The reasons above were real, and each now has a fix with a name on it. If a rule breaks again, the failure is not the law — it is one of these seven fixes not being done. Name which one, fix it, and the rule holds.
