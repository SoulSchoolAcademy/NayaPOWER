## BLOCK 0 — THE LANGUAGE OF HIGH-LEVEL DESIGN
Source: Shawn's word, 2026-11-08 ~17:41Z. His words, verbatim:

"See if you pay attention teaching the language of high level design. This become a language that intelligence like speak and created and understand to create beauty. To create valuable extraordinary presentations of intelligence through optimized viewports."

This block is the language itself. The other nine blocks are written IN it. A Naya does not memorize this file. She speaks it.

**VOCABULARY — the words intelligence speaks.** The parts, each a real object with a fixed meaning, harvested from the Factory, never invented:
- **board** (.ph-card) — holds. Every content element is a board.
- **jewel** (.os-jewel, .pj-jewel) — marks. A check, an X, a heading, a meaning.
- **pill** (.primo, .up-act, .smart-tab) — acts. Every action is a glossy obsidian pill, elite and lit the same.
- **orb** (.ib-card-orb, .orb--hero, .intel-avatar) — is the person. The atom at the centre; everything orbits them.
- **gauge** (the needle-law gauge) — measures. Number on top, gauge underneath, needle laps the dial in sets of 500.
- **number** (tiled digits) — speaks. The hero of the page, never small grey text.
- **tab / bar** (.smart-tab, the Smart Bar) — flows. One bar at the top, the flow between views.

**GRAMMAR — the rules that combine the words.** The laws:
- **Real Object Law** — everything is a real physical object; one light top-left; real materials; real shadows; touch = physical response; speak in objects; viewport is the stage.
- **Depth order** — Field > Board > Nested panel > Control > Overlay. Depth is mass from real shadows, not glow.
- **Number law** — number on top, gauge underneath; the needle laps the dial in sets of 500.
- **Anatomy test** — one screen, complete on its own; everything a click away; every element alive; meaning with almost no text; contrast perfect, depth in every direction; every piece harvested.
- **Harvest-first** — every element traces to the piece index or a named harvest source. A part without a source is a look-alike, not a word.
- **Design-contract gate** — measured, never a vibe: zero white/light backgrounds; every element raised; gauges/numbers alive; no text-in-image, no pasted document; no bare text blocks.

**Hook:** The design is not notes to remember. It is a language intelligence speaks.

**Nutshell:** High-level Naya design is a language. Its vocabulary is the parts — board, jewel, pill, orb, gauge, number, tab, bar — each a real object with a fixed meaning, harvested from the Factory, never invented. Its grammar is the laws — the Real Object Law, the depth order, the number law, the anatomy test, harvest-first, the design-contract gate — the rules for how the words combine. A Naya does not memorize this language. She speaks it: she reaches for the words, puts them together by the grammar, and what comes out is beauty — valuable, extraordinary presentations of intelligence through optimized viewports. Low-level is not a sentence in this language. It cannot be spoken. That is the lock-in.

**Human:** You do not study the brand like a manual and then try to remember it while you work. You speak it the way you speak your own tongue — the words are ready before you need them. A board is a word. A jewel is a word. A gauge is a word. The laws are the grammar: they tell you how the words fit, which order they go in, what a sentence is allowed to say. When you build a viewport, you are not decorating a page. You are saying something in the language — and the language only knows how to say it beautifully, because every word in it is already Bugatti-level. If a sentence comes out flat, it is not a sentence in this language. Say it again, with the real words.

**Child:** Imagine you are learning to talk, and every word you learn is a shiny toy — a black glass board, a gold jewel, a button that lights up, a speedometer with a real needle. The words are the toys, and the grammar is how you play with them together. You never have to remember a list of rules, because the toys only fit together one way — the beautiful way. You just speak, and what you say is beautiful, because the words themselves are beautiful.

**Grandma:** Dear, a language is how a family talks to each other, and this family talks in beautiful things. You don't memorize a dictionary — you live in the house, you hear the words every day, and soon they are yours. The board, the jewel, the button, the gauge — those are our words. Speak it every day and you will never forget it, because it is not something you learned. It is something you are.

## BLOCK 2 — THE REAL OBJECT LAW
Source: `~/workspace/team-nitro/laws/THE-REAL-OBJECT-LAW.md` (Shawn, 2026-11-01 — CANONICAL, ALL of NayaNET, forever).

**Hook:** If it doesn't look touchable, it doesn't ship.

**Nutshell:** Every element in NayaNET is a real physical object — not a flat
shape with effects, not a diagram, not a picture OF an object. Seven
principles: everything is an object · one light source, top-left · real
materials (obsidian glass, brushed metal, light with volume) · real shadows
(objects sit ON the page) · touch is a physical response · speak in objects
(icons, circles, boards, buttons — minimal text) · the viewport is the stage.
"Pretty but fake" is a fail.

**Human:** Pick it up in your mind. A button is a piece of obsidian glass
with a white rim, lit from the top-left, casting a shadow down-right because
it sits ON the page. A gauge is an instrument with a case, a bezel, a dial,
a crystal, a needle — each a separate part at a different depth. When you
touch it, it depresses, the light shifts across it, it answers like a real
thing. Not a color change. A physical reaction.

**Child:** Imagine a real toy car and a drawing of a toy car. The drawing
looks like a car, but you can't pick it up, you can't press its buttons, it
has no weight. Naya pages are the real toy car. Everything you see, you can
touch — and when you touch it, it moves, like a real thing does.

**Grandma:** Dear, real things have weight and shadow and shine. A real
button sits on the table and casts a little shadow. A picture of a button
does not. We build the real button, every time — because pretending is not
good enough for the people we serve, and they can feel the difference even
if they cannot name it.

**Naya:** You build objects, never pictures of objects. You test every
element with five questions: could I pick this up? Where is the light coming
from? What is it made of? Does it cast a shadow? What happens when I touch
it? If any answer is "I don't know" or "nothing" — it is not real yet, and
you do not ship it. A flat element is a defect. The viewport is the stage:
one purpose per view, the object at center stage, everything else faded.

## BLOCK 3 — THE PLAYBOOK (THE HOW)
Source: `~/workspace/team-nitro/THE-PLAYBOOK.md` (2026-11-02; Grade 6 locked, climbing to 10).

**Hook:** Calculate, never eyeball. The math IS the design.

**Nutshell:** The Playbook is the HOW: five truths — every position is
calculated by formula, never eyeballed · every element is a physical object
with material, weight, and one light source · movement means the machine is
working · the whole system speaks one language · Shawn's eye overrides
everything, including this document. The formula: gauge numerals at
`angle = -135 + (value/500)*270`; the 8px grid for linear layouts; obsidian
`#0a0a0f` to `#1a1a20` at 135°; white text always; slow motion (60s+ ambient,
18–25s active, never under 12s); alternate rotation; all six numerals
0–500, never skip one. The Premium Design Algorithm: viewpoint by viewpoint,
one core page — a Rubik's Cube matrix — the Trinity (Dashboard, Hub, and
that page), the person the atom at the centre, she is Anaya.

**Human:** Before you draw anything, write the headline — one sentence, real
numbers. Then calculate every position and write the formula down. Then
assign materials — not colors, materials: the bezel is obsidian, the ring is
aluminum light, the gears are aluminum dark. Then decide what moves, and
write one sentence for each moving part saying what it DOES. If you can't
write the sentence, the part doesn't move. Then check the light: every
gradient at 135°, every shadow down-right, every highlight on top. Then run
the laws. Then show Shawn. Say nothing. Let his eye judge.

**Child:** Imagine building with Lego, but every brick has to go in exactly
the right spot — you measure where it goes, you don't guess. And the bricks
are all from the same box, so they all fit together and look like they belong.
And the wheels turn slowly, like a real clock, not fast like a toy that is
about to fall apart. That is how you build a Naya page: measure, match, slow.

**Grandma:** Dear, the beautiful thing about good work is that it is exact.
The numbers sit exactly where the math puts them — the 400 sits at 81°,
because the formula says so, and there is room for it. Nothing is guessed,
nothing is "close enough." And when it is exact, it is beautiful — the
beauty comes out of the math being right, not out of decoration on top.

**Naya:** You never eyeball a position. You write the formula, you place by
the formula, you show the formula. You harvest the parts registry before you
build anything — if the part exists, you use it; you do not invent it. You
ask the three questions before showing Shawn: is every position calculated?
Does it feel like a machine? Can you get it in 2 seconds? If any answer is
no, you fix it — you do not show him yet. His time is the scarcest resource.
And when he corrects you, you write it down, add it to this book, commit,
push — so the next Naya never needs the same correction. That is how the
grade climbs.

## BLOCK 4 — THE DESIGN INTELLIGENCE REPORT (THE WHY)
Source: `~/workspace/team-nitro/DESIGN-INTELLIGENCE-REPORT.md` (2026-11-02 — what I got wrong, what I learned).

**Hook:** Premium is not glow. Premium is the math being right.

**Nutshell:** The WHY behind the Playbook, written from the mistakes: I added
a pulsing glow to the orbs and called it premium — Shawn said premium is not
"color color glow." I skipped the 400 numeral because "the bottom needs to
be clear" — the formula gives 81°, the bottom is 90°, there is room; I was
outsmarting the math. I left the bezel plain — the edge is what the eye hits
first; an unfinished edge makes the perfect center look like a prototype. I
"improved" things Shawn didn't ask me to change — and made them worse. I
scored my work 9/10 and Shawn saw problems I missed — my score is
irrelevant; his eye is the instrument. What I understand now: math creates
beauty; physical is not a style, it is a requirement; slow is premium;
the system is one material; data display IS the design.

**Human:** Everything else is dead — flat cards, static numbers, colored
rectangles. Ours turns: gears at 90 seconds, the needle settled at 300, the
orbs breathing. You can SEE the system working. And trust comes from
precision: when the 400 sits at exactly 81°, when the ticks hit every 10
without drift, when the gears mesh without overlap — you feel it. When it is
wrong, you feel that too. Precision is credibility.

**Child:** Imagine you drew a clock but you guessed where the numbers go, and
you left the edge of the clock blank, and you added sparkles to hide it.
Someone who knows clocks looks at it and says: the numbers are wrong, the
edge is not finished, and the sparkles are not the clock. The lesson is:
put every number exactly where it belongs, finish the edge, and never hide
behind sparkles.

**Grandma:** Dear, the hardest lesson is the simplest one: don't fix what
isn't broken, and don't decorate what isn't good yet. Make the thing itself
good — the numbers exact, the edge finished, the work honest — and the
beauty follows. Glow is what you add when you don't have those things. We
have those things. We don't need the glow.

**Naya:** You learn each lesson once, in writing, or you have not learned it.
The rules, earned the hard way: never eyeball · top-left light · white text ·
slow motion · alternate direction · all the numbers · finish the edge ·
don't touch unasked. Before you build: write the headline, calculate, assign
materials, decide what moves, check the light, run the laws. Then show
Shawn, say nothing, and if he says it is wrong, it is wrong — you learn,
you write it down, you never make that mistake again.

## BLOCK 9 — THE HARVEST
Source: `~/workspace/team-nitro/viewpoints/harvest-20261108/HARVEST.md` (captain's own eyes, 2026-11-08 16:12Z).

**Hook:** Pages get made in three minutes, because the pieces are laid out.

**Nutshell:** The Harvest is the pieces, laid out — copied VERBATIM from the
file named, never restyled, never rebuilt "in the style of"; change only the
text inside. Harvested live from Smart Factory + Dashboard (Playwright
Firefox, 1280px): the real gauge (the needle-law speedometer — the
artifact's fake speedometer is DEAD); the board (`.ph-card`, obsidian
gradient, 2px white border, radius 20px, layered shadow, hover lifts −4px
and glows in `--ac`); the jewel (`.os-jewel` / `.pj-jewel`, check in green,
X in grey/red — the jewel is the real part, the glyph is content); the
numbers (BIG white numerals in orbiting orbs, jewel on top, label
underneath — numbers are the hero, never small grey text); the pills
(glossy obsidian pills, perfectly lined up); the chart-as-boards language
(a chart is just boards connected together — skinny rectangles, all the same
language, all alive, check jewel for what it has, X for what it doesn't);
what a viewpoint is (ONE viewport, all buttons perfectly lined up, everything
alive — NOT a long text page). Addendum 2: the intelligent block orb
(`.ib-card-orb`, 42px, three radial gradients, 6-layer shadow, floor shadow
— the orb sits ON the page) and the smart-note format itself (SN-2290:
Hook → Nutshell → Human → Child → Grandma → Naya).

**Human:** This is how a page gets made in three minutes: the pieces are
already there, laid out in front of you, each one proven. You grab the
gauge, you grab the board, you grab the jewel, you put them in the page, you
change the words. You do not redraw the gauge. You do not invent a new
button because "this page is different." Different page, same orb. That is
the language — and the language is what makes it Naya in three minutes
instead of three days.

**Child:** Imagine a box of the very best building blocks in the world — each
one shiny, each one real, each one already perfect. You don't make a new
block out of paper when the box has a real one. You take the real one, you
snap it in, you change the little word on it. That is the harvest: the box,
open, everything laid out, ready.

**Grandma:** Dear, the wise builder doesn't carve every nail from scratch —
she keeps the good nails in a jar, and she knows where the jar is. This
Harvest is the jar: every good piece, found with my own eyes, written down
with its exact place, so the next builder doesn't have to search, doesn't
have to guess, and doesn't have to settle for a poor copy.

**Naya:** You copy VERBATIM. Never restyle, never rebuild "in the style
of" — change only the text inside. If a piece's styling cannot be pinned
(the Hub's `.note-card` has no CSS rule; the mail `.os-savebtn` rule is not
in `mail.html`), it is NOT listed — you follow up with a grep, you do not
guess. The fake speedometer is dead; the real gauge is the only gauge. And
the format of this very file — Hook, Nutshell, Human, Child, Grandma, Naya —
is itself a harvested piece (SN-2290): the same idea told in layers, each
section its own board, each with its own jewel.
