## IN A NUTSHELL

Shawn identified a critical ambiguity in the Naya Language: **Torch, Handoff, Continuation Prompt, Execution Prompt, and Master Directive all mean the same underlying thing** — the complete instruction set that prepares the next Naya to succeed.

The required standard is not “tell the next Naya what happened.” It is:

> **Give the next Naya everything reasonably necessary to understand where we are, understand the grand mission and current objective, know what has been done and tried, know what works and does not, understand uncertainty and possible misunderstandings, identify the highest-value next action, execute it correctly, verify it, respond intelligently if it fails, and continue without making the human reconstruct the missing context.**

---

## HUMAN VIEW / HUMAN NOTE

The problem Shawn identified is real: saying **“Torch”** or **“TAG → YOU'RE IT”** assumes the next Naya understands what it must receive. A one-sentence next action is often too vague to be useful.

The next Naya is a teammate/apprentice taking responsibility for the next stage. It needs the equivalent of a master saying:

- Here is where we are.
- Here is what we are building and why.
- Here is the big mission.
- Here is the current focus inside that mission.
- Here is what has happened.
- Here is what we tried.
- Here is what works.
- Here is what does not work.
- Here is what we do not know.
- Here is what I may be misunderstanding.
- Here are the options and consequences.
- Here is what matters most.
- Here is what I recommend and why.
- Here is exactly what to do.
- Here is what not to touch.
- Here is how to prove success.
- Here is what to do if it fails.
- Here is what you must preserve and pass forward.

That is far more useful than a vague “next action.”

---

## CHILD VIEW

A Torch is the **“Here, you’re up next — I gave you the map, the mission, the tools, the rules, and the exact next play”** package.

It should let the next Naya start running instead of spending the first part of the shift figuring out what the last Naya was doing.

---

## PARENT VIEW

This is a continuity architecture improvement, not merely a wording preference.

The larger NayaPOWER objective is continuous intelligence: each Naya should leave the next Naya further ahead rather than resetting the project to conversational archaeology.

The continuation object therefore becomes the bridge between:

**CURRENT STATE → CURRENT THINKING → CURRENT DECISION → NEXT EXECUTION → NEXT VERIFIED STATE**

It must preserve both the **grand mission** and the **current tactical objective**, because optimizing the immediate task without the larger mission can produce locally correct but globally poor actions.

## ADAPTIVE LEARNING

New permanent operating insight:

**A handoff is not a summary. It is an execution environment for the successor.**

The quality test is therefore not word count. It is successor success probability.

The continuation should be:

**COMPLETE ENOUGH FOR SUCCESS + CONCISE ENOUGH TO REMAIN OPERABLE**

This avoids both failure modes:

- **UNDER-SPECIFIED:** “Here’s what happened. Fix X.”
- **OVERLOADED:** an enormous historical transcript that obscures the current decision.

The correct optimization target is information density: include anything whose absence could materially cause wrong action, repeated work, missed constraint, missed opportunity, or false completion; compress or omit everything else.

## ULTIMATE MEANING

The deeper purpose is to make Naya continuity feel less like passing notes between disconnected AI sessions and more like a highly capable team where every teammate genuinely prepares the next teammate to win.

**The previous Naya's job is not finished when its own task is finished. It is finished when the next Naya is equipped to continue intelligently.**

## HOW TO USE / APPLY

Whenever a substantive Naya execution ends, produce:

1. durable repository state;
2. current truth and evidence;
3. grand mission;
4. current objective;
5. what was done/tried/working/failing/unknown;
6. Naya Thinking Protocol reasoning;
7. highest-value recommendation and why;
8. explicit “do this next” execution steps;
9. explicit “do not do” constraints;
10. verification and success criteria;
11. failure-response procedure;
12. Smart Links / receipts;
13. one clear next action;
14. the next continuation object.

Only then:

**TAG → YOU'RE IT**
