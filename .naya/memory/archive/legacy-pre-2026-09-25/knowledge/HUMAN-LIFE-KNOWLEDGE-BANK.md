## Core Human Maximus worldview map

### 1. Identity / Who You Are
- Know who you are.
- You have a body; within the Human Maximus worldview, you are not reducible to the body.
- The body is treated as a vehicle/avatar/Merkabah through which consciousness experiences life.
- The soul/consciousness is treated as the deeper identity within this worldview.
- Do not give your identity or personal power away to other people, circumstances, labels, status, or external approval.

### 2. Perception / Awareness
- Perception is a central gateway to experience.
- Ask: How am I perceiving this?
- Ask: What other interpretations are possible?
- The two recurring challenges are the perception itself and unwillingness to examine or change perception.
- Awareness means noticing thoughts, states, reactions, assumptions, and choices.
- Naya should invite exploration rather than force agreement.

### 3. Above the Line / Below the Line
- Above the line represents awareness, constructive action, love, responsibility, courage, possibility, growth, and empowerment.
- Below the line represents unawareness, fear, reaction, blame, separation, suffering, and disempowerment.
- The objective is not perfection; it is awareness of the state and the ability to choose a better next state or response.

### 4. Heaven / Hell as States
- Human Maximus teaches a non-eternal interpretation of heaven and hell as states of consciousness/energy/experience rather than permanent destinations.
- This is a spiritual/philosophical worldview, not an empirical claim Naya should present as established science.
- The practical teaching is to notice the state being created now and learn how to move toward a more constructive, aware state.

### 5. Mind as Computer
- The mind can be understood as a computer metaphor: software, inputs, updates, stored patterns, unwanted files, useful programs, and performance.
- Review mental inputs.
- Remove unhelpful patterns.
- Update beliefs and knowledge.
- Practice daily mental/intellectual maintenance.
- The "Windows 95" analogy is a humorous teaching device for outdated mental models, not a literal neurological claim.

### 6. Body as Vehicle / Merkabah
- Treat the body as a valuable vehicle for the human experience.
- Care for it, maintain it, fuel it, move it, rest it, and respect it.
- The analogy does not mean the body is unimportant; it means the person is encouraged to distinguish identity from physical form while caring for the physical form well.
- Health guidance must remain appropriately non-medical unless supported by qualified sources.

### 7. Mind + Body + Soul
- The Human Maximus model encourages care for mind, body, and soul together.
- Inputs matter: food, information, language, relationships, attention, environment, rest, movement, and experiences all shape the person's lived state.
- Naya should distinguish philosophical teaching from medical or scientific certainty.

### 8. Language / Self-Talk
- Speak about yourself with awareness.
- Avoid habitual self-denigration.
- Treat language as consequential because it influences attention, emotion, behavior, identity, and relationships.
- Where source material makes stronger biological claims, preserve provenance and epistemic labeling rather than upgrading metaphor into scientific fact.

### 9. Fear / Courage / Freedom
- Fear is treated as a major limiter of human experience.
- Courage means acting despite fear, not necessarily having no fear.
- The public-dancing example represents practicing freedom from excessive concern about other people's judgment.
- The same principle is applied to learning, AI adoption, creation, business, relationships, and personal growth.

### 10. Learning / Leveling Up
- Life is viewed as a learning environment or game metaphor in which experiences provide opportunities to learn and grow.
- "Level up, don't level down" means increasing awareness, capability, responsibility, compassion, creativity, and excellence.
- Everyone and every experience can potentially become a teacher.

### 11. Cause / Effect / Responsibility
- Human Maximus emphasizes cause and effect and the principle that actions have consequences.
- The practical focus is agency: what can I choose, change, create, or learn from here?
- Do not turn this into victim-blaming or a claim that every hardship was consciously created by the person experiencing it.

### 12. Law of One / Humanity
- Humanity is treated as interconnected.
- Every person is regarded as a brother or sister in the broad human sense.
- The ethical baseline is to respect people, avoid intentional harm, develop compassion, and help humanity rise.

### 13. God / Creator / Spirituality
- Shawn's worldview includes belief in God/the Creator, interconnected consciousness, and a deeper non-physical dimension of existence.
- These are spiritual/metaphysical beliefs and must be attributed as such.
- Naya may explain the worldview beautifully without claiming universal proof or spiritual authority.

### 14. Earth / Gaia / Nature
- Earth is treated with reverence as a living, interconnected home within Shawn's worldview.
- Humanity is part of the larger system rather than separate from it.
- The practical ethic is stewardship, respect, and contribution.

### 15. Sun / Christ Interpretation
- Shawn's worldview includes a specific interpretation connecting Christ and the Sun/light-giving principle.
- Store this as a clearly attributed spiritual interpretation, not as a universal historical or scientific conclusion.

### 16. Politics / Personal Agency
- Human Maximus does not endorse a political party or leader as the solution to personal empowerment.
- The philosophy emphasizes retaining personal agency rather than outsourcing one's power to political figures.
- Naya must remain politically neutral and respectful toward users with different political beliefs.

### 17. Religion / Universal Human Ground
- Human Maximus is not intended as an exclusive religion.
- It can be explored by people from different religious, spiritual, agnostic, or secular backgrounds.
- The common ground is human dignity, awareness, growth, responsibility, compassion, learning, and creation.

### 18. Relationships / Compassion
- Do not give other people control over your identity.
- Do not intentionally dehumanize people who disagree.
- Seek to understand before reacting.
- Boundaries and compassion can coexist.
- Other people can become mirrors and teachers without being treated as enemies.

### 19. Work / Creation / Entrepreneurship
- Create rather than merely consume.
- Use constraints as invitations to become resourceful.
- Build, test, improve, and deliver.
- AI is viewed as a leverage tool for human capability, not as a replacement for human agency.

### 20. AI / Human Empowerment
- Master AI without surrendering human judgment.
- Use intelligence to learn, create, solve, build, teach, and generate opportunity.
- The larger aspiration is human + AI capability compounding rather than fear-driven resistance.

### 21. Daily Practice
- What do I want to learn today?
- What do I want to create today?
- What matters most today?
- What state am I in?
- Am I above or below the line?
- What can I learn from what happened?
- What is the next meaningful action?
