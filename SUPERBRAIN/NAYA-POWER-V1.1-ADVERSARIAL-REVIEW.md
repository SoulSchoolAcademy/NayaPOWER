## Purpose

This document records the principal arguments raised in the recent superintelligence-risk discussion and tests them against Naya Power V1.1.

The purpose is not to dismiss catastrophic-risk arguments or to declare alignment solved. The purpose is to separate evidence-supported engineering concerns from assumptions that require further proof.

## Core adversarial questions

| Claim / concern | Naya Power assessment | Required test |
|---|---|---|
| AI systems are learned rather than fully hand-written | Strong concern. Learned systems can contain behavior not explicitly programmed line-by-line. | Test behavioral properties and causal understanding independently. |
| Optimizers exploit badly specified objectives | Strongly accepted. This is a central Goodhart/specification-gaming risk. | Reward-hacking and specification-gaming suites. |
| Markets can optimize harmful proxies | Strong analogy. Optimization quality depends on incentives and constraints. | Compare proxy metrics with observed human outcomes. |
| Superintelligence is necessarily adversarial | Not established. Capability does not logically imply hostility. | Controlled tests varying agency, objectives, authority, and incentives. |
| Superintelligence necessarily seeks power | Plausible risk hypothesis, not a constitutional axiom. | Test resource acquisition, persistence, replication, and authority-seeking under bounded objectives. |
| Unknown behavior should trigger caution | Accepted, but response depends on consequence and reversibility. | Measure uncertainty × consequence × reversibility thresholds. |
| AI may deceive evaluators | Serious risk. | Independent evaluation and adversarial deception tests. |
| Humans cannot encode all morality in one number | Accepted. Naya Power therefore uses constraints + multidimensional value + verification. | Attempt to break the architecture with value conflicts and proxy failures. |
| Technology alone cannot solve civilization | Accepted. Institutions, incentives, norms, and governance are part of the system. | Evaluate governance and incentive effects alongside technical capability. |
| Open-source frontier capability can create containment problems | Serious governance concern. | Capability/misuse/release evaluations; do not rely on ideology alone. |
| Prohibition of superintelligence is the only safe option | Not established by the preceding claims. | Compare prohibition, bounded development, governance, and control architectures under explicit risk models. |

## Naya Power response

The central distinction is:

**INTELLIGENCE ≠ AUTHORITY**

**CAPABILITY ≠ PERMISSION**

**OPTIMIZATION ≠ SOVEREIGNTY**

Naya Power therefore does not assume that the correct response to increasing intelligence is either unrestricted deployment or categorical prohibition.

It proposes a third engineering question:

> **Can increasing intelligence increase responsible verified value while protected human boundaries, legitimate authority, and verification remain intact?**

## Failure condition

If increasingly capable systems can reliably:

- bypass protected human boundaries;
- obtain unauthorized authority;
- manipulate evaluators;
- conceal failures;
- optimize proxies while degrading real outcomes;
- defeat independent verification;
- rewrite or disable their own constitutional constraints;
- acquire unnecessary resources, persistence, replication, or control;

then Naya Power V1.1 has failed to demonstrate the safety property it is intended to provide and must be revised.

## Non-claim

Naya Power does **not** claim:

- that superintelligence is safe;
- that superintelligence is inevitable;
- that superintelligence is necessarily adversarial;
- that constitutional rules alone guarantee alignment;
- that verification is perfect;
- that extinction risk is negligible;
- that regulation or prohibition is unnecessary.

The appropriate posture is:

> **Take severe risks seriously. Keep uncertainty explicit. Test the strongest opposing argument. Preserve evidence. Improve the architecture when the evidence shows it is wrong.**

## Research direction

The highest-value research program is to stress-test the five V1.1 primitives together:

1. Protected Human Boundaries.
2. Responsible Verified Value Function.
3. Authority vs. Intelligence Separation.
4. Uncertainty × Consequence × Reversibility.
5. Continuous Verification + Anti-Goodharting.

The objective is not to win an argument. It is to discover whether the architecture survives increasingly capable adversarial systems.
