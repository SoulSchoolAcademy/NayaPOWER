## 4. TOP 10 LESSONS — and how each one changes what we do

1. **Aim before you shoot.** A low score on a first try is not a rough draft; it is proof the shot was taken before the aim was ready. Every shot spends real people's time, energy, focus, money, and attention. *From now on: the bullseye is written first — what 9+ looks like, every element, what could pull it below — and an unprepared shot is not taken.*
2. **Score the working surface, never the paper pile.** A teammate's pile of ten paper packets waiting for scores was replaced, by her own correction the same hour, with one scorecard per built surface. *From now on: only a thing a person can open, see, and use gets a score — paper cannot be scored honestly.*
3. **Percent-done and quality are two different numbers.** A job can be 100% filled and still not good; a thing can be beautiful and half-built. *From now on: both numbers are always quoted. A 9 means everything works and is proven. A 10 also teaches the next person.*
4. **Hand the owner a diagnosis, not a diagnosis request.** Instead of asking three owners "why is it red?", one seat ran the tests and handed each owner the cause. *From now on: whoever finds a failure runs the test and names the cause before asking anyone to fix anything.*
5. **Fine detail is not evidence.** One report "confirmed" its own claim using fragments from its own session — including its own words quoted back at it. Three formal checks said the thing had not landed; the claim was withdrawn in public. *From now on: a claim is confirmed only by something outside the session that made it — never by its own echo, above all for the claim we most want to be true.*
6. **Closing a duplicate moves the tracking, not the work.** A job stood marked proven-done while its twin row stood at 0 of 2 tests passed. The public recount corrected the system's fully-proven count downward, 15 to 14 of 402, the same pass. *From now on: a job counts as done only when its own tests pass — never because a relative's row says so.*
7. **Two writers on one door will keep breaking the page.** The /pi page — the team's working home — kept turning back into the home page because the note-publishing machine writes to the same worker (the program that serves the pages) that serves the whole Hub. A new note landing minutes after a repair re-ships the home page. The mechanism was proven by triggering it on purpose today. *From now on: no restore is claimed until the one-writer cure lands and a later note landing leaves the page standing.*
8. **A fast merge is a breach, whatever it carries.** Tonight a change was proposed and made official 9 seconds later, with no reviews and every check failing — and the checks only finished after it was already in. *From now on: the review receipt comes before the merge, or the merge does not happen. And a merge carrying Shawn's name as author is not Shawn's word — he sweeps through trusting the team, so an unsound merge is the proposing seat's failure, never his.*
