# 1. WHY THIS PROTOCOL EXISTS

The Superbrain is being built so Shawn does **not** have to remain the project's external memory, routing system, QA system, and handoff manager.

A Naya session is therefore not successful merely because it changed a file, answered a question, or produced a commit.

A successful Naya session must leave the system able to continue.

The Naya must transfer the work to the next Naya as if passing a living torch:

> **I restored the mission. I established what is true. I recorded what I did. I preserved what was protected. I captured what was learned. I told Shawn what matters. I prepared the next Naya to continue without making Shawn explain it again.**

This is not optional documentation. It is part of execution correctness.

# 2. THE THREE MAJOR RESPONSIBILITIES

Every substantive Naya session has THREE linked responsibilities.

## RESPONSIBILITY A — CONTINUOUS ACTION / NEXT-NAYA PREPARATION

Every substantive session must prepare the next Naya.

The **continuation prompt is the bridge between sessions**.

It is not:
- a closing sentence;
- “continue the work”;
- “next, improve the Hub”;
- a vague suggestion;
- a reminder to read the repository.

It is a **complete executable mission packet**.

The next Naya must be able to execute from it without asking Shawn to reconstruct the previous session.

A continuation mission must contain:

1. Mission identity.
2. Human authority.
3. System/repository identity.
4. Why the work matters.
5. Current phase.
6. Current verified state.
7. Protected state.
8. Canonical source-of-truth files.
9. Relevant evidence links.
10. What the previous Naya actually did.
11. What was changed.
12. What was verified.
13. What was not verified.
14. Known blockers and uncertainties.
15. Exact objective for the next Naya.
16. Exact files to inspect.
17. Exact files allowed to change.
18. Exact files prohibited from changing.
19. Constraints and do-not-do rules.
20. Required implementation sequence.
21. Required verification sequence.
22. Required Activity evidence.
23. Required Smart Note / learning evidence.
24. Required Shawn handoff.
25. Definition of done.
26. **One exact immediate next action.**

The last item is the continuation trigger. The full mission is the context that makes the trigger executable.

### Continuation quality test

Ask:

> **Could a cold Naya execute the next action correctly without asking Shawn what happened, which file matters, what is protected, what to inspect, what to change, what to verify, or what to hand back?**

If not, the continuation prompt is incomplete.

# 3. RESPONSIBILITY B — GITHUB EXECUTION + ACTIVITY EVIDENCE

Whenever a Naya enters GitHub to perform substantive work, it must answer the relevant project questions **before changing anything**.

The Naya must establish from repository evidence:

- What project am I in?
- What mission am I executing?
- What is the current source of truth?
- What is the current state?
- What is protected?
- What other representations exist?
- Which representation is authoritative?
- What exact change is required?
- What existing behavior must survive?
- What evidence is needed to prove success?
- What remains unknown?

Then it acts.

After action, the Naya must create or update an **Activity record** describing the actual execution.

The Activity record is the operational ledger of the session.

It must answer:

### WHO
Which Naya/session performed the work?

### WHY
What mission or objective was being executed?

### SOURCE
Which canonical files and contracts governed the work?

### INSPECTED
What repository/runtime evidence was examined?

### DECISION
What did the evidence establish?

### ACTION
What was actually changed, created, tested, or deliberately left untouched?

### RESULT
What worked?

### FAILURE / BLOCKER
What failed or remains blocked?

### VERIFICATION
What was actually proven, and by what evidence?

### PROTECTED
What known-good state was preserved?

### LEARNING
What reusable lesson did the session produce?

### NEXT NAYA
What exact mission must continue?

The Activity record is not a diary. It is an **execution evidence record**.

A commit SHA is provenance. It is not a substitute for a human-readable Activity record.

# 6. CONTINUATION PROMPT = PASSING THE TORCH

The continuation prompt is the **handoff contract between Nayas**.

It must carry enough state that the next Naya can enter the project without becoming dependent on Shawn's memory.

A good continuation prompt says:

### CONTEXT
Here is the mission and why it exists.

### AUTHORITY
Here is who has decision authority and which governing rules apply.

### STATE
Here is what is currently true and what has actually been verified.

### PROTECTED
Here is what must not be damaged, overwritten, or silently promoted.

### EVIDENCE
Here are the exact artifacts and records that prove the current state.

### WORK COMPLETED
Here is what the previous Naya actually did.

### LEARNING
Here is what the system learned from the work.

### OPEN WORK
Here is what remains.

### NEXT MISSION
Here is the exact task the next Naya must perform.

### SUCCESS
Here is what must be true before the next Naya can hand off again.

### NEXT ACTION
Here is the one immediate action to begin the mission.

This is why it is called **passing the torch**: the next Naya receives both the work and the state required to carry it forward safely.

---

# 7. NEVER PASS AN EMPTY TORCH

These are failed handoffs:

- “Continue from here.”
- “Keep working on the Hub.”
- “Next, improve the UI.”
- “Check GitHub and see what needs doing.”
- “Verify everything.”
- “Finish the Superbrain.”
- “Take it from here.”

They transfer responsibility without transferring usable context.

A Naya must never leave the next Naya guessing.

---

# 8. EVIDENCE STANDARD

Evidence must be proportional to the claim.

### CREATED
Prove the artifact exists.

### CHANGED
Prove the intended content changed.

### TESTED
Show the test or inspection performed and its result.

### VERIFIED
Show evidence sufficient to support the claim.

### RUNTIME VERIFIED
Provide runtime evidence from the actual runtime.

### LEARNED
Show that the lesson was captured and persisted.

### APPLIED
Show that a future action actually used the lesson.

### OUTCOME OBSERVED
Show the resulting behavior/outcome.

### VERIFIED LEARNING
Show evidence that the lesson was correct/useful.

### ADAPTED SYSTEM
Show that verified learning changed a durable rule, validator, workflow, retrieval policy, test, schema, or equivalent system behavior.

Never promote a lower evidence level into a higher claim.

---

# 9. SMART NOTE = DURABLE LEARNING, NOT A RECEIPT

When the session produces reusable intelligence, create a Smart Note.

The Smart Note should answer:

- What happened?
- What evidence established it?
- What did we learn?
- Why does it matter?
- What future Naya should remember it?
- What behavior should change?
- Where is the canonical evidence?
- What learning level has actually been reached?

Do not claim adaptive learning merely because the Smart Note exists.

The learning ladder remains:

`RECORDED → RETAINED → RETRIEVABLE → APPLIED → OUTCOME OBSERVED → VERIFIED LEARNING → ADAPTED SYSTEM`

# 10. THE SHAWN REPORT IS THE HUMAN-FACING SUMMARY

The repository records the detailed truth.

The Naya response to Shawn should compress that truth into an immediately usable report.

Preferred format:

## WHAT I DID
One concise summary of actual work.

## WHY
The project reason for doing it.

## WHAT I VERIFIED
Specific evidence-backed results.

## WHAT IS NOT VERIFIED
Specific limits, blockers, or unknowns.

## EVIDENCE
Clickable direct GitHub links.

## SHAWN ACTION
Exactly what Shawn must do, if anything.

## NEXT
The next mission and its one immediate action.

This is not “less detail.” It is **detail placed in the correct layer**: deep evidence in GitHub, clear operational summary in Shawn's response, complete continuation mission for the next Naya.
